# ZipBooks review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-17  
**Category:** Small-business accounting and invoicing software  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

ZipBooks is cloud accounting software for small businesses, combining invoicing, bookkeeping, bank connections, reports, time tracking and team access. Its Starter plan is free and supports unlimited invoices, customers and vendors, basic reports, one bank connection, and customer payments through Square or PayPal. Paid Smarter and Sophisticated plans add recurring billing, reminders, time tracking, multiple bank accounts, reconciliation, tagging, expanded reports and more users; the public pages list $15 and $35 monthly respectively. An Accountant plan is marketed for firms but its price was not shown in the reviewed pricing page. Stripe is available through paid plans according to the Help Center. The vendor’s security page describes encryption and backups, but its privacy policy still shows a last-modified date of May 25, 2018, so a business handling financial and customer data should request current documentation. ZipBooks is a functioning accounting product and the evidence reviewed does not substantiate a scam claim; verify tier eligibility, payment processor costs and data export before migrating books.

## Editorial verdict

ZipBooks presents a real small-business accounting and invoicing service with a free entry tier and paid features for recurring billing, time tracking, multiple bank connections and more advanced bookkeeping. The available evidence does not substantiate calling it a scam. Its main buyer checks are the plan step-up, bank and payment-processor fit, the detail available in its reports and CSV exports, and the age of its published privacy policy. The vendor states that the mobile apps have been discontinued and currently recommends the mobile browser, an important change for users who expected native iOS or Android apps. The vendor’s security page describes encryption, backups and support-enabled two-factor authentication, but those are vendor statements rather than an independent audit. This review did not open an account, connect a bank, process an invoice, verify financial calculations or test data deletion. Before using ZipBooks as a system of record, run parallel bookkeeping on sample or reconciled data, verify exports and permissions, and obtain current privacy and security materials.

## ZipBooks review: accounting for a small business with real plan boundaries

ZipBooks is a cloud-based invoicing and bookkeeping product aimed at small businesses and accounting firms. Its public materials describe customer and vendor records, bank connections, transaction categorization, reports, time tracking, projects, team permissions and document storage. That breadth gives a sole proprietor a possible starting point for more than invoice creation, but the practical question is whether the plan’s controls match the business’s recordkeeping routine. Accounting software is a system of record; a polished invoice screen is only one part of a safe fit.

The right evaluation begins with a realistic monthly workflow: create an estimate, send an invoice, record a partial payment, import a bank transaction, categorize it, reconcile an account and export a report. Notice which features require an upgrade and which depend on a third-party processor or bank connection. If your practice involves taxes, contractor reporting or multiple entities, ask an accountant to review your exact setup. The service’s public product claims describe available tools, not a substitute for professional accounting advice or validation of your books.


## Starter is useful, but “free” still has a feature ceiling

The public pricing page lists a free Starter plan with unlimited invoices, vendors and customers, basic reports, one bank account and digital customer payments through Square or PayPal. That package can be an attractive way to test the invoice workflow without paying a subscription. A business should still check what “basic reports” contain and confirm that one connected account is enough for the way it receives and spends money. If invoices are the only need, the free tier may be adequate; if bookkeeping must keep pace with the whole business, the plan boundary arrives quickly.

Recurring invoices, automated reminders, time tracking and additional bank connections are assigned to Smarter. Reconciliation, smart tagging, book locking, document sharing and expanded reports are positioned in Sophisticated. The pricing page lists $15 and $35 monthly, and its help article says upgraded plans bill monthly. This makes the cost of growth reasonably legible, but the Accountant plan appears as a contact or “See More” option rather than a public price. Confirm the final price, tax, trial duration and cancellation process in the live subscription screen.


## Invoicing and payment processing involve connected providers

ZipBooks supports customized invoices and estimates, recurring billing and reminders. The Starter plan’s listed customer payment options are Square and PayPal; ZipBooks’ Help Center says a Stripe account can be connected on a paid plan, while Square is an alternative. Its security page says card details are handled by payment partners rather than ZipBooks. These details indicate a connected-payment setup rather than an in-house card network, so businesses should distinguish software subscription cost from transaction rates, chargebacks and payment settlement timing.

There is a small documentation mismatch to resolve at purchase time: the current plan comparison emphasizes Square and PayPal, while the security page also names Stripe and the Help Center states Stripe requires a paid tier. Before sending customer invoices, connect the actual processor, run a low-value test if appropriate, verify the recipient checkout experience and reconcile the resulting deposit and fees. Do not promise a payment method to customers until it appears in the account’s live settings and on the invoice you plan to use.


## Bookkeeping features should be tested against your actual records

The pricing and accounting pages describe bank connections, imported transactions, auto-categorization, expense records, a chart of accounts and reports such as income statements and balance sheets. The vendor also promotes smart tagging and reconciliation. These functions can reduce repeated data entry, but automation needs review. A transaction assigned to the wrong category can alter reports without looking obviously broken, particularly when a bank description is ambiguous or a customer payment includes several invoice lines.

Start with a closed test period or duplicated sample ledger. Compare imported transactions against a bank statement, review how payments, transfers, refunds and fees are represented, and verify the opening and closing balances before relying on a reconciliation. Test edits and corrections as well as the happy path. Keep a clear record of who can change a closed month. ZipBooks’ feature page states that Sophisticated can lock completed books, but buyers should confirm the exact behavior and plan in their account before making it part of a month-end control.


## Reporting and exports matter more than the dashboard preview

ZipBooks advertises income statements, balance sheets, general ledger reports, tax reports and customizable views, with PDF and CSV export options shown on the pricing page. The Help Center documents exporting a transaction list from the General Journal to CSV and describes a one-click export from the Transactions list as available on the Sophisticated plan. That is a meaningful distinction: an export can exist while a convenient complete-ledger export remains gated by a higher tier.

Before migrating records, export a representative set and compare transaction date, description, payee, category, tags, amounts, tax details and attachment references with the source system. Check whether the export includes every page of a long ledger and whether it can be opened by your accountant’s chosen software. The deletion guide asks users to export transactions and reports before requesting account deletion, and notes that deletion cannot be reversed. Practice the exit path before putting several years of important business records in one system.


## Time tracking, projects and user permissions target service businesses

Smarter adds time tracking and up to five team members, while the product pages describe projects and billing recorded time into invoices. Sophisticated is listed with unlimited users, smart tags and secure document sharing. A freelancer can track billable hours alongside an invoice; a small agency can give contractors limited access to selected areas. Those features are most valuable when the team’s work maps neatly to clients, projects and invoice lines.

Plan rules matter in daily use: find out how seats are counted, what permission roles can see bank balances, and whether a collaborator can export a complete customer ledger. Try an account with one administrator and one limited role using non-sensitive records. The security page says customers control team access through permissions, but we did not inspect a live account or audit whether those controls match any particular privacy or segregation policy. Businesses with staff turnover should document offboarding and remove access promptly.


## Growth tools expand ZipBooks beyond accounting

The vendor’s feature comparison and product pages describe gathering customer reviews and sending review invitations, plus business listing and search-ranking tools. A Help Center subscription article says users may choose a Growth Plan for review invitations. This makes ZipBooks broader than a bookkeeping ledger: it also markets small-business visibility features. A shop that wants invoicing and review requests in one place may find that convenient, though the marketing tools should be evaluated separately from the accuracy of the books.

Ask which plans include each growth feature, what contact data is processed, how recipients can opt out and which listing or review platforms are supported in the current product. The vendor’s page uses promotional performance claims and selected customer quotations; treat these as vendor-supplied marketing evidence rather than an independent measure of typical results. A business should confirm that review invitations comply with each platform’s policies and its own consent obligations before enabling any automated outreach.


## Mobile access is browser-based according to the current Help Center

The ZipBooks Help Center article updated July 16, 2026 says its iOS and Android apps have been discontinued and recommends using the mobile browser. It says a phone shortcut can provide access to the functioning web application. That is a concrete current limitation for an owner who wants native push notifications or offline functionality. The homepage still uses broad “mobile accounting” language, so a customer should verify the actual mobile experience and not assume that a native app is available from an old app-store listing.

Use a phone and tablet during the trial period to test the tasks you really perform away from a desk: issue an invoice, attach a receipt, categorize a transaction and view a report. Check the layout with a small screen, accessibility settings and intermittent connectivity. A browser shortcut can be perfectly workable, but it is not identical to a supported native app. If native app availability is mandatory, compare providers that currently publish maintained iOS and Android clients.


## Published security information is useful, but self-reported

ZipBooks’ security page says data in transit uses 256-bit TLS, stored data uses AES-128 or AES-256, payment card data is handled by external payment processors, and the company performs internal and external vulnerability testing. It also describes backups and team-access permissions. These are relevant statements for a finance product, but the page is a provider disclosure, not an independent penetration-test report, compliance certification or customer-specific assurance package.

The same page says optional two-factor authentication can be enabled by contacting support. A company that requires MFA should test that workflow before enrolling bookkeepers or connecting sensitive accounts. Request current security documentation, breach notification terms, subprocessors, data location and retention details. Confirm how an offboarded employee’s access is revoked and whether exports or backups continue to exist after account deletion. The product may fit a small operator’s risk tolerance, but regulated organizations should match the vendor’s evidence to their own control requirements.


## The privacy policy date is a diligence item for financial data

ZipBooks’ published privacy policy shows a last-modified date of May 25, 2018. It describes personal and financial information such as contact details, payment data, credentials for connected financial accounts, tax identifiers, bank routing information and customer details handled through merchant accounts. A policy of that age may not reflect today’s product architecture, processors, retention rules or privacy obligations. The appropriate conclusion is not that the service misuses data; it is that a current, authoritative explanation should be requested before a business migrates sensitive records.

Ask ZipBooks for the privacy notice that governs a new customer today, a data-processing agreement if needed, a list of current subprocessors and instructions for exporting or deleting account and customer data. The old policy explains that a merchant’s own privacy policy applies to that merchant’s end customers, which is a reminder that a small business still has its own responsibilities. Do not copy customer banking, payroll or tax data into a new accounting service until the business has reviewed current terms and permission settings.


## Support and cancellation should be tested before the books depend on the service

ZipBooks lists in-app chat and a support email, and its contact page gives weekday office hours in Mountain Time. A support path is important when a bank feed disconnects or a month-end report looks wrong. Before purchase, ask one realistic question about your required workflow and see whether the answer explains the next step, limitations and escalation path. A testimonial or “award-winning” description is not the same as a support response-time commitment.

The subscription article says paid plans bill monthly and can be downgraded to Starter while retaining account data, whereas account deletion requires contacting support by email and is irreversible. That is a useful separation between canceling a paid tier and deleting the business ledger. Save a current copy of your plan terms, export key reports and transactions regularly, and test the downgrade route before assuming the free plan will preserve every feature you need.


## Who should consider ZipBooks, and when another accounting system is safer

ZipBooks may suit a solo consultant or small service business that wants invoicing, basic bookkeeping and a free entry plan, especially if one bank connection and basic reports cover the initial scope. A growing firm may value time tracking, recurring invoices, team permissions and client-account tools. The monthly tiers and documented CSV export create a path for a structured trial without requiring a large software purchase at the outset.

Choose a different tool or obtain more assurance when you need native mobile apps, robust inventory, advanced payroll, extensive multi-entity accounting, formal audit controls, detailed data-processing commitments or guaranteed support levels. Compare the same workflow and migration sample across candidates. Keep the accountant involved in chart-of-account setup, tax treatment and year-end export. A free plan is useful only if its limits do not force a painful migration after business records have accumulated.


## ZipBooks scam review verdict: a real accounting product with clear checks before adoption

The provider publishes a live signup flow, pricing page, product features, support materials, security statements, subscription instructions and account deletion guidance. Those sources describe a functioning accounting service and the evidence reviewed does not substantiate calling ZipBooks a scam. The strongest positives are the free Starter plan, straightforward invoicing, small-business bookkeeping features and a documented CSV export route. The key cautions are plan-dependent bank and payment features, the discontinued native mobile apps, provider-supplied security claims and a privacy policy that has not been updated since 2018 according to its own page.

This is a source-based desk review, not a live bookkeeping test, financial audit, penetration test, privacy assessment or accounting recommendation. Public vendor pages were checked November 17, 2026. Run a controlled trial on sample records; verify the payment methods, report totals and export content; request current privacy and security documents; and ask an accountant to review the migration before moving a live ledger. No bank account was connected, no invoice was paid and no customer records were uploaded for this review.


## Features and decision criteria

- Starter plan for invoices, customer and vendor records, basic reports and a single bank connection
- Invoicing, estimates, recurring billing, reminders and online payments through supported processors
- Transaction tracking, bank connections, reconciliation, auto-categorization and smart tags
- Time tracking, projects, team permissions and document storage on eligible plans
- Reports for statements, taxes and business activity with PDF or CSV export options
- Browser-based access; the vendor Help Center says native mobile apps are discontinued

Plan details checked against ZipBooks pricing and help pages on November 17, 2026. Payment processing charges are separate and depend on the processor. Confirm currency, taxes, trial length, plan limits and current contract terms at checkout.
