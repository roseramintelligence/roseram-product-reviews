# 501 Auctions review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-17  
**Category:** Nonprofit auctions and event fundraising software  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

501 Auctions was an event auction and mobile-bidding product for nonprofits. GiveSmart’s official history says 501 Auctions, Gesture and GiveSmart were brought together under the GiveSmart platform in 2018, and that the former 501 team and technology became part of GiveSmart. The older 501 product page remains available and describes branded event sites, registration, mobile bidding, checkout, one-to-one help and equipment rentals. Current commercial information is published under GiveSmart: it offers an annual subscription or a $0-upfront Pay-As-You-Go plan with a transaction rate disclosed before signup, but does not publish a flat annual price on the page reviewed.

## Editorial verdict

The official pages establish a recognizable nonprofit event-fundraising product history and describe a current GiveSmart platform; the evidence reviewed does not substantiate calling 501 Auctions a scam. Buyers should treat 501 Auctions as a legacy product name whose features and commercial terms now sit within GiveSmart, then verify what is included in the current contract. The main diligence issues are quote-based annual pricing, a variable Pay-As-You-Go transaction rate, event-specific participant-data use, firm auction rules and the difference between included support and separately excluded training or customization. No organization account, event, payment, settlement, bidder flow or contract was independently tested.

## 501 Auctions review: a legacy name within the GiveSmart platform

501 Auctions began as auction and mobile-bidding software for nonprofit events. The legacy 501 page describes branded fundraising sites, bidder registration, smartphone bidding, checkout and event equipment. The product identity needs context before a buyer compares vendors: GiveSmart’s official company history says 501 Auctions, Gesture and GiveSmart were brought together under the GiveSmart platform in 2018, and that the team and technology from 501 Auctions became part of GiveSmart. The 501 product page still exists, but current product, pricing and support information is primarily presented under GiveSmart.

That history is not a complaint or warning by itself. It changes the purchasing question. An organization looking for “501 Auctions” should confirm whether it is buying a separately maintained SKU, migrating an old account or contracting for the present GiveSmart Events & Fundraise platform. The historical brand page is useful for understanding the auction workflow, but it should not be treated as a current contract or technical specification.


## Event pages, registration and mobile bidding

The 501 Auctions page describes custom-branded event sites with registration, an item catalogue and bidding interface. It lists ticket sales, bidder registration and the option to collect payment information before an event or at the door. The mobile-bidding flow is meant to let guests follow items on their phones, receive outbid notifications and make donations. For a gala or charity auction, these functions can bring ticketing, bids and event-night operations into one workflow instead of relying solely on paper bid sheets.

The current GiveSmart pricing page places auctions alongside online ticketing, guest management, donations, peer-to-peer campaigns, text-to-give and reporting. This breadth can reduce the number of tools an event team has to coordinate, but it also means staff need to decide which features they will actually configure. Before selecting a plan, map the registration list, auction items, payment method, bidder communications, tax receipts and check-in process. A demo should use the organization’s own event structure, not only a simplified showcase.


## Checkout, payments and event operations

The legacy page describes a Checkout Center for card charges, split payments, donation records and tax receipts, and says proceeds are automatically disbursed to an organization’s checking account within 48 hours. Treat the timing as a publisher statement that should be confirmed for the current product, payment method and organization approval status. Payment settlement can depend on processor rules, bank calendars, transaction review and the organization’s own contract; do not use an old product page as a guarantee of the present payout schedule.

The same page advertises event equipment packages such as registration stations, card readers and mobile-bidding devices, with technical support. Confirm whether those items are available in the current GiveSmart agreement, how shipping and return work, what happens if a device fails and whether on-site support is included or priced separately. If the event depends on connectivity, create a plan for poor wireless service, printed backup lists, check-in queues and late payments. The platform can support operations; it cannot remove the need for an event-night contingency.


## Current pricing: $0 start does not mean free processing

GiveSmart’s current pricing page offers an Annual Fee Subscription and Pay-As-You-Go. The annual plan is described as a fixed annual fee with no platform transaction fees. Pay-As-You-Go has no annual upfront subscription and starts at $0, but a transaction rate applies to payments and is disclosed at signup and in the customer agreement. The organization may let supporters cover that fee at checkout. The page does not publish the annual dollar amount or the Pay-As-You-Go percentage, so a buyer cannot calculate the full price from the public page alone.

Ask for a written quote using a realistic event scenario: ticket volume, auction sales, donations, refunds, payment types, annual campaign count, support needs and any equipment. Separate the platform rate from processor fees and any donor-paid surcharge. Model a quiet year as well as a strong fundraising year, and confirm whether the contract changes if volume rises. The current GiveSmart pricing describes its platform, not a separate 501 Auctions plan; historic quotes from a prior contract may use different products or fee terms.


## Participant information, text messages and event-level rules

GiveSmart’s auction terms say event participants’ information is provided to the auction sponsor and may be used for the sponsor’s programs and activities. They allow the sponsor to enter it into its database and use vendors that help with those activities, while restricting onward sale or sharing unless the participant gives specific permission. The terms also describe text messages about the event, bids and auction status for people who provide a mobile number, and state that SMS consent is not required to register or bid. Participants can text STOP to stop GiveSmart messages.

The Momentive privacy policy says customer organizations own the end-user information collected on their hosted sites and determine collection and use under their customer agreements. It also says the organization may administer accounts and access associated data. A nonprofit should therefore review both vendor terms and its own privacy notice, decide what contact fields are required, limit staff access, and explain how donor or bidder information will be used after the event. Do not assume the software vendor alone controls the outreach or retention decisions.


## Auction rules, refunds and buyer expectations

GiveSmart’s posted auction terms state that bids are final and binding, that the event promoter controls item descriptions, and that bidders should verify details with the promoter. They describe reserve prices, close times, treatment of ties and the option for the organizer to withdraw a lot if it cannot be awarded. The event organization remains responsible for accurate item details, fulfillment, bidder questions and applicable tax or charitable-receipt rules. A platform can register bids and payments, but the sponsor defines the lots and must resolve the real-world obligations attached to them.

Before launch, publish the event’s own rules for cancellations, refunds, pickup, shipping, experience dates, substitutions and nontransferable packages. Confirm whether the event’s terms are displayed and accepted before bidding, and test the complete bidder journey on a phone. The GiveSmart website terms are not a substitute for the nonprofit’s event-specific policies or legal advice.


## Support, implementation and staff responsibilities

GiveSmart advertises implementation assistance, a Client Success Manager and 24/7 support across its plan models. Its support coverage page says support can troubleshoot platform problems, clarify previously trained workflows and help users recover from mistakes. It separately lists product training, third-party integrations, tax guidance, custom CSS and some data repair as outside the usual support scope. This division is worth discussing when the event depends on custom design, a CRM connection or a complicated donor import.

A small organization should assign an event owner, technical contact, payment approver and backup administrator. Test bulk item imports, ticket limits, bidder notifications, check-in and closing the auction before the event opens to the public. Ask how account roles work and what historical reports remain available when a staff member leaves. The promise of extensive support is more useful when the agreement specifies what can be called in during a live event and what must be handled by the organization.


## Alternatives and how to compare fairly

Compare GiveSmart’s current auction platform with other nonprofit event-fundraising tools using the same event plan. Look at mobile bidding usability, ticketing and seating, donor CRM, recurring gifts, text messaging, payment settlement, data export, equipment, training, emergency support, and the ability to run both small online auctions and a large in-person gala. Annual subscription pricing may suit consistent event volume; a transaction-based model may be easier to enter but more expensive at scale. No competing product was benchmarked in this review.

Use a small pilot or a scripted demo with your own auction items and registration flow. Have a volunteer complete the process without coaching, test a bidder who changes their phone number, process a split payment, cancel a ticket and export the data. Record the time and support needed for each task. This exposes operational friction that a feature checklist may miss.


## Is 501 Auctions a scam? Evidence-based conclusion

Official company history explains that 501 Auctions was integrated with GiveSmart in 2018, and official product, pricing and support pages now describe a nonprofit auction and event-fundraising service under the GiveSmart name. The evidence reviewed does not substantiate calling 501 Auctions or GiveSmart a scam. The important practical issue is product continuity: the 501 Auctions name and feature page are legacy references, while current pricing and service commitments belong to GiveSmart and must be established in a current written agreement.

This is a source-based desk review, not an event launch, contract review, processor verification, settlement test or firsthand support evaluation. Before signing, confirm the exact platform, annual or transaction charges, processing costs, event equipment, payout timing, refund policy, data access, migration plan and event-specific terms with GiveSmart.

- Current GiveSmart platform adds campaign, ticketing, donation and reporting tools under shared plans
