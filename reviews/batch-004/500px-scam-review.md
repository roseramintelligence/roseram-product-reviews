# 500px review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-17  
**Category:** Photography community, portfolio and image licensing marketplace  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

500px is a photography community and portfolio platform with paid memberships and a separate contributor licensing program. Photographers may submit eligible images for review and potential licensing through Getty Images and Visual China Group. The contributor FAQ states that exclusive and non-exclusive submissions have different net-revenue shares, while licensing approval is not automatic and AI-generated or substantially AI-edited images are restricted. Membership pricing is presented through a dynamic upgrade page; a reliable static price was not available in this review capture. These features and policies were checked on November 17, 2026.

## Editorial verdict

500px publishes current terms, member support guidance, licensing rules and an account-deletion process, and the evidence reviewed does not support calling it a scam. The main contributor risks concern exclusivity, release forms, image-review acceptance, revenue variability and the distinction between gross sale price and the stated share of net sales. Account exit can also be constrained: a support article says accounts with current or past licensing sales or marketplace purchases cannot be permanently deleted, while deactivation can take up to 180 days to propagate licensing-photo removal to distribution partners. No paid membership, photo submission, licensing sale or payout was independently tested.

## 500px review: a photography community with a separate stock-licensing path

500px combines public photographer profiles and image discovery with paid membership features and an optional commercial licensing program. A photographer can build a portfolio and participate in the community without licensing images; licensing is a separate submission choice, subject to review by 500px and its distribution partners. This separation is useful when assessing the service: a membership can provide portfolio or community tools, but it does not automatically make every uploaded photo a stock image or create a sale.

The licensing FAQ names Getty Images and Visual China Group (VCG) as distribution partners for approved royalty-free photos. That is a meaningful route to market, but each submitted image must pass the platform’s editorial and partner review. The presence of a licensing dashboard should not be mistaken for guaranteed acceptance, placement, downloads or income.


## Photo licensing, exclusivity and contributor earnings

The contributor FAQ describes licensing as optional and says it costs nothing to submit. It distinguishes exclusive material, which is offered commercially only through 500px and its partners, from non-exclusive material that may also be available elsewhere or was licensed before. The published revenue share is 60% of net sales for exclusive images from Free members, up to 100% for Plus or Pro members, and 25% of net sales for non-exclusive images. The word “net” matters: these shares do not mean the same percentage of a customer-facing list price, and the platform does not promise that a specific image will sell.

A contributor should compare the higher stated exclusive share with the opportunity cost of giving 500px and its partners an exclusive commercial channel for that image. Read the applicable licensing agreement, define which files are submitted, verify whether past commercial licensing prevents exclusivity, and retain a record of releases and permissions. An image may still be usable for self-promotion, but the FAQ says it cannot be commercially licensed through another agency during an exclusive arrangement.


## Submission review and model or property releases

500px says submitted images are reviewed by its editors before buyers can see them and then inspected separately by distribution partners. The licensing dashboard can show statuses such as under review, changes required, accepted, declined or archived. The review process can take time, and a decision reflects editorial and commercial-viability criteria, not an assurance that the photograph lacks quality if it is declined.

The contributor FAQ says recognizable people generally require a model release, including self-portraits, and that a recognizable establishment or location may require a property release for commercial use. Contributors should retain original files and signed permissions, avoid submitting imagery they do not own or control, and check the current release rules for each photo. No particular portfolio was reviewed as part of this desk review.


## AI-generated and AI-edited image rules

500px’s licensing FAQ says AI-generated content is not eligible for its licensing program and extends the restriction to images where more than 10% has been edited with AI-powered tools. The company warns that submitting material against this rule could lead to account suspension or permanent banning. If a photograph is flagged by mistake, its FAQ asks the photographer to provide the image and original RAW file for review.

This is a licensing-program rule, not a universal statement about every kind of image a member may display in a community portfolio. Read the current policy for the intended use, preserve original capture files and edit history, and contact support if a genuine photograph is incorrectly identified. Do not assume that a photograph is licensing-eligible merely because it appears on a profile.


## Membership price, free trial and refund diligence

500px lists Free, Plus and Pro membership levels. The current upgrade page is dynamically rendered and did not provide a stable, readable price in this review capture, so this review does not quote a monthly or annual amount. Its paid-membership FAQ says first-time subscribers receive a 30-day free trial and may cancel during the trial to avoid being charged; it also says processed monthly payments and recurring annual payments are not refundable. Because pricing and checkout can vary, confirm the exact plan, trial end date, billing interval and renewal price in the signed-in checkout before starting.

Compare plan benefits with the actual work you need: storage and upload features, profile tools, priority or discovery features, portfolio functionality and any licensing-related revenue-share difference. A member should not subscribe solely on the premise of future licensing income. Estimate value from features that are useful even if no image sells, and check whether deleting the account ends an active membership without a refund.


## Account deletion, deactivation and distribution delays

500px’s support articles describe a 30-day processing period for a standard account-deletion request and note that search-engine results can continue to show content after deletion. Another article says accounts with current or past licensing sales or marketplace purchases cannot be permanently deleted. In that case, deactivation is offered: 500px says licensing photos are removed on its own service immediately and are processed for removal from distributor websites within 180 days. This is a significant exit limitation for a professional photographer who may later want to close or consolidate accounts.

Before submitting work for licensing, consider whether a delayed partner takedown and inability to permanently delete an account fit your future plans. Keep local copies and records of licenses, releases, sales and correspondence. Deactivation does not immediately erase third-party search indexes or guarantee that an already licensed copy can be recalled from every end user; consult the actual contributor contract for rights already granted.


## Privacy and practical alternatives

500px’s privacy FAQ says registration, profile and geographic data are among the categories it collects, and points members to its privacy policy for fuller detail. This review did not independently audit the current policy, data retention or account security. Photographers who need a straightforward portfolio can compare personal websites, specialist portfolio hosts, other photography communities and stock agencies. The comparison should cover storage and export, audience discovery, account closure, license scope, platform exclusivity, revenue share and the degree of control over customer contact.

Use the community profile for discovery only if the ongoing platform terms suit the portfolio. For sales, treat the licensing channel as an optional distribution route, keep independent masters and metadata, and do not base a business forecast on headline royalty percentages alone. Confirm any tax, payout minimum, payment-method or territory detail in the current contributor and payout documents.


## Is 500px a scam? Evidence-based conclusion

500px publishes membership and licensing information, specifies an image-review process, identifies Getty Images and VCG as licensing partners, and gives contributors detailed guidance on exclusivity, releases, AI content and account exit. The evidence reviewed does not substantiate calling it a scam. The key risks are contractual and commercial: revenue is not guaranteed, shares apply to net sales, exclusive licensing limits other commercial channels, photo approval is selective, and account deletion can be restricted after sales or marketplace activity.

This is a source-based desk review, not a membership purchase, portfolio test, licensing submission or payout verification. Current membership prices were not exposed in a stable static view and should be checked at checkout. Read the current Terms and Licensing Agreement, calculate the real value of paid features without assuming future sales, and preserve records of rights and releases before submitting any photograph.


## Features and decision criteria

- Photography profiles, community discovery and image galleries
- Free and paid membership options, with current features varying by plan
- Portfolio and image-management features advertised for Plus and Pro members
- Optional submission of eligible photographs for commercial royalty-free licensing
- Distribution of approved licensing photos through Getty Images and Visual China Group
- Submission review, acceptance/decline states and model or property release requirements

## Pricing considerations

A first-time paid subscriber is described by the membership FAQ as receiving a 30-day free trial, cancelable before billing. The same FAQ says processed monthly or recurring annual payments are not refundable. The live price page and applicable terms should be checked before starting because offers and billing vary by account or region.
