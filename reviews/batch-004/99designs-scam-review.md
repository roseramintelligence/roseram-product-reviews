# 99designs review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-17  
**Category:** Freelance design marketplace  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

99designs connects clients with freelance designers through design contests and one-to-one projects. Clients fund a contest in advance, review submissions and select a winning design for handover and rights transfer.

## Editorial verdict

The official sources describe an operating design marketplace and do not establish that 99designs is a scam. The most important purchase detail is that its advertised money-back guarantee has conditions, and is unavailable for guaranteed contests, contests in the final round or contests with a selected winner.

## 99designs scam review: a marketplace and contest model

99designs is a design marketplace where clients can launch a contest or hire a designer for a one-to-one project. In a contest, a client writes a design brief, pays before launch, receives submissions from participating designers, gives feedback and chooses a winner. The winning designer then prepares final files in a handover stage. The service is not simply a download store; its value depends on the brief, participation, communication, selection and final delivery process.

A search for “99designs scam review” deserves more than a verdict based on whether a client liked a logo or a designer received a prize quickly. The official materials identify the platform operator, explain its contest workflow, publish package prices, describe refund exceptions and document how rights transfer. These are useful checks for a real commercial marketplace. The evidence reviewed here does not establish that 99designs is a scam, though a contest can still be a poor fit, a disappointing outcome or a costly purchase for a client who misunderstood its conditions.

This is a desk review of 99designs’ publicly available product, support and legal materials, not a contest purchase, a designer’s account, a customer survey or a review of the originality of submitted artwork. Prices and policies can change. The practical question is whether the contest structure, refund conditions, designer compensation and final handover match the buyer’s requirements. A well-written brief and an understanding of the payment and ownership steps make the evaluation more concrete than a generic rating.


## How a 99designs design contest works

The contest process begins with the client’s brief and package choice. 99designs says designers from its marketplace can submit concepts that respond to the brief; the client can provide feedback and refine directions before choosing a winner. Its help center describes a standard contest as moving through qualifying and final rounds over about seven days, with fast-track choices and different timelines for some categories. The exact timeline depends on contest type, upgrades and the time needed to complete handover.

A contest can create a range of concepts from different designers in one project. That breadth can be helpful when a buyer has a clear creative direction but wants alternatives to compare. It is not the same as commissioning one designer for a deep discovery process, research, brand strategy, accessibility review or a complete identity system. The client still needs to explain the audience, usage context, constraints and examples; then respond with useful feedback. The platform cannot infer a brief that the buyer has not supplied.

The contest format also has consequences for designers: they invest time in speculative submissions before knowing which concept will win. Clients should give timely, specific feedback and avoid asking participants to imitate protected brands or submit work outside the agreed scope. Designers should read the brief and contest settings before entering. For either side, the platform’s workflow does not remove the need to agree on deliverables, revisions, file formats, ownership and payment.


## 99designs logo contest prices and extra options

The logo-design pricing page checked November 17, 2026 lists four fixed contest packages: Bronze at US$249, Silver at US$399, Gold at US$799 and Platinum at US$1,050. The page gives approximate design-concept counts and varies designer-level access and support by package. It also says listed prices exclude sales tax. These figures are the public logo-contest rates observed during this check; prices for web, app, print, brand bundles and other categories can differ.

A contest’s total can increase with optional items such as private handling, a faster timeline, promotion or a larger bundle. Some options change who can view the brief or how many designers notice it; others affect whether the prize is guaranteed. Check each option’s effect before accepting. A displayed package price should not be interpreted as a final invoice until taxes, upgrades, add-on work and any follow-on one-to-one project are included.

Estimate value by considering the number of concepts you can realistically review, how much feedback time you can provide, and whether you need editable source files or only finished images. A higher tier may affect marketplace participation and service features, but it cannot guarantee that a concept matches your taste or business strategy. If the project requires naming, trademark clearance, brand research, responsive design systems or implementation, quote those as separate professional tasks instead of assuming they are covered by a logo contest.


## The 99designs money-back guarantee has specific exclusions

The pricing page promotes a money-back guarantee, but the help center spells out its limits. It says a refund request must be made within 60 days of payment and that the guarantee does not apply to guaranteed contests, contests that have entered the final round or contests where a winner has been awarded. Its documentation says the final round automatically makes a contest guaranteed. The client should read the contest status and refund rule before choosing a finalist or selecting a guaranteed prize.

A guaranteed contest means the client has promised that a winner will be selected and the prize paid. The help article says that if no winner is selected in a guaranteed contest, the prize can be distributed among eligible designers after the contest is locked for 61 days. That commitment may encourage participation, but it removes the client’s refund option. “100% money-back” is therefore a conditional policy for eligible contests, not an unconditional assurance across every purchase or project.

If a client is uncertain, keep the contest non-guaranteed while evaluating entries and preserve a record of the payment date and relevant policy. Ask support for written confirmation if the account displays ambiguous wording. A one-to-one project is not covered by the contest money-back guarantee, according to the help center. When comparing the service to a direct freelancer, compare cancellation rights and dispute terms as well as the starting fee.


## Copyright ownership follows handover and payment

99designs says designers retain rights to their submissions until a client selects a winning design and the transfer process is completed. During handover, the designer uploads final files and both parties sign a Design Transfer Agreement. The help center says ownership transfers after the agreement is signed and the prize payment has been paid to the designer. A client should not treat every contest submission as work they are free to use; unselected concepts remain the designer’s property.

The handover stage lets the client inspect the final files and request small tweaks before approving payment. The support page describes a five-day review period for many contests and longer periods for certain bundled deliverables; it also explains that payment can be released automatically when the timer expires. Save the agreement, final file archive and payment record with the project. Check that the delivered vector, raster, font and usage files match what the brief and category require.

Copyright transfer does not automatically establish that a design is free of third-party material or cleared for every trademark use. Ask the designer to identify any stock assets, fonts or licensed elements and confirm their commercial-use terms. Run a trademark search appropriate to the markets where the logo will be used and obtain legal advice when brand registration matters. 99designs documents its handover process; it does not replace due diligence on the buyer’s intended use of a brand.


## Public versus private briefs and portfolio visibility

The contest brief may reveal a company name, product plan, target audience, launch date or packaging concept. 99designs’ launch materials advise clients to avoid including their brand name in a public contest title for privacy. The platform also says designers may showcase public contest entries on their profiles. A buyer who is preparing an unreleased brand or confidential product should decide whether public exposure is acceptable before submitting the brief.

A private contest option may restrict search visibility and require participating designers to sign a confidentiality agreement according to the purchase flow. Confirm its current scope: whether the contest page, entries, messages, files and final work are private; how long confidentiality applies; and whether portfolio display is disabled. Do not assume that selecting a privacy add-on makes every disclosure impossible or overrides legal obligations, platform terms or any separate agreement.

Limit the brief to what designers need to produce the work. Avoid uploading customer lists, passwords, unannounced financial results or other information unrelated to the design. If a project requires an NDA or a specific assignment, review the platform’s standard terms and obtain written agreement before launching. For a standard public contest, treat the brief and entries as information that may be visible to the design community and, where applicable, appear in portfolio examples.


## Payment timing and the designer’s point of view

99designs collects the client’s contest payment up front, before launch. The platform’s designer support article describes payment after a winning design is completed through handover: the client reviews the files and releases the prize, or a defined approval timer can expire. It then describes a five-business-day review period before the money is sent to Hyperwallet, followed by transfer timing that depends on the payment method and location. Its article gives an estimated total of six to ten or more business days after release, subject to those steps.

The payout page also describes a minimum payout threshold and country-specific transfer options. Those conditions matter to designers budgeting income and to clients who expect the prize to be handed over immediately. A contest prize is not necessarily a same-day payout, and payment rails may add time after platform review. Designers should verify current Hyperwallet availability, identity requirements, local fees and minimums in their country before relying on the earnings schedule.

The marketplace’s client and designer terms should be reviewed from each role’s perspective. A buyer should understand when funds can be refunded, when a prize becomes payable and what happens after a dispute. A designer should understand how the client approves files, what formats are required and when the rights transfer takes effect. Good documentation and a complete handover reduce the chance that either side assumes the other accepted a step that remains unfinished.


## A safer brief and a more useful contest evaluation

A strong brief gives designers a shared target. Describe the audience, where the design will appear, required text, constraints, competitors to avoid copying, and the formats needed at the end. Add a small set of visual references that explain the mood rather than asking for another company’s exact logo. State how the design will be judged and what constitutes a complete delivery. That makes it easier to compare concepts on the same criteria instead of selecting whichever looks most polished in a mockup.

During the contest, give focused feedback. Say what communicates well, what feels wrong and why. Avoid contradictory instructions to different designers, and make important brief changes visible to all participants. Before choosing a winner, test the leading concept at small and large sizes, in one color, on the intended background and in the real application. A visually striking presentation on a sample business card does not prove that a symbol is legible as a favicon or that the wordmark is distinctive.

For a one-to-one project, compare the designer’s portfolio, process, communication, revisions, schedule and contract directly. For a contest, account for the time needed to evaluate many entries and the fact that only the selected work transfers. If the project requires strategy, research or an ongoing design relationship, a focused commission may produce better continuity. The best choice depends on how much exploration the client needs and how much direction they can supply.


## Alternatives and what to compare before paying

Alternatives include hiring a freelance designer directly, working with a design agency, using an in-house team or commissioning a smaller fixed-scope design task. A contest can bring breadth; a direct project can offer deeper collaboration and more consistent authorship. An agency may include research, brand architecture and implementation planning, while a marketplace contest may be more accessible for a defined visual deliverable. Compare actual scope rather than equating every “logo” package.

Use the same brief to request comparable proposals. Ask for concept count, revision boundaries, deliverable formats, font and stock asset licenses, copyright assignment, cancellation, schedule and what happens if the work is not usable. Check whether a marketplace package includes the final editable files, a brand guide, website implementation or only design comps. For a website or app category, confirm whether coding, hosting, accessibility testing and post-launch support are separate.

Evaluate the total cost of the design and its future use. If the brand will appear on packaging, software, storefronts and paid advertising, plan for variants and usage rights from the outset. Reserve time for trademark review and internal approval. Do not choose solely on a promotion or an estimated number of concepts; the practical value comes from receiving a usable, original, properly transferred asset that fits the business.


## 99designs scam review: conclusion and buyer checklist

The official materials identify 99designs as a design marketplace, describe contest and one-to-one paths, publish package prices, explain payment timing and lay out a design handover and copyright-transfer process. The evidence reviewed here does not establish that 99designs is a scam. It does show that the money-back guarantee has exclusions that can materially change a buyer’s options, and that rights to an unselected concept do not transfer simply because it appeared in a contest.

This is a source-based desk review, not a contest purchase, test of design quality or assessment of individual designers. Logo-contest prices were checked on November 17, 2026 and are stated in U.S. dollars before sales tax; optional upgrades and other design categories differ. The buyer should read the live terms, contest settings and refund language at checkout because a guaranteed prize, final-round status or selected winner can remove the refund route.

99designs may suit a buyer who wants several design directions for a well-defined brief and can invest time in feedback and selection. It may be a poor fit for confidential work on a public contest, a project that needs deep collaborative strategy, or a buyer who expects an unconditional refund or immediate ownership of every entry. Confirm privacy, refund status, deliverables, file review and transfer before paying or publishing the final design.
