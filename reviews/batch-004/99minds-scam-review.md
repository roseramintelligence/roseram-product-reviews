# 99minds review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-17  
**Category:** Omnichannel customer loyalty and retention software  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

99minds is a customer-retention platform for ecommerce and physical retail, combining digital gift cards, loyalty points, store credit, coupons, referrals and wallet passes with ecommerce, POS and marketing integrations. Its public pricing is unusually concrete: monthly tiers include different transaction allowances and per-transaction overages. A merchant should still verify exactly what counts as a transaction, how refunds and reversals are treated, and whether the integrations and data controls match its production setup.

## Editorial verdict

99minds publishes product documentation, a tiered price table, API documentation and legal/security pages. Its public materials support a plausible retail-retention product, and no evidence reviewed supports a scam accusation. The main diligence items are the billable-transaction definition, overage behavior, store and integration caps, data export and deletion, and the fit of its privacy/security commitments. Some trust materials contain references to a different software category, so obtain current, product-specific security documents and contract commitments before processing customer loyalty data at scale.

## What 99minds does for a retailer

99minds sells software intended to tie customer-retention mechanics together across online stores and physical points of sale. The vendor lists digital gift cards, loyalty points, store credits, coupons, referral programs, VIP tiers, memberships, automated workflows and wallet passes. Rather than running each offer through a separate disconnected tool, a merchant can use the platform to coordinate balances and customer engagement across supported commerce and POS systems.

The product is therefore most relevant to a merchant with a defined retention strategy and an existing stack to connect. It is not simply a coupon generator: gift-card balances, customer identifiers, rewards, returns and redemptions can have accounting and support consequences. Before onboarding, map each balance type to the system that owns it, the location where it can be redeemed, how staff correct mistakes and what happens when a customer account is merged or deleted.


## Integrations and operational fit

The public materials describe integrations across ecommerce, POS, CRM and marketing systems, while the API documentation illustrates linking transactions to store identifiers. The available connector count depends on plan. The free tier allows one integration; the pricing page lists three for Basic, four for Pro and unlimited integrations for Ultra and Premium. Physical-store counts also vary by plan. These limits are not incidental details: a brand with separate online, marketplace and retail systems may outgrow an entry tier before its loyalty program reaches scale.

The quick-start guide says loyalty, store credit, coupons and workflows require at least Basic, even though the Free tier is presented as a way to begin with gift-card functionality. Ask the vendor to confirm the precise feature matrix for the products you intend to use. Then test a complete transaction path: issue, earn, redeem, partially refund, reverse, migrate between stores and reconcile against the commerce system. Confirm which integration owns the final balance and how failures are retried without duplicating credits.


## 99minds pricing, transaction allowances and overages

The current pricing page lists a free tier with 50 transactions, one campaign and one integration, then US$0.50 for each additional transaction. Basic is US$49 per month with 800 included monthly transactions and US$0.15 per additional transaction. Pro is US$99 with 1,500 included and the same US$0.15 overage. Ultra is US$199 with 3,000 included and US$0.10 overage. Premium is US$499 with 10,000 included and US$0.05 overage; Enterprise uses custom allowances and rates.

The listed monthly prices are only part of the decision. The page does not, in the captured price table, fully define whether a “transaction” means each loyalty event, gift-card issue, redemption, synchronization, customer order or another event. Do not estimate a bill by counting orders until the definition is written down. Ask whether failed or duplicated events count, whether overages are billed automatically or capped, whether the account can alert or pause at a threshold, and how taxes, annual discounts, onboarding and add-ons appear in checkout. Preserve a copy of the quoted terms.


## Security, privacy and customer-data diligence

The company publishes a security page, a data-processing addendum, a GDPR overview and a subprocessor listing. Its GDPR page says certain loyalty, coupon and gift-card data are retained during the subscription and can be requested in a portable format, with deletion procedures described. Those statements are useful starting points, but a merchant should rely on the current signed DPA and service agreement for actual duties, timing and remedies.

There is a documentation-quality issue worth resolving: the public security page includes passages describing a continuous testing cloud platform and browser/device test executions, which do not match the loyalty-retention product being reviewed. That could be legacy or reused copy, but it makes the page less reliable as a product-specific assurance. Ask for current audit reports or certificates, encryption details, incident timelines, data residency, deletion timelines, access controls, penetration-test scope and a confirmed processor list applicable to 99minds loyalty data. This observation is about published documentation fit, not evidence that the service is unsafe.


## Reporting, rollout and customer service

A retailer should evaluate what operational teams can see after launch. Confirm reports for points liability, outstanding gift-card balances, redemptions, expiration, campaign attribution and store-by-store activity. Reconcile sample reports to source transactions and accounting records before rolling the program to every location. Verify how staff permissions differ, whether branch managers can issue or adjust value, and whether adjustments leave an audit trail.

A phased rollout is safer than enabling every offer at once. Start with a single store or channel, a small group of staff and clear customer-service guidance. Test reversals, expired cards, duplicate customer profiles, delayed webhooks and connector downtime. Ask what support response is included, how urgent payment or balance errors are escalated, and whether implementation assistance is part of the selected tier. The vendor’s case studies describe customer outcomes, but these are vendor-reported examples and should not be treated as guaranteed results for another merchant.


## Alternatives and how to compare them

Compare 99minds with the native gift-card and loyalty tools in the merchant’s existing ecommerce platform, specialist loyalty applications and POS-native programs. Use the same test plan and include the cost of integrations, staff training, migration, transaction overages and reconciliation. A native tool may cover a single online store with fewer moving parts; an omnichannel platform may be more attractive when balances need to work across several retail systems.

Ask every vendor to demonstrate the hard cases: partial refunds, returns without receipts, offline POS operation, multi-currency balances, duplicate profiles, store transfers and customer-data export. Check how long records remain available after cancellation and whether the export includes event history or only current balances. No live 99minds account, integration, transaction ledger or security control was tested for this review.


## Is 99minds a scam? Review conclusion

The company maintains a public product site, tiered pricing, help documentation, API references and legal/security materials. Those sources support the existence of a defined retention product, and the evidence reviewed does not substantiate calling 99minds a scam. The most material buyer risks are the meaning and cost of billable events, connector-specific behavior and incomplete or mismatched security documentation.

Get transaction definitions and product-specific data-protection commitments in writing, then prove that a real gift-card and loyalty lifecycle reconciles correctly in a limited pilot. This review is based on public vendor material and was not a hands-on implementation, security audit or accounting assessment. Pricing and plan limits can change; verify the current checkout and agreement before committing.


## Features and decision criteria

- Gift cards, store credit, loyalty, coupons, referrals, VIP tiers and memberships
- Connectors for ecommerce, POS, CRM and marketing systems
- Apple Wallet and Google Wallet features on higher tiers
- Monthly usage allowances, integration/store caps and variable transaction overages
