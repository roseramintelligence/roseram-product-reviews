# 4o Image Generator review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-17  
**Category:** AI image generation software  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

4o Image is a browser-based image generator whose website lists Ghibli-style and chibi-avatar tools, a free core tier and paid credit plans. On November 17, 2026, its pricing page displayed Starter at US$5 for 120 credits/60 images, Standard at US$10 for 300 credits/150 images, and Premium at US$20 for 800 credits/400 images, with higher-resolution and workflow features at upper tiers. The page headline advertises commercial usage, but the Terms say rights vary by plan without spelling out the exact license. The Privacy Policy says free prompts and images are not retained long term; the Terms separately say prompts and generated images may be collected and stored for improvement and compliance.

## 4o Image Generator review: what the service is

4o Image is a web-based image creation service at 4o-image.com. Its navigation links to a Ghibli-style generator, a chibi-avatar generator, a task area and a pricing page. The site describes text-to-image creation and states in its Terms and About page that GPT-4o and Gemini power the service. Those are the operator’s own descriptions: our review did not inspect network requests, verify which model handles a prompt, or establish a commercial relationship with OpenAI or Google.

The distinction matters because a product name containing “4o” and references to large model providers can be read as an endorsement or official product. The pages reviewed describe 4o Image as its own service. Buyers should verify the provider, the model selected for a particular plan, whether images are routed to a third party, and whether the model can change without notice. The site’s own Terms reserve the right to modify or discontinue service features.


## Image credits and plan features

The pricing page displayed three paid tiers on November 17, 2026. Starter showed US$5 for 120 credits and 60 images; Standard showed US$10 for 300 credits and 150 images; Premium showed US$20 for 800 credits and 400 images. Each tier corresponds to two listed credits per image, although the page does not explain whether every generation, edit, retry or output size consumes the same number. It also shows older crossed-out values of US$10, US$20 and US$40. No period or expiration date appeared in the captured table.

Feature differences include one, four or eight simultaneous jobs; private generations on Standard and Premium; 4K output on Standard; and 4K/8K output, batch processing, advanced editing and a priority queue on Premium. These are advertised inclusions, not independently measured performance or proof that each format is available for every generation. Before planning a production workflow, ask how credits are consumed, whether failed jobs are refunded, what “private” means in storage and visibility terms, and what technical limits apply to image size, export format and queue time.


## Commercial use and image ownership need a clearer license

The pricing page promotes faster generation and “commercial usage,” while the Terms say images are supplied under usage terms that depend on a subscription plan. They describe limited rights for free users and extended rights for paid subscribers, but the reviewed Terms do not set out a complete plan-by-plan license or clearly define the commercial permissions. The same Terms warn that prompts and outputs may be subject to third-party rights and put responsibility for lawful use on the user.

That leaves a practical gap for a business. A headline about commercial usage is not a substitute for rules covering client work, resale, advertising, exclusivity, attribution, trademarked characters, generated people, model releases or copyright claims. Get the precise license and any restrictions in writing before using an image in a paid campaign or transferring it to a client. Do not assume “private generations” means exclusive ownership or that no other user can obtain a similar result.


## Privacy: the policy and Terms do not say the same thing

The Privacy Policy, last updated March 30, 2025, says it collects text prompts, generated images, basic analytics, technical information such as IP addresses and browser type, and optional premium-account information. It says prompts and images are processed in real time and temporarily stored as needed, with no long-term storage for free users and optional history for premium users. It also says prompts may be processed through OpenAI and Google services.

The Terms, also dated March 30, 2025, state that prompts and generated images may be collected and stored for service improvement and compliance. These statements might be reconcilable through distinctions in user tier, temporary processing, moderation or storage purpose, but the public wording does not explain those boundaries. Before submitting a client photograph, unreleased product, personal image or sensitive prompt, ask what is retained, for how long, which processors receive it, whether it can be used for model improvement, and how deletion works. We did not verify encryption, storage locations, subprocessors or deletion behavior.


## Checkout, renewals, refunds and operator identity

The public plan table provides an attractive entry price but not a billing interval. The Terms call paid plans subscriptions and say free use does not require registration or payment, yet the reviewed pages do not clearly state automatic renewal, cancellation timing, credit rollover, failed-generation refunds, trial conversion or a standard refund procedure. Those details should be visible before payment, not left to inference from a sale banner. Check the final checkout page and save its exact price, renewal date and cancellation instructions.

The reviewed About page is general marketing copy, and the Terms and Privacy Policy identify support@4o-image.com as the contact but do not name a legal contracting entity or registered address in their text. That does not prove misconduct, but it makes disputes, jurisdiction, data requests and accountability harder to assess. A buyer considering a subscription should request the operator’s legal name, governing law, support channel and invoice details before spending a meaningful amount.


## Model quality and alternatives: run a controlled comparison

No image-generation benchmark was conducted for this review, and there is no basis here to rank the service against larger image platforms. Its published plan page describes resolution labels, queue concurrency and editing features, but those labels do not establish fidelity, consistency, prompt adherence or commercial suitability. A prospective user can compare the same prompt, aspect ratio and target use across tools, then check text rendering, hands, composition, artifact rate, editability, file metadata and actual output resolution.

For a shortlisting exercise, use non-confidential prompts first. Generate several images, include one edit and one failed or rejected request, record credit usage, and export the original files. Then review the license and retention terms separately from the visual results. An image that looks good may still be unsuitable for a commercial job if rights, privacy or billing are unclear.


## Alternatives and buyer fit

4o Image may fit a hobbyist who wants an easy browser interface, is comfortable with a young service and can verify the plan details at checkout. The disclosed feature set may also interest a user who specifically wants avatar templates or multiple concurrent jobs. The main drawback is not simply price; it is the lack of precise public terms for subscription cadence, credit treatment, commercial permissions and prompt retention.

Compare it with providers whose current official documentation clearly states model access, output rights, privacy and subscription mechanics. Choose the same requirements for each quote: monthly cost, images included, overage, credit expiry, privacy setting, source model, allowed commercial uses, cancellation, refund rules and export resolution. We did not name or price individual competitors because no current, like-for-like comparison was verified for this review.


## Is 4o Image Generator a scam? Evidence-based conclusion

The reviewed pages establish a functioning product description, a credit-based pricing table, Terms, a Privacy Policy and a support email. They do not establish that the service is fraudulent, so the evidence does not justify calling 4o Image Generator a scam. They do establish questions a careful buyer should resolve: the paid plan’s billing period is not shown in the rendered pricing table, the commercial-use headline is broader than the license language, and the Terms and Privacy Policy differ on whether prompts and generated images may be stored. The named AI models and security measures are operator statements, not independently audited facts.

This is a source-based desk review, not a purchase, account test, privacy audit, copyright opinion or output-quality comparison. The legal pages were last updated March 30, 2025; pricing was checked November 17, 2026. Before uploading sensitive material or authorizing recurring payment, confirm the legal operator, billing cadence, cancellation/refund process, credit rules, data retention and commercial license in writing. If those answers are not clear at checkout, choose a provider whose terms match the needs of the work.
