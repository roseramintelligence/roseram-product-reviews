## What 4n6 File Converter does

4n6 File Converter is a Windows utility for opening, previewing and exporting a range of email, document and contact-data formats. The publisher lists Outlook PST and OST, Outlook for Mac OLM, MBOX, EML, MSG, OFT, DBX, Google Takeout, Maildir and vCard among the supported inputs, alongside Word, text and PDF documents. It describes more than 23 export formats and a workflow that lets a user inspect records and choose a destination.

This kind of application is most useful when a person is moving old mail to a new client, consolidating archives or preparing selected records for review. The exact input and output combination matters: “supports PST” is not enough to establish that a particular PST version, damaged archive, embedded calendar item or unusual attachment will be converted correctly. Check the specific format pair before purchasing.


## Preview, batch conversion and extraction features

The product page describes batch processing, file and folder selection, preview panels, search, filters, attachment extraction and options to preserve folder structures or rename outputs. It also says the program can work without the original email client installed. These are useful claims for a conversion utility because the value is often not simply changing an extension; it is getting messages, headers, dates, folders and attachments into a form that can be searched and used later.

The page states that the free edition is limited to 10 files per folder and the full edition removes the conversion cap. It lists version 3.8 as last updated in April 2026 and Windows 11, 10 and older versions among supported systems. The vendor states “100% accuracy” and makes security claims on its site; those are vendor marketing assertions, not independent verification. Compare the original with exported files before relying on them.


## Pricing, trial and refund expectations

The product page lists a minimum price of US$49, though the exact checkout price and license details should be checked for the selected converter and any current promotion. It promotes a 30-day money-back guarantee. The support page says paid tools carry a 30-day guarantee and directs customers to contact support with an order number within that period. Save the checkout screen and receipt, and confirm that the guarantee applies to the precise product and purchase channel.

The practical way to evaluate the product is to use the free trial with representative copies of the files you need to move. The limit of 10 files per folder can still be enough to check ordinary messages, attachments, folder names and timestamps. Ask whether a license is assigned to one user or device, whether reinstallations affect activation, how updates are delivered and what technical support covers. Do not assume that “lifetime” access means perpetual compatibility with future versions of Windows or every future email format.


## Data integrity and forensic use require extra care

The publisher markets the tool for both everyday migration and forensic investigators. Those are different standards of use. A personal mailbox conversion can be checked by comparing message counts and opening a sample of attachments. Evidence handling can require chain-of-custody records, hash verification, validated process documentation and repeatable results. A converter’s ability to preview headers or hex data does not, by itself, make it a certified forensic workflow.

Keep the original archive untouched, make a verified working copy and record the source file hash before processing. Test duplicate messages, Unicode, time zones, nested folders, long paths, encrypted items and large attachments. Compare item counts, dates, sender/recipient fields and attachment hashes after export. If a result will be used in an investigation or legal matter, consult the relevant forensic requirements and validate the method independently.


## Privacy and safe handling of mail archives

Email archives can contain highly sensitive personal, business and legal information. The vendor describes the application as a desktop tool and says the installer is served from its own site, but buyers should confirm whether conversion is entirely local, what telemetry or activation checks occur, and whether any files are uploaded for support. The privacy notice should be reviewed alongside the license agreement before processing confidential data.

Use a non-production sample first, obtain the installer from the official vendor domain, verify its digital signature and scan it under the organization’s normal endpoint controls. Restrict access to the source and destination folders, encrypt removable drives and delete temporary exports securely. Do not upload a mailbox or forensic sample to a support ticket unless the organization has approved that disclosure.


## Alternatives and a repeatable product test

Compare 4n6 with built-in email-client export tools, open-source mail utilities and specialist e-discovery or forensic products. For a one-time move between common email clients, free tools may be sufficient. For a large, heterogeneous archive or a regulated investigation, a tool with documented validation procedures and an auditable chain of processing may be a better fit even at a higher cost.

Run a controlled test set containing ordinary messages, attachments, nested folders, contacts and edge cases. Count the source records, convert them, then compare the output and document failures. Repeat the same operation on a second machine if the archive is important. This review did not install 4n6, inspect its executable or run conversions; the proposed checks are not presented as hands-on results.
