# 4Sync review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-17  
**Category:** Cloud storage, file synchronization and sharing  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

4Sync is a cloud-storage and synchronization service with web, desktop and mobile access, file sharing, and backup features. Its current Premium page lists 1 TB of storage, a 20 GB maximum upload size, a 60-day trial and a displayed price of $8.25 per month or $99 per year. The website describes a 100 GB monthly traffic baseline with extra traffic available for purchase, while a help article says Premium includes 1 TB of monthly traffic; this discrepancy should be clarified before relying on high-volume transfers. The public terms were last revised in 2019 and the help center says a trial account that does not upgrade is deactivated, with 30 additional days to reactivate or retrieve files.

## Editorial verdict

4Sync publishes a product site, Premium payment page, help center, privacy policy and terms; the available evidence does not substantiate calling it a scam. The material concerns are operational: trial files may become inaccessible after the trial unless the account is upgraded or data retrieved during the stated recovery window; the bandwidth figures conflict across official pages; account deletion for Premium users requires contacting support first; the terms’ refund provision is narrow; and the visible Terms of Service date to December 27, 2019. ## 4Sync review: online storage with synchronization and sharing

4Sync is a cloud-storage service for documents, photos, music and other files. Its public site describes access through a browser, desktop application and mobile devices, with synchronization between computers, tablets and phones. The Premium page also advertises folder permissions, direct links for sharing and mobile photo and video backup. This makes the product relevant to a person who wants a simple remote file shelf or an always-available copy of frequently used material.

The important distinction is between a storage quota and a complete backup strategy. A synced folder can propagate a local deletion or mistaken overwrite unless the service provides a usable version history or recovery path for that event. The reviewed product pages emphasize capacity and access but do not establish how every deleted, replaced or corrupted file can be recovered. Before making 4Sync the only repository for important records, ask about versioning, retention, restoration and the effect of cancellation, then test recovery with noncritical data.


## Storage, device support and file-sharing workflow

The vendor advertises 1 TB of Premium storage and a maximum upload size of 20 GB. Its product pages describe syncing files across computers, tablets and smartphones, backing up mobile photos and videos, and sharing folders with permissions or direct links. The help center says search operates on a user’s own stored files rather than searching across other customers’ files. Those are product descriptions; no upload, cross-device sync, bandwidth measurement or shared-link permission was independently tested.

A team should evaluate how shared links behave in practice: whether a link can be restricted, revoked, password-protected or made to expire; whether recipients can download or edit; and whether activity is logged. The public materials mention folder permissions but do not answer every control question. Test links with a separate recipient account and confirm that access disappears after revocation. For regulated or client material, keep an approved access list and do not rely on an unverified assumption that a URL is private.


## Premium price, trial and file-access deadline

The current Premium page displays $8.25 per month or $99 per year and advertises a 60-day free trial with 1 TB of storage. The help-center explanation says that if a user does not proceed to Premium, the account will be deactivated, but the user has another 30 days to reactivate it or take out the data. That is a meaningful distinction from an always-free tier: the trial can be useful for evaluating storage and sync, but a customer needs a plan for getting files back before relying on the account as a long-term archive.

The trial page and pricing copy should be checked carefully at signup: confirm whether a payment method is collected, when billing starts, how to cancel and whether cancellation leaves the trial active through its end. The official pages checked here do not make every checkout interaction visible. Set a calendar reminder well before day 60, keep an independent copy of anything important, and verify that the export or download route works while the account is still accessible.


## Traffic limits are not consistent across official pages

The payment page calls Premium bandwidth unlimited but its footnote says all users initially receive 100 GB of monthly traffic and can purchase extra traffic. A Help Center article instead says Premium offers unlimited bandwidth while describing users as initially getting 1 TB of monthly traffic, again with extra traffic available for purchase. These figures do not line up. The source wording could reflect different plan states, terminology or an older help article, but the pages reviewed do not resolve the difference.

This matters for photo libraries, video archives and shared folders used by multiple people: storage capacity is only one dimension of cost. Ask support to confirm the active monthly traffic allowance, what counts toward it, the price of additional traffic and whether shared downloads consume the owner’s quota. Get the answer in writing before a bulk upload or migration. A 1 TB storage headline does not by itself promise unlimited transfer capacity.


## Terms, refunds and the limits of the public legal material

The Terms of Service state that they were last revised on December 27, 2019. They define uploaded files and other customer content as User Data, require the user to have the right or consent to store it, place responsibility on the account holder to use reasonable security precautions and say the service is not responsible for unauthorized access to User Data. The same document states that the company will keep User Data secure, but it does not provide an independent audit result or a specific encryption architecture in the reviewed sections. The privacy notice likewise says the company takes reasonable measures while acknowledging that no method is perfectly secure.

The terms say Premium subscriptions are generally non-refundable, with a possible exception when a subscription was made by mistake and the customer requests a refund within three days; such requests are reviewed case by case. The product page’s free trial is separate from this limited refund language. The old revision date and generic service promises make it sensible for a business to ask for current terms, security documentation, data-processing terms and recovery commitments before moving sensitive or operationally essential files.


## Privacy, social sign-in and account closure

4Sync’s privacy page describes registration information, optional social-network connections, cookies, cross-border transfers and user rights. It says users who signed up through Google, Facebook or Twitter can manage connected services in account settings. It also provides a route to request access, correction or deletion of personal data and says access requests will be answered within 30 days. The policy says the service is intended for adults aged 18 and over. These are provider disclosures; this review did not inspect cookies, verify social permissions or audit the actual data flows.

The Help Center says users can delete an account from account settings, but Premium customers must first email the Premium support address. Before closing an account, download and verify all files, check whether any shared recipients still need access, cancel recurring billing through the correct route and retain a copy of the confirmation. The trial article separately sets out a 30-day retrieval period after deactivation. Do not assume account deletion, trial expiry and subscription cancellation are interchangeable actions.


## Alternatives and a fair cloud-storage comparison

A fair comparison should include mainstream cloud drives, specialist sync tools and a self-hosted option where appropriate. Compare storage per dollar, upload-size caps, traffic and download quotas, file versioning, restore procedures, encryption model, sharing controls, client support, mobile backup, identity integrations, support response and service-exit process. A low monthly price is less compelling if recovery or transfer limits do not match the workload.

Run a small migration pilot before copying an entire archive. Upload a representative mix of large and small files, rename and edit a synced copy on a second device, test a shared folder with a separate account, revoke its access, and restore a deliberately deleted test file. Measure upload and download time on the actual network. Keep a second independent backup until these checks pass. These are proposed due-diligence steps, not results of a test performed for this review.


## Is 4Sync a scam? Evidence-based conclusion

4Sync publishes an identifiable cloud-storage product, a current Premium payment page, product help, privacy material and Terms of Service. The evidence reviewed does not substantiate calling it a scam. The review instead finds concrete items a buyer should settle before use: the trial account’s deactivation and retrieval window, differing traffic figures across official pages, an old Terms of Service revision, narrow refund wording, and a support-contact requirement for deleting Premium accounts.

This is a source-based desk review, not a paid subscription, account-security audit, file-transfer test or legal opinion. Prices were observed on November 17, 2026 and the terms page says it was revised in 2019. Before storing valuable files, confirm the current quota and traffic rules, trial conversion, refund and deletion procedures, and the restore workflow; then keep a separate backup until recovery has been demonstrated with your own files.

Review the current checkout and terms before starting the trial.
