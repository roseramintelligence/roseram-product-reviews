# 3way.Social review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-17  
**Category:** Backlink exchange and link placement marketplace  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

3way.Social is a business-oriented marketplace where members buy, sell or exchange backlinks and guest posts using platform credits. Its current terms require listed links to be permanent and dofollow, while its cash-out rate changes with platform activity and payouts are handled manually through PayPal, usually every one to two weeks. The service checks whether a deal link is live, but its terms disclaim ranking, traffic and indexing outcomes and put search-guideline compliance on the user. Google expressly classifies buying or selling links for ranking purposes and excessive link exchanges as link spam unless applicable paid links are qualified. The product is identifiable and its mechanics are disclosed; the main issue for buyers is whether the link activity they choose is compatible with search policies and their own editorial standards.

## Editorial verdict

The evidence reviewed does not establish that 3way.Social is a scam. It does establish a real credit-based backlink marketplace with operational rules that deserve careful scrutiny: all placements must be dofollow under the terms, credits are not money, cash-out conversion floats and is manually paid, refunds are case by case, and there is no ranking promise. The service’s current privacy-policy URL contains generic WordPress sample wording rather than a clearly tailored description of the marketplace’s account, deal and payment data. Businesses focused on organic search should not buy links to manipulate rankings; before using any paid editorial placement, confirm whether it must be qualified as sponsored and whether the publisher will allow that. No account, deal, payout, live link or security control was independently tested for this desk review.

## 3way.Social review: what the backlink marketplace does

3way.Social describes itself as a marketplace for business users who want to buy, sell or exchange links and guest posts. A site owner can list a domain they own or are authorized to manage, select whether to give links, receive links or accept guest-post pitches, and set domain-specific guidelines. A buyer can browse listed sites, review their categories and displayed metrics, propose a target page and anchor text, and send a request. The other member can accept, decline or suggest a different page. Credits are locked while a deal is underway, and the platform says it checks that an agreed link is live before releasing them.

That workflow is more structured than finding an individual publisher by email: there is a request record, an in-platform conversation and a visible credit amount. It also makes the product’s core activity clear. The service is not merely a directory of publishers or a general outreach CRM; the paid value exchange is tied to link placements and guest posts, including direct purchases and reciprocal credit-funded deals. Buyers should therefore evaluate both the marketplace mechanics and the search-policy implications of the particular placement.


## The dofollow requirement is the key search-risk question

3way.Social’s terms say agreed links must be permanent, easy to find in the content and dofollow. Google’s current spam policies identify buying or selling links for ranking purposes, exchanging money for links or posts containing links, excessive reciprocal exchanges and links created primarily to manipulate rankings as examples of link spam. Google also says paid advertising or sponsorship links are not against policy when qualified with rel="sponsored" or rel="nofollow" as appropriate. The platform’s mandatory dofollow clause is in tension with that guidance whenever the commercial purpose is a paid placement or ranking signal.

This is not a finding that every member exchange automatically triggers a penalty, nor does Google guarantee that a particular link will be discounted or acted on. It does mean a business should not treat “do-follow,” “permanent,” link verification or a marketplace guarantee as proof of SEO safety. A link that is editorially appropriate because it helps readers may still need appropriate qualification if money, credits, services or other value were exchanged. Ask the publisher in writing whether qualification is possible, confirm how the link will appear, and avoid deals whose purpose is to pass ranking credit. If the platform will not permit the required attribute, do not treat that deal as a compliant paid sponsorship placement.


## Credits, prices and the difference between credits and money

The terms define credits as the marketplace unit used to settle link and guest-post deals. Members can earn them by publishing placements or buy them by card through Stripe. The domain valuation help page says the credit value depends on Ahrefs rating, monthly traffic and category; the terms add the platform’s own spam score and warn that third-party metrics may be inaccurate or change. A public calculator provides worked examples, but those examples should not be confused with a fixed price schedule for every domain or a quote for a particular placement.

Credits are not cash, legal tender or a deposit. A member can request a cash-out of available credits at the current rate, but the rate changes with platform activity, and the terms say a team member usually pays through PayPal manually within one to two weeks. Locked credits cannot be cashed out. That is a meaningful liquidity distinction: a dashboard balance is not the same as a dollar balance in a bank or PayPal account. Before buying credits, check the current rate, PayPal fees, withdrawal timing, deal lock rules and whether the placement you want can actually be obtained at the displayed value.


## Refunds, cancellations and buyer protection

For an unaccepted or pending request, the terms describe some paths for withdrawing or returning locked credits: a pending deal can be withdrawn before verification, a request left without action for 30 days is rejected automatically, and credits return if a deal is rejected, expires or is cancelled before placement. After the link is verified and credits released, that is a different state. Separately, refunds for purchased credits and done-for-you packages are handled case by case, so customers should not assume an unconditional cash refund just because the platform advertises credit protection or link restoration.

The homepage promotes a 100% backlink guarantee and says missing links may be restored or credits refunded. Treat that as a publisher claim whose exact scope must be read together with the terms and the specific deal. A guarantee that a link is live or replaced addresses delivery persistence, not its editorial value, referral traffic, conversions or ranking impact. Save the order details, accepted page and anchor, verification record, communications and any restoration promise. Check the package cancellation route and commitment period before purchasing recurring work.


## Website quality, metrics and what the platform verifies

The marketplace uses third-party domain metrics and its own spam scoring to assign a credit valuation. The help article describes Ahrefs rating and estimated traffic in that calculation; the terms explicitly say third-party metrics are not guaranteed to be accurate. These figures can help sort a large inventory, but they are estimates rather than independent proof of audience quality, topical fit, editorial standards, referral visits or business value.

The terms place responsibility on members to list sites they control and to avoid malicious, illegal or spammy destinations. They also describe acceptable-site criteria and say the platform checks whether a link is live. That is not equivalent to auditing each site’s audience, content history, ownership, outbound-link neighborhood, traffic source, indexation, conversion quality or compliance. Review the actual page and site yourself. Ask where a link will appear, whether the surrounding text is useful and original, whether the page is indexed for a reason beyond link selling, and whether the placement will remain clearly disclosed to readers.


## Privacy and account diligence

The current page at 3way.Social’s privacy URL contains generic WordPress sample copy, including phrases such as “Suggested text” and references to comments, Gravatar and password-reset emails. That wording does not appear tailored to a marketplace that handles domain records, link requests, messages, credits, card payments and PayPal cash-outs. The terms provide the operator’s contact details and mention retaining user-submitted domains, guidance, posts and messages as needed to run the service, but the public privacy URL reviewed here leaves important practical questions unanswered.

Before adding client or commercially sensitive information, ask the operator for the current privacy notice, data controller details, retention periods, subprocessors, international-transfer safeguards, account-deletion process and incident contact. Do not upload confidential client materials or credentials into domain guidelines or deal messages unless the service’s data terms cover that use. This review did not create an account, inspect the logged-in product, test its payment flow or verify its security controls.


## Who should consider 3way.Social, and who should pause

The interface may be useful to an experienced SEO team that wants to coordinate publisher conversations, compare available sites and track a defined link-delivery workflow. Its credit model allows a site owner to earn platform value by publishing for others and then spend it on a different placement, and its done-for-you offering provides another path for teams that prefer to delegate prospecting and content production. Those are workflow conveniences; they do not settle whether a given link is good for readers or suitable for search.

A buyer who expects guaranteed rankings, traffic, rapid indexing or a fixed dollar value from credits should pause. A buyer who cannot accept dofollow-only links, variable cash-out values, manual payouts, case-by-case refunds or ambiguity in the privacy notice should resolve those terms first. A safer decision process is to start with no client data and no large credit balance, manually inspect candidate sites, use links only when they serve readers, and document the commercial relationship and any needed link qualification.


## Is 3way.Social a scam? Evidence-based conclusion

The service has a published operator, dated terms, a documented marketplace process and specific credit and cash-out rules. The pages reviewed do not establish fraud, and the available evidence does not justify labeling the company a scam. The central concern is strategy and policy fit: the product facilitates credit-funded and monetary link placements while its terms require dofollow links, whereas Google identifies ranking-motivated buying, selling and excessive link exchange as link spam and says paid sponsorship links should be qualified when applicable.

Treat this as a due-diligence review rather than a verdict that every transaction is improper. Verify the selected publisher, destination page, anchor, disclosure, rel attribute, current credit-to-cash rate, refund rights and data terms before accepting a deal. No purchase, backlink, ranking change, penalty, payout or customer account was independently observed for this review; feature and guarantee descriptions are attributed to the company’s published materials.


## Features and decision criteria

- Marketplace to list authorized domains and request link insertions, link exchanges or guest posts
- Credit pricing based on third-party rating and traffic metrics, category and an internal spam score
- Deal workflow with member acceptance, credit locking, live-link verification and release or return of locked credits
- Optional purchase of credits by card and exchange of available credits for a variable USD cash-out through PayPal
- Separate done-for-you SEO packages with scope, price and delivery estimate shown at checkout

## Pricing considerations

Rates and packages can change. Confirm the credit purchase price, current exchange rate, PayPal fees, deal cancellation point, renewal commitment and refund outcome on the live checkout before funding. The platform says refunds for credits and packages are reviewed case by case.
