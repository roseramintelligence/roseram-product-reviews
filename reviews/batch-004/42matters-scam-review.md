# 42matters review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-17  
**Category:** Mobile app intelligence and app store data APIs  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

42matters sells app store metadata, market intelligence, API access and data dumps covering mobile and connected-TV catalogs. It is a specialist research data source; buyers should validate estimates, coverage, freshness, quotas and redistribution rights against their particular use case.

## Editorial verdict

The official documentation describes a substantial app-data product with useful search and history endpoints. The main diligence work is commercial and methodological: understand what is estimated, how hits are counted, how current each dataset is, and whether your product may expose the data. Public information does not establish fraud, and a catalog listing is not a user endorsement.

## 42matters review: what the app intelligence service is

A team comparing mobile products often starts with a simple question: what is listed, where is it available, and how has it changed? **42matters** is a data provider for that research job. Its official pages describe APIs and file dumps covering the Google Play Store, Apple App Store and other mobile and connected-TV catalogs, with metadata, discovery, historical indicators and related app intelligence. The service is aimed at organizations that need structured app-market information in their own analysis or workflows.

That distinction matters when reading a **42matters review**. This is not a consumer app that can be judged from a few minutes of clicking. The value depends on whether the endpoints, countries, fields and refresh schedule answer a defined business question. Public documentation identifies the available product areas; it does not establish that every number is complete, independently audited or appropriate as a substitute for a publisher’s private analytics.


## 42matters API coverage and research features

The product pages describe app lookup and search, country availability, reviews and review analysis, ratings and rank history, app changelog history, SDK integrations, permissions, app-ads data and app-download estimates. The API documentation also lists monthly active-user history for supported platforms. This mix can help an analyst connect a store listing to a broader research record: discover an app, examine its public metadata, follow selected historical signals, and compare its technical footprint with other catalog entries.

The service says its app intelligence spans more than 20 million applications and two million publishers, with data across 63 countries and 39 languages. These are provider-reported coverage figures, not a guarantee that every niche category or region has the same depth. The listed storefronts and fields should be checked against a sample of the exact apps, publishers and countries needed by your team before buying a larger package.


## Interpreting 42matters download and audience estimates

Estimated downloads and active-user histories can be useful for sizing a market or choosing which competitors deserve further investigation. They are still estimates. A sensible **42matters app download estimates** workflow treats the result as an input to a decision rather than an audited count. Record the app identifier, storefront, country and date; compare the result with public store ranges where available; and avoid converting a modelled signal into an exact claim about a publisher’s actual customers or revenue.

Freshness also varies by dataset. The product page says popular apps, top charts and app-rank histories update daily, while its general FAQ describes weekly updates for the broader dataset and offers a recrawl route for fresher app details. That is helpful operational context, but it means “current” should be defined per endpoint. A weekly catalog snapshot may be adequate for category research and inadequate for a short-lived campaign or a rapid product launch.


## 42matters pricing, API hits and file dump limits

The API is metered through monthly hits, and a request does not necessarily cost one hit: the documentation says complex searches can consume more than a lookup. Endpoint pages list the hit cost, while the account usage page or Account Status API can reveal remaining quota. Trial accounts have restricted endpoints and data, and the company currently advertises a 14-day trial. Treat the trial as an evaluation window, not proof that your production workload fits a paid package.

The pricing page is the right place to confirm current packages, but the public page does not expose a stable, complete price matrix in the text available for this review. Ask for the exact allowance, endpoint access, renewal terms and overage behavior that apply to your account. File dumps are separately permissioned, vary by platform and refresh frequency, and the documentation states a default download ceiling of one terabyte per month. Technical support beyond the documentation is offered as a separately purchased service.


## 42matters data licensing and privacy deserve careful review

The most important product constraint is what you may do with the output. The **42matters terms of service** say its App Intelligence Data cannot be published, distributed or otherwise made available to third parties without prior written consent; they also prohibit use to build a competing offering and restrict persistent storage of the data to 24 hours unless the parties agree otherwise in writing. A plan that looks suitable for internal analytics may therefore be unsuitable for a public directory, customer-facing dashboard or long-lived database.

The terms separately say public store data may include publisher contact details and that the customer is responsible for its own permitted use; they explicitly prohibit using those contacts for spam. The privacy policy explains collection from public app-store sources and provides a rights-contact route. Before importing records into a CRM or exposing fields in another product, map each field to the license, retention rule, purpose and privacy notice that governs it. Do not infer that “publicly visible” means unrestricted to republish.


## 42matters API reliability and fit for different teams

The documentation requires an access token, applies endpoint-specific rate limits and describes conventional error codes for invalid requests, excess traffic and server problems. Its availability statement is a reasonable-efforts target of at least 99% averaged across a calendar quarter, subject to listed exclusions. Buyers who rely on an API in a production pipeline should plan for rate-limit responses, retry safely, monitor quota and preserve a fallback source for time-sensitive work. That is normal integration diligence, not evidence that the service is unreliable.

This product is best evaluated with a real sample and a written field checklist. Test the markets and store identifiers you care about, compare several outputs to known sources, measure hit consumption for the actual queries, and verify that your intended use fits the license. If your team only needs a handful of public listings, direct store pages or smaller research tools may be enough. If it needs structured, cross-store discovery and recurring market analysis, 42matters may save engineering time—provided the data rights and package economics work.


## Is 42matters a scam? Evidence-based conclusion

The sources reviewed describe a named Swiss company, published product documentation, pricing and legal terms, API references and a privacy policy. That evidence supports treating 42matters as an identifiable commercial app-data provider. It does not prove every data point is accurate for a particular decision, guarantee uninterrupted service, or establish that a third-party seller claiming to represent 42matters is authorized.

No subscription was purchased, no API token was used and no independent accuracy or security test was performed for this review. A buyer should verify the contracting entity, trial scope, endpoint permissions, measured quota, current price and data-use rights in the account before committing. Based on the public material reviewed, the central question is fit and licensing—not a substantiated finding of a scam.
