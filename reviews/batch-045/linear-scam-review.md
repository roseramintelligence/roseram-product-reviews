# Linear review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-16  
**Category:** Engineering issue and project tracking  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Linear organizes issues, projects and recurring cycles. Consider it when a team needs a shared planning routine and somebody will keep priorities current.

## Editorial verdict

Linear documents its features and paid tiers. Buy it for a specific improvement in planning or cross-team visibility you can demonstrate in one cycle.

## Linear cycles and the work that rolls forward

On the last afternoon of a cycle, an engineer is waiting for a code review, a bug has interrupted planned work and a third task is waiting on a vendor. Add those three issues to a test cycle and let it end. Linear documents automatic rollover for incomplete issues. Open the next cycle together and decide which tasks deserve to keep their place. An issue moving ahead on a calendar can still be waiting on a decision from a person.

Try writing that decision in the **Linear cycle** itself. Assign an owner and add the next observable step. The following week, ask a teammate who missed the meeting to explain why the order changed. This is a useful test of the tracking setup because it measures what someone can learn from it when the author of the plan is away.


## Linear pricing per user and the seat count that matters

The pricing page separates free and paid tiers and expresses paid prices per user. Build a seat list from the people who actually create, route, plan or resolve issues. Engineering may need to work in the tool all week. A sales lead may only need a reliable summary of the fix they promised to a buyer. Verify guest access and reporting for the selected tier before counting either group.

Model the bill for the team you have today and the people you expect to add over the next quarter. Include the time spent moving open work and teaching colleagues how your cycle actually runs. If the new tracker saves one status meeting a week, give that saving a value and check it after a trial.


## Linear vs GitHub Issues for a small engineering team

Compare both using a bug that starts with a customer report, leads to a pull request and ends in a release note. A team already coordinating from its repository may have little to gain from moving the same conversation. A team spanning several repositories may value a separate place to see dependencies and priorities across projects.

Make the trial’s *definition of done* concrete. Someone should be able to identify the open dependency, the next owner and the change that reached customers. If the team gets there faster with Linear, adopt it for one team and one workflow first. That gives the next team a working example instead of a different set of forms to fill in.


## Features and decision criteria

- Engineering issue and project tracking
- Recurring cycles and rollover
- Plan-specific workspace and reporting features
