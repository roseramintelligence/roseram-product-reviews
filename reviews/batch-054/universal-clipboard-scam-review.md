# Universal Clipboard review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-24  
**Category:** Cross-device clipboard tools  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

UniClip describes an opt-in clipboard transfer workflow across Android, Apple devices and a browser. Its published free tier places limits on clip length and retention, while Pro adds longer history. Clipboard contents can be sensitive, so account linking, retention and deletion behavior deserve direct attention.

## Editorial verdict

Consider UniClip when a person frequently moves selected snippets between phones, tablets and a computer. The deliberate send action and one-minute free retention are relevant details to test, especially for codes or private text. Do not treat a clipboard sync tool as a secure vault.

## Map the device path before depending on it

List the devices that need to exchange a snippet and verify each one appears in the publisher’s supported workflow. The source describes Android, iPhone, iPad and a browser, with a Chrome extension route. A path that works on one device does not prove every pair is configured.

Try an ordinary nonsensitive link first, then confirm the receiving device displays the expected text. Check what happens when a device is unlinked or the user signs into a different account. This review did not connect a device or test a transfer.


## Understand how long the free clip lasts

The published free tier limits each clip to 200 characters and says clips expire after one minute. That can fit a quick handoff but is a poor match for an archive or a message someone expects to retrieve later. Pro is separately priced for users who need history.

Use a harmless test item and observe the actual lifecycle across the devices involved. Confirm the displayed subscription interval and renewal terms in the app before payment, because promotional display and regional checkout can differ.


## Treat codes and private text with care

Clipboard transfers can involve addresses, phone numbers and verification codes. The publisher says the app does not read the clipboard in the background and that only selected clips are sent; the page also describes cloud storage and encryption in transit and at rest. Those are publisher statements, not an independent security audit.

Avoid placing passwords, recovery phrases or other long-lived secrets into a general clipboard history. Verify account access, unlinking, expiration and deletion using a non-sensitive item before relying on the workflow for work.


## Decide whether history is worth the subscription

A quick cross-device handoff and a searchable long-term archive are different needs. Count how often a person must recover an older clip, then compare that need with the paid tier’s current limits. If most transfers are temporary, the free retention model may already answer the use case.

The official site supplies concrete platform, pricing and retention details that can guide a trial. This review does not validate the app binaries, cloud controls, privacy claims or availability across every device configuration.


## Features and decision criteria

- Android, iPhone, iPad and browser transfer
- User-selected clips
- Free tier with short retention
- Paid history and larger transfer limits

## Pricing considerations

The page also displays an introductory weekly price. Confirm billing interval, renewal and regional checkout terms in the app before subscribing.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the app copy everything from the device clipboard automatically?

The publisher says it handles items the user chooses to send. Verify the behavior on each operating system and version.

### Are free clips retained indefinitely?

No. The published pricing page says free clips expire after one minute.

### Is Pro a substitute for a password manager?

No. Clipboard history may contain sensitive material and should not be treated as a dedicated secrets vault.
