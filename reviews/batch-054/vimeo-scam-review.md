# Vimeo review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-18  
**Category:** Video hosting and creator platform  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Vimeo is a video hosting and creator platform whose plan structure changed in 2026. Its current Help Center lists Free, Creator, Core and Professional plans for individuals and Studio for businesses. Public videos that are not embeddable have unlimited storage and bandwidth across the new plans; private or embeddable videos use Managed storage. The listed annual monthly-equivalent prices are Free $0, Creator $9, Core $24.50, Professional $71 and Studio $400, with monthly billing also shown for individual tiers. New Free accounts receive 3 GB of Managed storage and 1 TB per month of Managed bandwidth. Canceling a paid plan keeps features until renewal, then returns the account to Free; private embed features and storage limits can change. Business accounts may be assigned Studio under Vimeo’s plan-usage policy. The reviewed sources do not substantiate a scam allegation.

## Editorial verdict

Vimeo is a legitimate video service, but its 2026 shift to unlimited public video and capped Managed storage makes privacy and embedding settings central to cost. Creators should distinguish public profile uploads from videos embedded privately on a business site, and business accounts should verify Studio eligibility and the actual price notice sent to their account. Plan cancellation can affect paid privacy and player features. This is a current-source desk review, not a live account test.

## The 2026 plan change separates public sharing from business hosting

Vimeo’s 2026 plan notice replaces older tiers with Free, Creator, Core and Professional for individuals, plus Studio for business use. Its central change is that public videos with embedding turned off have unlimited storage and bandwidth across current plans. Videos that are private or embeddable instead count against Managed storage and may also use a monthly Managed bandwidth allowance.

That split rewards public portfolio sharing while keeping controlled embeds and private material within paid capacity. A creator with public films and a company with an unlisted training library are therefore not using the same storage model. The privacy and embed toggles on each video can change the plan cost, so audit those settings rather than estimating capacity from the visible file count alone.


## Vimeo 2026 plan prices and storage limits

The current Help Center table lists Free at $0; Creator at $9 monthly on annual billing or $16 monthly; Core at $24.50 on annual billing or $40 monthly; Professional at $71 on annual billing or $120 monthly; and Studio at $400 per month billed annually. Free includes 3 GB of Managed storage and 1 TB per month of Managed bandwidth. The listed individual tiers increase Managed storage from 50 GB to 500 GB and 7 TB.

Studio is described for businesses, with 10 TB of Managed storage, 36 TB of yearly Managed bandwidth and ten seats. These figures are not a universal quote. Some legacy users retain more space after an automatic transition, and plan changes roll out over time. Check the account’s own price notice, renewal date, included seats and exact storage allowance before migrating a library.


## Which videos count against Managed storage?

Vimeo defines public storage as videos set to Public with embedding turned off. Embeddable videos and videos with a different privacy setting, including unlisted, password-protected, embed-only or private, count toward Managed storage. The help center explains that public storage is unlimited on the new plans while Managed storage is capped by tier.

This is easy to misunderstand if a business treats every video in its library as a public upload. Inspect each video’s privacy and embed settings and tally the file sizes of materials used on a company website or in a learning portal. If the service is being used to privately host customer or employee content, the managed-storage allowance is likely more relevant than the unlimited public figure.


## What happens when a Managed storage cap is exceeded?

Vimeo says existing videos are not deleted simply because an account moves to a plan with less Managed storage. However, videos can be locked if the account exceeds the new allowance. Locked items cannot be played through shared links or embeds, although Vimeo says they stay downloadable for six months. The system starts with the most recently uploaded items; older videos remain available while they fit within the cap.

This has a direct consequence for business pages and training libraries: a plan downgrade can break a currently working embed even when the source file still exists in the account. Before changing tiers, use Vimeo’s storage tracker and review the warning shown in Billing. Keep an independent copy of critical files, captions and thumbnails, and test any new privacy setting with the actual audience.


## Business accounts have a separate plan rule

Vimeo’s transition notice says accounts identified for business use are not allowed to use the new individual plans and are instead moved to Studio. Vimeo describes its classification as based on objective account-level factors and says affected users receive an email with the plan, price and transition date. Studio has different capacity, seats and support than the creator-focused tiers.

A company should not assume that the $9 Creator or $24.50 Core annual-equivalent price is available for a business library. Check the account’s usage classification and transition message. If an individual account appears to have been classified incorrectly, Vimeo says to contact support for review. We did not assess the classification of any specific account or infer it from a brand’s public site.


## The Free plan can be useful if videos are genuinely public

The current Free plan allows unlimited public videos with unlimited public bandwidth, while keeping non-public and embeddable media within a 3 GB Managed storage allocation and 1 TB monthly bandwidth allowance. Vimeo advertises ad-free public playback and other sharing features on its free-plan page. This can work for a creator sharing a portfolio where visitors watch on Vimeo.

It is a different proposition for a brand using the player inside its own website or requiring passwords and restricted embeds. Those items count as Managed storage. Choose the plan based on how videos will be distributed, not only how many public uploads the landing page promotes. Keep account inactivity and community rules in view as well; Vimeo’s Help Center says it may contact users before deleting a free account inactive for a full year.


## Cancellation does not immediately refund the subscription

Vimeo says cancellation stops the next renewal while paid features remain available until the end of the current billing cycle. At the end of the term, the account moves to Free. Vimeo notes that paid features such as Managed storage, player customization, additional privacy settings and video-on-demand titles may no longer be available. Refunds are not automatic when a user cancels; eligibility is handled under a separate refund policy.

Before canceling, check which embeds depend on non-public storage or paid player controls. Download or move the original files, note which privacy settings will change and replace business-critical embeds if necessary. If the subscription was purchased through Apple or Google, Vimeo says cancellation must be handled through that app store. A cancellation made in the wrong billing channel may not stop renewal.


## A safe Vimeo migration begins with a library inventory

Create a list of video URL, privacy state, embed destinations, storage size, owner and business purpose. Identify which files are public and which count against Managed storage. Export original media, captions and thumbnails under the account’s own tools, then check that the downloaded files open and play.

For an embedded library, test a small set of pages after any plan change. Verify password access, playback controls, mobile behavior and viewer permissions. Keep redirect and link records if public URLs are changing. A successful download alone does not preserve the player configuration, comments, analytics or viewer workflow, so define what must be migrated separately.


## Alternatives and the right comparison

Compare Vimeo with Wistia, a business video host, cloud storage with a video CDN, or the website platform’s own video function. Use the same public and private files, embed requirements, audience size, team seats and analytics question. Compare not just storage and price but privacy options, monthly bandwidth, player controls, marketing tools and what happens on a downgrade.

Vimeo’s free public limits can be compelling for open creator work; Wistia may be more aligned with marketing analytics and lead tools; a direct media host may fit a product with custom playback needs. Those are starting points, not universal rankings. Test the actual player in the target site and model a plan lapse before moving a large library.


## Who should choose Vimeo and who should pause

Vimeo may suit an individual creator who shares public work and values an ad-free player. A business may find the Studio tier more appropriate when it needs managed embed storage, seats, support and a business-use plan. Teams that carefully manage privacy settings can benefit from the clear distinction between public and Managed video.

Pause if the organization expects unlimited private embeds because public videos are unlimited, or if its budget assumes the individual Creator price applies to company use. Also pause before canceling if a high-volume library depends on the paid player. Those are concrete plan boundaries, not evidence of a deceptive service.

## Features and decision criteria

- Ad-free video hosting and sharing for public creator work
- Unlimited public, non-embeddable video storage on current plans
- Managed storage allowances for private or embeddable video
- Individual Creator, Core, Professional and business Studio plans

## Pricing considerations

These plan names, allowances and prices come from Vimeo’s 2026 change notice and were checked November 18, 2026. Vimeo is transitioning accounts in phases; legacy accounts may receive a different storage allowance or plan transition notice. New Core signups have 500 GB Managed storage, while some legacy accounts automatically moved to Core retain 2 TB. Business-use accounts may be required to use Studio. Confirm the account-specific plan, renewal price and video locks in Billing before changing or canceling.
