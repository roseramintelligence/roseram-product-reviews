# Webflow review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-16  
**Category:** Visual website design and hosting  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Webflow separates site hosting plans from workspace collaboration plans. Site pages, CMS capacity, bandwidth and custom-domain publishing depend on the selected plan.

## Editorial verdict

Webflow’s pricing and help pages describe separate Site and Workspace plans. Review the exact combination needed for design, hosting and publishing; this is not a hands-on performance test.

## Webflow review: distinguish the site plan from the workspace plan

Webflow’s pricing structure has two parts that buyers should not collapse into one number. A Site plan governs publishing a particular site and its capabilities; a Workspace plan covers staging and collaboration. A free Workspace does not mean a custom domain can be hosted for free. List each site, domain, staging need, editor and designer before estimating the bill. This simple inventory prevents the common mistake of pricing only the designer seat and overlooking production hosting.

Build a small prototype on a staging domain and test the exact publishing action you need. Confirm which plan enables a custom domain, forms, CMS items and the expected bandwidth. For an agency managing several client sites, calculate costs per site as well as seats. Keep the client’s domain and billing ownership clear so the site is not stranded in a contractor account.


## Webflow pricing, CMS capacity and publishing constraints

Webflow’s US pricing page displayed Starter at no charge, Basic at $15 per site monthly with annual billing and Premium at $25 per site monthly with annual billing when checked 2026-11-16. The page lists differing static-page, form, bandwidth and CMS allowances; Workspace plans and seats are separate. Those are dated publisher figures, not a quote. Verify which plan currently provides the CMS capacity and publishing features your project requires.

Estimate page count, form volume, CMS items and traffic from actual requirements. If the site publishes product or editorial pages from a CMS, test one representative collection and the expected editing process before migrating all content. Track any per-site or usage limits in a deployment checklist. A design that exceeds plan capacity is expensive to rework after a launch deadline.


## Webflow design handoff, code and ongoing maintenance

Test the path from visual design to a usable site on desktop and mobile. Have a content editor make a small copy change without changing layout, then have a designer adjust a component without breaking published pages. Document who reviews and who publishes. If custom code or integrations are part of the site, keep their source and ownership accessible to the business.

Webflow lists code export in certain Workspace tiers and notes that dynamic CMS content is not included in a static code export. Confirm that limitation against your intended exit plan. Export a sample and inspect whether the assets and behavior you depend on remain usable. Do not assume that an export is a complete substitute for the hosted CMS or a custom integration.


## Webflow forms, search visibility and accessibility checks

A visually polished page still needs forms that are understandable and accessible. Test labels, validation, keyboard use and confirmation messaging with a small form. Find out where submissions are stored, who can download them and how long they are retained. Confirm that the custom domain, redirects and canonical URLs behave as expected when the site is published.

For search visibility, check page titles, descriptions, headings, structured data and redirects against the requirements of the site. A builder can expose controls; it cannot guarantee indexing or rankings. Test pages after publishing to staging, and verify mobile rendering, load behavior and broken links before changing the live domain.


## Webflow alternatives and the complete ownership cost

Compare Webflow with a managed CMS and a code-based site using the same two-page prototype, form and content collection. Compare design flexibility, editor workflow, hosting, CMS capacity, migration, accessibility and who maintains updates. Include separate site, workspace and seat costs when applicable. A hosted visual editor may reduce some development work while creating dependence on platform-specific structures.

Ask who controls the domain, backups and analytics, and what happens when a site is transferred or cancelled. Include the time needed for a designer to maintain components and a developer to maintain custom code. Choose from the workload your team can support, not just the speed of the first mockup.


## Who should consider Webflow?

Webflow may suit a team that values visual control and can plan the distinct costs of designing, collaborating and hosting each site. Confirm the Site and Workspace plans, CMS limits, domain requirements and export behavior before building a production workflow. Use a staging site to validate both editor and visitor experiences.

Webflow’s official documentation supports the plan and feature descriptions above; it does not demonstrate a specific site’s speed, accessibility or search rank. This review is buyer diligence, not an allegation of fraud. Verify your exact configuration with a small project before committing to a broader migration.


## Features and decision criteria

- Visual website designer and publishing
- Site plans with static-page, bandwidth and CMS limits
- Workspace plans for staging and team collaboration

## Pricing considerations

Webflow’s pricing page showed these US site-plan amounts on 2026-11-16; Workspace plans and paid seats are separate. Prices are per site, taxes may apply and CMS, bandwidth or publishing limits vary. Confirm the current Site plus Workspace combination you need.
