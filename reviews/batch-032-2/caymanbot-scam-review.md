## Platform Core Functions and Data Coverage

At its foundation, CaymanBot functions as an options flow, whale tracking, and market intelligence interface. It is designed to aggregate institutional options prints alongside secondary financial records that typical retail brokerage terminals scatter across disconnected tabs. The software monitors trades executed on United States exchanges, identifying transaction flags such as aggressive market sweeps, high-dollar whale premiums, and anomalous volume relative to existing open interest. Rather than functioning as an automated trade execution platform, it operates as an analytics console where users observe institutional tape prints.

Beyond core options flow, the platform surfaces data from non-traditional and regulatory sources. It monitors event-driven odds and prominent wallet transactions across on-chain prediction platforms like Polymarket. Concurrently, it aggregates mandatory government filings, tracking stock purchases and sales by United States lawmakers under the STOCK Act, executive insider trade registrations submitted via SEC Form 4, and institutional investment adjustments reported quarterly through SEC Form 13F filings. Bringing these streams into one interface gives users contextual intelligence to cross-check unusual options spikes with institutional filings.

For market structure analysis, the software provides specialized heatmaps covering gamma exposure, commonly abbreviated as GEX. These visual heatmaps help traders observe calculated gamma levels across active strikes, revealing modeled dealer hedging regimes, potential acceleration zones, and flip points for high-volume exchange-traded funds and major equities like SPY, QQQ, and NVDA. The tool also provides VEX and Charm metrics, attempting to capture volatility sensitivity and time-decay exposure among market-making entities managing extensive options books.


## Onboarding Friction and the Educational Workflow

A common barrier for newer traders entering the options space is deciphering the raw exchange tape. CaymanBot addresses this challenge by incorporating a structured Learning Mode alongside its real-time data screens. Rather than presenting raw transaction rows without context, the early stages of Learning Mode translate complex transaction records into plain English sentences. For example, the platform demonstrates this translation by explaining that an aggressive print reflects an entity purchasing short-dated call options at a specific strike, alongside a note explaining that the contract gains value if the underlying equity moves higher.

Crucially, these plain-language interpretations do not replace the underlying tape data. The software displays the synthetic explanation directly next to the original trade components: the underlying ticker, expiration date, strike price, side, executed contract size, and total dollar premium paid. This visual pairing gives retail users a reference point to verify how human language maps to raw exchange variables. As users progress through the learning stages, the interface introduces intermediate options concepts, progressing from basic call and put mechanics up to implied volatility metrics and Greeks.

To let users practice without financial danger, the service includes a virtual paper trading account equipped with a simulated $100,000 balance. Users can execute simulated positions directly against incoming market data. This paper trading environment allows traders to test portfolio allocation, examine the practical drag of option decay, and practice trade management without deploying real capital into a brokerage account. The vendor explicitly documents that simulated paper trading results represent hypothetical scenarios that cannot predict live market performance.


## Pricing Structure and Commercial Transparency

Subscription transparency is a major focus for traders reviewing platform authenticity. CaymanBot approaches pricing with a clearly delineated structure across free and premium access. A permanent Free plan is available at no charge, granting ongoing access to delayed options flow, the $100,000 virtual balance, Learning Mode modules, and several free calculators. New sign-ups receive an automatic 14-day trial of the Premium tier without being asked to submit credit card details. When the 14 days expire, accounts transition to the Free plan unless the user chooses to purchase a subscription.

For users who decide to upgrade, standard Premium access costs $24.99 per month when billed on a monthly schedule, or $224.99 per year when billed annually, which reduces the effective cost to roughly $18.75 per month. In addition, the publisher outlines a founding member promotion for its first 100 paying customers, offering access at $14.99 per month or $119 annually, which remains locked for as long as the subscription renews without interruption. The vendor emphasizes a single-tier policy where one price unlocks all premium capabilities without requiring secondary add-ons, higher tiers, or surprise module fees.

Cancellation procedures can be managed directly through the user profile settings, and accounts retain active paid access through the completion of the current billing cycle. However, prospective buyers should carefully note that CaymanBot explicitly treats payments as non-refundable once settled. The presence of a credit-card-free 14-day trial serves as the designated window for traders to test latency, user experience, and visual relevance before making a financial commitment.


## Data Latency and Exchange Attestation Realities

Options flow analytics depend heavily on timestamp precision, making data delays a critical factor for any prospective buyer. CaymanBot clearly segments its data latency across three distinct tiers. Public visitors browsing the site without logging in see tape information delayed by 60 minutes. Users who register a standard Free account see options flow delayed by 16 minutes. Premium subscribers receive real-time options flow data, but only after they satisfy regulatory exchange compliance procedures.

The software operates as a licensed vendor receiving consolidated options tape data from the Options Price Reporting Authority, widely known as OPRA. To view real-time data under OPRA guidelines, users must complete a formal non-professional attestation. This declaration confirms that the subscriber is using the financial data for personal investment purposes rather than on behalf of a financial institution, hedge fund, or registered advisory firm. Without this signed attestation, the platform cannot deliver live data, and feeds remain delayed. Conspicuously marking delayed feeds is an explicit OPRA regulatory mandate.

Prospective buyers must recognize how data delay impacts specific trading styles. A 16-minute delay is entirely adequate for swing traders, macro analysts, and beginners studying the mechanics of unusual sweeps after hours. Conversely, scalpers and day traders operating around intraday momentum require true real-time visibility, because a short-dated 0DTE options trade can experience major price swings within minutes of an institutional print crossing the tape. Buyers must account for these timing rules when evaluating whether the free tier meets their needs.

## Documented Platform Limitations and Operational Boundaries

While CaymanBot documents a solid feature set, prospective buyers must understand its clear operational limits. First and foremost, the platform is strictly a research and education interface, not an execution broker. Users cannot place live orders through CaymanBot to execute real trades in the market. Every position opened on the site is confined to its simulated $100,000 paper trading environment. Traders must execute actual orders separately through their own brokerage accounts, which introduces unavoidable friction between observing an alert and getting filled.

Another operational boundary stems from the mathematical models powering the gamma exposure metrics. While options prints, insider forms, and 13F disclosures represent observed historical data, GEX heatmaps, VEX charts, and Charm levels are derived from dealer hedging models. These models calculate probable dealer behavior under theoretical assumptions, which cannot account for private off-exchange hedging or cross-asset balance sheet adjustments. Traders should treat gamma flip levels as modeled reference zones rather than absolute support or resistance barriers.

Lastly, users must consider the practical limits of tracking political and institutional filings. Although congressional transactions and corporate Form 4 records provide valuable market context, they are backward-looking by nature. Federal law allows lawmakers and corporate executives statutory grace periods before filing disclosures, meaning public reports often appear weeks after the actual trades took place. Expecting delayed political disclosures to serve as immediate short-term trading signals is an operational mistake.
