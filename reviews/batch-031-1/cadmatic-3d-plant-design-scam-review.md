## Cadmatic One License Mechanics and Token Allocation

Cadmatic has introduced the Cadmatic One License structure, designated as COL, as its primary subscription licensing system. Moving away from rigid perpetual seat assignments dedicated to single named users or standalone tools, COL uses a shared pool of concurrent tokens. Customers subscribe to a calculated quantity of tokens on either monthly or annual contracts. The vendor claims that this token architecture eliminates restrictive software packaging, minimizes idle software overhead, and enables engineering organizations to adapt access dynamically across evolving project stages.

The practical mechanism of the Cadmatic One License operates through a three-stage lifecycle: subscribe, borrow, and return. Under this model, an enterprise establishes a collective pool of tokens based on its estimated simultaneous user capacity. When an engineer opens Cadmatic 3D Plant Design, the application automatically borrows a predetermined allotment of tokens from the common pool. While the program remains active, those tokens are unavailable to other team members. Once the engineer finishes their tasks and closes the application, the system automatically returns those tokens to the corporate pool without requiring manual administrative intervention.

This borrowing structure enables multi-disciplinary teams to shift access across different tools without purchasing redundant dedicated seats for every discipline. An engineer might borrow tokens to execute preliminary schematic layouts in Cadmatic P&ID during the conceptual phase, and then return those tokens so that a structural detailer can check out tokens to work within Cadmatic 3D Plant Design during detailed modeling. However, the viability of this model hinges entirely on predictable concurrent usage math, which buyers must scrutinize closely during contract discussions.


## Perpetual Licensing Options and Long-Term Ownership

Recognizing that some industrial engineering enterprises, shipyards, and public utilities reject recurring subscription software models, Cadmatic continues to offer traditional perpetual licensing paired with maintenance agreements. Under this purchasing framework, the client organization makes a one-time capital investment to secure perpetual software ownership. This purchase is accompanied by a mandatory three-year initial maintenance agreement, which subsequently transitions to automatic one-year maintenance renewals to provide ongoing software updates, technical patches, and vendor support services.

Cadmatic positions this traditional perpetual licensing path as an ideal solution for organizations that maintain predictable, stable design workloads over extended timeframes and prefer capitalized balance-sheet investments over operational software expenses. The vendor notes that perpetual customers can also supplement their base software licenses by purchasing short-term Cadmatic One License token pools when temporary project peaks require additional engineering seats. This hybrid option offers an avenue for handling sudden surges in drafting demand without permanently expanding fixed capital assets.

Evaluating perpetual licensing against token subscriptions requires thorough financial modeling by engineering leadership. While perpetual ownership insulates a firm from recurring software subscription fee increases and guarantees long-term tool availability for maintaining operational plant assets, it demands substantial upfront capital. Furthermore, mandatory maintenance agreements represent ongoing cash commitments that must be factored into the total cost of ownership. Prospective buyers must evaluate their historical project volume consistency to determine whether capitalized perpetual ownership or flexible token subscriptions yields better capital efficiency.
