# Calamari review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-18  
**Category:** Modular HR, time-off and attendance software  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Calamari is a cloud HR platform sold as separate modules for time off, time and attendance, Core HR, performance and staff scheduling. Its modular approach can keep a small team from buying a larger suite, but pricing is per active user and per module, with a minimum equivalent to 10 seats for each module. The annual rates listed on the current FAQ are $2 per user/month for Time Off and Core HR, $2.50 for Time & Attendance, Performance and Scheduling, and $0.80 for a SAML add-on; prices exclude VAT. A 14-day trial is listed without a credit card. The operational decision is less about the low starting rate than whether the chosen modules, role permissions, attendance methods and data handling match your policies. The vendor describes security controls and a GDPR DPA, but those claims and documents should be reviewed against your own requirements. Roseram did not test a live account or audit Calamari. The sources reviewed do not substantiate fraud.

## Editorial verdict

The evidence supports evaluating Calamari as a focused, modular HR operations tool—not calling it a scam. Its strongest fit is a team that wants a configurable leave calendar, attendance capture, employee records or scheduling without purchasing every module in a broader suite. The key cost detail is easy to miss: the annual per-user price applies separately to each chosen module and the minimum charge is equivalent to ten active seats per module. VAT is extra. Before loading employee records, map roles and document visibility, check the optional GPS and kiosk attendance methods against your workplace policy, obtain the DPA, and test exports. Calamari’s support materials say cancellation leaves access through the paid term, unused time is not refunded, and account data removal begins 60 days after validity ends with permanent deletion after 90 days. Plan your export and exit before canceling. Publisher security statements are not an independent audit, and the vendor says it is still preparing for its own ISO 27001 certification. No live account, payroll process or security control was tested for this review.

## Calamari is a set of HR modules, not one fixed bundle

Calamari groups several workplace processes under one account: Time Off, Time & Attendance, Core HR, Performance and Scheduling. That structure can be useful when a company wants to replace a leave spreadsheet or add attendance tracking without buying every HR function. It also means the phrase “Calamari pricing” is incomplete until you name the modules and user count.

The product’s fit depends on the work you need to standardize. A team that only needs leave requests and balances should price Time Off alone. A shift-based employer may need attendance and Scheduling as well. An organization storing employment documents will need to consider Core HR permissions and its data lifecycle. These choices change both the workflow and the bill.


## Time Off is more than a shared calendar

Calamari describes configurable leave categories, accrual and carryover rules, public-holiday calendars, request restrictions and multi-step or automatic approval flows. Policies can differ by team, contract type or employee. Those capabilities matter when a company has several locations or employment arrangements and one spreadsheet has become a patchwork of exceptions.

Before purchase, model two or three real policies: a standard vacation request, a leave type that accrues monthly, and a case requiring a substitute or a different approver. Check the resulting balance and audit trail. A polished calendar is not enough if administrators cannot reproduce the organization’s written rules accurately.


## Attendance options need a workplace-policy review

The vendor FAQ lists web and mobile clock-in, optional GPS, QR-code kiosks, NFC cards or tags, iBeacons, geofencing and API integration with physical devices. A shared tablet at a warehouse entrance and a mobile punch for a field team solve different problems. Pick the simplest method that gives payroll and managers reliable records.

Location tracking is a particularly important configuration choice. The vendor describes GPS as optional for mobile attendance, but an employer should confirm precisely when location is collected, who can inspect it, whether the feature can be limited to clock-in events and how employees are informed. Document the policy before enabling it; do not treat a technical toggle as a substitute for workplace or privacy review.


## Core HR makes permissions a first-order buying requirement

Core HR is described as a place for employee profiles, custom fields, documents and requests. Those records can include contracts, payslips, identity information or other sensitive material. Calamari’s help materials describe employee, manager and administrator roles, while its FAQ says administrators can set visibility and editing permissions at field, document-catalog and module levels.

Test those boundaries before importing live data. Create an employee, a direct manager, a team manager and an administrator in a trial account. Check which profiles, documents, timesheets and reports each can read or change. Verify a departing employee’s access and the administrator offboarding path. The organization remains responsible for deciding which roles should see each record.


## Performance and Scheduling add distinct workflows

The Performance module covers review processes, goals and one-to-one meetings; Scheduling is a separate module for planning shifts. A company may want both, but neither is automatically required to get value from leave tracking. Ask the managers who will operate each process whether they need it in the same tool or already have a system that staff use consistently.

Integration across modules is useful only if the resulting records are correct. Test whether a published shift appears where managers expect, how a leave request affects availability, and who can edit the schedule. Make sure a schedule change does not silently alter payroll assumptions or employee expectations. The vendor’s module descriptions help frame the test; the buyer should verify it in the actual configuration.


## Integrations and API limits belong in the implementation plan

Calamari lists connections with workplace services such as Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, Slack and Teams, along with a public REST API. Its FAQ gives a standard API quota of two requests per second, 600 per hour and 6,000 per day, covering employee data, leave requests and timesheet entries. Those limits can matter to payroll syncs and bulk administrative updates.

Do not assume every integration is available in every module or plan. Identify which system owns employee identity, leave balances and time records. Test a single update in each direction, including a correction and a disabled user. If a connector is business-critical, confirm support, permissions, rate limits and error handling with Calamari before making it part of payroll operations.


## The 10-seat minimum changes the low headline price

The annual rates in the current FAQ are $2 per user per month for Time Off and Core HR, and $2.50 for Time & Attendance, Performance and Scheduling. The FAQ also lists a $0.80 SAML add-on. The minimum fee is equivalent to ten seats for each module. Prices are shown before VAT, and the pricing page has a billing selector and a calculator.

For a concrete illustration, all five listed modules at their annual rates total $11.50 per user per month. At ten seats, that is $115 per month equivalent, before VAT, billed annually. That calculation is not a quote: the selected term, account currency, user count, active modules and current checkout determine the actual charge. A three-person company buying one module should still ask how the ten-seat minimum applies.


## Use the 14-day trial to test policy edge cases

The publisher advertises a 14-day trial without a credit card and says it includes full access. Use the trial to test the real request and approval path rather than only reviewing sample screens. Import a small, non-sensitive CSV, configure one leave policy and one shift pattern, then ask an employee and manager to complete the steps on both desktop and mobile.

Try an exception: a request that violates a notice rule, a balance that carries over, a substitute who is unavailable, or a time entry that needs a correction. Check whether the right person can correct the record and whether the change is visible in a report. Confirm which data remains if the trial converts to a paid subscription and which deletion schedule applies if it does not.


## Cancellation is straightforward, but plan the export first

The help center says an administrator can cancel through subscription management. Access remains active until the end of the paid billing period; unused time is not refunded. Monthly customers should cancel before renewal, while annual customers should confirm cancellation to avoid the next renewal. Payment method and billing details may differ by term.

Before the account closes, export reports and any employee data the organization must retain. Calamari says data removal begins 60 days after account validity ends and becomes permanent after 90 days. The time between cancellation and deletion is a migration window, not a reason to postpone the export. Save the contract and cancellation confirmation alongside the data-retention plan.


## Employee archiving and account deletion are different

For an individual who leaves the company, Calamari’s help center describes setting a termination date, clearing future absence requests and archiving the profile. Archived employee records remain available for reports and the vendor says archived users are not charged. An administrator can later permanently delete an archived profile, which the help article warns cannot be undone.

That employee-level process is different from the account-level deletion clock after a subscription ends. Decide what history HR, finance or legal teams need before deleting a person, and what records must be exported before the organization’s whole account is removed. Use least-privilege access during the retention period, especially for archived employee documents.


## Read security statements as vendor claims to verify

Calamari’s security page describes backups, customer data separation, encryption at rest and in transit, audit logs, vulnerability scanning, penetration tests, access reviews and MFA for internal systems. It says the service is hosted on AWS and references certifications held by its hosting provider. These are publisher statements; Roseram did not independently verify controls, test the service or review a third-party audit report.

The same security page says Calamari was preparing for its own ISO 27001 certification. Do not treat AWS’s certifications as proof that Calamari itself holds that certification. If your procurement process requires a current SOC report, penetration-test summary, incident terms, recovery targets or subprocessor list, request the documents and evaluate their scope and date before uploading sensitive employee data.


## Get the DPA and map your data responsibilities

Calamari’s help center says an English and Polish data processing agreement are available. Its GDPR guidance describes the employer as the data controller for employee records and Calamari as a processor. That allocation is a useful starting point for review, but the actual signed DPA and your use case determine the contractual details.

Before rollout, list the categories of employee data you will store, which modules and integrations will process them, who can access them and how requests for access or deletion will be handled. Check the DPA’s security, subprocessors, international transfer, incident-notice and deletion language. Do not rely on a general “GDPR compliant” claim as a substitute for a documented configuration.


## Onboarding effort is part of the price

The FAQ says typical onboarding takes about five days and describes customer-success support for initial configuration and employee imports; larger-team setup may cost extra. A configured HR tool can save repeated spreadsheet work, but the first week still requires decisions about employee data, approval routes, policies, roles and integrations.

Estimate the internal time needed from HR, payroll, managers and IT. Clean the source spreadsheet before import, decide who approves each request and define how old records will be reconciled. Ask which implementation tasks are included, whether data migration is charged separately and who handles a failed import. These details help distinguish a low subscription price from the full cost of a reliable rollout.


## Who is likely to benefit

Calamari is worth considering when a small or midsize organization needs configurable leave, attendance or employee-record workflows and wants to select modules independently. It may fit distributed teams that use several holiday calendars, or shift-based workplaces that need kiosk and mobile clocking. The published support materials and integration list give buyers concrete items to test.

The clearest benefit is consolidating a process the organization already understands: request, approval, record, report. Start with one module and a limited group if that is enough to verify the workflow. Add a module when staff can explain what work it replaces and how the output will be used.


## Who should compare other approaches first

A very small team with one simple leave policy may find a shared calendar and a well-maintained spreadsheet cheaper and easier to govern. A company needing payroll processing, recruiting, benefits administration or a broader HR suite should list those requirements separately; Calamari’s published modules do not by themselves prove that the platform covers every adjacent HR task.

Organizations that require on-premise hosting should note that Calamari says it is cloud-only. Teams with strict data residency, identity or audit requirements should request the contract and security evidence before trial data is entered. If an essential feature is available only through an API or paid add-on, include that cost and implementation work in the comparison.


## A practical comparison and purchase checklist

Compare Calamari with your existing HRIS, a focused time-off tool or a broader suite using the same policies, employee count and billing period. Record the total after module minimums and VAT; permission boundaries; API and integration limits; export formats; and the path to cancel. Test the employee, manager and administrator views using dummy records before importing real data.

Ask for the current DPA, security materials and a written answer on location tracking if you intend to use GPS or geofencing. Confirm the exact annual renewal, the account deletion schedule and whether setup for your organization is included. If those conditions fit, the modular trial provides a reasonable way to check whether daily HR tasks are actually simpler.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Calamari a scam?

The official materials reviewed describe a commercial HR platform with module pricing, a help center, security information and a published data processing agreement. They do not substantiate fraud. SCAM Review is a buyer-check label, not a legal finding. Roseram did not test the service or audit its security.

### How much does Calamari cost?

The FAQ lists annual rates from $2 to $2.50 per active user per month for each module, plus a $0.80 SAML add-on. There is a minimum equivalent to ten seats per module, and VAT is extra. The actual total depends on modules, billing period, user count and currency in the pricing calculator.

### Can I buy only one Calamari module?

Yes. Calamari says modules can be selected separately and added later. However, its published minimum fee is equivalent to ten active seats for each module, so a small organization may pay for the minimum even if fewer people use that module. Confirm the minimum and total in checkout.

### Does Calamari offer a free trial?

The current pricing page and FAQ describe a 14-day trial with no credit card required. The FAQ says trial data remains if you activate a paid subscription. If you do not continue, check the account’s trial expiry and data-deletion schedule before importing real employee information.

### Can Calamari track employee GPS location?

The FAQ lists optional GPS tracking in its mobile clock-in method and also lists QR, NFC, geofencing and other methods. Before enabling location features, confirm what is recorded, which roles can see it, how long records remain, and whether your employee notices and workplace rules cover the use.

### What happens to Calamari data after cancellation?

The help center says account access continues until the end of the paid period, unused time is not refunded, data removal starts 60 days after account validity ends and data is permanently deleted after 90 days. Export reports and employee records before that window closes, and confirm the current policy for your contract.
