# Circle review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-24  
**Category:** Community platform and membership software  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Circle is a hosted community and membership platform with courses, events, a website, member profiles and payment features. Its current public pricing page lists Professional at US$89 per month and an additional 2% Circle fee on paywall transactions, alongside Stripe processing fees.

## Editorial verdict

Circle is a candidate for a business that wants community, courses, events, member access and paywalls under one hosted service. The monthly subscription and transaction fee should be considered together, and features such as workflows vary by tier. Compare the member experience, plan limits, processor costs and data-export path before migrating. This is an editorial review of official information, not a hands-on test or a report of customer outcomes.

## See whether one member space can simplify your setup

Circle combines a hosted community with courses, events, member profiles and payment features. That can help when a business wants a central home for members rather than directing them between a discussion tool, course site and event calendar. It still needs a clear structure: a unified product can become noisy if every feature is turned on without a member journey.

Map the first visit, first course, first event and support request. Identify which pages a new member should see and who maintains each one. If your audience needs only a private discussion group, compare a simpler option before paying for a broader platform.


## Add transaction fees to the subscription calculation

Circle’s current public pricing page lists Professional at US$89 per month, Business at US$199 and Scale at US$419. Its paywall fee page describes Circle fees of 2% on Professional, 1% on Business and Scale, and 0.5% on Circle Plus. Stripe processing fees are additional and vary with location and payment method.

Estimate your costs with a realistic order value and number of transactions. A higher monthly plan may reduce the platform fee percentage but can still cost more overall at low sales volume. Include refunds and international payments in the model, and check how the rate applies to the exact paywall or checkout feature you intend to use.


## Compare plan features, not just price

The pricing page lists member, admin, space, storage, live-stream and workflow limits that differ by tier. For example, Professional has fewer admins and spaces than higher plans, while workflows and APIs appear on Business and above. A team should check whether the features it needs are included before building around them.

List the features that affect your routine: automated onboarding, member segmentation, custom fields, integrations, branded email, migration assistance and mobile access. Mark each as essential or optional. Then compare the needed tier against the cost of the separate tools you would otherwise keep.


## Consider the implications of moving a paid community

Moving a membership can affect recurring charges, member access, course progress, links and support history. Ask what data and content can be exported, which subscriptions need to be recreated and what notice members will receive. Keep a migration plan and a rollback copy of the material you own.

Run a small, private pilot. Test a new member account, a payment, a cancellation, a moderator action and the mobile experience. Check that each transaction appears in both Circle and Stripe records as expected. These are recommended purchase checks, not tests conducted by Roseram.


## Evaluate the policy and operating details

Circle’s Creator Terms describe service and paywall fees, while the support article explains how Stripe processing adds to the total. Read those terms with the privacy policy and current subscription terms. Confirm how member data is handled, what happens after cancellation and whether the content remains exportable.

A community business also needs transparent membership terms. State the renewal interval, cancellation route, access rules, refund policy and support contact in language members can understand. Circle can provide hosting and payment tools; it cannot establish demand or guarantee that a paid group will be profitable.


## Features and decision criteria

- Community spaces, member profiles and directory
- Courses, live events and a hosted website
- Paywalls and member payments with Stripe processing
- Workflows, APIs and limits vary by plan

## Pricing considerations

Circle says paid plans include a 14-day trial without a credit card on its public page. Paywall fees are in addition to Stripe processing, which varies by card, country and payment method. The pricing page can change; confirm current amounts, plan limits, tax, renewal and cancellation details before enrollment.
