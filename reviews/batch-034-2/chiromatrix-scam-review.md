# ChiroMatrix review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-18  
**Category:** Chiropractic practice marketing services  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

ChiroMatrix, a chiropractic-focused iMatrix marketing offering, describes websites, local SEO, Google Ads, reputation management, social media, listings and patient education content. Its public page says packages start at $99 but does not state the billing period in the reviewed answer. It also publishes performance claims that should be treated as vendor-reported marketing, not as a forecast for a particular clinic. This review sets out how a practice can inspect scope, ad spend, lead attribution and contract terms. It reports no client campaign test or finding of fraud.

## Editorial verdict

ChiroMatrix is presented as a specialist marketing service for chiropractic practices, combining a practice website with local visibility, advertising, reputation and patient-education options. The niche positioning may reduce the work required to explain common clinic services, but a practice still needs to know which deliverables, advertising budget and account assets are included in its own package. The publisher says plans start at $99 and makes larger outcome claims on its marketing page; the public material reviewed does not establish a universally applicable return or guarantee. Ask for the precise billing period, term, ad-spend treatment, ownership and cancellation provisions. The source review does not allege fraud and did not measure leads or revenue.

## A specialist marketing package, not a single software subscription

ChiroMatrix's public offering combines several services rather than describing one self-serve application. The page lists practice websites, SEO, Google Ads, reputation management, social media, business listings, patient relationship management and education materials. This can appeal to a clinic that wants one vendor to coordinate multiple marketing tasks. It also means that the package name alone is not a scope of work. Ask which work is performed by the agency, which accounts remain under the clinic's control, how often content is updated, and which responsibilities stay with the practice.

The site describes chiropractic-specific website content and tools such as a 3D spine simulator and patient education pages. Those items may help explain the practice's services, but a clinic should inspect the actual pages for medical accuracy, tone, accessibility and fit with its clinical policies. Marketing content can support a patient conversation; it should not be represented as a diagnosis or as a promise of treatment results. Review the draft before publication and keep a named practice owner responsible for approvals.


## Assess performance claims with a clinic-level measurement plan

ChiroMatrix's page advertises figures such as patients delivered to client websites, average monthly leads and potential return. Those are publisher claims in a marketing context; the page does not establish that an individual clinic will receive the same outcome. Results depend on geography, specialty, current website, competition, budget, call handling and the definition of a qualified lead. Ask for the date range, cohort, denominator and measurement method behind any benchmark that influences a contract decision.

Before a campaign begins, record a baseline and agree on what will be counted: calls, completed appointment requests, new patients, or another clinic-defined outcome. Verify how the tracking number, forms and ad account attribute an inquiry. Ask whether the practice can access those reports and the underlying accounts without the agency. Compare cost per qualified inquiry over a defined period and keep advertising spend separate from the management fee. A ranking screenshot or number of web visits is a marketing signal, not by itself evidence of revenue or patient benefit.


## Price, advertising spend and contract scope

The marketing page's FAQ says packages start at $99, but the answer reviewed does not specify whether that is monthly, which services are included, whether the figure is an introductory offer, or whether ad spend is separate. The site directs prospects to compare packages and schedule a free consultation. Treat the starting figure as a lead-in, not a complete quote. Ask for the price of the exact website, SEO and advertising work the clinic wants and a line-by-line explanation of recurring and one-time charges.

Clarify minimum term, renewal, campaign pauses, cancellation notice, domain and website ownership, content exports, and what happens to tracking numbers if the clinic leaves. If the package runs paid advertising, identify who funds the media budget and whose Google Ads account is used. Confirm that ad spend is billed by the platform or transparently passed through, and that the practice can retain historical campaign data. No pricing comparison is sound until two proposals use the same scope and budget.


## Clinic data, reputation work and a safe pilot

A clinic's marketing systems can collect contact details and appointment inquiries. Ask how form submissions, call recordings and lead reports are handled, who can access them and how long they are retained. Avoid sharing medical histories or other unnecessary patient information with a marketing provider. If the service touches patient data, the clinic should obtain the appropriate privacy and contractual review for its jurisdiction before enabling the integration. The reviewed marketing pages do not establish a specific compliance arrangement for every client package.

A bounded pilot can use one practice location and one approved service page. Review the site draft, verify contact details, test a form using synthetic information, inspect tracking, and document what the agency will change in search listings or ad accounts. Establish a written approval process for clinical claims and patient testimonials. At the pilot's end, inspect the actual leads, response time, spend and ownership of assets. This is a proposed buyer check, not a claim that Roseram evaluated a ChiroMatrix client account.

## Pricing considerations

The publisher says plans start at $99 and directs practices to a tailored consultation and plan comparison. Obtain a written price that distinguishes service fees, advertising budget, setup or migration, term, renewal, taxes and cancellation charges.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is ChiroMatrix a scam?

The reviewed first-party pages describe a chiropractic marketing service, plan options and a consultation process. This review found no basis for a fraud finding and did not test a campaign or client contract. Practices should verify scope, billing, ad-account ownership and measurable deliverables.

### How much does ChiroMatrix cost?

The published FAQ says packages start at $99 but does not state the billing period or all included services. Request an itemized quote separating service fees, ad spend, setup, contract term and cancellation.

### Does ChiroMatrix guarantee new patients or a specific return?

The marketing page presents performance claims and expected improvements, but the reviewed public material does not establish a guaranteed result for a particular practice. Ask for measurement definitions and track clinic-specific outcomes against a baseline.

### Who owns the website and advertising accounts?

The public marketing page reviewed does not settle ownership for an individual package. Put domain, site files, analytics, Google Ads access, tracking numbers and content rights in the written agreement before work starts.
