# Chatim review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-18  
**Category:** Live chat and AI chatbot software  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Chatim sells website live chat, visual chatbot automation, contact buttons, and an AI chatbot connected to a business knowledge base. Its published pricing page lists free, $9, $29, and $59 monthly tiers; paid plans describe unlimited live chats, chatbot-flow capacity, and team members, while AI is capped at 200 or 500 conversations on Pro and Ultimate. Chatim’s privacy policy and DPA describe widget telemetry, message handling, retention periods, subprocessors, and the customer/controller relationship. The reviewed pages do not establish fraud or independently verify service performance. ## What Chatim appears to sell

Chatim is presented as a website customer-engagement tool built around a live-chat widget. A visitor can open a conversation, a staff member can answer from the dashboard, and configured chatbot flows can handle routine prompts when a person is unavailable. The publisher also describes an AI chatbot trained on URLs, sitemaps, or manually entered knowledge. That combination makes Chatim more than a contact button, but it does not make it a full customer-support department by itself. Buyers still need to decide who watches incoming conversations, which questions automation may answer, and where a difficult request should go.

The company's public pages describe a named service and identify Chatim LLC in Sacramento, California. They also publish plan prices, usage limits, support documentation, a privacy policy, and a DPA. Those are useful signs of a defined commercial offer, not proof of product quality or legal compliance. This review assesses what the publisher says and what a buyer should verify. ## Keep live chat, flows, and AI in separate columns

The product page groups several jobs under one brand. Live chat is the human exchange: a visitor asks, and an available person replies. A flow is a set of configured questions, buttons, and handoffs. AI is a generated response based on the context the business supplies. These modes have different failure patterns. A human can misunderstand an unusual request; a fixed flow can reach a dead end; an AI model can answer confidently beyond the source material. The buyer should be able to see which mode handled an interaction and hand an uncertain case to a person without making the visitor start again.

Before choosing a plan, write down the top five questions visitors ask and classify each one. If the answer is a stable fact, a flow or knowledge-base response may be appropriate. If it concerns a refund, a health or safety issue, account access, or a special exception, make the human route obvious. Chatim's marketing describes round-the-clock automation, but an automated reply is not the same thing as an employee being available at every hour. Make that distinction clear in your own customer-service promise.


## The installation path is simple on paper; test the real site

Chatim documents a JavaScript widget and platform-specific installation routes, including WordPress, Shopify, Wix, Squarespace, Webflow, Magento, Next.js, and Google Tag Manager. Its homepage calls the setup lightweight and says the SDK can control the widget and pass custom data. That breadth can reduce the effort of adding a chat window, but the practical question is whether the script behaves correctly with the site's consent manager, content security policy, caching, and mobile layout. A one-line embed can still affect a page's load sequence or conflict with another script.

A sensible pilot starts on a staging page or one low-risk public page. Test the first load, a returning visitor, a closed widget, keyboard focus, a small phone screen, and a slow connection. Then verify the handoff from bot to staff and check that transcripts appear only in the accounts and integrations you expect. If the site uses a consent banner, confirm whether the widget or its storage runs before the relevant permission is granted. The marketing page's performance claims are vendor statements, not a measurement of your own Core Web Vitals.


## The listed prices are approachable, but compare the tier boundaries

The pricing-page snapshot reviewed for this article lists Free at $0 per month, Starter at $9, Pro at $29, and Ultimate at $59. Free is described as including basic live chat, a chatbot flow, and a contact button. Starter adds team access, email notifications, and custom branding. Pro is where the page places Chatim AI, with an allowance of up to 200 conversations, alongside Slack, premium blocks, a JavaScript SDK, and integrations. Ultimate raises the AI allowance to 500 and lists advanced reporting, white labeling, API access, dedicated support, and an account manager.

Those amounts are useful for a first budget, but the page available to this review was marked as crawled last month. It is not a checkout receipt and may lag a current offer, tax treatment, or regional price. Confirm whether the price is monthly or subject to an annual commitment, when the subscription renews, and which exact features are enabled in the account. For an agency or web studio, the page directs resellers to request separate pricing; the single-business numbers should not be used as an assumed white-label resale rate.


## Unlimited live chat does not mean unlimited AI

Chatim's pricing page says paid plans include unlimited live chats, chatbot-flow capacity, and team members, while AI conversations are metered. It lists up to 200 AI conversations on Pro and up to 500 on Ultimate. The word 'unlimited' therefore applies to specific parts of the service, not every generated answer or every resource. A business comparing Chatim with a per-agent or per-chat competitor should price the whole expected workflow: ordinary messages, AI interactions, team seats, add-on integrations, and any third-party services it already pays for.

An AI conversation allowance is not a simple token budget. A short visitor question and a long back-and-forth may consume the same named unit or be counted under a provider rule that is not obvious from the headline. Ask how a conversation is defined, whether repeat sessions count again, what happens when the monthly allowance is reached, and whether the AI stops, hands off, or can trigger an overage. Test those answers in the dashboard or obtain them in writing. Do not assume a fixed plan price guarantees unlimited AI replies.


## The AI disclosure makes the configuration matter

Chatim's privacy policy says that when a site owner enables its AI chatbot using Anthropic Claude, the service sends the visitor's recent conversation messages—up to 20 for context—along with a configured website description and, optionally, knowledge-base content. The policy says the request is sent over HTTPS and that the feature is active only when the owner configures it. Another part of the policy refers to automated replies through Google Vertex AI or Anthropic Claude. A buyer should therefore confirm which provider is selected in the actual project and which data is passed for that configuration.

The policy says directly identifying fields such as names, email addresses, phone numbers, and IP addresses are not intentionally included in the Claude request. That is a description of the request format, not a guarantee that a visitor will never type identifying information into the message itself. A customer-support conversation can contain order numbers, medical details, or account credentials without the business intending it. Put a short warning near the widget, limit the knowledge base to approved material, and decide how staff should handle messages that contain sensitive data.


## The widget handles more than the text a visitor types

The privacy policy's widget section says that page load can involve collection of an IP address through server-side Cloudflare headers, approximate location derived from that address, browser and operating-system details, device information, screen dimensions, language, time zone, referring URL, and the current page URL. It says the widget stores session and client identifiers and widget state in local storage, with a configurable time to live that defaults to 24 hours. Messages, button choices, form submissions, and uploaded files are stored when a visitor interacts with the chat.

The same disclosure says names, emails, and phone numbers are collected when a visitor voluntarily supplies them through a form or message, and that the site's owner can pass additional fields through the JavaScript SDK. This means the data footprint depends partly on how the business configures the product. Keep custom fields minimal, avoid sending internal IDs unless there is a clear need, and check which URLs may expose tokens or private query parameters to a chat transcript. A buyer should map this processing into its own privacy notice and consent design rather than copying a generic vendor sentence.


## Read the privacy policy and DPA as documents with different scopes

Chatim's DPA says the customer is the controller of customer personal data, or a processor where the customer has that role, and Chatim acts as processor for the service. It also says Chatim acts as an independent controller for its own account administration, billing, security monitoring, and aggregated service analytics. That split is important: the vendor may have one relationship to data collected on its marketing website and another to the visitor transcripts a customer routes through an embedded widget.

The privacy policy for chatim.app includes disclosures for analytics, advertising, remarketing, and a 'sold or shared' section under U.S. state-law terminology. The DPA separately says Chatim will not sell or share customer personal information that it processes on the customer's behalf. These passages should not be collapsed into one claim about every visitor record. A business buyer should identify which data is its own customer data, which data relates to a user's visit to chatim.app, and which optional third-party services are enabled. If the distinction affects a contract or compliance decision, request a written explanation from Chatim and preserve the response.


## Retention periods affect the support workflow

The published privacy schedule lists chat records and conversation history as retained for 24 months after last activity; visitor browsing data and abandoned chats for 90 days; inactive accounts for 24 months followed by a 60-day warning; billing and payment records for seven years; and error or diagnostic logs for 90 days. It also says widget local-storage data is configurable per project and defaults to 24 hours. The DPA points customers to this schedule and says data can be exported through the dashboard during the agreement and for 30 days after termination.

A retention schedule is more useful when it aligns with how the business actually handles support. Decide who may export a transcript, whether files should be retained as long as the text, when an employee's access is removed, and how a deletion request moves from the business to the vendor. The DPA describes deletion or return at the customer's request subject to legal-retention exceptions, while the privacy policy provides its own timetable. Ask support how a customer-specific deletion request interacts with backups and any open billing or legal hold. A 24-month chat history may be convenient, but it is not automatically the right setting for every organization.


## The DPA contains specific commitments buyers can assess

The DPA identifies AWS, Cloudflare, Stripe, and Twilio/SendGrid as core subprocessors, and says other providers receive customer data only when the customer affirmatively enables the related integration. It describes data processing primarily in the United States and refers to the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework or Standard Contractual Clauses for certain transfers. It also provides a 30-day notice process for adding or replacing subprocessors and a channel for a customer to object on documented data-protection grounds.

For a buyer, this is concrete enough to review against a vendor register, but the DPA does not answer every deployment question for a particular account. Check the current Annex B list, the enabled integrations, where transcripts are stored, and whether any product configuration changes the transfer path. A company with a formal vendor review should retain the exact DPA version accepted at signup and the corresponding subprocessor list. The DPA is a useful contractual artifact; it is not a substitute for a security review or a determination that the service meets a particular regulation.


## Security statements are still vendor statements

Chatim's DPA annex says data in transit uses TLS 1.2 or higher and data at rest uses AES-256 or equivalent on managed cloud storage and databases. It lists role-based dashboard access, optional multi-factor authentication, administrative audit logs, Cloudflare firewalling and bot management, patching, dependency scanning, rate limits, encrypted backups, and point-in-time recovery. The DPA also commits to notifying a customer without undue delay and within 72 hours after Chatim becomes aware of a breach affecting customer personal data.

Those are meaningful commitments to ask about, but the source is the provider's own DPA. The public document does not amount to an independent audit of Chatim's implementation. It says that third-party certifications or reports, such as SOC 2 or ISO 27001, may be made available for subprocessors where available. If your procurement standard requires a report for Chatim itself, ask for the relevant current evidence rather than inferring certification from the listed cloud vendors. Also ask whether MFA is optional or enforced for the roles that can export conversations, and how administrative logs can be retrieved.


## Check integration availability instead of relying on a long list

Chatim's homepage lists integrations and setup routes including Slack, WordPress, Shopify, Wix, Squarespace, Webflow, Magento, Next.js, Google Tag Manager, webhooks, Pipedrive, and Site123. The documentation index describes a widget SDK, visitor management, and tools for flows and knowledge bases. However, the same homepage labels some channels and connectors—including WhatsApp, Messenger and Instagram direct messages, Zapier, and HubSpot—as coming soon. The DPA discusses integrations that may receive customer data when enabled, while the help center describes capabilities at a general product level.

A listed connector is not proof that it is enabled for every account or available in the same way on every plan. Before designing a support process around a connection, check the account's integration screen, supported direction of sync, required plan, and the data fields sent. For a webhook, use a test endpoint, validate the signature, and reject unexpected fields. For a CRM, confirm whether a lead is created once or on every conversation. Ask Chatim to clarify the status of any feature marked 'coming soon' before using it in a launch plan.


## Trial and cancellation wording deserves a clean operational check

The pricing-page snapshot says paid plans have a 14-day free trial, that a credit card is not required to start the free plan or trial, and that the account can subscribe or downgrade to Free when the trial ends. The same page says upgrades or downgrades take effect in the next billing cycle. These statements give a buyer a reasonable starting expectation, but the page was crawled last month and the account checkout is the controlling surface for the offer shown to a particular user.

The terms say users can terminate their account through the available account tools, but account termination cannot occur until a paid subscription period has expired. That is not necessarily the same control as cancelling an auto-renewing subscription, so buyers should inspect both actions and their effective dates. Save the trial start date, renewal date, cancellation confirmation, and any refund terms. If the service is being considered by a business with more than one admin, decide who has authority to start a paid plan and who will receive billing notices.


## A two-week pilot can answer the practical questions

Use the advertised trial to test the actual work, not just the welcome screen. Choose a small set of pages, prepare five common questions with approved answers, and decide which cases must go to a human. Record whether the widget opens reliably, whether the configured flow reaches the right handoff, and whether an AI answer stays within the supplied knowledge. Include one deliberately ambiguous question and confirm that the system does not invent a company policy. Keep the test account clear of real customer data until the privacy configuration is approved.

At the end of each day, review transcripts, AI conversation usage, response ownership, and the visitor data collected. Test an export and confirm the file includes the expected fields. Toggle integrations on only for a controlled sample, then inspect their payloads and remove them if the destination is not ready. Before trial expiry, calculate the likely monthly cost using the real number of AI conversations and ask how over-limit behavior works. This is a proposed evaluation plan, not a claim that Roseram conducted the pilot.


## Who may find Chatim a reasonable fit

Chatim's visible offer may appeal to a small website team that wants live visitor chat, a basic no-code flow, and a contact button, then wants to add a measured AI layer on a higher tier. The Free and Starter plans provide a way to explore a basic interaction without paying the Pro price immediately. Teams already using a listed connector may appreciate an SDK or webhook path, provided that the integration is available in the current account and the data transfer fits their policy.

It may be a weaker fit for an organization that needs a fully managed help desk, extensive approval workflows, a documented service-level guarantee, strict data residency outside the United States, or high volumes of uncapped AI conversations. Those requirements may be available through a custom arrangement or another product, but the public pages reviewed do not establish that. Write non-negotiables down before comparing vendors. A lower entry price is not a saving if a later plan, extra tooling, or manual queue management is needed to cover an unspoken requirement.


## Compare the job, not just the chat bubble

A basic live-chat widget, a help-desk suite, a CRM with messaging, and an AI chatbot platform overlap but are not interchangeable. A small team may be well served by a simple inbox and contact form. A support organization with assignments, service-level targets, reporting, and a large knowledge base may prefer a help desk built around ticket ownership. A business that primarily wants automation may compare flow editing, source citations, escalation behavior, and the price of actual AI usage. Chatim's public pages point to several of these elements, but the buyer should judge them in a trial rather than treating the product category as proof of fit.

When comparing alternatives, use the same five scenarios and the same data-handling questions for each vendor. Count the monthly expense at your expected volume, include the cost of required connectors, and ask whether the plan is per agent, per conversation, or metered by AI usage. Compare how an export or deletion request works. Do not use an advertised 'unlimited' label as a substitute for a written definition of excluded usage, fair-use limits, or vendor-side rate controls. The best option is the one that fits the service process your team can reliably operate.

## The buyer's short checklist

Before signing up, confirm the current plan price and trial end date in the account flow; ask how the product counts an AI conversation; verify whether your required connector is live and included; and identify which AI provider your workspace actually uses. Read the DPA and current subprocessor list, check the default retention schedule, and decide whether the widget should collect location, page URL, or custom SDK fields. Keep a human escalation route for sensitive or uncertain requests and test the data export before moving real conversations into the system.

For a small pilot, one owner should approve the widget script, another person should review transcripts, and someone should own the renewal reminder. Use test records, document any fields sent to Slack or a webhook, and remove the integration when the test is over. If those steps are too much for the team, choose a simpler channel until privacy and support ownership are ready. That is a product-readiness decision, not a judgment about Chatim's honesty.


## Bottom line: a defined offer, with configuration details to verify

Chatim presents a recognizable live-chat and chatbot product with published Free, Starter, Pro, and Ultimate prices, a stated 14-day trial, and product documentation. The plan boundary is important: paid tiers describe unlimited chats and team members, but AI conversation capacity is capped on the listed AI plans. Its privacy policy and DPA provide useful detail, including visitor telemetry, retention, subprocessors, U.S.-based processing, and contractual security claims. Buyers should distinguish Chatim's handling of its own website visitors from Chatim acting as a processor for a customer's chat data.

Based on the primary-source material reviewed, Roseram found no basis to call Chatim a scam. That conclusion is limited: no live account or customer deployment was tested, and vendor-published commitments were not independently audited. If the $9 to $59 monthly range, the current AI allowance, the widget's data collection, and the available integration set fit your needs after verification, a controlled trial is a proportionate next step. If they do not, compare products around the support workflow and privacy requirements you actually have.
