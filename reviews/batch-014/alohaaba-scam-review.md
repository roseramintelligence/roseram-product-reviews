# AlohaABA review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-25  
**Category:** ABA therapy practice management and clinical data software  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

AlohaABA offers practice-management software for applied behavior analysis organizations, with billing, scheduling, payroll, authorization management, client portals, accounts receivable and reporting described on its public pages. The vendor also lists clinical data collection with session documentation, customizable mastery criteria, templates, visualizations and data sync. Pricing is staff-based for practice management, while data collection is priced per client and requires the practice-management subscription. Review the business associate agreement, staff-count billing rules, data handling, and feature availability before migrating protected health information.

## Editorial verdict

AlohaABA is designed for ABA providers that want operational workflows and clinical data collection in a specialized system. It may be relevant when authorization tracking, staff scheduling, claims and session records need to connect, but buyers should validate the workflows with their own roles and documentation requirements. Because the product handles sensitive clinical information, verify security documentation, business associate terms, access controls, retention, exports and incident handling with the vendor before loading live client records.

## Map the clinical and administrative workflows first

AlohaABA’s public material describes an ABA-focused practice workspace that brings operational and clinical tasks together. The pricing page lists billing, scheduling, payroll, authorization management, a client portal, accounts receivable and reports. Its practice-management page also describes staff and client fields, payer and appointment tracking, authorization utilization, teams and custom permissions. The data-collection offering is described separately, with session documentation, mastery criteria, templates, visualizations and real-time sync.

Before a demo, write down how one client moves through intake, authorization, scheduling, session documentation, claim preparation and follow-up. Include who creates each record, who approves a change and how a missing authorization or canceled session is handled. Ask the vendor to show the steps in the product using a test account. Confirm whether each capability is currently available on the quoted plan; the public feature page labels some items as coming soon, so do not infer availability from a general feature list.


## Understand staff and client billing units

The pricing page lists Practice Management at $29.99 per staff member monthly and Data Collection at $12 or $10 per client monthly, with the latter available only alongside Practice Management. The support article explains that billing uses the number of staff profiles active during the prior month and is not prorated when someone is active for only part of the month. An unused but still-active profile may therefore count.

Ask for a quote defining active staff, billable clients, temporary workers, minimums, data-collection tiers and effective dates for changes. Verify whether a 30-day trial has limits, requires a payment method or converts automatically. Model cost using the highest normal staffing and client counts, not only today’s roster. Save the written billing terms and check how suspended or inactive accounts are handled.


## Review privacy, permissions and clinical data handling

ABA practice systems may hold identifiable health information, clinical notes, payer details and staff records. Before importing production data, review the BAA, privacy terms, security documentation, hosting and backup arrangements, role permissions, audit logs, retention, deletion, breach notification and support access. Confirm which systems receive data and how it is returned when the service ends. Ask for a sample export that includes notes, attachments and audit history, then test whether it remains readable without the application.

Set up roles using fictional records first. Check whether clinicians can see only assigned clients, whether billing staff can edit clinical notes, who can download reports and how accounts are disabled when staff leave. Confirm mobile devices, texts and email notifications follow the organization’s policies. A vendor statement that a product supports HIPAA does not establish that a customer’s configuration, contracts or use meet all obligations.


## Pilot documentation and claims before switching systems

Run one representative workflow from scheduling through session documentation and claim submission in a test environment. Check templates against clinical and payer requirements, how corrections are recorded, whether authorizations warn before limits are reached, and how a denial traces back to its source record. Verify treatment of offline sessions, duplicate entries, corrections and synchronization delays.

Test migration with a small permitted sample and compare client, payer, authorization, appointment, note and billing totals against the source system. Decide how history remains accessible and who approves cutover. Ask what training is included and how urgent issues are escalated. This guide summarizes public materials checked on the stated date; it is not hands-on assessment, legal advice, clinical recommendation or security audit.


## Features and decision criteria

- Practice-management capabilities include billing, scheduling, payroll, authorization management, client portal and accounts receivable
- Clinical data collection pages describe real-time collection, customizable mastery criteria, session documentation, templates, visualizations and sync
- Practice-management pricing is listed per active staff member; separate data collection is per client and only available with practice management
- The product site links to privacy terms and a business associate agreement; healthcare buyers should review applicable documents directly

## Pricing considerations

The pricing page displays $29.99 per staff monthly and shows $12 and $10 per client monthly for data collection without clearly exposing which data-collection price condition applies in the captured view. The support article says active staff are counted based on the prior month without prorating. Confirm contract, trial, minimums, taxes, add-ons and exact data-collection pricing in a written quote.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does AlohaABA include?

Its public pages describe ABA practice management, billing, scheduling, payroll, authorization management, client and payer records, reporting, and a separate clinical data-collection offering.

### How does AlohaABA charge for staff?

The support article lists $29.99 per staff member per month and says billing uses total staff profiles active during the prior month without prorating. Confirm current terms directly.

### Does AlohaABA replace a security review for clinical records?

No. Vendor privacy or HIPAA statements do not replace your organization’s review of the agreement, controls, access model, retention and compliance obligations.
