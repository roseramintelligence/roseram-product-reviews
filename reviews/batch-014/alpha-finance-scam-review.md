# Alpha Finance review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-18  
**Category:** Crypto market data and research terminal  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Alpha Finance at alphafinance.io is a free crypto market-data terminal and research publisher operated by Block Media Pty Ltd in Sydney, according to its current About page. The site says it has no paywall, relies on disclosed exchange affiliate links, and offers dozens of dashboards fed by named exchanges and data vendors. A limited Alpha AI beta summarizes a snapshot of selected terminal data. The domain was previously used by Alpha Finance Lab; the current publisher expressly says it is not affiliated with that DeFi project. This review concerns the present data-and-research site, not the older protocol. No trade, account, or paid transaction was tested, and this source review makes no fraud finding.

## Free access, affiliate funding, and what the price means

The current About page says the terminal is free and needs no account. The affiliate disclosure says there is no paywall and that the company is funded by affiliate links to exchange sign-up pages. If a visitor opens an account through one of those links, the exchange may pay Block Media a referral fee or a share of trading fees. The disclosure says this does not add a charge to the visitor and that the exchange’s standard terms apply unless a link says otherwise.

That model means the terminal has no published subscription rate in the pages reviewed, but “free” does not mean trading is free or riskless. An exchange account can have fees, eligibility rules, identity checks, geographic restrictions, and financial risks. The affiliate disclosure says referral arrangements do not affect scores, coverage, or which exchanges supply data; that is the publisher’s stated policy, not an independent audit of editorial decisions. Read the box label and the destination before following any sign-up link, then review the exchange’s own fees and legal terms separately.


## What the terminal brings together

The homepage describes a broad set of crypto dashboards, including prices, liquidations, liquidation heatmaps, funding rates, open interest and options, long/short ratios, whale positions, exchange flows, ETF flows, token unlocks, corporate treasuries, blockchain activity, project revenue, DeFi yields, prediction markets, sentiment indicators, news, and calculators. The publisher says there are 26 live dashboards and that marked live data can lag by up to 60 seconds. Dashboard coverage changes over time, so treat these as page claims checked on November 18, 2026 rather than a permanent inventory.

The value proposition is aggregation: a trader or analyst can look across multiple feed categories without opening every underlying site first. That convenience can shorten a research pass, but it also creates a new question: what does each number include? A funding rate from six named venues is not necessarily the whole market. Exchange flows depend on wallet labels. An ETF flow may be reported after the trading session. A news cluster is not the same as reading the original article. Start with the panel’s coverage and timestamp rather than assuming a single dashboard represents all available data.


## How Alpha Finance says its data is sourced

The methodology page says that live figures come from named exchanges and data providers such as Coinglass, CoinGecko, Polymarket, DefiLlama, Coin Metrics, alternative.me, and exchange public feeds. It says requests are often routed through the publisher’s own proxy, cached for a few minutes, and labeled with the time the figure was read. The page describes displaying reported figures as the provider publishes them, with units, instead of smoothing or changing them.

That is a useful sourcing framework because it gives readers a path to check a number. It does not establish that every source has the same definitions, coverage, update frequency, or quality controls. The methodology itself warns that feeds can be delayed, incomplete, or wrong and says a dashboard may show a saved capture when a live feed does not answer. Check the timestamp and source on the exact dashboard you are using. If a live feed is missing, a last saved value should not be silently treated as current.


## Reported figures, estimates, and calculations are different

Alpha Finance distinguishes between figures reported by a source, estimates supplied by a source, and arithmetic the site performs. Its methodology uses the liquidation heatmap as an example of a model estimate based on open interest and price. It lists calculated indicators such as funding APR, Mayer Multiple, and MVRV Z-Score and says formulas are stated so readers can reproduce them. This distinction matters because a calculation can be transparent while still inheriting assumptions from its inputs.

For instance, multiplying a funding rate across a year assumes the rate will persist, a condition the methodology says will not hold. A past-price indicator summarizes historical data; it is not a forecast. A prediction-market price reflects what participants are paying, not a publisher’s prediction. The product has value when it shows those boundaries beside the figure. A user should still read the method, compare the input period, and resist converting a descriptive metric into a buy, sell, or leverage decision.


## Platform scores and on-chain risk flags

The methodology also describes scores for exchanges and platforms, DeFi pool trust scores, and DEX risk flags. Exchange scores use five categories such as security and solvency, regulation, liquidity, fees, and products. The page says the measures rely on public documents and fixed weights, and that scores are an opinion. A DeFi pool score uses six components, while some facts can cap the result. A DEX flag can reflect thresholds such as low liquidity, a very new pool, or limited trading wallets.

These tools are screening aids, not guarantees that a venue or pool is safe. The Terms page explicitly says a high score does not assure safety and that the absence of a risk flag does not mean there is no risk. A score depends on what the method measures and what the available sources reveal. The listed DEX flags do not inspect token contracts or holder lists, according to the methodology. If an investment depends on a contract audit, legal status, reserve quality, or custody arrangement, verify that evidence directly rather than inferring it from a composite score.


## Alpha AI is a limited, separate beta

Alpha Finance offers Alpha AI as a beta for asking questions about a snapshot of selected terminal data. The page says the model is Anthropic’s Claude and the snapshot can include market tables, liquidations, the Fear & Greed Index, some ETF flows, token unlocks, and trending decentralized-exchange pools. It says the answer can use only that snapshot and available news headlines, should identify missing or stale data, and must not recommend trades, size positions, or predict prices.

The same page warns that answers may be wrong or incomplete, are not reviewed by a person before delivery, and are not research. It describes a small daily question budget, a 600-character question limit, short responses, and a reset at midnight UTC. Those are important limits for a beta. Use it to ask what a cited snapshot contains, then verify important facts in the named feed. Do not use an answer as a substitute for licensed financial advice or ask it to make a decision about your money.


## Privacy for visitors, account holders, and Alpha AI users

The privacy policy says the terminal works without an account and that an optional My Terminal account stores an email address and saved dashboard configuration. It describes server logs and temporary request counters tied to IP addresses, with certain counters deleted within two days. It says the site’s own code does not set tracking cookies or run an analytics service, while naming third parties involved in particular features. Supabase handles data proxies and accounts; Resend delivers sign-in email; some dashboards request data directly from providers; TradingView loads only when selected.

For Alpha AI, the policy says a question and recent conversation turns are sent to Anthropic and are not stored by Alpha Finance. The page says not to include personal, account, or wallet details. The publisher also names Google Fonts, publisher-hosted images, and affiliate-linked exchanges as possible third-party connections. A visitor should check the relevant policy before enabling an optional chart or following an external sign-up link. The privacy page is more detailed than a generic statement, but it is still the operator’s policy and does not replace the separate terms of every named provider.


## Editorial policy, fact checking, and corrections

The editorial policy separates data dashboards from human-written research. It says each research article is checked against primary sources, sources are listed with a date last opened, and corrections are added with dated notes rather than silently removed. It identifies the publisher, editorial standards lead, and fact checker on the About page. It says no exchange or partner sees a research article before publication or can pay for coverage, ranking position, or a score, and it discloses that authors may hold digital assets.

These are stated standards that readers can use to evaluate a publication. The page says its AI writing tools may assist drafting but are not sources, and that figures are checked by a person. That is a policy commitment; the public materials do not independently audit every article. A useful review test is to pick one current article, open each source, check the date and relevant passage, and see whether corrections are visible. The site also provides a report-an-error route. Editorial transparency matters most when readers can inspect the sources and the corrections, not merely read a statement that checking occurs.


## The legal role and limits of the service

The Terms page describes Alpha Finance as general information about digital-asset markets, not financial, legal, or tax advice, and not a recommendation to buy, sell, or hold. It says Block Media Pty Ltd does not hold an Australian financial services licence, does not provide financial product advice or dealing services, holds no client money, and executes no trades. It also warns that digital assets are volatile and that leverage can magnify losses.

The terms say feeds can be delayed, incomplete, or wrong; tools may change or disappear; and the site is provided without a promise of uninterrupted availability. They also state that the publisher does not warrant the accuracy or currency of a figure and ask users to confirm it with its source. Those disclaimers set expectations for an information service, not a broker or managed account. If you need personalized advice, execution, custody, or a contractual service-level commitment, this terminal is not presented as that service.


## Who might use Alpha Finance, and for what?

A reader who wants a free, no-account overview of market data may find the terminal useful as an index into named feeds. A trader may use the dashboards to see what several exchanges and data providers report, while an analyst may use the source and method notes to locate a relevant figure. The optional saved dashboard may help a returning user keep a preferred panel layout. Those use cases depend on treating the interface as a starting point for verification rather than a final authority.

The product is a weaker fit for anyone seeking personalized recommendations, a guarantee that all market data is complete, an execution venue, custody, or a human-checked answer from Alpha AI. Users who are uncomfortable with external data providers, affiliate links, optional third-party charts, or crypto-market risk should take time to inspect the privacy and affiliate disclosures. The site is aimed at adults and is not a place to seek financial advice. Set a clear purpose before opening a dashboard, and stop if you cannot distinguish its timestamp, feed, and calculation.


## A practical evaluation checklist

Choose one dashboard and trace one figure from the card to its named feed. Check the timestamp, unit, exchange or provider coverage, and whether the value is reported, estimated, or calculated. Repeat with a figure that looks unusual. If the feed fails, note whether the page marks the value as stale or missing. For a score or risk flag, read the underlying formula and identify facts it does not inspect. This small exercise tells you more about the product’s transparency than a tour of every panel.

Then review the account and privacy settings. Use the terminal without signing in first if a saved layout is not needed. If you sign in, understand that the optional account stores the configuration and email. If you try Alpha AI, use a non-sensitive question and compare its answer to the source snapshot. Before leaving for an exchange, identify whether the link is affiliate-marked and read that exchange’s own eligibility, fees, and risks. ## Alternatives and comparison criteria

Compare Alpha Finance with the original exchanges, data providers, and research publications that cover the same metric. An exchange’s own feed may be more direct for its prices and funding rates, while a multi-source terminal can make cross-venue comparison quicker. A specialist analytics provider may publish deeper definitions or longer history, and a research publication may provide context that a chart cannot. Those are categories to compare, not claims that any named competitor is better or worse.

Use the same asset, market, time window, and unit for each comparison. Note whether one service includes a venue the other omits, whether an estimated liquidation map is being compared with reported trades, and whether timestamps reflect the same time zone. Compare not only features but also the source licences, update delays, account requirements, price, export options, and data-use policies. Alpha Finance’s current materials describe free use; that makes it easy to inspect, but it does not make its results interchangeable with every underlying feed.


## Final verdict: a transparent starting point, not a trading signal

Alpha Finance’s current product is a free crypto-data and research terminal with a clearly stated publisher, a published methodology, source labels, a privacy policy, terms, and an affiliate disclosure. Its strongest buying point is the attempt to put many source feeds in one place and distinguish reported figures from estimates and calculations. The explicit statement that it is separate from the older Alpha Finance Lab is especially important for a domain whose prior identity can surface in older references.

The meaningful limits are just as clear: third-party data can be stale or wrong, scores are the publisher’s opinion, a risk flag cannot inspect everything that matters, and Alpha AI is a small beta rather than reviewed research. Affiliate links support the free service, and users still need to assess each destination independently. This review found no basis in the reviewed first-party material to call the current service a scam; it also does not certify the product, its company, or the market data. Use it to find and compare information, verify consequential figures at their source, and keep investment decisions separate from a dashboard or generated answer.
