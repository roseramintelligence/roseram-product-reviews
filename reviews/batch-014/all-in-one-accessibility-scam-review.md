# All in One Accessibility review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-25  
**Category:** Website accessibility widget and automated evaluation tools  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

All in One Accessibility is a website widget and dashboard from Skynet Technologies. The vendor lists accessibility profiles, interface controls, language support, automated scanning and optional services such as manual audits, remediation and document accessibility work. Its pricing varies by monthly page-view limits and whether a license covers one site or multiple sites. A widget or automated scan can support an accessibility program, but neither by itself proves that a site conforms to WCAG or satisfies a legal obligation; W3C says human judgment is required for aspects tools cannot evaluate automatically.

## Editorial verdict

All in One Accessibility may help teams add visitor-facing controls and organize scans or accessibility work across websites. It should be evaluated as one component of an accessibility process, not purchased as a one-click compliance guarantee. Review the exact features, page-view ceiling, privacy and analytics behavior, add-on costs, site count and support commitments. Separately test the underlying website with keyboard use, assistive technology and people with disabilities, then fix barriers in the site itself.

## Identify whether you need a visitor widget, scanning or remediation

Skynet Technologies describes All in One Accessibility as a visitor-facing website widget with interface controls, profiles and language support, accompanied by a dashboard and automated scanning. Its product page also lists services such as manual accessibility audits, remediation, VPAT or ACR reports, PDF remediation and translation as paid add-ons. These are related but distinct jobs: a toolbar changes some presentation controls, a scanner flags certain conditions, and remediation changes the underlying content or code.

List the barriers your visitors are reporting before comparing plans. If users cannot complete a checkout, navigate a menu by keyboard or understand an image without sight, determine which part of the site causes the barrier and what must change in the site itself. Ask the vendor for a demonstration against those exact flows, including pages behind sign-in and dynamic content. Confirm which products are included in the quoted subscription and which require a separate engagement.


## Treat automated scans and compliance language with care

The vendor markets the widget and scan in relation to accessibility standards and laws. That language should not be read as a legal certification. W3C’s guidance says evaluation tools can identify potential issues but cannot check all accessibility aspects automatically; human judgment is required, and no tool alone can determine whether a site is accessible. A score or scan report is evidence of a tool’s output, not proof that every user can complete every task.

Use automation as one part of an ongoing evaluation: scan representative templates, inspect the underlying code, test keyboard focus and screen-reader output, and involve people with disabilities in evaluating real tasks. Correct the source issue where possible, then rerun the test. Keep records of the pages, methods, standard version and human review performed. Consult qualified accessibility counsel for legal interpretation rather than treating a vendor widget as a substitute for it.


## Compare page-view limits, site count and add-on costs

When checked, the official product page listed Small Site at $25 per month for up to 50,000 page views, Medium at $39 for 100,000, Large at $99 for 500,000 and Extra Large at $139 for 1,000,000. The same page presented yearly savings and separate multisite tiers. Check the exact site count, monthly page-view definition, analytics source, overage behavior and billing term before choosing a level. If the website has seasonal traffic or several subdomains, estimate a normal month and a peak month.

Ask for a line-item quote for manual audit, remediation, document accessibility, white labeling, and any additional websites. The vendor’s page describes some of these as add-ons; a base widget price should not be assumed to include them. Compare the annual commitment and renewal amount with a smaller pilot. Make sure the team can export reports and remove the script cleanly if the product is discontinued or does not meet its requirements.


## Test privacy, assistive technology behavior and user feedback

Before adding a third-party script, review its privacy statement, data flows, subprocessors, regional hosting statements, cookies, analytics events and retention. The product page makes privacy and certification claims; request current supporting documents and confirm which specific service and data-processing configuration they apply to. Test whether the widget sends page URLs or user choices to the provider and how analytics integrations are configured. Do not place it on sensitive account or health pages without an appropriate review.

Test on the actual site with keyboard-only navigation, screen readers, zoom and mobile devices. Check menus, forms, dialogs, video captions, validation errors and third-party checkout. Ask users with disabilities whether the widget helps or interferes with their chosen assistive technology. Provide a visible way to send feedback and route reports to someone who can fix the underlying interface. This buyer guide summarizes public information and W3C evaluation guidance; it is not a legal opinion, independent audit or certification.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the All in One Accessibility widget make a site WCAG compliant?

A widget or automated scan alone cannot establish conformance. W3C says accessibility evaluation tools cannot check every aspect automatically and that human judgment is required. Evaluate the site itself against the applicable standard and legal requirements.

### How is All in One Accessibility priced?

Its public page lists single-site monthly plans based on page-view limits and separate multisite options. The page showed Small Site at $25 per month for up to 50,000 page views and higher tiers at $39, $99 and $139 per month with larger limits when checked.

### What should I ask before installing an accessibility overlay?

Confirm which barriers it can address, which code changes remain your responsibility, how it affects keyboard and screen-reader behavior, how page-view counts are measured, what data it collects, and whether audits or remediation are separate paid services.
