# Capsule CRM review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-24  
**Category:** Small business CRM  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Capsule is a cloud CRM built around contacts, sales pipelines, tasks, email history, projects and integrations. It offers a limited free tier and paid plans with higher contact limits, reporting, automation, AI features and team controls.

## Editorial verdict

Capsule is a reasonable shortlist choice for a small team that wants a focused relationship manager with a relatively approachable free entry point. The free plan is constrained by its contact and user caps, while paid pricing rises with automation, reporting and team needs. Before migrating, test the email sync, permission model, data export and any feature the team relies on daily.

## Match the contact limit to the real business

Capsule’s free offer is bounded: the pricing page lists up to two users, 250 contacts, five custom fields and one sales pipeline. That can be adequate for a small operator who is organizing an early set of relationships. It becomes restrictive once a business imports a sizable address book or needs multiple pipelines and richer account structure.

Estimate active contacts, archived records, duplicate cleanup and expected growth before importing. A CRM that looks free can become a paid purchase once the contact cap is exceeded, so compare the first paid tier against the tools the business already uses for email, tasks and reporting.


## Evaluate email and activity history carefully

Capsule advertises connections with Gmail and Outlook, email templates and synchronization features across its paid tiers. The official plan page differentiates ordinary email connection from advanced mail-server options. Check which messages are logged, whether the sync is one-way or two-way for the selected plan and which users can view communication history.

Use a small test group and a limited set of contacts to validate the sync behavior. Confirm that private messages, shared mailboxes and aliases are handled according to company policy. CRM email history can be useful for continuity, but broad access to communications should be designed intentionally.


## Review permissions, exports and integration dependencies

Capsule’s setup material describes access restrictions for teams that need different departments to see different information. Map those roles to the actual business before inviting everyone. Test what a user with limited permissions can view, edit, export and delete, then decide whether the plan’s controls meet the organization’s requirements.

A CRM can become a central system of record, so test export formats and integration behavior before committing. Verify how contacts, notes, activities, attachments and custom fields transfer to another system. Confirm API or integration availability for the exact tier and document how to disconnect third-party mail access if a user leaves.


## Use the free trial to test specific workflows

Capsule’s plan page advertises a 14-day trial without a card and says trial users can access features and integrations. Use that period to create a realistic pipeline, import sample contacts, test reporting, connect a mailbox and invite one teammate. A trial that is not tied to a defined workflow may end without answering whether the CRM is a fit.

Set acceptance questions in advance: Can users find the right record quickly? Are follow-ups visible? Can managers see only appropriate data? Does a standard export contain what the team expects? This review is based on public product materials and does not claim hands-on testing or guarantee sales results.


## Features and decision criteria

- Contacts, sales pipelines, tasks and activity history
- Free plan for up to two users and 250 contacts
- Paid tiers increase contact limits and add reporting, email sync, automation and AI features
- Permission settings and integrations vary by plan

## Pricing considerations

The vendor offers a 14-day trial without a card according to its plan page. Confirm exact feature allocation, contact caps, taxes and renewal prices before choosing a tier; the plan page may update independently.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a two-person business start without paying?

The official pricing page lists a Free plan for up to two users and 250 contacts with one pipeline.

### Does the trial require a credit card?

The pricing page says paid plans have a 14-day free trial with no card required.

### Will a free account retain its data if the trial ends?

The vendor says users can remain on a selected plan, switch or cancel, and should add a payment card to keep data already in the account; verify the current policy before relying on it.
