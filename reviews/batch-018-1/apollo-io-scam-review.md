# Apollo.io review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-18  
**Category:** B2B sales intelligence, prospecting and engagement platform  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Apollo.io combines a B2B contact and company database with prospecting, email sequences, CRM integrations, enrichment and AI-assisted sales tools. Its current pricing page lists a free plan, paid per-seat plans and a custom option; it describes Basic at US$49 per seat monthly when billed annually and Professional at US$79 under the same billing presentation, while credit allowances and product features vary by plan and may change. Apollo’s credits are used for data access and certain actions; unused credits do not roll over, and add-on credits can recur each billing cycle until removed. Apollo’s own privacy materials say the database uses public sources, third-party providers and information contributed through customer use, with a Privacy Center for access and removal requests. The company says removed records are suppressed to reduce reappearance. Standard plans are for internal business use; the pricing page says reselling or using Apollo data in external products requires a separate agreement. The evidence reviewed supports a real sales platform, not a substantiated scam allegation, while data accuracy, lawful outreach and opt-out handling remain buyer responsibilities.

## Editorial verdict

Apollo offers a broad prospecting and engagement workflow with disclosed seat plans, a credit system, API documentation, privacy notices and self-serve privacy controls. The main buyer risks are variable data quality, unused credits expiring, recurring credit add-ons, annual commitments, personal-data compliance and the prohibition on reselling database data under standard plans. Test the target segment, source and verification date of records, credit charges, opt-out workflow, cancellation timing and export route before scaling. Nothing reviewed substantiates calling Apollo a scam. This is a public-source desk review; it does not verify the accuracy of any individual contact or run an outbound campaign.

## Apollo.io SCAM review: database, sales workflow and data rights

Apollo.io combines B2B company and contact discovery with sales engagement. Its public pricing and help pages describe prospecting filters, email sequences, enrichment, CRM connections, calling and AI-assisted tools. The product is designed to help a sales team move from a target account list to researched prospects and follow-up activity. That is a substantial workflow, so a useful evaluation must examine not only the interface but also data provenance, credits, mailbox setup and the rules governing contact use.

This review uses “SCAM Review” as the catalog’s label; it does not assert that Apollo has committed fraud. Apollo publishes pricing, API documentation, privacy explanations and a data-subject Privacy Center. Those primary materials support the existence of an operating service. They do not independently verify the current accuracy of each contact record, the performance of every campaign or the legality of a buyer’s particular outreach. The most consequential questions are whether the records match the target market, whether the plan’s credits fit expected activity and whether the company can honor privacy and opt-out obligations.


## Apollo’s core workflow: identify, enrich and engage

The platform’s public pages describe a path from filtering companies and people to enriching records, building sequences and managing deals or CRM updates. Its pricing tiers distinguish free exploration from individual sales usage and larger-team capabilities. For a sales manager, the benefit is having prospect research and outreach steps in one system. For an operator, the cost is learning how data, credits, seats, mailboxes and integrations interact.

Map a realistic workflow before buying. Choose one customer segment, decide which fields are truly necessary, identify the legal basis and notice required for outreach, then test a small set of records. Verify company, title, region and email status using an independent business source where appropriate. Do not assume that a filter result is a qualified lead or that an available email address is permission to send marketing. The tool can organize a process; it cannot replace targeting judgment or compliance review.


## Plan prices are per seat and billing terms change the comparison

Apollo’s pricing page lists Free, Basic, Professional and Custom tiers. The page currently shows Basic at US$49 per seat per month when billed annually and Professional at US$79 per seat per month under the same annual-billing view. It also describes credit bundles for each tier. These numbers are a snapshot, not a permanent quote; introductory pricing, regions, add-ons, seats and billing choices can change the amount due.

Compare the complete annual commitment, not only the monthly equivalent. Calculate the number of seats, credits required, email accounts, domains or mailboxes, and any extra add-on credits. The pricing page says all seats in an account are on the same plan, which can affect a mixed-use team. Before placing a paid order, inspect the review screen and save the plan, renewal date, credit allocation and cancellation method. Avoid relying on a third-party directory’s older price when the official checkout is available.


## Credits are a central cost control and can expire unused

Apollo explains that credits may be used for business email, phone numbers, enrichment, AI research and other actions depending on the plan and endpoint. The About Credits page says credits refresh at the start of a billing cycle and do not roll over. Annual plans may grant credits upfront or on a schedule depending on the selected billing arrangement. Add-on credits can be charged immediately and recur each cycle until reduced or removed.

Model actual activity before scaling: searches that return no qualifying data may be charged differently from matched enrichment, and API endpoints vary in credit usage. Apollo’s API documentation advises testing a small request and checking the account’s usage before running a large job. Set per-user caps if the plan supports them, monitor the credit dashboard and remove unused recurring add-ons before renewal. “Unlimited” features may still be governed by fair-use rules; it does not imply unlimited contact data or unlimited credits.


## Credit accounting can vary between product surface and API endpoint

The API pricing documentation says endpoint usage depends on the request and the data returned; some endpoints are free while enrichment and insight actions may consume credits. It gives examples where people-enrichment charges depend on returned fields and match confidence. This is more precise than a generic “one credit per lead” assumption, but it requires the buyer to understand the endpoint and plan-specific rate before launching an integration.

Create a small test with representative inputs, inspect both the API response and the billing dashboard, and compare the actual charge with your expected result. Include pagination, retries and repeated searches in the estimate because the documentation warns that a large workflow can accumulate charges across requests. Do not build a monthly budget from a single successful call. If the account uses a legacy plan, Apollo says the applicable credit rules can differ; check the in-product billing panel.


## Data sourcing and the contributor network

Apollo’s Privacy Policy says it obtains business contact information from public sources, third-party providers and customer contributions. It says customers may provide contacts and engagement details through integrations or uploads, and that submitted information can be used to grow, enrich and verify its Contributor Database. Apollo’s separate contributor-network article describes sharing elements associated with use of features such as connected mailboxes, calendars, CRM data or uploaded contact files, subject to plan and agreement.

This is material for organizations that connect a mailbox or upload a prospect list. Read the current privacy notice, data-sharing explanation, customer terms and any enterprise-specific settings before enabling integrations. Confirm what is contributed, whether the organization can opt out or negotiate different terms, and what happens to previously contributed data after a connection is removed. Avoid uploading consumer data, sensitive information or client records unless your contract, notice and lawful basis permit it. This review does not independently inspect Apollo’s internal collection systems.


## Privacy rights and removal controls are visible, but require follow-through

Apollo publishes a Privacy Center where individuals can request access or removal from its services. Its privacy policy says that, after an opt-out or deletion request, it may keep minimum information on a suppression list to prevent the record from being added again. Apollo’s Data Overview also describes refreshing data against removal requests and says a removed contact should no longer appear in prospecting results. Those are the company’s own descriptions of its process.

A business customer still needs its own way to honor objections and suppress contacts in campaigns, CRM systems and downstream exports. A removal request in Apollo does not automatically prove that every customer copy is erased from every connected system. Document how your team receives objections, propagates them to tools and prevents re-import. If a person asks why their details are present, be ready to identify the source and the request route. Test suppression behavior before using Apollo at scale.


## Email and calendar integrations are powerful data connections

Apollo’s Privacy Policy explains that, when authorized features require it, the service may access email or calendar accounts and may process headers, subject lines, message content, attachments, meeting details and related activity. The precise collection depends on the enabled service. The policy also describes session-replay and heatmap tools on Apollo’s own site and says it configures them to mask sensitive form inputs. That disclosure helps define the categories of information at stake, but it should be reviewed in context with the particular integrations a buyer plans to enable.

Connect the narrowest mailbox and calendar scope that supports the workflow, review OAuth permissions, limit access to trained users, and establish a process to revoke unused integrations. Ask whether email content is accessed for the features you intend to use and how long activity logs persist. For regulated or confidential communications, obtain security and contractual review before connecting. Do not assume that a list of privacy controls means every integration is suitable for every organization.


## Outreach compliance remains the customer’s responsibility

Apollo provides prospect data and outreach features, but the customer selects recipients, content, jurisdiction, frequency and purpose. A business email address is still personal data in many contexts, and email, telemarketing, privacy and platform rules vary by location. The company’s own data overview and privacy materials explain its practices; they do not certify that every customer campaign meets every applicable law.

Use verified business context, identify the sender honestly, include required unsubscribe and contact information, and maintain suppression lists. Respect opt-outs promptly across sequences and connected systems. Check local rules before calling or texting, and do not treat a “verified” field as consent. If using purchased or enriched information, document the lawful basis and notice obligations that apply to your organization. This is operational due diligence, not legal advice.


## Cancellation, downgrades and annual credits

Apollo’s plan-management help says customers can cancel from the Plan Overview, with cancellation scheduled for the end of the current term; access and available credits remain during the pending period. It also says a downgrade can take effect immediately for access while the lower price begins next term, and no refund is issued for unused time or the price difference. Annual billing is a 12-month commitment, and annual-to-monthly changes require the annual plan to expire.

Add-on credits are recurring until removed, and the plan page says unused credits expire rather than rolling over. Put the renewal and cancellation date on a calendar, remove add-ons before the cycle cutoff if they are no longer needed, and export records in a usable format before access changes. Capture the cancellation confirmation and verify the next invoice. These rules are available in Apollo’s own plan guidance and are a reason to size the purchase cautiously, not evidence of improper billing.


## Standard plans restrict use of Apollo data in external products

Apollo’s pricing page states that standard plans are for internal business use and do not permit using Apollo data to power an external product, sharing it with customers or reselling it. Such use requires a separate agreement and custom pricing. This is an important product boundary for agencies, data vendors and developers building prospecting tools for clients.

If your intended workflow involves a customer-facing dashboard, enrichment API, resale or data transfer to a client, obtain written permission and the correct contract before implementation. A normal subscription and API key do not necessarily grant redistribution rights. Document which records can be exported, who may see them and how downstream copies are controlled. Do not infer broad data rights from the ability to click “export” or call an endpoint.


## Who should evaluate Apollo and who may prefer another approach

Apollo may suit B2B sales teams that need prospect search, email sequencing and CRM integrations and that have a clear process for verifying, governing and suppressing contact records. The free plan can help an individual inspect the interface and workflow, while larger teams may value shared credit caps and administration. It may be a poor fit if the team does not have a compliant outbound program, needs highly specialized regional records, or expects unlimited enrichment for a flat subscription.

A small business with an existing CRM may compare Apollo with the CRM’s own data tools, a specialist sales-intelligence vendor, manually researched account lists or inbound marketing. A team with strict data residency or retention requirements may prefer a source it can audit more directly. The right alternative depends on coverage, freshness, permission model, integration and total cost—not on a vendor’s headline database size.


## A careful pilot before committing to a large list

Select a narrow target segment and a small sample. Check organization, role, geography, email status and source recency against information the team can independently confirm. Run the exact enrichment and sequence steps you expect to use, track every credit charge and compare returned records with the account’s CRM. Test an unsubscribe, an objection and a removal request through the process your team will use in production.

Set a success threshold before the pilot: for example, verified contact coverage, acceptable correction rate, actual cost per usable record, and time saved after deduplication. Include mailbox configuration and deliverability monitoring. If results fail the threshold, adjust the segment or compare another vendor; do not simply buy more credits. This is a recommended buyer test. Roseram did not create an Apollo account, purchase data or send a campaign.


## Apollo.io SCAM review verdict: real sales platform, scrutinize data and credits

Apollo’s official pages describe B2B prospecting, contact enrichment, sequencing, CRM integrations, a credit-based pricing model and a self-serve privacy process. They disclose that data can come from public and third-party sources as well as customer contributions; they explain removal and suppression; and they prohibit external resale under standard plans. Pricing and credit rules are detailed but variable by plan, usage, seat and billing cycle.

The evidence reviewed does not substantiate calling Apollo a scam. It does not independently validate a specific record or promise outreach performance. Before subscribing, test data quality for your exact market, audit integration permissions, estimate credits, read data-sharing and privacy terms, confirm annual renewal and remove unused add-ons.
