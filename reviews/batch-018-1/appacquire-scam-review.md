## AppAcquire SCAM Review: separate marketplace verification from acquisition approval

AppAcquire describes a marketplace dedicated to buying and selling iOS and Android applications. The captured pages emphasize connected revenue or usage information, seller-controlled contact requirements and transactions funded through Escrow.com. Those functions address discovery, identity and transfer coordination. They do not establish that an application is profitable after all costs, that its code is maintainable or that a buyer should acquire it. The service needs to be assessed as a marketplace workflow rather than a guarantee about every listed asset.

## Listing totals and asking prices are observations, not transaction results

The captured homepage shows a live-listing count, example asking prices, monthly revenue figures and download information. It also displays a sold section. These are snapshots of publisher-presented inventory, not a current census checked for this review. An asking price is the seller's proposal, whereas a completed price, the assets actually transferred and the buyer's later operating result are different facts. A sold badge alone does not reveal every term or establish that another listing has comparable economics.

Use the listing to decide what evidence needs clarification rather than treating a headline number as a completed assessment. Distinguish owner-reported download detail from a public store range when the listing labels them differently. Preserve the date and scope of every figure used in a comparison. This review has not recalculated a valuation or verified a sale. It avoids reproducing individual deals as success examples, because the captured presentation is not independent proof of the buyer's experience or the investment outcome.


## A Verified listing has a stated scope

The homepage explains that a Verified listing involves a signed-in seller, proof of ownership, a public store link and a connected source. It names several possible sources, including store and analytics systems and public verified-revenue pages. Those connections can provide more useful provenance than an unsupported number typed into a form. They still answer different questions. A usage connection may support an active-user figure without establishing revenue, while a revenue source does not independently verify every operating expense or intellectual-property right.

Read the provenance attached to each metric and identify the period it covers. Ask which information remains seller-reported and which was obtained from a connected source. Confirm whether a number represents gross revenue, proceeds, recurring revenue or another measure before comparing it with another listing. The review has not inspected a connection or audited a financial statement. A verification badge should therefore be read according to its stated evidence scope, rather than expanded into a blanket endorsement of the entire asset.


## Integration launch status needs reconciliation across pages

The captured homepage includes Google Play Console among sources discussed for verification, while the documentation index labels that integration coming soon. The about page similarly describes it as next. The docs also label AdMob verification coming soon. This inconsistency is actionable: a seller relying on a specific Android or advertising-earnings connection needs to confirm the actual available route. It does not establish deception, and it does not justify presenting an announced capability as independently demonstrated.

Before beginning a listing, verify which connection is currently supported for the exact asset and metric. Keep a record of any alternative evidence accepted by the marketplace, and distinguish that alternative from the originally advertised integration. If a future connection is central to the decision, obtain a demonstrated release rather than treating an announcement as an implementation. No connection was exercised here. The review preserves the conflict instead of resolving it through an assumption about which captured page is more recent.


## Read-only connections still need a data-access review

AppAcquire's documentation index says connections are read-only and describes different methods for different providers. It mentions an App Store Connect key, a restricted Stripe key used once, public RevenueCat or TrustMRR pages and read-only Google sign-in for Firebase Analytics. These are not identical access mechanisms. A public page link and an account credential have different handling requirements, while read-only access still permits information to be read. The captured descriptions should not be combined into one assumed permission model.

Confirm the actual scopes and data retention for the selected connection before authorization. Follow the provider's current revocation process and determine what remains visible on a listing after a connection is removed. This review did not inspect the more detailed retention page linked by the index, so it does not invent a comprehensive deletion policy. No credential was requested or used. The meaningful safeguard to evaluate is the documented and demonstrated behavior of the specific integration, not the word read-only in isolation.


## Buyer identity checks differ from financial capacity

The verified-buyer guide describes an optional identity process involving a government document and selfie through Didit. It says sellers see a badge rather than the documents or legal name, while AppAcquire retains a result, issuing country and reference. Those are publisher statements about an identity workflow, not an independently audited privacy control. An identity check also does not establish that a buyer can fund a purchase, understands the asset or will complete an agreed transfer.

The same guide distinguishes any signed-in buyer, buyers with a profile and ID-verified buyers as seller-selectable contact groups. A profile's budget and timeline provide context, but they are not the same evidence as secured funds. Escrow.com is described as performing its own identity check later in the payment process. No identity document was submitted for this review. Treat each stage according to what it verifies and avoid describing a badge as proof of solvency, seriousness or future performance.


## Contact gates and private listings address different disclosure stages

The seller's contact setting applies to both messages and offers because an offer opens a conversation, according to the verified-buyer guide. Separately, the publisher describes private listings that hide the application's name until a buyer signs an NDA and the seller approves access. These controls address different parts of a disclosure workflow. Restricting who can send a message is not necessarily the same as deciding who can see every sensitive metric or asset document.

Map the material to be disclosed at each stage: public description, initial questions, confidential financial information and eventual technical access. Confirm what the actual private-listing interface exposes before uploading restricted material. An NDA can define obligations, but the existence of a signing step does not establish technical prevention of copying or a particular legal remedy. This review has not created a private listing or inspected a buyer's view. Its scope is to identify the boundaries that a demonstrated workflow should resolve.


## The asset agreement needs a specific inventory

The closing guide describes an asset purchase agreement listing apps, source code, domains, accounts and seller-confirmed figures. It also says changing a document after someone signs clears signatures so both sides sign the final version. That is a concrete version-control behavior to understand. A completed form cannot ensure the inventory is complete if the parties have omitted a backend dependency, licensed component or essential operating account. Agreement preparation and technical discovery remain connected but different tasks.

Record the assets and access paths that the application actually needs, including the services that continue after a store transfer. Identify material that cannot simply be handed over under its existing terms and obtain appropriate review. The publisher states it is not a party to the agreement and does not provide legal advice. This review has not assessed a contract or transfer eligibility. It does not present a template or electronic signature as a substitute for a scoped review of the actual transaction.


## Secured payment is a distinct milestone from payment initiation

The documented sequence starts with an offer and agreements, then an Escrow.com transaction, identity checks and buyer payment. It says the seller also has to agree and choose a payout method before the buyer's payment can be approved. That distinction matters to coordination: a buyer saying payment was sent is not necessarily the same as the escrow service confirming secured funds. The guide places the transfer after confirmation that the funds are secured.

Verify transaction status through the actual authorized service rather than a message or forwarded screenshot. Keep the transaction identity and participants consistent with the accepted agreement. The publisher tells users not to pay or accept payment outside Escrow.com; this review reports that instruction without independently certifying the integration or partner relationship. No payment was initiated or inspected. It also does not infer that an escrow workflow removes every dispute or ensures that a transferred asset matches all of the buyer's expectations.


## An inspection deadline can affect release without an explicit acceptance

The closing documentation says the buyer has the inspection period defined in the agreement, and that either acceptance or allowing the period to end releases money to the seller. That is more specific than a general headline saying the seller is paid after the buyer accepts the app. A buyer evaluating the workflow should understand how time affects the transaction, including what evidence or action is required if a transfer is incomplete or a material issue remains unresolved.

Plan inspection around the asset inventory and the agreed deliverables rather than leaving it until the last day. Establish who checks code, deployment, access and metrics, and how an issue is communicated through the transaction's actual procedure. This is a workflow evaluation point, not advice on a particular dispute or interpretation of enforceable terms. The review has not tested the inspection interface, deadlines or remedies. It preserves the documented expiry behavior instead of simplifying escrow into an unconditional protection claim.


## Marketplace charges and escrow charges are separate line items

The saved closing guide describes an AppAcquire fee of 1.5 percent from each side, with the buyer paying on top and the seller paying out of proceeds. It separately describes Escrow.com's own charge as split between the parties, with minimums and an exact amount shown in the transaction. These are captured terms, not a quote for a proposed purchase. The homepage also mentions optional paid promotion, so a no-listing-fee statement should not be extended to mean every visibility option is free.

Obtain the actual itemized charges before agreeing to a transaction and distinguish the asset price from each service fee. Confirm the terms for a transaction that does not complete rather than inferring every third-party cost from the marketplace's short summary. The guide describes a single lump-sum US-dollar payment and says instalments, earn-outs and seller financing are unsupported. No fee calculation, invoice or refund was tested here. The review reports workflow boundaries without recommending a financing structure or estimating a return.


## Store transfer does not complete the operating handover

The publisher correctly distinguishes a mobile application from a domain-only sale in its marketplace description: apps live on store accounts, while code, backend services and other assets may require separate handover. The captured pages say the buyer needs developer accounts able to receive the apps. Those statements do not establish that every individual application or connected service qualifies for the intended transfer. A listing that combines iOS and Android versions also needs clarity about what each transfer includes.

Use an explicit handover checklist grounded in the actual architecture and the current provider requirements. Verify that the recipient can operate the delivered application and that obsolete access is addressed through the agreed process. Treat source-code delivery, credential replacement and store ownership as separate completion evidence. No app was transferred for this review, and it does not certify preservation of reviews, rankings, users or revenue for a particular transaction. Publisher generalizations need confirmation against the specific app and receiving accounts.


## Portfolio totals can hide different dependency and revenue patterns

The about page describes cross-platform listings and portfolios of up to ten applications, with individual figures and combined revenue. Grouping can simplify discovery, but an aggregate should not erase differences between assets. One app may depend on subscriptions, another on advertising and another on a service shared across the portfolio. A combined monthly figure does not by itself reveal whether assets can be separated, which costs remain shared or what happens if one store transfer is delayed.

Request an asset-by-asset record of the information material to the proposed deal, and distinguish portfolio totals from the evidence supporting each component. This review does not estimate revenue quality or acquisition value. Its point is documentary clarity: the package being described should match the package being agreed and inspected. No portfolio listing was analyzed beyond saved publisher material. The advertised grouping feature is not presented as proof that a bundled acquisition has lower risk, greater diversification or a particular operating advantage.


## Testimonials and brokerage promises need their own evidence

The homepage includes buyer and seller quotations about discovery, relevant inquiries and the listing process. It also advertises free brokerage assistance and round-the-clock support. These are publisher-selected statements and service promises, not this review's measured experience. Repeated appearances of the same quotations in the captured page are not additional independent customers. No contributor was authenticated, transaction verified or support response timed, and no testimonial is invented to fill that evidence gap.

Ask what brokerage assistance covers in the proposed transaction and what remains the parties' responsibility. Keep support availability distinct from a guaranteed resolution time or a completed due-diligence service. If customer references are important, request appropriately scoped evidence rather than assuming a short quotation establishes the outcome for a different asset. This review did not contact staff, users or sellers. The distinction protects the usefulness of the analysis: marketing testimony remains visible as attributed material without being converted into an independent endorsement.
