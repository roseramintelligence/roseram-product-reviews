# Kagi review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-24  
**Category:** Subscription web search  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Kagi is a subscription search service that describes itself as user-funded and advertising-free. The practical question is whether its results and search experience improve your own recurring research enough to justify a separate subscription.

## Editorial verdict

Kagi is most useful to evaluate with searches you already perform, including the difficult ones. Its free trial can support that comparison. Paying for search does not remove the need to inspect a source, and a cleaner results page does not establish that every answer is correct.

## A search subscription competes with a habit

Most people do not choose a search engine from a feature table every morning. They type into the browser and continue with their day. Kagi therefore needs to earn its place in a routine. A technically interesting feature has little personal value if you never use it or keep returning to the old search engine for the actual work.

Save a short list of searches from a normal week: a product manual, an unfamiliar error, a local service and a question requiring a primary source. Compare how much correction and repeated searching each service needs. Keep the test tied to your activities instead of relying on a demonstration query selected by someone else.


## Less advertising is a preference, not a source-quality guarantee

Kagi’s funding model may appeal to a person who wants a different relationship with search. That preference can be a legitimate reason to pay, but it should be considered separately from the quality of any particular result. An attractive search experience can still lead to an outdated page or an article that does not support its headline.

Open the source when a claim matters. Check whether the page actually answers your question, explains its evidence and applies to the current version or location. This review did not independently audit Kagi’s privacy practices or benchmark its index against competing search engines.


## Match the plan to a measurable routine

A limited search allowance can be sufficient for occasional research while feeling restrictive to someone who searches throughout the working day. Estimate your actual pattern before selecting a tier. Avoid treating the highest plan as the default merely because it includes more product names.

Keep search and AI assistance distinct in the comparison. A subscription label describing unlimited searches does not settle the allowances for another feature. If you need programmatic access, inspect the API arrangement separately rather than assuming a browser subscription supplies an application integration.


## Use the trial to decide what you would miss

At the end of a trial, identify the specific improvement you would miss if you stopped using the service. Perhaps the result organization suits your research, or perhaps the change did not materially improve your day. Both outcomes are more informative than a vague feeling that a paid tool ought to be better.

A sensible decision includes the recurring cost, the devices where you search and the effort required to maintain a changed routine. This is an editorial evaluation framework based on public documentation, without a claimed Roseram hands-on comparison or guaranteed productivity saving.


## Features and decision criteria

- User-funded web search
- Provider-described advertising-free experience
- Trial with a defined search allowance
- Separate subscription tiers and API pricing navigation

## Pricing considerations

Monthly individual prices shown on the checked page. Search allowances and Assistant limits are different; review current fair-use rules and checkout before purchasing.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Kagi free forever?

The checked pricing page offers a free trial with 100 searches. Continued use beyond the trial requires an applicable paid arrangement.

### Does unlimited search mean unlimited use of every AI feature?

No. The pricing page describes separate Assistant allowances and a fair-use policy. Compare the feature you intend to use rather than assuming all limits are identical.

### How should I compare Kagi with free search?

Use the same set of everyday and difficult queries, then record useful sources found, repeated searching and time spent checking results.
