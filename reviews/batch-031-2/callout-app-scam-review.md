## Core Function and the Engineering Workflow

Callout.app is structured around an automated screening workflow designed to examine digital construction drawings before they reach the desk of a certifying engineer. The user begins by dragging and dropping a PDF drawing set into the browser interface, selecting the applicable governing codes across relevant disciplines, and designating any regional amendments. The operator then picks a review depth setting and initiates the analysis. According to the publisher, the tool returns findings in seconds, organizing each flagged issue with a severity classification, a confidence rating, an exact code citation, and a recommended resolution.

Once the system generates its findings, the human reviewer is expected to triage the output. Users can accept, reject, or edit each observation directly within the application. The publisher states that the system incorporates these triage decisions over time, calibrating future runs to align more closely with the firm's specific engineering judgment. The final output can be shared via read-only browser links or exported as CSV, Excel, or PDF files for internal quality control logs or project coordination meetings.

It is vital to understand that the product does not produce stamped drawings or autonomous design modifications. Rather, its documented purpose is to act as an automated net for catching routine code oversights, coordination discrepancies, and documentation gaps. The vendor emphasizes throughout its materials that the software is merely an assistive first-pass filter. The ultimate legal and technical responsibility for the design remains entirely with the licensed professional engineer overseeing the project.


## Model Code Coverage and Jurisdictional Customization

A critical benchmark for any plan review software is the depth and accuracy of its regulatory library. Callout.app reports coverage of more than 43 model codes and standards spanning seven primary engineering disciplines: architectural, structural, mechanical/HVAC, electrical, plumbing, energy, and fire and life safety. Its reference list includes major baselines such as the International Building Code (IBC 2024/2021), International Mechanical Code (IMC 2024/2021), International Plumbing Code (IPC 2024/2021), International Energy Conservation Code (IECC 2024/2021/2018), National Electrical Code (NEC 2023/2020), and specific standards from NFPA, ASHRAE, ASCE, ACI, and AISC.

Because local municipal authorities frequently adopt modified versions of national baselines, standard model code matching is often insufficient for permit submittals. Callout.app addresses this reality by allowing users to upload municipal, county, or state jurisdiction amendment documents in PDF format. During setup, reviewers select the relevant local amendment, and the underlying AI cross-references the custom rules against the base model codes, flagging instances where local statutes supersede national guidelines.

Additionally, the platform incorporates a Firm Standards Library. This feature enables engineering offices to input up to 20 internal design standards in plain English, covering internal preferences such as minimum service clearances around mechanical equipment, ductwork leakage thresholds, or thermostat placement elevations. The tool evaluates these proprietary rules alongside statutory codes during each review pass. Prospective buyers should verify during onboarding whether their regional municipal ordinances can be parsed accurately when embedded inside non-standard municipal PDF layouts.


## Credit Consumption and Pricing Structure

Callout.app departs from conventional enterprise software licensing by rejecting monthly per-seat subscriptions in favor of a pay-per-sheet credit system. The publisher argues that seat licenses penalize firms with cyclical project volumes. Instead, users purchase bundles of credits that never expire, drawing down balances solely when processing drawing sheets. If a technical fault occurs or the processing service fails, the platform automatically returns the deducted credits to the user's account balance.

Credit usage is governed directly by the chosen review depth. A Quick Review requires 2 credits per sheet, generating approximately 5 to 8 comments focused primarily on critical life-safety and fatal permit issues. A Standard Review costs 3 credits per sheet, producing 10 to 15 comments covering routine coordination and code checks. The Thorough Review consumes 4 credits per sheet, delivering 18 to 25 comments spanning permit blockers down to advisory observations. At the starter entry tier, a package costs $8 for 100 credits (covering up to 50 sheets under Quick settings). A Professional Pack is listed at $49 for 700 credits (up to 350 sheets), while a Studio Pack is priced at $99 for 1,500 credits (up to 750 sheets).

The vendor highlights an estimated cost range of $0.13 to $0.32 per sheet reviewed, contrasting this against manual engineering reviews that typically bill between $75 and $250 per sheet at standard professional rates. However, buyers should recognize that this starting pack pricing reflects preliminary self-service credit purchases. While the unit cost per sheet appears modest, large multi-trade sets processed through repeated revisions under the Thorough setting will consume credit balances quickly. Engineering managers should model their typical sheet throughput across multiple revision cycles rather than treating initial credit costs as a fixed operational budget.


## Strict Technical Limits on Files and Formats

Before considering integration into an active production pipeline, technical directors must evaluate Callout.app's rigid document processing boundaries. The platform operates exclusively on PDF files. It does not ingest native building information modeling formats, such as Autodesk Revit models, IFC data exchanges, Bentley systems, or raw DWG CAD files. Firms must render their drawing sheets into flattened or vector PDF packages before initiating an analysis.

Furthermore, the service enforces strict hardware session caps: each review upload cannot exceed 20 megabytes in total file size or 50 drawing sheets per run. In commercial construction, comprehensive drawing sets for multi-story hospitality, healthcare, or industrial facilities routinely exceed hundreds of sheets and several hundred megabytes. Processing such developments requires teams to manually split drawing packages into smaller sub-bundles, review them in discrete batches, and then consolidate exported findings.

These constraints mean Callout.app cannot serve as an uninterrupted background monitor across an entire enterprise repository. It is engineered as a discrete, sheet-by-sheet inspection station. Buyers with massive drawing packages must consider the labor overhead associated with breaking apart large sets, managing file sizes under 20 megabytes, and tracking review outputs across multiple fragmented sessions.


## Zero-Retention Architecture and Data Security

Transmitting unreleased architectural drawings, client floor plans, and critical infrastructure diagrams to external servers introduces sensitive confidentiality and intellectual property questions. Engineering firms frequently operate under non-disclosure agreements that forbid sharing unapproved plans with unauthorized third-party processors. Callout.app addresses these obligations through a documented zero-retention data pipeline, designed to prevent proprietary project assets from lingering on remote infrastructure.

According to the vendor, submitted drawing PDFs are held in temporary server memory solely for processing and are erased immediately once the review completes. Processing is executed via Anthropic's API, which operates under commercial terms that prohibit retaining customer inputs or utilizing submitted drawings for training foundational machine learning models. Application data and review metadata are maintained within United States data centers, and stored account review findings are encrypted at rest.

While this architecture protects drawing files from long-term external storage, user accounts do retain tabular review results, comment records, and project metadata in their history tab until an administrator manually purges them. Completed reviews can also be shared with external collaborators using unique read-only web links that do not require an active Callout account. Firm security officers must verify whether generating accessible read-only links aligns with their internal data handling policies and client confidentiality covenants.


## Classification of Findings and the Calibration Loop

Automated drawing analysis produces value only if its output can be interpreted quickly without drowning senior engineers in false alarms. Callout.app organizes its observations using a dual-tiered classification framework that rates both issue severity and algorithmic confidence. Understanding how the system categorizes these alerts is essential for determining how much triage labor will be required after each pass.

Severity is divided into four distinct tiers: Critical (flagging life-safety hazards or immediate permit blockers), Major (identifying code violations necessitating formal design revisions), Minor (non-blocking discrepancies that should be corrected for clean documentation), and Advisory (pointing out industry best practices). Concurrently, the platform attaches a confidence score: High represents a clear code conflict with an explicit statutory citation, Medium indicates a probable conflict requiring local project context, and Low marks a potential concern intended as a discretionary suggestion.

To refine this output over repeated engagements, Callout.app incorporates a calibration mechanism. When an engineer accepts, edits, or dismisses an advisory comment, the platform logs the intervention in a dedicated training section. The publisher claims that this continuous feedback trains the workspace to mirror the specific risk tolerance and stylistic nuances of the evaluating firm. Prospective users should run comparative tests across successive drawing iterations to measure how rapidly the system adjusts to their feedback and whether dismissals permanently eliminate recurring false positives.


## The Human In The Loop Mandate

A central requirement documented across Callout.app's public documentation is that the software cannot, under any circumstance, replace an engineering review by a licensed professional. The platform is not an autonomous engineering entity. It lacks physical context, cannot inspect field installations, and cannot bear legal liability for structural collapse, egress failures, or mechanical malfunctions. Every code citation, coordination alert, and suggested revision generated by the software must be evaluated by a qualified practitioner.

The vendor highlights that for newer regulatory cycles—such as the 2024 model code releases—section numberings and cross-references often shift relative to previous editions. While Callout.app is programmed to cite explicit sections rather than vague generalizations, the publisher warns users to cross-check cited clauses against their published physical code volumes. Relying blindly on an automated citation without professional verification introduces the risk of citing outdated or renumbered statutory provisions on official plan submittals.

Consequently, the primary operational benefit of the platform is time reclamation rather than labor elimination. By automating the mechanical scanning required to catch missing labels, clearance lapses, or baseline code divergences, the tool frees senior engineers to concentrate on intricate systems integration and complex engineering judgment. Any organization considering adopting Callout.app must establish firm protocols ensuring that automated reports serve strictly as preliminary discovery notes rather than direct redlines.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can Callout.app replace a formal review by a licensed professional engineer?

No. The publisher explicitly documents that Callout.app is strictly an AI-assisted first-pass screening utility. Every detected issue, severity rating, and suggested resolution must be independently examined, vetted, and validated by a licensed professional engineer before any work enters construction documents or permit submittals.

### What document formats and file size constraints does Callout.app enforce?

The platform accepts only PDF files, supporting single sheets, multi-sheet packages, and multi-file uploads. A single review session is strictly capped at a maximum file size of 20 megabytes and no more than 50 sheets.

### How are credits consumed across different review settings?

Usage is calculated per sheet based on review depth: Quick Review costs 2 credits per sheet (generating 5 to 8 comments), Standard Review costs 3 credits per sheet (10 to 15 comments), and Thorough Review costs 4 credits per sheet (18 to 25 comments). Starter packages begin at $8 for 100 credits.

### How does the platform handle drawing data security and retention?

According to the publisher, drawing PDFs are held in memory temporarily during processing and removed immediately after analysis, using Anthropic's zero-retention API without training public models. Stored user account records include review results and metadata, which users can manually clear from their history.
