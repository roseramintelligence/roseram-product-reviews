# Pipedrive review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-18  
**Category:** Sales CRM and pipeline management  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Pipedrive is a sales CRM built around leads, deals, contacts and customizable pipelines, with email sync, automations, reporting and add-ons varying by plan. Its pricing page lists Lite, Growth, Premium and Ultimate, starting at US$14, $39, $59 and $79 per seat per month when billed annually, with annual payment collected upfront. The company offers a 14-day trial without a card; adding billing details can start a paid subscription at trial end. Buyers should model every paid seat, add-on and top-up, and export data before canceling.

## Editorial verdict

The reviewed public materials do not substantiate a scam allegation. Pipedrive documents its current plan names, per-seat pricing, add-ons, trial conditions and account cancellation guidance. The most important buyer risks are annual upfront billing, unused seats still counting, add-on and top-up costs, and losing account access after a paid billing period. Test the actual sales workflow and export a representative dataset before moving the whole pipeline.

## Pipedrive SCAM review: page label, scope and evidence

“SCAM Review” is this directory’s page label, not a finding that Pipedrive is fraudulent. This desk review checks the company’s public product, plan and billing materials and its support guidance on trials, cancellation and data access. We did not subscribe to Pipedrive, import a sales database or test support. The article therefore reports what the first-party pages say and turns those details into questions a sales team can verify before purchasing.

Pipedrive is a customer relationship management service organized around leads, deals, contacts and sales pipelines. It can centralize follow-up, communication history and team activity. That value depends on whether representatives maintain the records and whether managers agree on pipeline stages. A CRM does not make an incomplete sales process reliable by itself; poor stage definitions and duplicated records can simply move into a more polished interface.


## Pipedrive Lite, Growth, Premium and Ultimate

Pipedrive’s current pricing page lists four plans: Lite, Growth, Premium and Ultimate. The official page describes lead, calendar and pipeline management on Lite; email sync and automation on Growth; lead generation, scoring and broader collaboration on Premium; and additional security and sandbox capabilities on Ultimate. The exact features and limits vary by tier, so a team should verify its requirements against the current table rather than rely on an old plan name or comparison article.

The pricing page showed annual-billing starting prices of US$14, $39, $59 and $79 per seat per month respectively, with the annual amount paid upfront. It displayed totals of $168, $468, $708 and $948 per seat per year. These are starting prices observed in USD, not a quote for every region, billing cadence, tax situation or number of licenses. Monthly billing is also available and should be compared at checkout.


## Seat counts, add-ons and top-ups change the total

Pipedrive’s billing guide says the total depends on plan, billing cycle, number of seats, add-ons, top-ups, currency and location. It specifically notes that a seat is charged whether it is assigned to a user or not. Its add-ons can include lead-generation, project, document, campaign or website-visitor capabilities, and the plan guide lists usage top-ups for items such as API tokens, leads and deals, reports, automations, enrichment and team controls.

A fair estimate should count every purchased seat, including seats reserved for seasonal staff or integrations. Add the cost of each optional feature the workflow actually needs, then model limits that might require a top-up. For an example, Pipedrive’s documentation calculates a Growth annual plan with four seats as four times the annual price, before adding the chosen LeadBooster cost. Confirm the billing currency and renewal amount for the specific account.


## Pipedrive 14-day trial and the billing handoff

Pipedrive advertises a 14-day free trial with no credit card required. Its help article says that if billing details are added during the trial, the account is automatically converted to a paid account when the trial ends and the new subscription cycle is invoiced. If the user does not enter billing details, account access is restricted when the trial expires, and the company recommends exporting data first.

This is a standard conversion path but one that deserves an explicit calendar reminder. Identify who can enter billing details, write down the trial’s end date and decide before that date whether the CRM has passed the team’s workflow tests. A trial can contain real customer and deal data; removing card details or leaving a workspace does not replace the documented cancellation or export steps.


## Trial cancellation and what paid cancellation means

Pipedrive’s trial-cancellation guide says a user who has added billing details can cancel the trial before the subscription begins in Billing settings; access remains through the remainder of the 14-day period. The paid-account guide says cancellation is available to an account-settings user, access continues to the end of the billing cycle, and the user will lose access to the account data after that period. The guide recommends exporting before closing the account.

A team should confirm who holds account-settings permission, complete the cancellation flow and retain the confirmation email. For a paid plan, determine the exact billing-cycle end date and schedule exports before access expires. Do not assume removing an individual user or disabling an integration cancels the company subscription. Review the account’s own billing screen, because the terms and available controls depend on its plan and location.


## Account deletion and retention need an exit plan

Pipedrive’s support material distinguishes loss of access after cancellation from permanent deletion. The account-deletion page says a canceled paid account and its data are typically scheduled for permanent deletion within 180 days of closure; a closed trial is typically deleted within 60 days. It also says an immediate deletion request can be made through support and may take up to 48 hours. These timelines make an export plan important before closing the workspace.

Decide which data should be preserved, which is subject to retention obligations, and who will verify the export. Test whether contacts, deals, activities, notes, files, products, emails and custom fields are represented as expected. Keep credentials and files out of an uncontrolled export. If an account is already closed, follow Pipedrive’s stated recovery and support process rather than assuming a trial or paid workspace remains accessible indefinitely.


## CRM workflow and implementation checks

Before selecting a tier, write down a single representative sales journey: lead capture, qualification, deal creation, follow-up, proposal, won or lost outcome and reporting. Test it with sample records. Check whether users can see only the records intended for them, how duplicates are handled and whether the system records an email sent outside its interface. Identify which actions are native, which require an add-on and which depend on a connected application.

During migration, choose a cutover date and a source of truth. Reconcile counts after import, verify that dates and owners map correctly, and inspect a sample of attachments and activity history. Run both systems in parallel only if the overlap is managed to prevent duplicate outreach. A CRM migration is a process change as well as a software purchase, so include training time and ongoing administration in its cost.


## Integrations, automation and permission controls

Pipedrive’s page advertises more than 500 integrations, while plan and add-on tiers control some advanced automation, data-enrichment and team capabilities. Integration count alone does not answer whether a specific connector syncs the fields and events a business needs. Review the scopes it requests, what direction data flows, how failures are surfaced and whether duplicate records are created when updates arrive from both systems.

Limit administrative access, review the user and visibility settings and establish an owner for each automation. Test a workflow against a representative record before enabling it for the entire database. Add a manual approval step for customer-facing actions with financial or legal consequences. If a connector is removed, verify what happens to its stored credentials and copied data.


## Reports, pipeline definitions and sales-quality claims

Pipedrive markets reporting and forecasting tools that can summarize pipeline stages and sales activity. Their usefulness depends on accurate definitions, dates and updates from users. A dashboard can show a persuasive total while deals are stale, duplicated or assigned to the wrong stage. Treat forecasts as decision aids, not as proof that a particular adoption will increase revenue.

Agree on what counts as a qualified lead, an active opportunity and a completed sale. Record the rules before comparing reports across months. Validate the data against the team’s accounting or order system where appropriate, and check how historical records are treated when stages change. A CRM can help reveal process gaps; it cannot resolve every disagreement about what a metric means.


## Alternatives and a like-for-like comparison

Compare Pipedrive with the CRM included in an existing business suite, a lightweight contact manager and more configurable sales platforms. Use the same number of users, contacts, integrations, automation steps, reporting needs and expected annual billing. Include optional prospecting, marketing or document products in each quote. Monthly headline prices alone can disguise differences in seats, feature gating and add-on obligations.

Pipedrive may suit teams that want a visually structured pipeline with sales-focused workflows and can keep records current. A company needing sophisticated service, marketing, finance or product analytics may prefer a broader system or a connected set of tools. Run a short pilot using actual use cases, not a large import, and decide how the organization will leave before committing years of customer history.


## Who should consider Pipedrive

A small or mid-sized sales team that follows leads through a clearly defined pipeline may value the quick visual overview and plan-based feature set. The free trial lets a prospect explore the workflow before paying, and the per-seat pricing makes it possible to model staffing changes. Teams that already use email and calendar integrations should test those exact connectors early in the trial.

Pipedrive may not be an easy fit for a company that expects a trial to remain open indefinitely, wants every feature in the base tier, or has no owner for data hygiene and permissions. A business with strict record-retention obligations should examine terms and exports before importing sensitive customer records. The right CRM is the one the team can operate consistently and exit cleanly.


## Pipedrive buyer checklist before starting a subscription

Confirm the plan name, monthly or annual cadence, price per seat, upfront annual total, tax, currency, seat count and renewal date. List every add-on and top-up that the sales process requires. During the trial, test permissions, imports, automation, email sync, reporting and the export of key objects. Keep billing access limited to the people who are authorized to approve a recurring charge.

Before moving a live pipeline, document data fields, owners, stages, integrations and rollback steps. Decide how a team would cancel, export records and confirm deletion. If billing details are added to a trial, place the end date on the calendar and make an explicit keep-or-cancel decision. Recheck the live order summary, which is the source for the actual account price.


## Pipedrive SCAM review verdict: a sales CRM with a clear billing model to verify

Pipedrive’s published pages describe four current plans, annual and monthly billing, add-ons, a 14-day no-card trial and cancellation and data-retention guidance. The reviewed public sources do not substantiate a scam allegation. They do show why the buyer should take care with seats and billing: annual plans are paid upfront, unused seats count, add-ons and top-ups can add recurring costs, and canceled accounts lose access after the billing cycle before scheduled deletion.

The safest evaluation is a focused trial, an itemized price estimate and a tested export. This dated public-source review does not include a live CRM test, paid subscription, support interaction or legal assessment. Starting prices and product terms were checked November 18, 2026 and may change; review the current order screen and agreement before purchasing.


## Features and decision criteria

- Lead, deal, contact and pipeline management
- Email synchronization, sequences and workflow automation by plan
- Reports, team permissions, lead generation and data enrichment add-ons
- 14-day trial and seat-based monthly or annual subscription choices
