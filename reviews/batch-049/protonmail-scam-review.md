# ProtonMail review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-24  
**Category:** Privacy-oriented hosted email  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

The catalog’s ProtonMail entry points to Proton Mail, a hosted email service with free and paid plans and privacy-oriented encryption positioning. Its official site distinguishes personal and business offerings, native applications and a separate Bridge path for desktop-client integration.

## Editorial verdict

Proton Mail is a relevant candidate when a person or business wants to evaluate a different hosted mailbox service and can plan the migration. Compare ordinary communication, recovery and the actual plan requirements. Encryption language should be understood through the documented behavior of each communication path, not treated as a universal guarantee.

## A mailbox move needs an address plan

Changing an email service affects how other people reach you and how accounts send recovery messages. Inventory the addresses that matter before moving a routine. A service trial should include ordinary incoming and outgoing mail without putting a critical address at risk.

For a business domain, define the migration and verification work independently of the user interface. A new inbox looking ready does not establish that every intended message reaches it. This review did not change DNS, import mail or test delivery.


## Choose the application path deliberately

Proton’s site presents native applications and a separate Bridge option. A person accustomed to another desktop client should inspect the actual integration requirements rather than assuming any hosted mailbox works the same way in every application.

Try the path the team intends to maintain and include search, attachments and the devices used away from the main computer. Keep plan entitlements and operating requirements in that comparison. A product navigation link does not establish access for every account.


## Privacy claims need a concrete communication context

A provider’s encryption model can differ across storage, same-service communication and messages exchanged through other paths. Read the current documentation for the situation you intend to use. Avoid reducing that model to a claim that every email interaction has identical properties.

Also understand recovery and device access before relying on a mailbox for important information. Roseram did not audit cryptography, test recovery or certify compliance with a standard. The review’s role is to identify questions supported by the provider’s public positioning.


## Compare a complete working week

A useful trial includes finding an older message, replying to a thread, handling an attachment and using the calendar or domain features you actually need. Check what happens after a temporary connection problem and how the latest intended state becomes visible.

Make the purchase decision from that routine and the current plan terms. This source-based review does not establish a guaranteed privacy, delivery or productivity outcome. It separates a documented hosted service from a completed migration and operational acceptance.


## Features and decision criteria

- Hosted mailbox service
- Personal and business product paths
- Native web, mobile and desktop applications
- Separate Bridge integration path

## Pricing considerations

The checked site offers free and paid plans and separate business pricing. Verify storage, address, domain, application and integration entitlements in the current plan; temporary promotions are not permanent rates.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Proton Mail only an email client for an existing unrelated mailbox?

It is a hosted email service. Evaluate account and message migration separately from installing an application.

### Is Bridge the same thing as a native Proton application?

No. The official navigation presents it as a desktop client add-on, separately from native applications.

### Has Roseram independently audited the service’s encryption?

No. Privacy and encryption positioning in this review is attributed to the provider’s official materials.
