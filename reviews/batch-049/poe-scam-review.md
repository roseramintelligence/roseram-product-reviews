# Poe review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-16  
**Category:** Multi-model AI chat, bot and app platform  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Poe is Quora’s platform for using third-party AI bots and apps in a single interface. It includes provider-created models, user-created bots and interactive apps, with usage measured through points whose cost depends on the selected bot and task. Subscription benefits and point grants vary by plan, platform and account.

## Editorial verdict

Poe publishes subscription, point-metering, data-use, and bot-provider terms. The reviewed evidence does not support calling it a scam. Its central buying question is whether a single multi-model interface is worth managing bot-specific point costs, changing provider availability, third-party processing, and subscription rules tied to the place where the purchase was made.

## Poe review: one interface, many independent bots

Poe is a consumer and developer platform from Quora that brings a large directory of bots and apps into one account. Official provider bots can include models from companies such as OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and other publishers; creators can also publish bots with instructions, knowledge sources or interactive behavior. Poe is the interface and access layer. The underlying model, its capabilities, its provider’s rules and its availability can differ from one bot to another. A Poe subscription is therefore not the same thing as owning a direct subscription with each model provider.

A useful evaluation begins with a short list of real tasks: explain a document, draft a response, solve a coding problem, generate an image, or compare two models on the same prompt. Check each bot’s profile, creator identity, cost indicator and restrictions before using it. A bot name that resembles a known model does not prove that it is official or powered by the provider the user expects. Use the platform’s provider and bot details to distinguish official listings from user-created bots, and verify an important answer against independent sources rather than treating fluent text as evidence.


## Poe pricing and compute points: why the subscription amount is not the full comparison

Poe uses points as a common usage meter across bots. The official FAQ says a bot’s displayed rate can be fixed, such as a cost per image, or variable based on how much text the bot reads and writes. Users can open a bot’s Rates view, inspect an approximate first-message cost and review point use for an earlier message. This is more informative than counting chats, but it does mean that one message is not a consistent unit of value across a short text reply, a long-context analysis and a generated video.

The purchase FAQ describes both monthly and annual subscription options. Subscription points can be granted daily, monthly or according to the specific plan, and unused points do not roll over unless the plan specifies an exception. The FAQ’s approximate $30 per one million points is an add-on reference, not the subscription price and not a universal price per response. Before subscribing, compare the displayed plan allowance with the rates for the models and media tasks you intend to use. A low monthly headline can represent little value if the chosen bot consumes the allowance quickly.


## Free daily points, add-ons and point-expiry rules

Non-paying users receive a daily point allowance that the FAQ says resets at the end of each 24-hour period; unused daily points do not roll over. Subscribers receive a larger allowance and may buy add-on points. Poe says purchased add-on points can be used for one year and are not refundable, transferable or redeemable for cash. These distinctions matter because points in the account are service entitlements, not money stored in a wallet. If an account closes or loses access, the published terms say points do not have cash value.

Treat each bucket separately when estimating a project. Check the balance page and point history, watch costs on sample prompts, and note which balance is consumed first. For a team or shared subscription, verify who may use the points and whether the owner can limit individual consumption. The purchase FAQ says group members share access to the subscriber’s point pool and the subscriber can remove a member; the FAQ also describes aggregate usage visibility to the subscription holder. A shared plan can therefore simplify access while making internal allocation less granular.


## Bot selection, model changes and answer quality

The catalog mixes provider bots, image and video generators, audio tools, and creator-built bots. That breadth is the appeal, but a catalog listing is not a fixed capability contract. Providers may release, rename or retire models; bot creators can change prompts and connected data; and a third-party model can have its own restrictions. Use the bot profile to check who made it, what it claims to do and whether it has special instructions or pricing. For a consequential task, compare more than one source and preserve the exact bot name and settings used.

Poe’s own FAQ cautions that bots can be wrong, may lack current information and should not be relied upon for medical, legal or investment decisions. It also describes context limits and an auto-manage setting that can omit older conversation turns to reduce points. A longer thread is not automatically a complete memory. If a task depends on a previous fact, restate or attach it and confirm the bot received the relevant context. For financial, regulatory, health or safety work, treat Poe as an aid to research and drafting, not as the decision-maker.


## Poe privacy: chats and uploaded files may reach model providers

Poe’s Privacy Policy says that third-party AI model providers and developers may receive details of interactions, including chat content and uploaded photos or documents, to provide and generally improve their services, subject to the settings and provider terms. It warns users that information and files supplied to bots and apps are shared with the model providers and developers powering them. That means “one interface” does not mean one data recipient. Before uploading a contract, codebase, customer export or internal report, inspect the selected bot’s provider, the current privacy details and any available controls.

The policy also says the service collects account identifiers, device information and IP address, and that the optional Memory feature can carry helpful context between interactions with different providers. The policy says Memory is off by default and can be managed in Settings. This is a potentially useful continuity feature and also a reason to review what context may travel between models. Poe states that a user’s account name and email are not shared with providers except limited information in group chats; chat content and files are a separate category. Avoid uploading secrets, credentials, payment-card data or regulated information unless the organization has approved the provider and processing path.


## Poe subscriptions, app stores and cancellation friction

Poe subscriptions can be purchased on the web, through Apple or through Google Play. The purchase FAQ says a subscription is managed on the platform where it was bought; a customer who wants to move platforms may need to cancel the original subscription and wait for the current benefits to end before subscribing elsewhere. The FAQ says access continues to the end of the billing period after cancellation. This split is easy to miss when a customer later opens Settings on a different device and cannot see the expected upgrade or cancellation control.

Before confirming a payment, record the platform, plan, billing cadence, currency, next renewal date and method for cancellation. Poe says web users can manage the subscription in Poe settings, while mobile purchases are managed through the corresponding Apple or Google account. The purchase FAQ describes subscription charges as generally non-refundable except as provided by its refund policy or determined by Quora. Do not assume deleting the app cancels a subscription. Use the original billing channel and keep the cancellation confirmation.


## Poe creator bots, APIs and ownership of a published experience

Poe also provides ways for eligible creators to build bots and apps. Its Terms describe third-party model providers, user-created bots, usage guidelines and separate API terms. The Creator Monetization FAQ says eligible creators can set a per-message price and that participation depends on program terms and region; payment setup and tax information are required for payouts. This is a creator platform, not a promise that every bot earns money, receives users or remains listed. A creator should read model-provider rules, review the audience experience and know where the bot’s API or knowledge files are hosted.

Bot creation also changes the data responsibility. Poe’s Privacy Policy says developers using APIs may view and store chats on their own servers, and bot developers are responsible for their bot content and compliance under the terms. A creator should explain what the bot does, avoid misleading claims about affiliation, and not request sensitive data without a clear need. Test prompt injection, accidental disclosure and failure responses with non-sensitive sample content. If monetizing a bot, test the user-facing point cost, how changes in price appear, payout eligibility and how the service handles a removed bot or disputed charge.


## Poe alternatives and the workflow-based buyer test

Compare Poe with direct ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini plans, model-provider APIs, Microsoft Copilot, Perplexity, and open-model tools. A direct subscription may provide a deeper first-party feature set, while an API can offer tighter application control at a usage-based cost. A multi-model hub can reduce the number of accounts a user manages but introduces points, provider routing and additional policy layers. Open-source models may offer different deployment and data-control choices, with infrastructure and maintenance responsibilities moved to the operator.

Run a controlled test across the two or three tasks you perform most often. Keep the prompt, input and acceptance checklist constant, then record model identity, first-response time, point charge, output quality, file handling and whether a human must correct the answer. Test a fresh conversation and a long conversation to observe context behavior. This is a buyer-test method, not a Roseram benchmark. Compare the annual subscription total plus add-on points with the actual amount of work completed, and include the value of direct-provider features that may not transfer through an aggregator.


## Poe SCAM Review: evidence, limits and buying conclusion

Poe publishes product help, point-metering instructions, purchase and cancellation guidance, privacy disclosures, creator program details and Terms of Service. The documents describe a multi-model bot platform with a points-based usage system and third-party providers; they do not substantiate a claim that Poe is a scam. This article is a desk review of publisher and policy material, not an independent audit, customer testimonial or hands-on performance report. “SCAM Review” is the directory’s review label and should not be read as an accusation against Quora or a particular bot creator.

Poe may suit someone who values model discovery and wants a single place to try different bots. It is a weaker fit for a buyer who needs a guaranteed fixed cost per task, a direct relationship with each model vendor, or a single data-processing boundary without checking provider terms. Before purchase, verify the local subscription price and point grant, inspect bot-specific rates, test representative workloads, decide whether Memory is appropriate, and confirm which platform will manage renewal. For work involving sensitive files, get organizational approval before uploading them. Those checks answer the practical question better than a broad claim that one interface is always cheaper or safer.


## Features and decision criteria

- Chats with bots from multiple third-party AI providers in one account
- Public and private user-created bots and interactive apps
- Bot-level point rates, message cost details and usage history
- Creator APIs, bot monetization, shared subscriptions and mobile apps subject to plan and policy eligibility

## Pricing considerations

Poe’s current help pages describe a points economy, not a fixed cost per prompt: each bot shows an estimated rate, and actual use may depend on input/output size or media task. The $30-per-million reference on the FAQ is an approximate add-on-points reference, not a universal subscription price or a promise that every bot has the same effective cost. The account and platform checkout display the applicable subscription price, grant, currency and renewal terms.
