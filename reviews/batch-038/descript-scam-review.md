# Descript review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-18  
**Category:** Audio and video editing software  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Descript combines recording, transcription, text-based audio and video editing, publishing and AI-assisted media tools. Its paid tiers separate media hours from AI credits, so buyers should model both against their actual workload.

## Editorial verdict

The sources reviewed describe an operating media editor with published pricing, terms and privacy documentation. They do not substantiate a scam finding or establish the performance of a particular project. Verify plan limits, data controls, consent and exports with a small project before relying on it for production.

## Descript SCAM review: what this review can and cannot establish

Descript presents itself as a media editor in which a transcript can serve as the editing surface for audio and video. Its published materials also describe recording, transcription, screen capture, exports, collaboration and generative tools. This review examines those claims against the company’s pricing page, Terms of Service and Privacy Policy. It does not report a hands-on trial, a controlled export test or an interview with a customer.

The word “SCAM” appears in the page label to match the review directory. It is not a finding that Descript committed fraud. The evidence reviewed does not support that accusation. The useful buying questions are more specific: which usage units will constrain a project, how custom voice and avatar information is handled, what happens to project files and account data, and whether a team can reproduce and deliver its work after a subscription changes.


## How Descript’s text-based editing changes an audio or video workflow

Descript describes its App as a media word processor: users can transcribe a recording, edit audio or video by changing transcript text, export the result, refine it in a waveform editor, collaborate and use AI tools. That structure may help a speaker, producer or editor remove a phrase without first learning a traditional multitrack timeline. It also makes transcript quality and speaker labels part of the editing process rather than a separate clerical step.

A buyer should test the whole handoff, not only the appealing transcript view. Import a short recording with overlapping speakers, names, pauses and a sentence that must remain intact. Correct the transcript, remove one phrase, inspect the media around the cut, then export and play the delivered file in the player the audience will use. A smooth text edit is useful only if the resulting timing, audio and picture remain acceptable.


## Descript media hours, transcription and AI credits are different limits

The current pricing page defines media minutes as the amount of imported or recorded media processed in Descript. It lists 60 minutes on Free, 10 hours for Hobbyist, 30 for Creator and 40 for Business, with limits expressed per editor. The same page describes AI credits as a separate pool for tools such as Underlord, Studio Sound, Green Screen, Eye Contact and generated media or avatars. Transcription is listed separately in the comparison table.

Do not treat one hour of transcription as one hour of media capacity or assume a media-hour allowance is a general AI allowance. Write down the exact tasks a project uses: recording length, imported footage, transcription, speech synthesis and any generative edits. Compare each task with the specific unit named in the plan. The distinction matters because one long recording may consume media capacity even when a user chooses not to transcribe it, while a separate AI operation may spend credits.


## Descript pricing and plan selection for an individual creator

Descript’s pricing page displays Free, Hobbyist, Creator, Business and Enterprise options, with a monthly or annual billing choice. It lists 100 one-time AI credits for Free, 400 per month for Hobbyist, 800 for Creator and 1,500 for Business. The paid plans also differ in media hours, export resolution, watermark-free exports, stock-library access, collaboration and top-ups. Prices and plan packaging can change; the checkout page is the appropriate place to verify the amount for the selected billing term and region.

A solo podcaster who edits a small number of short episodes may value the entry plan’s simpler allowance, while a creator producing client videos may need the higher resolution and more monthly media hours. Estimate one ordinary month and one busy month separately. Include revisions, remote recordings and an occasional long interview. If the choice only works when every credit and minute is used perfectly, leave headroom or compare another editor with a cost structure that better matches irregular production.


## Descript Creator and Business plans for a small production team

The pricing page says Creator can scale to a team of three with separately billed seats, while Business can scale to five. It lists higher media and AI-credit pools on Business, plus team-oriented options such as Brand Studio, translation and dubbing, custom avatars and priority support with an SLA. Some benefits are not interchangeable: a larger credit balance does not itself provide brand governance, and an additional seat may change the recurring bill.

Before inviting collaborators, calculate the number of editors who need paid features and the number who only need to review or comment. Check which role can export, change shared brand materials, publish links or access billing. Set a project owner, backup administrator and approval path for public exports. A team plan can simplify cooperation, but it cannot decide which version is approved or prevent a staff member from delivering an unfinished draft without internal review.


## Free Descript plan: a practical pilot, not a promise of unlimited production

Descript’s official pricing material describes a free option, says a credit card is not required to try it, and lists capped media and AI allowances. That provides a useful way to see whether transcript editing fits a real task before paying. The page also lists limits on export resolution and stock-library search results. A pilot should use a sample that resembles the planned work rather than an unusually clean one-minute clip.

Check which restrictions appear at the moment of export and whether the project can be moved or delivered as expected. Use a test that contains the accent, technical vocabulary, background noise or speaker overlap likely in production. Record the time spent correcting the transcript and compare it with the time saved in editing. A no-cost start is valuable for fit assessment; it should not be confused with a guarantee that a finished commercial workflow will stay within Free limits.


## Text-based editing still needs a quality pass against the original recording

A transcript is an interpretation of speech, not a source of truth. Names, acronyms, numbers, negations and words spoken over another person are common points where a transcript edit can alter meaning. If a producer removes a sentence in text, the cut should be reviewed against the original waveform and picture. A sentence may depend on a pause, reaction or visual cue that is not apparent from the words alone.

Create a repeatable review checklist: compare the final transcript with the source audio, inspect each cut in context, verify speaker attribution, play the whole export and check captions for spelling and timing. For legal, medical, financial or public-policy material, keep a subject-matter reviewer involved. Descript’s editing model can make revisions accessible, but the editor remains responsible for preserving context, consent and factual accuracy.


## AI credits, generative tools and output expectations

Descript says AI credits measure usage of AI features and lists tools for audio enhancement, removing filler words, green-screen effects, eye-contact changes, generated media and avatars. It also describes custom voice clones and video regeneration on paid plans. The pricing page’s credit table and top-up availability should be checked before a production team builds a repeatable workflow around one tool. Credits can change the total project cost even when the timeline work itself is straightforward.

Run a representative edit with a human review step. Listen for artifacts, unnatural pauses, altered emphasis or a change that makes a speaker appear to say something they did not intend. Preserve an unaltered source and label generated or modified content where the audience could otherwise be misled. For sensitive work, define which AI tools are permitted, who approves their use and how the team will respond if a generated output is inaccurate or unsuitable.


## Descript custom voice cloning and voiceprint considerations

The Privacy Policy discusses AI Speakers, recordings used to create a voice model, voiceprints that may be biometric data in some jurisdictions, and retention connected to authentication and fraud prevention. It describes a voice-consent statement and says voiceprints are retained until the purpose is satisfied or for up to three years after the account was last accessed, whichever occurs first. A buyer should read the current policy and the applicable product flow before submitting anyone’s voice.

Obtain informed permission from the actual speaker, document the authorized purpose and limit access to the resulting voice model. Do not upload a coworker, customer, public figure or family member’s voice simply because a recording is available. Ask how a voice can be disabled, deleted or transferred when a contractor leaves. The vendor’s consent flow is only one part of the process; the organization must also honor employment, publicity, privacy and recording laws that apply to its use.


## Project privacy, AI improvement and sharing settings

Descript’s Privacy Policy says it collects account details, uploaded media, device and usage information, and uses service providers for operations. It states that Project Information is treated as confidential and describes limits on human access, while also saying Projects may be analyzed to improve the service and that users can disable the Share Data with Descript setting. The Terms separately address inputs and outputs used with generative tools and describe AI-provider access needed to deliver those features.

These details make settings and contract review relevant for confidential interviews, unreleased products or client footage. Confirm the data-sharing control in the account you will actually use, check whether it applies to the intended AI features and review enterprise controls if a regulated team needs custom retention or an opt-out. Avoid placing credentials, customer records or sensitive personal information in a media project unless the organization has approved the service and the data path.


## User content, training material and third-party rights

The Terms say users are responsible for their User Content and include restrictions against infringement and misuse. The Privacy Policy addresses project information and separate handling for AI voice features and avatars. A user should therefore confirm that they have rights to upload each recording, image, music track, logo and voice sample, and should check the terms of any third-party stock material before embedding it in a client deliverable.

Keep a simple asset register for commercial work: source, license, permitted channels, attribution and any expiration or seat limits. Get written participant releases where needed and do not treat an AI voice feature as permission to reproduce a person’s identity. If a client supplies the media, define who verifies the rights and who bears responsibility for a questionable asset. A clean production handoff includes permissions as well as the exported video.


## Export resolution, shareable pages and client delivery

The pricing comparison distinguishes local video export resolution and shareable web-link resolution. It lists 720p for Free, 1080p for Hobbyist and 4K for higher plans; it also lists duration limits for web-link exports. Audio export is shown as unlimited across those tiers. These are plan-level specifications rather than a measurement of visual quality, codec compatibility or upload speed for a particular source file.

Export a representative file at the delivery resolution and open it on the recipient’s device. Check frame rate, audio loudness, captions, aspect ratio and whether the viewer can download or only stream. For a shareable page, verify its access settings from a logged-out browser and confirm that the link does not expose internal comments or drafts. If a client requires project files as well as a rendered video, include them explicitly in the statement of work and test that handoff.


## Cancellation, account deletion and keeping project backups

Descript’s pricing FAQ says users can cancel a subscription through the subscription page and separately says users can delete an account and permanently wipe data from its servers. The Terms also place responsibility on users to back up User Content and discuss deletion when access ends. These actions are different: stopping a recurring plan should not be assumed to export every project, and a deletion request should not be made until required assets and records have been secured.

Before changing or canceling a paid plan, export finished media, preserve source files, transcripts, captions, project assets and license evidence, then verify a restore from the local backup. Confirm which editing or collaboration features remain available after the billing change and whether a shared link still works. Keep a written inventory of what is stored only in the cloud. A graceful exit plan protects the creator from a billing surprise and protects the client from losing editable work.


## Descript alternatives and a fair comparison checklist

Alternatives range from conventional nonlinear video editors to transcript-first podcast editors, browser-based collaboration suites and dedicated transcription services. The right comparison depends on whether the bottleneck is editing speed, remote recording, text accuracy, brand controls, team review, stock media or synthetic speech. Comparing only the headline subscription price misses the separate cost of media hours, AI credits, seats, storage, top-ups and time spent correcting generated output.

Use the same short project in each finalist. Measure transcript correction, cut review, export and handoff time; check resolution and format support; price a typical month and a peak month; test access roles and deletion; inspect consent and AI-data terms. If a team mainly needs a transcript, a simpler transcription product may be cheaper. If it needs deep color grading and complex compositing, a traditional editor may be stronger. Let the workflow, not a broad “all-in-one” label, decide.


## Descript SCAM review verdict: transparent limits, verify privacy and workflow fit

The reviewed official pages describe Descript as an audio and video editor with transcription, text-based editing, AI features and subscription tiers. The pricing page explains media hours and AI credits as separate usage measures, lists export differences and allows cancellation through the account. Its Terms and Privacy Policy describe user-content responsibilities, generative tools, project-data practices and voice-related processing. That evidence establishes published product terms, not a guarantee that every feature will fit every buyer.

The material reviewed does not substantiate a claim that Descript is a scam. Before buying, calculate media hours, AI credits and seats; review the current data-sharing settings; secure consent for voices and avatars; test the final export; and back up project files before canceling or deleting an account. This is a public-source desk review, not a paid trial, security audit or performance test. Official plan and policy pages were reviewed November 18, 2026; confirm current pricing, limits and terms at checkout.
