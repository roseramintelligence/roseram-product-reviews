# AIORadar review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-10-28  
**Category:** SEO and AI search analytics  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

AIORadar advertises Google AI Overview and paid AI Mode citation checks alongside Search Console, GA4 assistant-referral reporting and agency workspaces. This source-led review examines the difference between observed citations and attributed visits, authorization scopes, plan capacity, usage credits and client-reporting limits.

## Editorial verdict

This desk review does not establish that AIORadar is a scam or certify the business as safe. The cited publisher material describes an offer that can be evaluated against your requirements. Before paying, verify the seller, the current terms, any cancellation rules and the capabilities that matter to your use case. The discussion below separates publisher claims from suggested buyer checks; it does not report hands-on testing or an independent security audit.

## What AIORadar measures and who its offer is for

AIORadar presents itself as a workspace for SEO specialists, consultants, and agencies investigating AI search visibility alongside ordinary search performance. Its captured homepage describes Google AI Overview citation checks, recognized AI assistant referrals in Google Analytics 4, Search Console analysis, technical SEO tools, and client reporting. Those are several related workflows rather than a single universal visibility score. A useful evaluation starts by identifying which question your team needs answered: whether a selected Google result cites a domain, whether an assistant referred a visitor, or whether an existing page is losing search traffic.

This AIORadar SCAM Review examines the publisher's saved homepage, pricing page, setup documentation, and AI Overview tracking explanation. The assessment concerns the advertised measurement boundaries, account connections, usage costs, and decisions a buyer can reasonably make from the documented data. It does not report a signed-in trial, a comparison against live search results, or a customer support test. The primary material supports descriptions of what AIORadar says it offers. It cannot establish the reliability of a particular account, guarantee future citations, or settle whether the tool will produce a commercial return for an individual business.


## Google AI Overview citations are selected observations

The AI Overview tracking page says a check records whether an overview appears for a chosen query, which sources it links to, and whether the customer's domain is among those sources. It attaches a market, language, device, and check time to the observation. This framing matters. A positive result describes the configured check, not every possible search by every prospective customer. Two observations gathered under different profiles should not automatically be treated as a gain or loss in the same series. Ask whether the interface and exported report retain enough context to explain each result.

The publisher describes daily, weekly, fortnightly, and monthly schedules, plus on-demand checks. Choosing a frequency is a budget and interpretation decision. A fluctuating result may be relevant, but repeated checks do not by themselves explain why a source appeared or disappeared. Build a query set around actual buyer questions, record the profile settings, and decide what change would trigger an editorial investigation. The tool's documented purpose is to help observe citations and compare sources. It should not be interpreted as proof that a particular page edit caused a citation change merely because the two events occurred near each other.


## Assistant referrals do not reveal every assistant answer

One of the clearest boundaries on the homepage is the distinction between answer visibility and referral measurement. AIORadar says connected GA4 reporting groups recognized visits and conversions referred by assistants including ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude, and Copilot. It expressly separates that outcome reporting from reading citations inside those assistants' answers. A business researching its appearance in conversational tools should notice this limit before buying. A referral can show that a measurable visit arrived through a recognized source; it does not provide an inventory of all answers in which the business was mentioned.

The converse also needs care. An absence of recognized referrals in an analytics view does not establish that an assistant never mentioned the brand. A mention might not produce a click, and a visit might not arrive with an attribution signal the reporting method recognizes. These are general limits of interpreting referral observations, not a test finding about AIORadar's implementation. Ask how the product identifies assistant traffic, what source categories it displays, and whether the underlying GA4 view can be inspected. Keep citation checks and attributed visits separate when presenting a client result, even if both appear on the same dashboard.


## AI Mode and ordinary rankings answer different questions

The captured tracker page describes paid-plan checks for Google AI Mode and states that they are tracked separately from AI Overviews. It advertises a five-credit cost per AI Mode check, subject to plan-specific monthly limits. This is relevant for buyers who might otherwise assume every AI-related feature is included in the same query allowance. Confirm which surface you want monitored, what a check actually requests, and which allowance it consumes. The existence of an AI Overview result should not be silently substituted for an AI Mode observation or represented as coverage of an unrelated assistant.

The homepage also says the Rank Tracker uses connected Search Console data rather than scraping ordinary search results. That is another important measurement distinction. When comparing vendors, ask exactly what each position value represents and whether the selected date, country, device, and query context agree. Similar-looking ranking charts can draw on different collection methods. AIORadar's advertised advantage is putting several signals beside each other; the useful comparison depends on preserving their meanings. A citation count, an analytics referral total, and a Search Console position should support different questions rather than being combined into an unexplained score that suggests more precision than the inputs provide.


## What can be explored without connecting Google accounts

The setup documentation lists AI Search tracking, Schema Studio, the Site Audit crawler, AI Access checks, Meta Writer, migration mapping, and the Live Page Analyzer as public-data features that do not require a Google connection. Features built on Search Console, GA4, or Business Profile data require the matching integration. This creates a useful way to evaluate the public-facing tools before authorizing access to a client's reporting accounts. It also means an empty connected-data view should be investigated as an access or setup question before anyone interprets it as evidence that a website has no activity.

Begin with one correctly identified domain and a short list of representative queries. Inspect whether the public-data output names its source, check time, and relevant profile. If you later add Google connections, compare the selected properties with the site the workspace is intended to represent. A wrong property can yield a convincing-looking report about the wrong business. The documentation describes a separate workspace for each client, including its domain, integrations, saved runs, and reports. Treat that separation as something to verify in your evaluation, especially if different people manage different clients or an agency needs to export material for external recipients.


## Authorization scopes deserve attention before integration

According to the captured setup page, Search Console and GA4 connections request read-only reporting scopes. The documentation makes a different statement about Google Business Profile: it describes a management scope that can apply listing changes the user explicitly approves. Those are different levels of authority. A team that only needs search reporting can evaluate whether it needs the Business Profile connection at all. Before proceeding through authorization, inspect the actual consent screen, confirm the intended Google account, and compare the requested permissions with the documented task. A marketing description of access does not replace that account-level check.

The publisher says Business Profile posts are drafted for users to review and publish themselves, while certain listing edits can be written back after approval. A local-marketing team should clarify that distinction before assigning responsibilities. Establish who may connect the listing, approve an edit, and remove access when a client relationship ends. The homepage says integrations can be removed in settings, but this desk review has not exercised revocation or verified its downstream effects. An agency should include connection removal and export requirements in its own handover process rather than assuming that deleting a report and disconnecting an account are the same operation.


## Plan price and credit consumption both determine cost

The captured pricing material lists Free, Starter, Growth, and Agency plans. The advertised monthly subscription prices are $0, $29, $59, and $149 respectively, with paid monthly credit allowances of 2,500, 5,500, and 14,000. The free offer includes 100 one-time credits rather than a recurring monthly allowance. These are saved-page observations, and buyers should verify the current checkout, taxes, and offer before purchasing. The important budgeting point is that subscription capacity and usage credits are separate constraints: having room for another workspace does not necessarily mean every desired check fits the available allowance.

The tracker documentation prices an AI Overview query check at three credits and says a fresh available result can be reused without another charge. As a planning illustration, twenty queries checked daily for thirty days would require 1,800 credits if every check incurred that stated rate and none were reused. That is arithmetic based on the advertised unit price, not a bill estimate from an actual account. Other paid actions could use additional credits, and feature limits still apply. Build a usage plan from the tasks you actually need, then inspect the cost preview the publisher says is shown before paid actions run.


## Workspace limits, seats and reports shape agency suitability

The saved pages advertise one workspace on Free, five on Starter, fifteen on Growth, and unlimited workspaces on Agency. Growth is described as including up to three seats, while Agency advertises unlimited team seats. For an agency, the right comparison is therefore broader than the monthly subscription amount. Count active client domains, the staff who need access, the reports required each month, and the checks planned for each account. An unlimited workspace allowance is not the same as unlimited live usage, because the Agency description still includes a defined monthly credit allocation and limits for some actions.

The pricing page describes white-label PDF and Word reports on paid plans and additional custom branding on Agency. That could support a client-delivery workflow, but a report format is valuable only if the recipient can understand the data. During evaluation, inspect an exported example for dates, market context, source labels, unresolved checks, and the distinction between observed results and recommendations. The homepage labels its displayed product-tour data as illustrative. Do not quote those sample citation counts or traffic figures as customer outcomes. The relevant question is whether a real report from your own properly configured workspace accurately communicates what was measured and what remains uncertain.


## Top-ups, cancellation and refunds need separate reading

The captured pricing page advertises one-time credit packs usable on any plan and says purchased pack credits do not expire and remain after downgrading or canceling. It describes a limited rollover of unused monthly credits on paid plans. Those two balances have different stated treatment, so ask how the interface distinguishes them and in what order they are consumed. The same page says credit-consuming actions pause when credits run out until a refill or top-up. A campaign calendar should account for that possibility rather than assuming a scheduled check will keep running indefinitely after the available allowance is exhausted.

Cancellation and refunds are also separate concepts in the saved offer. The pricing page says cancellation prevents renewal while leaving the plan active until the paid period ends. It describes subscriptions and credit packs as non-refundable except where law requires a refund or the named merchant of record grants one. This review is reporting the publisher's written terms, not determining their legal effect in a particular jurisdiction. Inspect the current checkout and refund policy before committing, especially when considering a large top-up. If a pre-sales answer appears to conflict with the written offer, obtain clarification before relying on it for a purchasing decision.


## Content recommendations still need an accountable editor

AIORadar's homepage describes content-decay monitoring, topic clusters, internal-link analysis, page optimization, and an Agency-only workflow that moves from topic to brief, draft, and quality review with a human gate before publication. Those functions can help organize editorial work, but a generated recommendation is not evidence that a new claim is true. A team should check factual statements, source attribution, product accuracy, and the intended reader's question before publishing. This matters particularly for comparison pages and reviews, where a confident paragraph can imply testing or a negative business finding that the available evidence does not support.

If a team needs to build a separate application around its editorial workflow, the Roseram workspace provides a place to develop that project. That contextual option does not establish a feature-for-feature substitute for AIORadar's documented tracking and connected reporting. Define which system gathers observations, which system drafts content, and who approves the final output. The sensible goal is a traceable editorial decision, not a large volume of pages solely because a workflow can create them. Record the source supporting each material claim, keep recommendations distinguishable from measurements, and reject articles that cannot answer the reader's actual product-specific question.


## A useful trial compares observations with their sources

An evaluation can start with a small baseline: a few queries that matter to a real customer journey, one market and device profile, and a correctly selected domain. Save the check times and inspect the cited sources. When adding Search Console or GA4, compare a narrow period and a clearly defined metric with the original reporting account. This is a proposed trial procedure, not a test this article performed. The purpose is to discover whether the product's labels and exports make its observations understandable enough for the decisions your team expects to make.

Failure states deserve the same attention as successful checks. The tracker page explicitly distinguishes cited, absent, changed, partial, failed, and unavailable outcomes and says incomplete checks are not silently counted as results. Verify how those states appear in charts and exported reports. A query that could not be checked should not be narrated as a confirmed absence of citation. Similarly, a partial report should make its coverage understandable to the recipient. These details can matter more than a visually impressive dashboard because they determine whether a client or editor can distinguish a genuine visibility observation from an unresolved collection problem.


## Decision summary and the limits of this SCAM Review

AIORadar's documented offer is most relevant to teams that want selected Google AI citation checks, recognized assistant-referral reporting, and conventional SEO context in a client workspace. Its stated boundaries are central to evaluating the purchase: it does not claim to inspect every assistant answer, its ranking view uses Search Console data, and paid usage involves both credits and plan-specific limits. A prospective buyer should judge those capabilities against a written reporting need. If the requirement is comprehensive monitoring of every conversational mention, the saved description does not establish that coverage and should not be stretched to imply it.

No independent customer testimonial is included because the captured sources do not supply a verified customer account suitable for quotation here. The illustrative screens are product examples, not performance evidence. This article also does not establish that AIORadar is fraudulent or certify a seller as safe. Before buying, inspect the current offer, understand the connection scopes, evaluate a representative report, and calculate the likely usage pattern. Continue only if those checks support the specific measurement problem you need to solve. A measured improvement in one observed series should be described within its actual scope, rather than promoted into a guarantee of traffic, citations, rankings, or revenue.


## Features and decision criteria

- Publisher-described Google AI Overview checks tied to query, market, device and time.
- GA4 reporting for recognized assistant referrals, distinct from answer citation monitoring.
- Search Console analysis, client workspaces and paid white-label reports.
- Subscription capacity and usage credits with separate allowances and action costs.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does AIORadar inspect every ChatGPT or Perplexity answer?

The captured homepage says it measures recognized assistant referrals through connected GA4 data and does not claim to inspect citations inside those assistants. Its direct citation checks concern Google AI Overviews and paid Google AI Mode checks.

### Does AIORadar need Google account access to start?

Its documentation says public-data tools including AI Overview tracking can run without Google connections. Search Console, GA4 and Business Profile modules require their respective integrations.

### How much does AIORadar cost?

The saved pricing page lists Free plus Starter at $29, Growth at $59 and Agency at $149 monthly. Paid plans include monthly credits, and action limits also apply. Verify current pricing, taxes and terms before purchase.

### Are AIORadar Google integrations all read-only?

The documentation describes Search Console and GA4 as read-only reporting connections. It describes Business Profile as using a management scope that can apply explicitly approved listing edits, so inspect that authorization separately.
