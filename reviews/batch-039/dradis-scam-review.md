# Dradis Community Edition review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-24  
**Category:** Open-source security assessment reporting  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Dradis Community Edition is an open-source, self-hosted framework for security assessment collaboration and reporting. The project describes the Community Edition as free for one project at a time; paid Dradis plans add team collaboration, integrations, support and workflow features.

## Editorial verdict

Dradis Community Edition may suit a student or small assessment team that can operate a self-hosted reporting tool and work within its single-project limit. Consultancies handling several simultaneous engagements may need a paid tier with more projects, integrations and support. The product organizes findings and reports; it does not conduct a penetration test or independently validate findings. This review does not assess security-test quality or report customer results.

## Understand Dradis as a reporting and collaboration tool

Dradis Community Edition is an open-source framework for organizing security assessment findings and producing reports. It is not a scanner and does not independently decide whether a vulnerability is real. A tester still needs to gather evidence, validate each finding and apply professional judgment before a report is delivered.

The product may be useful when a small team wants a repeatable reporting structure and can operate its own server. Compare the workflow with the tools already used for issue tracking, evidence storage and report generation. The important question is whether it reduces duplicate work without weakening review of the underlying findings.


## Know the Community Edition boundary

Dradis describes the Community Edition as free and open source, with one project at a time. The site positions it for individuals and small groups; paid plans increase project capacity and add collaboration and integrations. For a consultancy with concurrent client work, the single-project boundary may quickly become the deciding limitation.

Because CE is self-hosted, the apparent zero license cost does not include server operation, backups, updates or staff time. Check the project’s installation requirements, supported versions and backup process. A small pilot can reveal whether the team is comfortable maintaining it in the environments where client findings are stored.


## Compare paid plans and minimum seat commitments

Dradis lists Assess at US$3,600 per year for a minimum of three seats and Remediate at US$7,200 per year, also starting at three seats. The pricing page describes additional reporting and integrations, with remediation tracking on Remediate. Enterprise is custom and begins at five seats.

A team should calculate the annual minimum rather than treating the per-seat monthly equivalent as a month-to-month purchase. Compare the plan’s integrations and support with the exact software your consultants use. Confirm any trial, refund guarantee, contract and renewal terms in writing before moving client work into a paid service.


## Protect client evidence in a self-hosted deployment

Security findings may reveal sensitive systems, credentials, network details or business weaknesses. For a self-hosted deployment, decide who can reach the server, how access is authenticated, where backups are stored and how the system is patched. Use separate client projects and verify permissions before sharing a report.

Dradis says Community Edition runs on infrastructure the user controls. That can support local or isolated environments, but the organization becomes responsible for maintaining that infrastructure. Validate backup restoration and access revocation with non-sensitive sample data before onboarding a client engagement.


## Review findings and reports before delivery

A structured reporting tool can improve consistency, but a template cannot replace technical review. Validate reproduction steps, evidence, severity, affected assets and remediation guidance. Separate confirmed findings from hypotheses and document any testing limitations in the final report.

Test an example project from data entry through export. Check whether the generated document preserves evidence and formatting, whether references remain clear and whether reviewers can trace changes. These are buyer checks, not proof that Roseram tested Dradis or that any specific report meets a client’s standard.


## Features and decision criteria

- Open-source self-hosted security-assessment reporting framework
- Community Edition is free and limited to one project at a time
- Paid plans add projects, team collaboration, integrations and support
- Deployment can run on organization-controlled infrastructure

## Pricing considerations

Dradis’s pricing page lists annual totals and a 30-day money-back guarantee for paid plans, plus a separate 14-day team trial option. Paid minimum seats increase the total commitment. Verify current terms, support, hosting requirements and plan contents before purchasing.
