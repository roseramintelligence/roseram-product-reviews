## Importing a menu is a draft-production step

The homepage describes importing from POS systems, PDFs, photos or an existing site. Those inputs may contain different levels of structure and accuracy. A clean product export, a photographed printed menu and an old webpage can disagree about prices, variants or availability. Identify the authoritative source before import and compare the resulting records with it. The ability to extract text does not establish that every item was interpreted correctly.

Use a small representative sample with modifiers, a special price and an unavailable item. These are proposed acceptance checks, not an import performed here. The homepage explicitly describes review and approval before publication, which is an important boundary to preserve. A fast first version should be treated as something to inspect rather than as a completed operational menu simply because the page can be generated quickly.


## Food-related tags need authoritative human verification

ARP.top advertises automatic detection of allergens, dietary categories, spice and nutrition-related information. Those are consequential claims, and a menu's short description may not contain all the information needed to classify a dish correctly. Do not infer suitability or absence of an ingredient from a generated label alone. The responsible business should verify published information against its authoritative ingredients and preparation information through an appropriate process.

This review does not provide dietary or medical advice and has not tested classification accuracy. It does not certify a dish or a menu for any individual. The source says the operator chooses which tags and filters to display; that control should support careful review rather than become a reason to publish every generated field by default. Software assistance and a verified statement about the actual food are separate milestones.


## Translation must preserve the operational meaning

The homepage describes multiple languages for administration and public menus, while the pricing limits the number of public languages by plan. Translation should preserve prices, modifiers, exclusions and important qualifications, not merely produce fluent promotional text. A dish name can carry cultural or ingredient meaning that requires more care than a generic sentence. Review the destination-language version with someone competent to assess the intended information.

These are proposed editorial checks, not translations generated here. The saved pages contain different broad language-count descriptions across sections, so confirm the actual plan and public-site configuration rather than assume the largest number applies everywhere. A multilingual interface is not proof that every generated description is accurate or appropriate. Keep operator language support, public language allowances and translation quality distinct in the buying decision.


## Generated dish images are illustrations that need an honest presentation

That may help establish a visual starting point, but a generated image should not misrepresent the portion, ingredients or appearance a customer will receive. Define the intended use and review the relationship between the image and actual offering before publishing it. Visual polish is not a substitute for truthful product representation.

Evaluate a small set against the real menu and the business's presentation standards. These are proposed checks, not images generated or inspected here. The saved source does not establish commercial output rights, photorealistic accuracy or a particular image-generation success rate. Obtain the applicable current terms where those details matter. The review does not invent an endorsement or assume a generated picture has been verified against a kitchen's actual work.


## POS integration needs an authoritative update direction

The homepage names Toast, Square and Clover and describes updating information once across connected views. That is a useful operational objective, but the business should confirm which system owns each field and how changes propagate. A price edited in the website and a different price maintained in the POS can create customer and staff confusion. The exact integration, supported records and error behavior need to be understood for the intended account.

A pilot should include an item change and an exception where the downstream system cannot accept the update. These are proposed acceptance scenarios, not POS calls made here. The source does not establish every connector entitlement or synchronization guarantee. A marketing statement about instant updates should not become a promise that every external dependency always agrees without delay or human reconciliation.


## Ordering and payment need separate confirmation states

ARP.top describes online ordering and payments, with notifications through messages or an intended POS route. An order being created is different from a kitchen accepting it or a payment completing. Define what the customer sees at each stage and who resolves an uncertain result. A confirmation page should not imply that every downstream action succeeded if the system is still waiting for a response.

Use an appropriate test workflow and inspect the operator and customer records together. These are proposed checks, not an order or transaction performed here. No communication was sent. The review does not certify payment processing, tax configuration or settlement. Obtain the actual current terms and implementation details before treating the platform's broad ordering capability as a completed operating procedure.

A menu import, image generation and translation may not consume the same amount. ## Analytics should distinguish interest from fulfilled orders

ARP.top describes measuring searches, engagement, orders and customer interest. Those observations have different meanings. A visitor filtering a menu or viewing an item is not necessarily a customer who placed, paid for and received an order. Define which event represents useful progress and preserve the distinction in reporting. Otherwise a favorable interaction count can be mistaken for a verified sales outcome.

The review has not inspected an analytics account or measured commercial uplift. A proposed evaluation should use the actual event definitions and a known sample, with suitable privacy and access choices. The saved pages do not establish every measurement or marketing integration detail. No contact was enrolled, message sent or consent inferred here.
