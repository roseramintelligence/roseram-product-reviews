# Skool review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-24  
**Category:** Community platform and course hosting  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Skool combines community discussion, classroom/course areas, live calls and paid memberships. Its official pricing FAQ describes a Hobby plan at US$9 per month with a 10% transaction fee and a Pro plan at US$99 per month with payment-processing fees of 2.9% plus US$0.30 for qualifying charges.

## Editorial verdict

Skool’s straightforward community-and-classroom format may suit a creator who wants to run a focused paid group without assembling a larger learning stack. The plan choice changes the fee structure: compare the monthly subscription plus transaction costs against your expected sales volume. A community tool cannot guarantee audience growth, engagement or income, and this review does not claim a hands-on product test.

## Understand the combined community and classroom model

Skool brings a group feed and a classroom area together. That can make a paid learning group easier to explain: members have a place to talk and a place to find course material. Before signing up, sketch the first week of a member’s experience and check that the product can support it without confusing people between discussion and lessons.

A compact platform is useful only if its structure matches how you teach. A library of self-paced lessons, cohort-based classes and a peer-support group each need different navigation and moderation. List the spaces, resources and recurring events you plan to offer, then compare those requirements with the current interface and plan limits.


## Do the fee math before choosing Hobby or Pro

Skool’s official pricing FAQ lists Hobby at US$9 monthly with a 10% transaction fee plus US$0.30, while Pro is listed at US$99 monthly with card charges of 2.9% plus US$0.30 for purchases up to US$899. The FAQ gives a different 3.9% plus US$0.30 rate for charges above US$900. These terms make gross sales volume an important part of the decision.

A simple break-even estimate can help: compare the extra US$90 monthly subscription cost of Pro with the difference between the two percentage fees on your expected processed sales. Then include fixed per-transaction charges, refunds, payment method mix and taxes. This calculation is a planning aid, not a prediction of sales. Verify the fee table and actual account terms before pricing a membership.


## Make the member offer specific

A community needs a clear reason to join beyond access to a chat. Describe who the group serves, what members can do, and how often the host will participate. Name the lessons, feedback or events a member receives, and be clear about what is not included. Avoid implying that membership alone produces a job, a business result or a particular income.

Try the offer with a small set of people who fit the audience. Ask whether they understand the value, what question remains before joining, and whether the stated price is clear. Use feedback to improve the offer rather than counting a waitlist as guaranteed revenue.


## Test moderation and learning workflows

Before a public launch, create a sample course and one discussion prompt. Check what a new member sees, how announcements appear, how a host removes spam and where a student finds the next lesson. Test from mobile and with an account that does not have administrator privileges.

Set community rules that explain acceptable promotion, privacy, refunds and where members can request support. Assign a responsible moderator if the group grows. These are recommended checks, not observations from a Roseram-run Skool account.


## Know what the product does not settle

Skool can host community and course activity, but it does not decide whether your topic has demand or whether members will remain active. The host still needs useful material, respectful facilitation, support and a plan for cancellations. Estimate those hours as part of the cost.

Confirm what data and content you can export if you leave, how cancellation works, and whether your course or community links will continue to function afterward. A successful test should include a member leaving as well as one joining. This review is based on Skool’s cited product and fee information, not independent verification of customer results.


## Features and decision criteria

- Community feed and member interaction
- Classroom area for courses and resources
- Live calls and paid memberships described in plan materials
- Subscription tiers use different platform-fee structures

## Pricing considerations

Skool’s FAQ says its 2.9% + US$0.30 Pro rate applies to charges up to US$899, while charges over US$900 are listed at 3.9% + US$0.30. Hobby is listed at 10% + US$0.30. Payment fees may be nonrefundable and other payment methods or regions can differ. Verify the fee schedule and billing currency at checkout.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does Skool cost?

Skool lists Hobby at US$9 per month and Pro at US$99 per month on its current pricing page; transaction fees differ.

### Is the Hobby plan really lower cost?

Its monthly fee is lower, but the official FAQ lists a 10% transaction fee plus US$0.30 per transaction. Compare total cost at your expected sales volume.

### Does Skool guarantee community income?

No. The software provides tools for a community and classroom; sales and retention depend on the offer, audience and ongoing work.
