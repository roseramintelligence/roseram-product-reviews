**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-18  
**Category:** Electronic signature and document workflow software  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Its pricing deserves close inspection: public materials list Business, Business Premium and Enterprise at $20/$30/$50 per month or $8/$15/$30 on annual billing, while newer help articles say paid plans include unlimited no-cost seats and allowances of 100 invites per user annually or 10 monthly. ## SignNow review: a document-signing tool with usage-based economics

SignNow, also presented as airSlate SignNow, is a cloud service for preparing documents, collecting electronic signatures and tracking the work around them. The official feature overview describes fillable fields, reusable templates, signer roles, signing links, document history and mobile access. The product is aimed at a familiar administrative problem: a document is ready, several people need to act on it, and the sender wants a record of who has completed the request. This makes SignNow a direct e-signature tool, not automatically a full contract-lifecycle platform or a legal review service.

The unusual part of the purchase decision is not the signature field; it is how invitations, users and billable usage fit together. The official plan overview and newer support articles do not use fully consistent language about per-user pricing and free seats. Help materials also define included invites and extra invite charges in detail. Those facts can materially change the cost for a busy team. A useful SignNow review therefore has to look at the document workflow and the billing mechanics together, rather than repeat the entry price as if it were the complete monthly bill.


## How SignNow prepares and sends documents for signature

The documented flow begins with an account and a document, then moves through preparation, signer assignment and an invitation. Users can add signature and fillable fields, mark information required or optional, and assign roles to people who will sign. SignNow also describes conditional fields, templates and signing links. These pieces suit recurring work such as order forms, client agreements or internal acknowledgements, where senders need the same basic structure each time but still need to enter transaction-specific details.

The way to evaluate this workflow is to map it to the organization’s real approval order. Create a test document with two signers, a date field and one required response. Check whether each person sees the right task, whether the first signer must complete before the second, and how the sender handles a correction. Then compare the final PDF to the original and inspect the history. A product can have the necessary fields yet still be a poor fit if staff cannot correct mistakes or explain the signing steps to a recipient.


## SignNow templates, roles and conditional form fields

Templates let a team reuse a prepared document instead of repositioning fields on every send. The publisher describes template creation and collaboration, assigned signer roles, fillable fields and conditions that control when some fields appear. For forms that repeat, those capabilities can reduce setup and make an agreement easier to complete. A signing link can also be useful when the sender does not want to enter an email address for every recipient, though the intended public or restricted access should be understood before a link is shared.

Test template permissions and revision control as carefully as the layout. Identify who can create, edit, share and retire a template, and what happens to requests already in progress if a template changes. Check whether conditional logic behaves correctly on mobile, whether a signer can attach a required file and whether field validation catches missing information. A reusable template is an operational control only when the current version is easy to identify and old copies are not accidentally used for new agreements.


## SignNow audit trail and document history

SignNow’s security materials describe a document history that records actions such as access, viewing, completion and signing, with details including names, email addresses, IP addresses and timestamps. The account guide describes downloading completed files with or without history and exporting to cloud storage. That can help an administrator keep the agreement and its process record together, but the organization still needs to decide where the authoritative copy lives and who is allowed to retrieve it.

Run a sample from the sender’s account to the completed export. Check whether the downloaded file includes the history you need, how it is named, and whether automatic export works for the storage provider your organization uses. Confirm what an administrator can see and whether the audit settings vary by plan. A history log is useful evidence about system activity; it does not by itself resolve a disagreement over authority to sign, the signer’s intent or the legal treatment of a particular document.


## SignNow invite limits: 100 yearly or 10 monthly per user

A SignNow Help Center article dated September 14, 2026 says paid plans include an annual allowance of 100 signature invites per user or a monthly allowance of 10 per user, depending on billing cadence. It says unused annual invites do not roll forward and describes allowance as shared across the account. One invite can contain multiple documents and recipients. Email and SMS invitations count when sent; a signing link counts after the recipient completes the required fields and clicks Finish. The rules mean that ‘100 invites’ is not 100 separate pages or signers.

Follow-up actions such as reminders, resends, forwarding and replacing a signer do not consume additional allowance under the same article, but cancelling an invite does not restore it; cancelling and re-sending can count twice. Separate daily sending limits also apply. Buyers should estimate invitation volume from real workflows, including cancelled or misaddressed requests, rather than assume that a user count and a document count are equivalent. Confirm whether the account’s pool is shared and how it behaves for multiple seats before planning a large rollout.


## SignNow pricing: list prices and a notable seat-billing mismatch

The official plan overview displayed Business at $20 per month or $8 on annual billing, Business Premium at $30 or $15, and Enterprise at $50 or $30. It also listed the Site License from $1.50 per signature invite. The page’s FAQ labels the subscription prices per user. Newer SignNow Help Center articles dated September 2026 instead say every paid self-serve plan includes unlimited free seats, while each person contributes to an account’s invite allowance. Both are publisher sources, but the difference is material: the actual bill depends on whether the static pricing label is current and how the account’s allowance and charges are configured.

Do not choose based on a monthly figure alone. Before checkout, confirm in writing whether the subscription price is per account or user, what invite allowance is included, whether usage is shared, which actions count, the overage price and how additional charges are triggered. The support article lists additional invite fees of $0.96 for Business, $1.80 for Business Premium and $3.60 for Enterprise. SignNow advertises its Site License from $1.50 per invite for higher volumes. For a team that sends more than its allowance, those marginal amounts may matter more than the headline plan rate.


## SignNow extra invite fees and usage-billing safeguards

The invite policy says extra requests can be billed automatically after the included allowance is used. It describes charges when a package of extra invites is completed and at month end when there are at least ten unpaid extra invites; fewer than ten unpaid invites do not trigger that particular month-end charge. The page says an administrator receives notices before and after a charge, including the total and tax. It also says a failed charge can temporarily block the Invite to Sign function until resolved. These rules are unusually consequential for budget forecasting and deserve a close read.

A cautious rollout would set an owner for usage monitoring, check the dashboard during the pilot and test the alert path before sending at scale. Forecast a normal month and a peak month, include resends that require cancelling and reissuing, and compare the overage scenario with a Site License quote. The existence of a warning email is not the same as a hard spending cap. Ask whether the account can restrict users, pause sends or set a maximum budget. Do not assume overage costs are refundable; SignNow’s refund policy says usage-based charges are non-refundable.


## SignNow Site License for higher-volume teams

SignNow positions its Site License as a usage-based option for larger organizations, advertising a starting rate of $1.50 per invite and volume discounts above 3,000 invites. Its comparison page says the package is intended for more than 1,000 invites per year and includes e-signature plus API access, while a separate enterprise plan is shown with a limit up to 1,000 invites. The Site License pitch emphasizes using the web application and API together without buying more seats. These are vendor descriptions; a buyer should obtain a tailored quote and verify which workflows are in scope.

A usage-based package can be easier to align with fluctuating demand than a user-based subscription, but only if the total projected invite cost is clear. Ask whether $1.50 is the applicable rate at your volume, how the discount tiers work, what counts as an invite and whether a cancelled request is billed. Confirm API limits, implementation costs, support and any minimum commitment. Compare the written quote against the self-serve plan plus likely overages using the same annual number of requests. Without that calculation, the ‘from’ price is a starting point rather than a forecast.


## SignNow trial and refund policy

The official product page advertises a seven-day free trial. SignNow’s help page says self-serve subscribers may request a refund within 30 days of the initial purchase, but the vendor may decline a request when the service has been used significantly. It also states that usage-based fees, including charges beyond the included invite limit, are non-refundable. Purchases made through sales, Apple’s App Store, Google Play or other marketplaces may have different refund and cancellation routes. A trial, refund window and usage charges are separate terms; do not treat them as one guarantee.

Before starting, record the trial end date and ask whether a payment method is required. For a paid purchase, save the confirmation, plan, renewal date and refund steps. Avoid sending production documents until the team has verified the workflow and data terms. If you evaluate high-volume sending during the refund period, remember that the policy may treat significant usage differently and that overage charges may be excluded. The review summarizes the publisher’s policy but does not predict the outcome of an individual refund request.


## SignNow security and compliance statements

SignNow says it encrypts data in transit with TLS 1.2/1.3 and stores data using AES-256. Its security page describes SOC 2 Type II, GDPR, HIPAA, PCI DSS, eIDAS and 21 CFR Part 11 materials, and mentions two-factor signer authentication, access settings and document history. These are statements made by the provider. They can help a procurement team identify the documents to request, but they do not establish that every plan has every control or that a customer’s use satisfies a regulatory obligation.

For sensitive workflows, request the current SOC report, scope, data-processing addendum, subprocessor list, retention commitments and any business associate agreement that applies. Verify whether authentication and session controls are available on the selected plan, and how the business can remove a user or recover access. Test account permissions using a member and administrator rather than assuming a default role is appropriate. No security audit was conducted for this review, and no security failure is alleged. The practical conclusion is to treat vendor claims as a starting point for evidence, configuration review and contractual diligence.


## SignNow privacy notice and uploaded-document handling

The SignNow Privacy Notice was updated October 13, 2026. It describes collection of account and payment details, uploaded documents, signatures, device and usage information, IP addresses and transactional information about document participants. It discusses retention, privacy rights and the use of service providers. Its scope distinguishes a person interacting directly with SignNow from an end user whose data is processed for an enterprise customer; for that situation, the customer’s own privacy notice and controller responsibilities also matter. A business sending documents should understand both its contract with SignNow and the notice shown to its recipients.

The notice says services involve transfers to the United States and references privacy frameworks and contractual safeguards for international transfers. Whether those terms meet a particular organization’s requirements depends on location, data category, account configuration and applicable law. Ask where records are processed, what the DPA covers and how deletion works at account closure. The notice also explains that a deletion request may not remove every record immediately when retention is required for legal or business reasons. This is a summary of the public document, not an independent privacy assessment or legal conclusion.


## SignNow integrations, cloud storage and mobile access

SignNow’s account guide describes connecting cloud storage such as Google Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive for Business, Box, Egnyte and DocuShare, then exporting completed documents manually or automatically. It also describes integrations that can let teams prepare, send, track and archive documents without switching tabs, while noting some connections require a quote or enablement. The product page presents iOS and Android apps and offline work. These integrations can reduce repetitive handling, but their availability and behavior depend on account permissions and plan configuration.

Use a test account to verify the exact route: import a sample file, prepare it, complete the signing flow and check where the final PDF and history land. Revoke an integration and see whether queued or completed files remain accessible. For mobile use, test screen size, offline recovery and notification behavior with the actual devices the team uses. If integration is critical, confirm which app is supported and what is bundled before purchase. A catalog of connected logos is not proof that a specific tenant’s setup will work without administration or paid enablement.


## SignNow payment fields and document workflows

SignNow’s help materials describe connecting a supported payment service, adding a payment field to a document or template, assigning it to a signer and collecting a specified amount and currency when the signing is completed. The document audit log is described as reflecting the payment event, while payment confirmations are handled by the payment provider. This can be relevant to order forms, service agreements or applications where payment belongs in the same recipient flow, but it also creates a dependency on the payment integration and its own terms.

Before using this for live charges, test a small amount in the intended provider environment and inspect a successful payment, a declined transaction, a changed amount and a cancellation. Confirm who handles receipts, refunds, disputes and payment data. Check whether the payment field is available on the selected SignNow plan and region. The e-signature vendor’s page does not replace the payment processor’s documentation or your finance team’s reconciliation controls. No payment integration was configured or tested for this review.

## SignNow limitations and purchase risks to check

The most material limit is the invite allowance and how extra activity is billed. Annual and monthly counts differ; unused invitations do not roll over; cancelled requests can consume an invite; and daily caps operate separately. The static pricing page and newer seat-policy articles also use different language about per-user billing. A buyer who overlooks these details may budget from the wrong number. Other considerations include a short seven-day trial, conditions on the refund policy, add-on availability, integration enablement and the need to preserve records before closing an account.

Operationally, electronic signing covers one part of an agreement process. It may not include the negotiation, clause governance, document drafting or policy approval a larger legal team expects. Security and compliance claims need current supporting materials and a configuration review. None of these issues establishes that the service is dishonest; they are terms and boundaries that affect suitability. Ask support or sales to resolve the pricing mismatch in writing before committing, and keep the response with the order. If the answer is unclear, compare another provider using your same volume and acceptance criteria.

If the team is small, an included-user structure may be more useful than a large feature catalog.

For each candidate, record how many people can send, how invites are counted, what happens after cancellation, which advanced fields and authentication steps are included, and whether the final record exports with its audit history. Check privacy terms and support routes as well. Capterra’s SignNow alternatives page can help build a shortlist, but current official provider terms should settle price and functionality. An alternative is suitable only when it matches your requirements at a known cost; the highest rating or lowest advertised starting price is not enough.


## How to evaluate SignNow before a subscription

Use a sanitized document that resembles a normal transaction. Add two signers and a required field, create a template, send one email invitation and one signing link, then finish the workflow on web and mobile. Download the document with its history and export it to the storage system your organization uses. Try a missing field, a replaced signer and a cancelled request. This proposed test will surface workflow issues and clarify which actions consume an invite. It is not a test Roseram performed.

In parallel, build a cost model: expected invites per month and year, unused allowance, likely cancellations, overage pricing, and whether the Site License quote is more predictable. Ask SignNow to clarify whether the base amount is per user or account, and how added members share the pool. Set a spend owner and usage alert before inviting a whole department. Check refund timing and cancellation responsibilities. A successful test is not only a signature that completes; it is a repeatable process the organization can administer, export and stop without losing control of its records.


## Who should consider SignNow?

SignNow may suit individuals and teams that need repeatable document signatures, flexible fields, signer roles, status tracking and storage connections. Teams that want to add users without a separate seat charge may find its newer account model interesting, provided the shared invite allowance and overages fit their volume. Small organizations can start with a trial and a clear workflow, while high-volume senders can request a Site License quote and compare it with self-serve fees. The fit depends on the organization’s exact billing terms, not solely on the vendor’s broad positioning.

Organizations with complex contract negotiations, strict regional data controls, specialized identity assurance or detailed legal review workflows should compare dedicated contract-lifecycle or regulated-process products as well. Ask for evidence that a selected plan supports the controls, and involve IT or counsel where appropriate. If the pricing model is still unclear, that is a reason to pause rather than guess. Choose SignNow when it demonstrably completes the required process with transparent invite economics, usable records and acceptable support—not simply because a trial begins quickly.

## Final verdict: verify the invite math before choosing SignNow

SignNow has a broad practical e-signature toolkit: templates, fields, roles, document history, mobile access and integrations. Its help materials explain invite usage and overage billing unusually clearly, which helps buyers model the actual workflow. At the same time, the public plan page and newer seat guidance do not align perfectly on whether pricing is per user or whether seats are free. That discrepancy must be resolved against the exact checkout before the advertised rate can be treated as a dependable budget.
