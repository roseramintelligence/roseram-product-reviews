# Grammarly review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-18  
**Category:** Writing assistance and generative AI software  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Grammarly provides writing suggestions across supported apps and websites, with grammar, tone, rewriting, plagiarism and generative AI features that vary by plan. The current plan page lists Free with 100 AI prompts per month and Pro with 2,000 prompts per member per month; support lists Pro at $30 monthly, $60 quarterly or $144 annually per member. Subscriptions auto-renew unless canceled. Grammarly documents an account-level Product Improvement and Training control for eligible individual accounts, and says third-party LLM providers are not allowed to train on user content.

## Editorial verdict

The reviewed official materials do not substantiate a scam allegation. Grammarly documents plan differences, recurring billing, cancellation, privacy choices and AI usage controls. Buyers should check the current account price and prompt allowance, understand that paid plans renew automatically, and set the content-training preference to match their privacy expectations. For sensitive material, review the product’s access scope and organization terms before enabling it in a browser, desktop or document workflow.

## Grammarly Free and Pro feature differences

The current plans page separates Free, Pro and Enterprise. Its comparison lists core writing help and 100 AI prompts per month on Free, while Pro adds sentence rewrites, tone controls, plagiarism checking, AI-detection features and 2,000 AI prompts per member per month. Enterprise is sold through a sales process and adds organization-level controls such as managed roles and data-loss prevention. Features and AI allowances can change, so the account’s live comparison is the best confirmation.

A prompt allowance is not the same as unrestricted use of every AI feature, and an AI detector is not proof that a text was or was not generated by a person. Review the specific feature’s limits and output before relying on it for academic, legal or editorial decisions. For a team, compare the individual Pro plan with the Enterprise offer and verify which privacy and administration controls apply to the contract.


## Grammarly Pro pricing and per-member costs

Grammarly Support lists Pro at US$30 per member per month, US$60 per member for three months, or US$144 per member per year, which it describes as a US$12 monthly equivalent when billed annually. The same page says the plan can support up to 149 seats and additional seats are charged at the applicable rate, prorated for the remaining period. Regional currency options are shown at checkout.

Compare the full annual outlay rather than the monthly equivalent alone. For a single user, an annual plan requires a larger upfront payment than one month of service; for a team, each additional member affects the total. Promotions may last only for one subscription term and can renew at the standard rate. Taxes and regional rates may differ. Record the exact charge and renewal price shown before confirming purchase.


## Automatic renewal and cancellation

Grammarly’s billing policy says paid subscriptions renew automatically at the standard rate at the end of the current term unless canceled. The cancellation guide says cancellation stops automatic renewal while paid features remain available through the end of the current subscription period. Users generally cancel in the subscription settings, but purchases through an app store may need to be canceled through that store.

Set a reminder before a trial or discounted period ends and check which email account owns the subscription. Grammarly’s support page says promotions apply for one subscription period and renewal is at the standard rate. Its terms state that payments are generally non-refundable to the fullest extent permitted by applicable law, while its billing support says it can review a report of a mistaken charge. Verify the current terms and local consumer rights rather than assuming a cancellation automatically refunds unused time.


## AI prompts, output limits and responsible use

Grammarly’s Terms say generative AI access and usage limits depend on the subscription plan. If a user exceeds an allowance, the company may require an additional purchase or units to continue. It also cautions that outputs may be similar across users and that users are responsible for their inputs and outputs. These conditions make human review important, particularly when generated text will make factual, legal or business commitments.

Use the tool to improve clarity while preserving ownership of the ideas and verifying factual claims. Review names, figures, citations, tone and any suggested rewrite before publishing or sending. Do not paste confidential data into an AI prompt unless the organization has approved that workflow and the terms allow it. An AI suggestion is a draft, not an authoritative source or a guarantee of correctness.


## Content privacy and the training control

Grammarly’s support FAQ says users can manage whether content is used for product improvement and training through the Product Improvement and Training control. It states that this may include sampled, aggregated and de-identified content unless a user has opted out. The separate control page describes the setting for individual Free, Premium and single-user Pro accounts and says qualifying multi-user or sales-purchased organization plans have it automatically off by default.

The FAQ also says language-model service providers that receive information for generative AI features are not allowed to train their models on user content. That is distinct from Grammarly’s own product-improvement setting. Check the current privacy page and account setting, and determine how the product operates in a browser, desktop app or document editor. An organization account may be governed by a business agreement rather than the consumer policy.


## What Grammarly stores and where content may be processed

Grammarly’s privacy FAQ says documents created in Grammarly Editor remain stored until a user deletes them or requests deletion after a contract ends. It describes other processed text as being handled as needed to provide and administer the service. The same page says stored data is hosted on AWS in the United States, describes encryption in transit and at rest, and explains that access to content is restricted and may require permission for troubleshooting.

The exact data path depends on the product surface and permissions granted to connected apps. Review browser-extension access, document integrations and AI prompts before enabling the service in a workspace that holds sensitive information. For company use, evaluate the organization’s data-processing terms, retention settings, security materials and administrative controls instead of assuming that individual-account terms apply.


## Browser, desktop and document integrations

Grammarly offers browser extensions, desktop products and connections for document and productivity tools. That reach makes suggestions available in many writing contexts, but it also means the user should understand which surfaces are active and what text they can process. The privacy FAQ says users can control where Grammarly operates, which can affect the amount of data it handles.

Start with the smallest workflow: enable one surface, confirm what text appears in the editor and test a non-sensitive document. Review the permissions requested by integrations and remove apps that are no longer needed. For a managed device, ask an administrator which components are installed and what retention or product-improvement settings have been selected. Convenience across apps should not erase the user’s awareness of where text is being processed.


## AI detection and plagiarism features need human interpretation

The plans page lists plagiarism and AI-detection features on Pro, but the page does not make those features a substitute for source verification or an editorial decision. A detector can be useful as one signal, yet a score is not proof of authorship. A similarity checker can flag overlap, but the writer still needs to inspect citations, quoted material and source context.

If a school, publisher or employer has a policy for AI tools, follow that policy and disclose assistance where required. Do not use a detector output to make a serious accusation without independent evidence. The product’s Terms also caution that generated outputs may not be factually accurate or suitable for every purpose. The user remains responsible for the final text.


## Alternatives and a meaningful comparison

Compare Grammarly with built-in word-processor editing, open-source spell checking, style tools and AI writing products using the same draft and privacy requirements. Consider supported languages, grammar quality, sentence rewrites, citation needs, AI allowance, team controls and deployment surfaces. Include the time spent checking suggestions and the subscription renewal amount in the comparison.

Grammarly may suit someone who wants inline writing guidance in many apps. A user who only needs spelling corrections may already have enough in a document editor, while a company handling regulated text may require tighter contract and deployment controls. Try the intended workflow with non-sensitive content before paying, and do not treat advertised accuracy or a feature list as an independent performance benchmark.


## Who should consider Grammarly

Grammarly can be useful to writers who want ongoing spelling, grammar and tone suggestions while working across supported applications. Pro may make sense when the user consistently needs full-sentence rewrites, plagiarism checks or more AI prompts. Enterprise buyers should assess team administration, privacy, security and data-loss-prevention needs through the organization’s contract and plan.

It may be a poor fit where the main concern is confidential text that cannot be processed by a third party, or where a team cannot govern prompt use and content settings. A free plan can help someone decide whether the corrections are useful, but its AI prompt limit should be considered before a workflow depends on generation. Choose based on specific writing tasks, not a generalized promise to make every message perfect.


## Grammarly buyer checklist before subscribing

Confirm whether the plan is Free, individual Pro or an organization purchase. Check the included prompt allowance, seats, current price, currency, taxes, billing term and renewal amount. Locate the Product Improvement and Training preference, read the relevant privacy terms, and identify the browser, desktop or document integrations that will be enabled. If a team account is involved, determine whether an administrator controls the settings.

Try representative non-sensitive text, verify suggestions and inspect the cancellation process. Set a reminder before the renewal date, especially for a trial or promotion. For high-stakes writing, require a person to review factual claims, citations and legal commitments. Recheck the current support and terms pages before purchasing because pricing, brand structure, prompt limits and settings can change.
