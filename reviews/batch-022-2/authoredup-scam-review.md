# AuthoredUp review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-10-29  
**Category:** LinkedIn content creation, scheduling and analytics  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

AuthoredUp describes a LinkedIn editor, draft library, calendar, scheduling and analytics for individuals and teams. This review examines account access, publishing mechanics, plan limits, data interpretation, collaboration and buyer testing.

## Editorial verdict

This desk review does not establish that AuthoredUp is a scam or certify the business as safe. The cited publisher material describes an offer that can be evaluated against your requirements. Before paying, verify the seller, the current terms, any cancellation rules and the capabilities that matter to your use case. The discussion below separates publisher claims from suggested buyer checks; it does not report hands-on testing or an independent security audit.

## What this AuthoredUp review covers

AuthoredUp describes a LinkedIn-focused content workspace with a post editor, drafts, calendar, profile and company-page support, analytics, saved posts and reusable writing elements. This review examines how those pieces fit together, the plan and profile limits in the captured pricing page, and what a buyer should verify about publishing, data, browser access and performance measurement. It does not establish that AuthoredUp is a scam, certify it as safe or report a hands-on test.

The evidence comes from saved publisher pages for the product, pricing, dashboard, editor and drafts. It supports a detailed description of advertised workflows. It does not independently verify that the browser extension works on every account, that analytics are complete, that a schedule always publishes, or that a writing aid increases reach. Statements about endorsements and user experiences are publisher-presented testimonials and have not been independently authenticated in this review.

Social publishing tools sit between a user's content and a separate platform. That makes the mechanics important. A buyer should understand exactly how content is stored, scheduled and posted, what permissions are requested, what happens if the browser is offline or LinkedIn changes its interface, and whether the workflow complies with the current platform rules. The captured marketing pages do not answer every implementation or account-specific question.


## The product's intended audience and scope

AuthoredUp positions itself as a LinkedIn content creation and analytics tool for individuals, teams and businesses. The pages describe drafting, formatting, previewing, storing, scheduling and reviewing posts in one place. Some copy describes a Chrome extension and the ability to work within LinkedIn; other descriptions show a central dashboard and calendar. A buyer should determine which interface handles each action and whether desktop, mobile and company-page workflows differ.

The product appears centered on LinkedIn rather than a general multi-network publisher. That narrower scope can be useful for a person who wants dedicated writing and analytics features for one professional network. It also means a team needing Instagram, Facebook, TikTok or cross-channel scheduling should not assume those channels are included. Confirm current integrations and supported account types from the live product before using AuthoredUp as a replacement for a broader social-media suite.

The publisher's examples highlight post writing, saved content, performance review and reuse. That feature combination can support a repeatable editorial process, but it does not decide a brand's claims, audience or publishing strategy. A user remains responsible for accuracy, rights to included media, disclosures, tone and audience suitability. Draft assistance and formatting are tools; they are not editorial approval or a guarantee of results.


## Editor, formatting and preview

The editor page describes rich text styling, bullets, numbering, emoji, readability scoring, word counts and previews across devices. Those facilities can help users inspect a post before publishing. They should be tested against the actual LinkedIn composer and feed, especially where formatting characters, links, images or line breaks may be transformed. A preview within a third-party editor is an approximation unless the publisher demonstrates and documents that it matches the live rendering.

Use several representative post types during evaluation: a short announcement, a longer story, a list, a post with a link and an image, and content with special characters. Compare what is drafted, what is shown in the preview and what appears on the intended profile or page. Check whether pasted text retains formatting and whether an edit made after scheduling changes the queued version. This review performed none of those actions.

The publisher describes hooks, endings and snippets as reusable aids. A library of prompts or templates can speed up blank-page work, but a template does not establish that a hook is truthful, audience-specific or appropriate for the topic. Review generated or suggested wording for unsupported facts and repetitive phrasing. Teams should create an approval rule for regulated or sensitive subjects and preserve source links for factual claims.


## Drafts, organization and collaboration

The drafts page describes capturing ideas, attaching images, PDFs or links, and finding drafts by keyword, tag, profile or page, status or date. It also describes draft history and the ability to continue, duplicate or remove an item. These details suggest a useful content library, but the buyer should test how attachments are stored, who can see them and what the export or deletion path looks like.

The page also describes assigning drafts to profiles or pages and collaborating with teammates. Before placing client or company material in a shared account, establish workspace roles, author permissions, review steps and offboarding behavior. Confirm whether a collaborator can publish directly or only edit, whether an administrator can see pending work and whether history identifies who made a change. The marketing capture does not provide enough detail to certify these controls.

A well-organized draft list can reduce duplicate work, but it can also become a second content repository. Agree on naming, ownership, retention and the source of truth for approved text. Test whether removing a draft also removes attachments, whether deleted content is recoverable, and how the system distinguishes an unpublished idea from a post already live on LinkedIn. These operational details matter for a team with review or recordkeeping requirements.


## Calendar, reminders and scheduled posts

AuthoredUp's product pages describe a calendar showing published and scheduled posts, planning by date and scheduling content. The drafts page describes planning a draft and scheduling it for a selected time. This is a substantive capability, yet the publisher also uses the phrase “No automation. No cookies” elsewhere in its product material. A buyer should ask what that phrase means in technical terms and how a scheduled post is delivered.

Specifically, determine whether scheduled publication requires a browser session or extension to be active, whether it uses a documented platform connection, what happens if a device is offline, whether a reminder is shown for a user to publish manually, and which actions are available for company pages. The saved pages describe scheduling but do not establish those implementation details. Do not rely on a campaign calendar until a low-risk scheduled post has been observed through the complete path.

Test time zones, daylight-saving changes, edit-after-schedule behavior, failed-post notifications and duplicate prevention. Confirm whether the calendar is a record of what AuthoredUp intends to publish or a confirmed record of what LinkedIn accepted. These are different states. For important announcements, keep a human check of the actual live post and retain a fallback method if the scheduled item does not appear.


## Analytics and interpretation

The dashboard page describes tracking follower growth, posting consistency, audience activity, impressions and engagement over time. The broader product page says users can review individual posts and filter by date or content type. These measures can help organize a retrospective. Their meaning depends on what LinkedIn makes available, the account's history and the definitions used for each metric.

An increase in impressions or interactions does not by itself prove that a writing tool caused the change. Audience size, topic, timing, platform distribution and external events may all contribute. Treat the dashboard as descriptive analytics unless a controlled method establishes a stronger conclusion. Compare similar periods, record changes in posting cadence and avoid claiming causal impact from a simple before-and-after chart.

Ask whether the system can export raw results, how historical data are refreshed, how deleted or edited posts appear, and whether company-page and personal-profile metrics are reported consistently. Verify the data against LinkedIn's own interface for a sample period. The saved materials describe overview and filtering features but do not define every metric, data delay or API limitation for each account.


## Pricing and profile limits in the saved capture

The captured pricing page lists an Individual plan for one profile at $19.95 per month and a Business plan starting at three or more profiles at $14.95 per profile per month. It also lists annual billing examples of $199.50 per year for Individual and $149.50 per profile per year for Business, displayed as monthly equivalents. These are saved-page values, not a current quote. Confirm currency, tax, renewal and whether the displayed annual amount is charged upfront.

The same page associates the Individual plan with an editor, extension and platform access, drafts, snippets, company pages, hooks, post reuse and content analytics. The Business plan adds multiple profiles, subaccounts, organization management, team analytics and draft collaboration, with a three-profile minimum in the capture. Verify whether “profile” means a personal profile, a company page or a connected account, and whether a company page consumes a separate seat or profile allowance.

The publisher also describes a custom Growth plan with onboarding and training and a minimum of ten profiles. Ask for the actual scope and quote if that tier is relevant. Confirm trial duration, whether a card is required, cancellation timing, refund conditions and what happens to stored drafts and analytics after cancellation. The capture says “cancel anytime” and “no credit card required” for the trial, but a buyer should confirm the current checkout flow and contract.


## Browser extension and account access

The product material refers to a Chrome extension and says the platform operates without cookies or automation. Those phrases need a precise explanation because the described product also includes scheduled posts and direct in-platform workflows. Ask which data are stored locally, which are sent to AuthoredUp, whether the extension reads page content, what account permissions are requested and how publishing interacts with LinkedIn.

Install only through the publisher's verified distribution route and inspect the requested browser permissions. Review the current privacy policy, data processing terms and extension update history. If the tool accesses a professional account, use an organization-approved account and named user rather than sharing credentials. Confirm how to revoke access, remove the extension and delete associated data when a team member leaves.

Also review LinkedIn's current terms for the particular publishing method. This review does not determine platform compliance. A vendor statement that there are no cookies or automation does not substitute for understanding the actual behavior of the installed extension or scheduled-post mechanism. Ask AuthoredUp for written technical documentation if this issue affects account risk or company policy.


## Privacy, security and content rights

The saved product pages are not a security certification or complete privacy disclosure. Before adding unpublished strategy, client work or personal information, request details on encryption, access controls, retention, backups, subprocessors, incident response and deletion. Confirm where drafts and attachments are processed and whether content is used to improve any model or service. The public product description alone cannot answer those questions.

Review the ownership and licensing rules for posts and attachments. The buyer should confirm responsibility for rights to images, documents, quotes and third-party materials. For teams, define who can reuse a post and whether saved posts include content from other users. A tool that stores ideas or supports reuse does not grant rights to publish material owned by someone else.

For regulated communications, create an approval and retention policy before connecting accounts. Decide who reviews claims, who can schedule, who can publish and how corrections are recorded. Test the deletion and export path with non-sensitive content. Keep the result of this review distinct from an independent security test, which was not performed here.


## Templates, hooks and content quality

AuthoredUp describes libraries of hooks, endings and snippets, plus readability feedback. This can make it easier to structure regular posts, particularly for users who want a consistent format. The risk is that repeated templates can make different ideas sound interchangeable or encourage a promise that the body does not support. Treat suggestions as editable prompts and check that the opening accurately represents the content that follows.

Readability scores are signals from a particular method, not a universal measure of clarity or credibility. A post for a technical audience may need domain language that a simple score penalizes. A shorter sentence is not automatically more informative. Use previews and scores to find possible friction, then review whether the post serves the audience and preserves the intended meaning.

The editor can support a disciplined workflow: keep the source, create a draft, have a subject-matter reviewer check it, preview it, schedule or publish, then inspect the result. That process is more important than a large template library. For each reusable snippet, set an owner and review date so outdated offers, links or claims do not continue appearing in new posts.


## Who may benefit and what to test

AuthoredUp may suit individuals who publish frequently on LinkedIn and want a dedicated editor, draft library and analytics view. Agencies and teams may value shared drafts, multiple profiles and company-page workflows if the business plan's minimum and collaboration tools fit their roster. Users who need broad social-network support or automated multi-channel campaigns should confirm whether a separate product is still required.

Before subscribing, test the actual account types you use. Draft a representative post, compare the preview with LinkedIn, invite one collaborator, assign a draft, schedule a low-risk item and inspect the resulting status. Check whether the item requires a reminder, a browser session or another action. Compare a sample analytics window with LinkedIn's own reporting and export the data you would need for your regular report.

Price the plan against profiles rather than individual seats alone. Include the minimum number of business profiles, annual billing, taxes and the effort required to review content. If an essential capability depends on a Chrome extension, confirm the browser and device requirements. Ask for written answers about publishing mechanics, privacy, data retention and account permissions before connecting an organizational profile.
