# Tally.so review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-02  
**Category:** Online forms & business intake workflows  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Tally is a document-style online form builder with a free offering and paid customization and administration features. This review examines conditional questions, response handling, billing periods, integrations and the work required to turn a submitted form into a dependable business process.

## What a document-style form builder can simplify

Tally presents a form-creation experience that resembles editing a document. Its publisher describes conditional questions and response-related features across a free and paid offer. This is relevant when the person closest to a business process needs to change questions without waiting for a software release. It does not remove the need to decide what information belongs in the process. A form asking for everything the organization might someday want can be easy to build and still difficult for a visitor to finish.

Begin with the decision the submission enables. A project enquiry may need a contact method, a short description and a rough timeframe; the detailed brief can follow after someone confirms fit. An event registration may need different information depending on attendance type. Write the minimum useful response first, then add conditional follow-up only when it changes an actual next step. This approach evaluates the form builder against a practical objective: collecting enough accurate information to move work forward without making the visitor perform unnecessary administration.


## Read the free offer alongside its fair-use boundaries

The publisher advertises a broad free offer with a fair-use qualification. Treat the qualification as part of the offer rather than dropping it when comparing products. A small internal enquiry form and a public campaign receiving bursts of activity are different operating contexts. Before deciding that an allowance is sufficient, identify the likely volume, attachment use and connected services. A marketing phrase about submissions does not establish that every surrounding email, storage, payment-processing or automation cost is included at no charge.

For an evaluation, separate features you need today from attractive possibilities. Build a small representative form and record which requirement leads you to consider an upgrade. That might be presentation control, administration or a specific response-handling feature. Paying is easier to justify when the paid capability removes a real constraint; staying free is easier to justify when the tested workflow already works within the applicable terms. Neither outcome should depend on treating an advertised unlimited allowance as permission to ignore the published policy or your own operational capacity.


## Tally pricing: monthly subscriptions and annual equivalents differ

The checked Pro help page lists $29 per month or $290 per year, while the Business help page lists $89 per month or $890 per year. The main pricing view also displays rounded lower monthly figures alongside a yearly selector. Do not compare an annual monthly equivalent with a competitor’s month-to-month bill as though the commitments were identical. This review records those publisher observations without converting them into a personalized checkout quote. Confirm the current currency, tax treatment, billing period and selected account offer before paying.

Put the annual cash commitment and the monthly alternative on separate lines in your own budget. Then include the people and connected services needed to handle responses. A form subscription can be a modest part of the complete process if every submission creates manual cleanup elsewhere. Evaluate cost against correctly handled enquiries, registrations or requests rather than the number of forms an editor can produce. The best purchase is the one that resolves your actual constraint at an understood total cost, with a renewal owner and a clear reason for the selected billing period.


## Conditional logic should survive changed answers and missing inputs

Use a simple branching scenario for the first test. A visitor selects one service, sees a relevant follow-up, then changes the original choice. Inspect whether the final submission contains the information the reviewer expects and whether an earlier answer needs to be cleared or explained. Test the empty path as well as the complete one. An editor preview showing the intended branch is useful, but the real acceptance check is the saved response that reaches the person responsible for acting on it.

Keep a *written map* of important branches even when the visual editor makes them easy to change. The map should explain why a field is required and which decision it supports. If a colleague adds another service option later, the map helps identify the path that also needs updating. This review does not claim to have found a Tally branching defect. It proposes a controlled way to check the form you build, especially when a small wording change could alter who sees a question or whether a valid response can be completed.


## Minimize respondent effort without hiding important choices

A good intake flow asks for the next useful piece of information at a time the visitor understands. Use recognizable labels, explain unusual requests briefly and avoid making an optional detail feel mandatory. Test the form on a narrow mobile screen with the same keyboard and scrolling constraints a visitor will face. A desktop preview cannot establish that a long dropdown, attachment control or validation message is comfortable to use on a phone. Ask someone unfamiliar with the form to explain what they believe will happen after submitting.

Keep confirmation language proportionate to the actual process. A saved enquiry is not necessarily an approved application, a reserved appointment or an accepted order. If someone must review the response, say that in the completion step and identify a reasonable route for follow-up. The interface should reduce uncertainty rather than make the visitor infer an entire business policy from a cheerful success screen. These are design decisions the organization owns, whether it uses a hosted form builder or develops an original intake application.


## File uploads, private answers and access need a lifecycle

Before collecting attachments or detailed personal information, define why each item is needed, who can open it and when it should be removed. Start development with synthetic files. Do not use a live customer document simply because it is convenient for a demonstration. The settings required for a particular workflow should be checked against the current product documentation and your organization’s obligations. A general privacy statement is not an independent certification that every possible form someone creates is appropriate for every category of information.

Also consider copies outside the form system. An email attachment, exported spreadsheet or connected destination may retain information after the original response changes. Map those locations and assign an owner to each. If the team cannot explain where a sensitive answer goes, reduce the data collected until that question is resolved. Retention controls can support a policy, but the policy must include the wider process. The useful evaluation asks whether the organization can find and handle the necessary record throughout its lifecycle without granting broader access than the task requires.


## A webhook delivery is not the same as a completed handoff

When submissions feed another application, identify the authoritative record and the signal that the handoff completed. A form may save a response while a later notification or import fails. Design a review queue that lets an operator see the difference. During an authorized integration test, use a harmless submission and confirm where it appears downstream, which fields map to it and how the responsible person knows it arrived. A connected-service logo alone does not verify your specific field mapping or retry behavior.

Test a duplicate delivery and an unavailable destination before enabling unattended operations. The application should have a stable way to recognize the same request rather than creating a second customer, ticket or booking each time a retry occurs. Keep correction and replay actions visible to the operator. These are proposed integration checks, not a claim that Roseram tested every Tally connector. A reliable handoff is an observable business state, with enough context for someone to repair an exception without guessing whether the original visitor needs to submit again.


## Payment collection needs a separate order-state decision

If your form is intended to collect payment, decide which system confirms the transaction and which record controls fulfillment. A visitor reaching a thank-you page should not be the sole evidence that a payment settled. Use the supported test environment for the payment provider and inspect successful, canceled and failed cases. Keep the amount, currency and purchased item clear before confirmation. This article does not perform a transaction or claim that any plan replaces a payment processor’s account, fees, refund process or settlement requirements.

Make the delivery promise explicit as well. A paid consultation, a downloadable item and a custom order require different next steps. Assign a person or verified workflow to that step, and provide a way to reconcile it with the transaction record. An attractive form can be a useful front door while the order process behind it still needs work. Evaluate both parts before promoting the form to customers. If you cannot explain what happens after an interrupted payment or a later refund, keep the payment flow in testing until the explanation is supported by evidence.

## Tally form-builder decision: evaluate the saved outcome

Before choosing a plan, ask whether an unfamiliar person can complete the intended task with only the information actually required. Check the saved record after changed answers, a failed submission and a repeated attempt. Confirm that the right staff can review it and that downstream actions are visible rather than assumed. Read the selected billing period and assign renewal ownership. Those answers provide a defensible basis for deciding whether a hosted form builder fits or whether the business needs a more specific application around its intake process.
