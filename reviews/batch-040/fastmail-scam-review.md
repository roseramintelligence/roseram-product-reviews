# Fastmail review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-24  
**Category:** Private email and productivity suite  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Fastmail is a paid email, calendar and contacts service with personal and business plans. Its US pricing page lists Individual at US$6 monthly or US$5 per month when billed yearly, while business tiers start at US$4 per user monthly for Basic, US$6 for Standard and US$10 for Professional on the monthly display.

## Editorial verdict

Fastmail is an option for people and organizations looking for paid email with custom domains, calendars, contacts and administrative features. It is not a free mailbox provider, and business plans vary in storage and retention. Compare migration, domain setup, shared addresses, user administration, data location and the renewal amount before switching an established business inbox.

## Decide whether paid email fits your needs

Fastmail bundles email with calendars and contacts, and its business plans support custom domains and shared addresses. A paid mailbox can be appealing when an organization wants a branded address and a defined set of productivity features without advertising. Begin by listing which functions your team currently uses and where the associated data lives.

Check whether you need one person’s private mailbox, shared support addresses or centrally managed employee accounts. A plan designed for a family or individual does not necessarily include business administration. If a company needs user provisioning, shared calendars or retention, compare the exact business tiers.


## Compare monthly and annual amounts accurately

Fastmail’s US pricing page lists Individual at US$6 monthly or US$5 per month billed yearly. It lists Business Basic at US$4 per user monthly, Standard at US$6 and Professional at US$10 in the monthly display, with lower annual effective rates. The page says prices exclude tax.

Calculate the bill for the actual number of accounts and the billing period you intend to choose. Include domain registration, any required migration service and storage needs. Do not compare the annual effective rate with a month-to-month competitor price without accounting for the upfront commitment and cancellation terms.


## Plan a careful email and domain migration

Email migration can affect historical messages, contacts, calendars, aliases, filters and customer replies. Make a list of every address and domain before changing DNS. Identify mailboxes that must remain available, notify people who send to shared addresses and keep the old service active until delivery is confirmed.

Test a small mailbox first. Verify inbound and outbound mail, spam handling, aliases, mobile setup, calendar invitations and password recovery. Confirm how long it takes for DNS records to propagate and keep a rollback plan. A marketing page’s migration language is not proof that every legacy configuration will transfer automatically.


## Review storage, privacy and administration

Fastmail’s pricing page distinguishes storage and administration by plan. Business Basic lists 6 GB per user, Standard 60 GB and Professional 150 GB, while Professional also lists an email retention archive. Confirm whether shared inboxes count as users and what happens when an employee leaves.

Read the current privacy and service terms to understand data residency choices, encryption descriptions and retention behavior. “Private” is a broad product promise; it should not be mistaken for end-to-end encryption of every mail exchange or a substitute for an organization’s own security controls.


## Use the trial to validate daily operations

A trial of up to 30 days gives time to check the web and mobile apps with a real but limited workflow. Test the tasks your team performs daily: search, rules, scheduled send, calendar sharing, file attachments and support-address access. Confirm which features are available on the plan you expect to buy.

Before the trial ends, verify the renewal amount, billing interval and cancellation route. Export or retain copies of messages and contact records according to your organization’s policy.
