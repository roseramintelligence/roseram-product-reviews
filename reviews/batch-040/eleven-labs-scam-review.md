# Eleven Labs review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-18  
**Category:** AI voice, speech and audio production  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

ElevenLabs is a generative audio company with text-to-speech, speech-to-text, sound effects, music, dubbing, voice design, image and video tools, and developer APIs. Its products share credit allowances, while the cost of a task varies by model and operation. Its free plan is limited to non-commercial use with attribution; paid plans include commercial rights subject to the applicable terms. Voice cloning has Instant and Professional paths and includes identity safeguards, so buyers should consider consent, privacy, licensing and the exact workflow as carefully as output quality.

## Editorial verdict

This source-based review does not make a fraud allegation or certify audio quality. ElevenLabs publishes a product catalog, plan comparison, billing guide, voice-cloning documentation and privacy terms. Those sources support an evaluation of advertised features and stated limits, not a claim that Roseram generated or independently tested audio. Before relying on a clone or generated track, verify that the speaker and source material are authorized, that the selected plan grants the rights you need, and that the output meets your use case.

## ElevenLabs is an audio suite with a shared usage budget

The product range extends beyond a single text-to-speech box. The publisher groups speech generation, transcription, voice design and cloning, sound effects, music, dubbing and studio production, with image and video features also appearing in the current plan comparison. Developers can use API endpoints in addition to the web interface. This breadth may make it convenient to prototype a complete audio workflow in one account, but the capabilities do not all consume resources in the same way.

The practical unit to compare is a finished deliverable: a minute of narration, a translated clip, a set of sound effects or a transcribed interview. A large monthly credit number does not tell you how many finished assets your particular workflow will produce. Choose one normal project, list the tools it needs and check the displayed credit estimate for each step. Keep a record of retries and editing time, because an attractive first sample is not the same as a dependable production process.


## The plan comparison is useful only when the offer and billing interval are clear

The pricing page showed a free tier, four named paid tiers and a custom Enterprise offer when checked on November 18, 2026. Starter and Creator displayed introductory first-month prices before their regular monthly rates. That promotion can make an initial charge look unlike the recurring subscription, so compare the full sequence: amount today, renewal amount, billing date, taxes and the credit allowance that resets with the cycle. The publisher says prices exclude taxes, levies and duties.

The included credit pool rises by tier, but seats and specialized features change too. For example, the listed plans associate Professional Voice Cloning with Creator or above and describe more workspace seats at Scale and Business. Do not upgrade based only on a headline quota. If one person produces occasional narration, a team tier may add little value; a studio producing multilingual campaigns may care more about shared administration, output quality, concurrency and commercial terms. Recheck the live checkout because pricing and introductory offers change.


## Credit accounting changes how teams should estimate an audio job

ElevenLabs describes a common credit allowance across multiple products, while each operation can consume a different amount. Text length, selected speech model, audio duration, dubbing mode and other settings affect the total. The amount quoted for text-to-speech should not be reused as the budget for transcription, music or voice conversion. The API and web workflow may also have different cost details. Confirm the selected product's meter rather than assuming every feature draws at the same rate.

Create a small cost worksheet with the exact inputs and desired outputs. Estimate a first pass, then add likely revisions, language variants and the occasional failed generation. Decide what happens when the monthly allowance runs out: pause, buy prepaid credits or enable eligible usage billing. The billing guide explains pay-as-you-go options and credit behavior, but plan eligibility and balances can differ. This is a budgeting exercise for a buyer; no Roseram account or consumption test was performed for this review.


## Voice cloning requires permission, identity checks and careful source audio

Instant Voice Cloning and Professional Voice Cloning are distinct workflows. The publisher describes the first as creating a clone from shorter samples and the second as training a dedicated voice model from a larger set of recordings. More source material does not remove the need for authorization. The verification step is an important safeguard, but the documentation also cautions that verification cannot by itself guarantee legal ownership of a recording or prove every permission needed for a commercial use.

Use only your own voice or a voice for which you have clear, documented consent covering the intended use. Keep the release, scope, duration, distribution territories and revocation process with the project files. Avoid uploading customer calls, private interviews or third-party narration until you understand the applicable consent and data terms. Ask whether the selected plan permits the exact purpose, and whether the person whose voice is used can review or withdraw permission. These are essential production controls, not optional quality refinements.


## Commercial rights depend on the plan and current license terms

The official billing documentation says content generated on paid plans receives commercial rights, while free-plan output is non-commercial and requires attribution. Read the terms that apply to the specific product and plan before putting a generated voice, music track or effect into an advertisement, client deliverable or monetized publication. A plan card is a useful summary, but it does not replace the full license, restrictions on prohibited use or any third-party rights in supplied material.

Store the subscription receipt and a copy of the applicable terms with each production. Record which account and plan generated the asset, what source materials were provided and where the result is published. If the content is sensitive or customer-facing at scale, ask counsel to assess the license and consent requirements. The review does not establish that any particular generated output is clear of copyright, publicity or contractual claims. Human review and rights documentation remain part of the workflow.


## Privacy choices matter when audio can identify a speaker

Voice recordings can contain personal information and identifying characteristics. ElevenLabs publishes a privacy policy and a help article about whether data is used to improve its models. The latter describes settings for opting out in applicable cases and notes different handling for Enterprise customers. These statements are a starting point, not a complete privacy assessment of a specific recording or workspace. Before uploading, identify what the clip contains, who can access it, how long it is retained and what deletion or export controls apply.

For a company workflow, map the data path from upload through generation, storage, sharing and deletion. Confirm the region and contractual terms that apply to the account, and decide whether sensitive work needs an Enterprise arrangement or another system. Limit access to people responsible for the production. Do not treat an opt-out as a guarantee that every copy disappears immediately or that all legal obligations are satisfied. Verify the current setting in the account and preserve the vendor's written answer for any requirements that affect procurement.


## Evaluate the output with a repeatable listening and editing test

Prepare a short script containing the names, numbers, punctuation and emotional shifts your real work includes. Generate it with the voices and model you intend to use. Listen on headphones and on a phone speaker, then check pronunciation, pacing, emphasis, breaths and edits. If the job is multilingual, ask a qualified speaker to review meaning and pronunciation. If the job is dubbing, compare timing and speaker attribution against the original instead of judging the result from fluency alone.

Track how many takes are needed before a reviewer accepts the clip, the credits consumed, the cleanup time and the rights checks completed. Include an intentionally difficult sentence and a correction request. A clear benchmark can show whether the feature saves effort for your team; a polished demo cannot. This proposed buyer test is not a claim that Roseram measured ElevenLabs output or compared its voice quality against competing services.


## Who should consider ElevenLabs, and what alternatives should be compared?

ElevenLabs may suit creators and developers who need several speech and audio tasks, voice tools or APIs under one product family. It may be less compelling if you need a single simple narration, have strict limits on uploading identifiable audio, or require rights and retention terms that the selected plan does not provide. Compare the entire workflow, not just a sample voice: editing, languages, export format, API usage, shared credits, support and a safe way to remove source recordings all affect the decision.

Shortlist services that solve the same job, then use one authorized sample, one script and one acceptance rubric across them. Compare total monthly cost at your expected volume and include correction time. A dedicated recording workflow, an in-house narrator or a different speech API may be a better fit for some teams. The important question is not whether a tool can generate audio; it is whether it produces an asset your intended audience can trust and your organization is permitted to use.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Eleven Labs a scam?

This review found no source-based reason to label ElevenLabs a scam. Its publisher documents its audio and creative products, plans and usage rules. That does not verify every output, guarantee a business result, or certify a user's chosen configuration.

### Does ElevenLabs have a free plan?

The official pricing page showed a free plan with 10,000 monthly credits when checked on November 18, 2026. The billing documentation says free-plan content is for non-commercial use with attribution. Check the current offer and license terms before using output in a commercial project.

### Can I clone another person's voice?

The official voice-cloning workflow includes verification safeguards and places responsibility for authorized use on the creator. Only use a voice and recordings you have rights and permission to use; do not treat technical access as consent.

### Are ElevenLabs credits the same for every feature?

No. Credit use varies by product, model, duration and operation. The plan allowance is shared across supported products, so using one feature leaves fewer credits for other work. Review the displayed cost for the selected task.
