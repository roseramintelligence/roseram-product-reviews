# EmailOctopus review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-18  
**Category:** Email marketing software  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

EmailOctopus is an email marketing service with a free Starter tier and a paid Pro plan priced according to subscribed contacts and send volume. Published terms describe automatic tier changes, annual prepayment, cancellation effects and account retention; teams should model duplicate subscribers, campaign volume and the post-cancellation workflow before migrating.

## Editorial verdict

EmailOctopus publishes current pricing, billing, cancellation, acceptable-use and privacy documentation. The sources reviewed do not substantiate a scam finding. The main purchase risks are predictable and reviewable: automatic plan changes, contact counting across lists, sending quotas, consent rules, account inactivity and loss of paid features after cancellation.

## EmailOctopus SCAM review: start with its contact-counting rules

EmailOctopus describes itself as a hosted email marketing platform with campaign design, forms, landing pages and automation. It offers a Free Starter plan and a paid Pro plan. The current price page, billing guide, terms and privacy policy provide unusually practical details about contact counts, sending limits, automatic upgrades, cancellation and retention. This review is based on those published sources and does not claim that Roseram sent a live campaign or measured inbox placement.

“SCAM Review” is the page label used in the directory, not a finding of fraud. The public evidence reviewed does not substantiate such an accusation. The main buyer risks are operational: a list can count more contacts than expected across multiple audiences, plan tiers can change automatically as usage grows, and canceling Pro can restrict campaigns and automation. Those are issues a buyer can evaluate before importing a production list.


## EmailOctopus and EmailOctopus Connect are separate choices

The pricing help article explicitly says its rules apply to EmailOctopus and directs Amazon SES users to a separate EmailOctopus Connect article. That distinction matters because a similar name does not mean identical billing, delivery infrastructure or operating responsibilities. The standard EmailOctopus plan describes contact and send allowances, whereas Connect is framed around using Amazon SES. Review the product selection before comparing prices or integrating an existing mail system.

Write down whether the organization wants a managed campaign platform or a layer over its own Amazon SES account. Confirm who owns the sending domain, who manages the SES reputation and limits, how contact consent is stored, and which party handles delivery failures. If the team already has an SES setup, compare the total cost and support burden instead of assuming one product is automatically a cheaper version of the other.


## EmailOctopus Free Starter pricing and what the allowance includes

The pricing page reviewed lists a Free Starter plan with up to 2,500 subscribers and 10,000 emails per month. It also shows EmailOctopus branding on messages, reports available for 30 days, one landing page, one form and access for one user. The page says the free plan has no time limit and does not require a credit card. These are meaningful limits: a contact ceiling and a monthly send ceiling are separate constraints.

A list of 2,000 people does not mean the organization can send an unlimited number of campaigns. Estimate the audience, the number of sends per month and the likely growth before selecting a tier. Check whether the brand footer, report-retention window, user count and number of forms meet the business requirement. Free is useful for a small permission-based pilot, but it is not a data archive or a promise of unlimited sending.


## EmailOctopus Pro price depends on subscriber and email volume

The current pricing page displays Pro with a subscriber selector, monthly sending allowance and a displayed yearly-billing example beginning at US$9 per month. It lists full design control, reports available forever, unlimited landing pages and forms, unlimited users and the ability to cancel at any time. The selected subscriber tier and email volume determine the quote, and the page’s monthly versus yearly toggle affects how the charge is collected.

Record the exact tier, currency, billing cadence and included send volume before upgrading. The price shown in a headline is not necessarily the cost for a larger list or unusually high sending. A business with seasonal campaigns should model its busiest month rather than multiplying an average month by twelve. If the subscription is billed yearly, the annual cash charge and renewal date matter even when the page presents an equivalent monthly amount.


## Subscribed contacts, duplicates and billing counts

EmailOctopus says its paid plan is based on subscribed contacts, provided the account stays within the sending limit. Its help article explains that each list is independent, so the same person appearing as a subscriber in multiple lists can be counted more than once. Unsubscribed contacts and records removed because of a bounce or complaint are not included in the subscriber total according to its pricing guide.

Before import, deduplicate the audience or decide whether separate lists are operationally necessary. Ask a sample of records to confirm which status is active, unsubscribed, bounced or complained. Keep the source and permission history for each person. A cleanup should never erase suppression evidence or make an opted-out address appear eligible again. The contact total shown in a CRM may differ from the count used by a mailing platform because the products define active subscribers differently.


## Monthly email limits are separate from subscriber tiers

EmailOctopus lists a monthly send cap alongside subscribers. Its price page says the Free plan has 10,000 emails a month, while the help material says users who need a higher send volume can upgrade. These units track messages sent, not just the size of the stored audience. A campaign to a large list and several automated follow-ups can consume the monthly allowance faster than expected.

Create a simple send forecast: active subscribers multiplied by campaign frequency, plus welcome messages, transactional or operational messages where applicable, and resends. Confirm which messages count against the plan before relying on the tool for critical communication. Monitor usage before a major announcement and keep a fallback plan for password resets or receipts if those are routed through a separate system. Do not treat a technical ability to upload addresses as permission to email them.


## Automatic upgrades can affect the bill as the list grows

EmailOctopus’s pricing documentation says upgrades can happen automatically when the list exceeds the current subscriber tier. Its terms also say that if use exceeds plan limits, including contacts or content sent, the account can be moved to a tier matching that usage and the added fee can be payable immediately. Annual upgrades may be prorated for the remainder of the subscription period. The pricing page says the company will email when an automatic contact-tier change happens.

Set a list-growth alert and assign someone to review tier-change notices. Before a lead-generation campaign, estimate signups and any imported audience that would become active subscribers. Make sure finance understands that growth can change the tier and that the business may need to approve or budget for it. This mechanism is disclosed in the vendor’s materials; it should be understood as part of the pricing model rather than discovered after a campaign expands the list.


## Annual billing is paid upfront and downgrades follow the billing cycle

The pricing guide says annual plans charge for twelve months upfront. It describes a monthly-to-yearly change as charging the full annual price minus the unused portion of the monthly plan. It also explains that an automatic downgrade based on a smaller subscriber count takes effect at the start of the next billing period; on an annual plan, that may be after the current twelve-month term. Manual downgrades similarly switch at the next billing date.

Compare the total annual commitment with the total cost of paying monthly for the expected use period. If the list is seasonal or recently imported, wait until its normal size is clear before locking in an annual tier. Ask whether an over-limit upgrade affects the paid term and preserve the quote or confirmation. A lower subscriber count may reduce future cost, but it does not necessarily refund unused months or create an immediate downgrade.


## Campaign tools, landing pages and automations need a content check

The official feature comparison describes a drag-and-drop editor, custom HTML, templates, gradual sending, smart timing, merge tags, automations, forms, landing pages, reports, integrations and support. The Free plan’s number of forms and reporting period are limited, while Pro removes some of those caps. The feature list is useful for planning, but an account should still be tested with its own brand assets, signup process and email clients.

Build a test form with clear consent language and verify that a new subscriber receives the expected confirmation. Send the same campaign to desktop and mobile clients, inspect the links, test unsubscribe, and verify that merge tags do not expose an empty or malformed name. Confirm whether the intended automation is available on the tier and what happens when a contact changes status. A feature label does not establish that the resulting message looks right or reaches the inbox.


## Deliverability and sender reputation remain the customer’s responsibility

A sending platform can provide infrastructure and campaign tools, but delivery depends on domain authentication, recipient consent, list quality, engagement, message content, complaints and sending history. EmailOctopus publishes an Acceptable Use Policy and says it vets accounts before and during use. Its policy requires permission for contacts and addresses dormant contacts, prohibiting unsolicited or unlawful campaigns.

Authenticate the sending domain, start with a small group of recent opt-ins and monitor bounces, complaints and unsubscribes. Do not upload scraped or purchased lists. Reconfirm permission before resuming mail to contacts who have been dormant for a long time, as the policy directs. If an account is suspended, use the documented appeal or support process and keep evidence of consent. No plan tier guarantees inbox placement or exempts a sender from anti-spam laws.


## Privacy roles, contact data and AI use

EmailOctopus’s Privacy and Cookie Policy, updated October 1, 2026, distinguishes its role as controller for customer account information from its role as processor for contact lists and campaign content uploaded by customers. It says that it does not use customers’ personal data, content or Contact data to train generally available AI models and describes contractual restrictions on AI providers. The policy also lists account information, usage, device and payment-token data that it processes.

The customer still determines why a contact is on the list and which messages are appropriate. Record the source, date and wording of consent, honor unsubscribe and access requests, and avoid uploading sensitive data prohibited by the provider’s policies. Review the subprocessor list and data-processing schedule for the organization’s use case. A vendor’s role as a processor does not transfer the marketer’s responsibility for lawful collection, accurate notices or the handling of a deletion request.


## Retention, inactive free accounts and backup windows

The privacy policy says account data can be retained while the account is open and for up to 25 months after last use. It says free accounts unused for 24 months are closed, with data deleted within a month after closure, after an attempt to notify the account email. It separately describes customer contact lists and content, backup copies that may remain for up to 90 days after deletion, invoices retained for six years and support correspondence for up to five years where needed.

Treat the account as a working campaign system, not a permanent archive. Export lists, consent records, templates, reports and campaign assets before closing an account or allowing it to become inactive. Confirm the destination can store suppression states so that an export does not accidentally re-enable opted-out subscribers. Keep the account-owner inbox monitored so inactivity notices do not go unseen. Deletion from the active account and expiration of backup copies can happen on different timelines.


## Canceling Pro changes features when the paid period ends

EmailOctopus says a Pro subscription can be canceled from the Billing section and its terms state cancellation takes effect at the end of the current subscription or renewal period. The Help Center describes what changes after expiry: campaigns cannot be sent if more than 2,500 contacts remain, automations pause, reports older than 30 days become unavailable, additional users cannot be added and other features such as forms and landing pages become more limited.

Before cancellation, export the records and assets needed for future operations, reduce or move the list if the Free tier is intended, and map each paused automation to a replacement. Verify the exact end date and current feature behavior in the account. Canceling does not mean that an entire mailing history is available forever, nor does a backup of subscribers automatically preserve permission evidence. Keep the confirmation and verify that the next renewal does not occur.


## EmailOctopus alternatives and a practical migration comparison

Alternatives include newsletter tools, broader marketing-automation suites, customer relationship management platforms and direct email-service-provider integrations. Compare whether pricing is based on stored contacts, active subscribers, messages sent, seats or a combination. Also compare automation, form limits, report history, integrations, support, data exports, suppression handling and the effort required to warm a new sending domain.

Run one permission-based pilot with the same segment, content and expected volume on each candidate. Check the form, confirmation flow, mobile rendering, unsubscribe behavior, bounce suppression, reporting and export. Calculate a normal month and peak month, including any automatic tier changes. If the organization mainly needs a simple newsletter, a lighter tool may be sufficient. If it needs a CRM or multichannel automation, EmailOctopus may not replace those systems; buy for the requirement, not the number of features listed.


## EmailOctopus SCAM review verdict: clear pricing rules, plan for growth and exit

EmailOctopus publishes a Free plan’s subscriber and monthly-send limits, a Pro selector, billing guidance, automatic-upgrade rules, cancellation steps, acceptable-use requirements and a detailed privacy policy. Its documentation explains that annual plans are charged upfront, duplicate subscribers across separate lists can count multiple times, and Pro cancellation changes access to automation, reports and sending above the Free contact limit. These are specific terms a buyer can review before importing contacts.

The public evidence reviewed does not substantiate a scam finding. The main safeguards are to keep a permission ledger, deduplicate lists, forecast sends, monitor plan-change notices, test the cancellation path and export contacts and suppression states before ending the subscription. This is a public-source desk review, not a live campaign, security audit or deliverability test. Official pricing, help, terms and privacy materials were reviewed November 18, 2026; check the current account quote and policy before purchase.
