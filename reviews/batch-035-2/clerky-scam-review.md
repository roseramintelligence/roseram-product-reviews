## Clerky Purpose and Core Automation Focus

Clerky is an online document generation and state filing software system designed exclusively for venture-scale technology startups. In contrast to broad commercial filing services that handle small businesses like dry cleaners, retail storefronts, and local trade contractors, Clerky narrows its functional scope strictly to high-growth companies. The service was established by two former Silicon Valley startup attorneys from the law firm Orrick, Herrington & Sutcliffe who observed that routine administrative drafting frequently consumed costly billable hours and caused expensive clerical mistakes when founders attempted manual work. The core mission of the software is eliminating administrative drudgery through specialized, standard document templates.

The software architecture guides founders through successive corporate milestones, organizing legal tasks into structured chronological stages. Rather than presenting generic fill-in-the-blank forms, the system applies legal defaults and logic developed for venture-backed companies. Workflows span from initial corporate creation through director appointments, equity allocations, initial team onboarding, and early-stage capital fundraising using standard convertible instruments. The system tracks task completion and ensures corporate actions are executed in the required legal sequence, reducing the procedural friction commonly encountered by early-stage builders.

Prospective buyers must recognize, however, that Clerky operates strictly as an automation tool and does not provide legal representation. The platform states clearly in published materials that it is not a law firm and does not provide legal advice. Users are responsible for inputting their own corporate terms, selecting their capitalization numbers, and ensuring their corporate actions comply with relevant local and federal regulations. The software accelerates clerical execution, but it does not evaluate the commercial wisdom or strategic suitability of the corporate choices a founder makes.


## Formation Scope and Delaware Specialization

The operational core of Clerky's formation product line is built entirely around the Delaware C corporation. This specific legal entity is the recognized standard for venture-backed high-growth companies due to Delaware's predictable corporate case law, specialized Court of Chancery, and investor familiarity. Clerky does not provide formation services for limited liability companies, partnerships, sole proprietorships, or S corporations, nor does it support incorporating companies directly into other states. This tight jurisdictional focus ensures the underlying formation templates remain aligned with institutional venture expectations.

When a founder submits a formation workflow, Clerky transmits the certificate of incorporation electronically to the Delaware Secretary of State. The publisher notes that state processing typically takes between one and three business days, supported by Delaware expedited filing handling. The formation service includes a state name availability check, specialized rejection analysis to address clerical filing obstacles, and first-year registered agent representation in Delaware, which is mandatory for any corporation lacking a physical corporate presence in the state.

Restricting the platform's formation engine exclusively to Delaware C corporations carries both clear operational advantages and significant organizational constraints. For entrepreneurs planning to bootstrap a modest regional enterprise, establish a freelance agency, or operate a family-owned business, establishing a Delaware C corporation introduces unnecessary administrative complexity and tax overhead. Buyers must carefully examine their long-term capitalization strategy before selecting Clerky, confirming that their business actually requires the formal corporate architecture of a Delaware entity rather than a simpler regional LLC.


## Post-Incorporation Governance and Equity Issuance

A critical mistake made by first-time startup founders is assuming that corporate existence is complete once the state approves the certificate of incorporation. Without post-incorporation setup, a corporation remains an empty legal shell without adopted bylaws, appointed officers, an elected board of directors, or validly issued equity. Clerky addresses this organizational gap through a dedicated post-incorporation setup module that systematically executes the Action of Incorporator, establishes formal company bylaws, and records the initial board consents required to activate the corporation legally.

Equity allocation represents the most delicate phase of this workflow. Through the platform, founders can issue restricted common stock to the founding team using Restricted Stock Purchase Agreements with customizable vesting schedules. Applying standard vesting safeguards the venture against situations where an early co-founder departs prematurely while retaining an unearned equity stake. Additionally, the software executes Confidential Information and Invention Assignment Agreements, legally conveying intellectual property, proprietary software code, and commercial designs created by the founding team directly into the corporate entity's ownership.

Another vital administrative workflow inside the post-incorporation phase is handling federal tax elections under Section 83(b) of the Internal Revenue Code. When founders receive restricted stock subject to vesting, filing an 83(b) election with the IRS within a strict thirty-day statutory window is essential to avoid potential ordinary income taxes on stock value appreciation. Clerky provides pre-filled 83(b) election forms, step-by-step mailing instructions, and automated calendar filing reminders, helping founders mitigate what is historically one of the most financially damaging oversights in company formation.


## Fundraising Instruments and Investor Signature Escrow

As an early-stage startup prepares to secure outside capital, Clerky offers automated documentation for standard seed financing instruments. The platform supports Simple Agreements for Future Equity, commonly known as Safes, alongside traditional convertible promissory notes. Founders can generate agreements based on standard market variations, including valuation caps, conversion discounts, combined valuation cap and discount structures, and Most Favored Nation clauses. The system automatically drafts corresponding board consents authorizing the financing, ensuring corporate governance records remain complete and enforceable.

Managing multiple angel investors across a rolling seed round frequently introduces operational chaos, such as tracking unexecuted agreements or chasing outstanding bank wires. Clerky incorporates a financing checklist to help founders track which investors have signed agreements and which individuals have wired their investment funds. This checklist functions as an administrative dashboard, preventing founders from closing a funding round without executing the requisite corporate resolutions or properly finalizing transaction records.

A notable utility within Clerky's fundraising module is its signature escrow mechanism. In typical early-stage funding rounds, a founder risks distributing fully executed convertible contracts before an investor's capital transfer actually clears the company bank account. Clerky's signature escrow allows founders to gather and hold electronic investor signatures in digital escrow, releasing the finalized contracts only after the startup confirms that investment funds have been deposited. This safeguard ensures that investors cannot claim equity rights without fulfilling their funding commitments.


## Hiring Workflows and Equity Plan Adoption

As a newly formed startup begins building out its team, legal compliance shifts toward employment agreements and equity incentives. Clerky provides automated document generation for onboarding employees, independent contractors, and corporate advisors. Each onboarding package includes tailored offer letters or consulting contracts, bundled with mandatory confidentiality and intellectual property assignment provisions. These covenants ensure that all technological assets and proprietary materials created by new personnel belong fully to the startup rather than the individual worker.

To incentivize early team members, startups typically establish an employee equity incentive pool. Clerky provides a stock plan adoption module that allows a startup's board and shareholders to authorize an option pool formally. Once adopted, the platform enables the company to issue equity compensation out of the approved pool, supporting standard stock options, early-exercisable stock options, and direct restricted stock awards. The software handles the required board consents and notice of grant paperwork for each individual equity transaction.

Clerky also notes that it maintains managed legal forms, monitoring statutory updates, regulatory shifts, and venture industry standards to keep onboarding documents up to date. When employment standards or case law evolve, the system flags the availability of refreshed document templates. For startups expanding their engineering and operational teams, having standardized, regularly updated onboarding paperwork helps maintain consistent intellectual property protection and clean equity records across successive hiring cycles.


## Pricing Mechanics and Total Cost of Ownership

Evaluating Clerky requires looking past initial marketing headlines to calculate the full financial cost of corporate legal operations. The platform offers two distinct purchasing structures: a pay-per-use transactional model and a bundled Company Lifetime Package. Under the pay-per-use structure, initial Delaware incorporation is listed as a one-time fee of four hundred twenty-seven dollars, which covers expedited state filing fees and the first year of Delaware registered agent service. Subsequent modules require separate payments, such as two hundred ninety-nine dollars for post-incorporation setup and one hundred ninety-nine dollars for stock plan adoption.

Alternatively, founders can purchase the Company Lifetime Package for an upfront one-time payment of eight hundred nineteen dollars. This comprehensive package bundles standard Delaware incorporation and post-incorporation setup with unlimited access to Clerky's core fundraising, hiring, commercial non-disclosure, and company maintenance modules for the lifespan of the company. For startups planning to issue multiple Safes, onboard contractors, and execute mutual non-disclosure agreements, the upfront package frequently provides a more predictable software expenditure than accumulating multiple per-transaction fees.

However, buyers must distinguish between software platform fees and mandatory third-party statutory expenses. The Company Lifetime Package does not cover ongoing state expenses, such as the mandatory annual Delaware franchise tax, subsequent annual registered agent fees after year one, or state foreign qualification filing fees. For instance, registering to do business outside Delaware involves separate state filing fees, and charter amendments like increasing authorized shares incur additional state filing charges. Founders must budget for these recurring external costs independently of their Clerky software purchase.


## Practical Operating Boundaries and Explicit Limitations

Despite its utility for standard startup formation, Clerky operates within strict practical and legal boundaries. The platform is not a law firm, does not establish an attorney-client relationship, and cannot provide strategic legal counsel or represent founders in disputes. If a company requires bespoke corporate architecture, such as dual-class voting shares, complex preferred equity rights, or intricate joint venture holding structures, Clerky's automated templates cannot accommodate those customizations. Founders navigating unusual corporate ownership arrangements must hire specialized legal counsel directly.

Another operational limitation involves geographic and entity diversity. Clerky is entirely unsuited for businesses seeking to organize as LLCs or incorporate outside Delaware. Furthermore, while the platform provides direct software workflows for foreign qualification in California and New York for an additional fee, startups operating in other states must navigate qualification through third-party partners or handle filings manually. Similarly, while Clerky offers an in-house electronic filing service for non-US founders seeking an IRS Employer Identification Number without a fax machine, foreign founders must still independently navigate cross-border tax treaties, local corporate compliance, and foreign asset reporting rules.

Finally, automated paperwork generation does not protect against user input errors. If a founder enters incorrect share counts, authorizes an unbalanced capitalization schedule, or miscalculates vesting dates, the software will generate legally binding paperwork reflecting those flawed inputs. Automated software tools execute clerical instructions accurately, but they cannot evaluate whether the underlying numbers make commercial or strategic sense for the venture. Founders must review every document carefully before signing.


## Collaboration Controls and Outside Attorney Roles

Rather than positioning itself as a replacement for startup lawyers, Clerky is built to operate alongside outside legal counsel. The platform incorporates role-based collaboration features that allow founders to invite their company attorneys and paralegals into the workspace. Legal advisors can directly inspect draft documents, suggest modifications, review customized terms, and monitor pending corporate filings. Attorneys can also establish free accounts to manage multiple startup clients across a centralized interface, standardizing routine paperwork across their firm's portfolio.

The software allows founders to configure legal oversight based on their specific operational preferences. For routine transactions like standard Safes or employee confidentiality agreements, outside counsel can be assigned as an observer who automatically receives final executed copies for firm recordkeeping. For more sensitive matters, such as custom charter amendments or unique advisor equity vesting terms, attorneys can be configured as mandatory reviewers who must inspect and approve document terms before paperwork is distributed for digital signatures.

This collaborative dynamic allows startups to balance legal spending against operational speed. Founders can handle repetitive clerical tasks and document generation through software while reserving expensive attorney billable hours for high-value strategic matters, like negotiating venture capital term sheets, structuring commercial licensing deals, or resolving governance disputes. Before initiating formation, founders should confirm that their chosen legal counsel is comfortable working with Clerky's standardized templates, as attorney familiarity significantly influences workflow efficiency.

## Market Alternatives and Comparative Tradeoffs

Founders assessing legal management tools face three distinct alternatives: broad commercial filing websites, modern all-in-one equity management platforms, and traditional boutique startup law firms. Broad commercial filing services cater primarily to traditional small businesses, offering affordable LLC and corporate registrations. However, these generalized services frequently omit standard venture provisions, such as founder vesting restrictions, formal intellectual property assignment covenants, and pre-filled 83(b) tax forms, creating legal deficiencies that institutional investors often require startups to clean up later at significant expense.

Modern equity management platforms represent another popular alternative, bundling Delaware incorporation with banking setups, corporate charge cards, and capitalization table tracking software, often financed through recurring subscription models. While convenient, some founders prefer Clerky's transactional or fixed lifetime package to avoid ongoing monthly platform fees or platform lock-in. Specialized document automation platforms allow startups to maintain independence between their legal documentation, banking partners, and capitalization management software, giving founders greater flexibility as their operational needs evolve.

Finally, engaging a boutique startup law firm provides fully customized legal advice, bespoke equity structuring, and dedicated personal representation. However, this approach entails hourly billing rates that can quickly deplete pre-seed working capital. Choosing between these paths involves balancing financial constraints, structural complexity, and the need for personalized counsel. Startups with straightforward founding teams often leverage automated document software for routine setup, while ventures with complex ownership or specialized regulatory requirements typically opt for traditional law firm representation.


## A Structured Buyer Due Diligence Roadmap

Before purchasing Clerky's software packages, founders should conduct a systematic due diligence assessment across four practical areas. First, verify structural eligibility by confirming that your venture actually requires a Delaware C corporation. If your business model is designed as a bootstrapped lifestyle business, a local consulting agency, or a real estate holding vehicle, an LLC formed in your home state is usually a more suitable, cost-effective structure. Delaware C corporations carry annual corporate franchise taxes and state filing obligations that may be unnecessary for companies not pursuing outside venture capital.

Second, consult with an independent certified public accountant or qualified tax advisor regarding founder equity allocation and tax filings. Founders must understand the tax implications of restricted stock purchases, the necessity of filing an 83(b) election with the IRS within the statutory thirty-day deadline, and state-level foreign qualification requirements in the states where founders physically reside. While Clerky provides pre-filled 83(b) forms, verifying individual tax strategies with an independent professional ensures your equity vesting schedule aligns with your broader financial plan.

Third, review the full fee landscape beyond the headline software purchase price. Founders should account for ongoing statutory expenses, such as the annual Delaware franchise tax, annual registered agent renewal fees after year one, and state foreign qualification filing charges. Finally, confirm that your intended outside startup attorney is comfortable reviewing and managing documents generated on Clerky. By executing this methodical due diligence roadmap, founders can utilize automated document software effectively while maintaining full visibility over their corporate governance and compliance obligations.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Clerky a licensed law firm that gives legal advice?

No. Clerky explicitly states on its website that it is not a law firm and does not act as an attorney for users. The software is an automated clerical tool created by former startup lawyers to generate standard corporate paperwork based on user input.

### Can founders form an LLC or a company outside Delaware using Clerky?

Clerky focuses exclusively on Delaware C corporations, which are the standard entity structure preferred by venture investors. It does not support limited liability companies (LLCs) or direct business incorporations in other American states, though it offers foreign qualification support.

### Does purchasing the Company Lifetime Package cover all future corporate costs?

No. The one-time software package fee covers Clerky's platform tools and initial formation filings, but excludes statutory expenses like recurring annual Delaware franchise taxes, registered agent renewals after the first year, state foreign qualification fees, and third-party charter amendment filing charges.

### How does the platform's signature escrow mechanism operate during fundraising?

During early fundraising using Safes or convertible promissory notes, the platform allows founders to collect and securely hold signed investor documents in digital escrow. The documents are only released and marked complete after the company confirms that the investor's wire transfer has cleared.
