## Core Purpose and Operational Marketplace Architecture

The underlying architecture of Band Space addresses the fragmentation that plagues music rehearsal management and private music instruction. Traditionally, bands locate practice rooms by posting across social media groups, calling studio managers, and negotiating open evenings over text messaging. Studio owners simultaneously juggle paper notebooks, personal calendars, and manual electronic bank transfers. Band Space replaces these scattered communication channels with a centralized digital catalog and unified booking engine that records transaction details, schedule times, and equipment availability in a single dashboard.

On the supply side, the platform serves three distinct vendor profiles: commercial rehearsal rooms, independent music teachers, and specialized music schools. Partners create public listings detailing their address, transport directions, pricing tiers, and specialized studio assets such as drum kits, public address amplifiers, or keyboard setups. Potential clients browse matching listings, view unreserved hours in real time, and confirm sessions without telephone calls. Both parties receive automated schedule alerts, and past session records remain stored in the user profile for administrative auditing.

On the demand side, individual musicians, bands, and students gain an uncluttered reservation pathway. An instrumentalist seeking vocal coaching or weekly drum practice can compare venues side by side, review room amenities, and complete reservations using integrated digital checkout tools. By linking the renter, the instructor, and the studio within one transactional flow, Band Space removes the recurring back-and-forth friction that often derails rehearsal planning.

## Pricing Structure, Commission Rates, and Software Costs

A key factor in any commercial software evaluation is its pricing transparency and fee structure. Band Space publishes a multi-tiered commercial model tailored to rehearsal spaces, independent tutors, and music academies. To lower onboarding resistance, the service provides an introductory promotional incentive granting two months of full platform features at zero cost for new partners entering the network.

Following the introductory promotional window, partner facilities select between structured subscription tiers. The Basic plan carries an ongoing subscription fee of 49 PLN per month. On this entry tier, Band Space assesses a 10% commission on bookings completed via online payments, while bookings settled outside the platform incur a 13% commission fee. This plan supplies fundamental scheduling tools, a public marketplace directory profile, online payment processing, and basic booking notifications.

For growing facilities, the Premium tier is priced at 249 PLN per month. In exchange for the higher baseline subscription, the platform lowers transaction commissions to 5% for online reservations and 8% for external settlements. The Premium plan unlocks advanced business tools, including integrated customer messaging, external Google Calendar synchronization, studio analytics, and TTLock smart lock support. Prospective studio partners must project their monthly reservation turnover to determine which combination of fixed subscription fees and variable commissions optimizes their bottom line.


## Suitability Profile and Practical Limitations

Band Space delivers clear benefits for solo rehearsal studio operators, multi-room rehearsal complexes, and busy private music instructors operating within supported regional hubs. Its interface is designed specifically for musical use cases, incorporating fields for audio gear, drum kits, and sound equipment that generic scheduling software lacks. Operators struggling with late-night phone calls, scattered text messages, and unpaid session cancellations will find substantial relief in its automated booking flows.

However, the platform presents practical limitations that prospective buyers must acknowledge. First-party documentation highlights active vendor clusters in major Polish urban centers like Warsaw, meaning platform directory visibility will naturally prove less impactful for studios situated in smaller rural areas or international locations outside Poland. A studio operating outside the platform's primary marketing radius will function primarily as a private scheduling portal rather than gaining significant customer discovery from marketplace foot traffic.

Furthermore, international music businesses seeking broad multi-currency support, complex enterprise accounting integrations, or native compatibility with access hardware outside the TTLock system will encounter operational constraints. Facilities that rely on enterprise-grade payroll systems or customized point-of-sale hardware must verify whether export mechanisms or manual data transfers will be necessary to balance platform records with external company accounting books.
