## Platform Mission and Core Target Audience

The stated objective of AngelHub.app is to eliminate the friction and operational fragmentation associated with tracking early-stage startup investments. Historically, private investors manage their holdings across disorganized personal spreadsheets, local hard drive folders, and overflowing email inboxes containing sporadic founder updates. AngelHub positions itself as a streamlined hub that consolidates deal sourcing pipelines, SAFE note contracts, valuation changes, and performance analytics into a single responsive interface, avoiding the steep learning curves and heavy setup fees of legacy venture capital platforms.

The tool primarily targets individual angel investors, syndicates, and emerging micro-fund managers who need structured visibility into their financial exposure without paying thousands of dollars for institutional asset management suites. By catering to individuals with portfolios ranging from a single early bet to dozens of active syndicate positions, the software attempts to bridge the gap between simple personal accounting spreadsheets and heavyweight corporate treasury tools. It focuses heavily on standard startup legal instruments, such as Simple Agreements for Future Equity, common stock purchase agreements, and convertible notes.

A practical evaluation of this positioning requires considering whether your personal investing style matches a self-directed software dashboard. Investors who participate purely as passive limited partners in large venture syndicates may find dedicated portfolio software redundant if their syndicate leads already provide periodic consolidated statements. Conversely, active direct angels who independently negotiate side letters, exercise pro-rata participation rights, and review quarterly progress reports will find greater value in having an organized operational system to monitor their active stakes.


## Portfolio Analytics: MOIC, IRR, and Dilution Metrics

At the center of AngelHub's value proposition is its automated analytical dashboard, which calculates standard venture capital performance metrics including Multiple on Invested Capital, annualized Internal Rate of Return, and Return on Investment. Calculating IRR accurately across irregular investment horizons, multiple convertible funding rounds, and staged seed tranches is notoriously tedious when attempted in standard spreadsheet software. AngelHub automates these formulas as users log transaction records, funding dates, and updated company valuations.

In addition to basic investment multiples, the platform promotes advanced analytics covering stage diversification, vintage year cohorts, and follow-on dilution tracking. For example, a prospective buyer can test the platform by inputting a hypothetical initial investment of twenty-five thousand dollars into a seed-stage SAFE note with a valuation cap, followed two years later by an equity conversion in a priced Series A round. Evaluating how transparently the system displays the resulting dilution, post-money valuation adjustments, and blended holding periods allows users to confirm that the calculations align with industry accounting standards.

While these calculations are marketed as institutional-grade, users must recognize that any analytics engine is strictly dependent on the accuracy of the underlying valuation data. Because early-stage startup investments are inherently illiquid and lack continuous market pricing, book valuations often remain static until a company raises a subsequent priced round or experiences a clear liquidity event. Investors must verify whether the dashboard allows manual valuation overrides or discount adjustments when companies raise unpriced bridge notes or suffer down-rounds during challenging fundraising environments.


## SAFE Notes and Document Management Capacity

Document storage represents a critical operational pillar for private startup investors. AngelHub provides dedicated repositories to store SAFE notes, stock purchase agreements, capitalization tables, board meeting materials, and quarterly updates. Storing these binding contracts alongside financial analytics ensures that critical legal terms, such as information rights, liquidation preferences, and pro-rata thresholds, remain easily accessible whenever portfolio companies initiate follow-on financing rounds.

However, buyers must carefully evaluate the platform's tiered document storage limits against their expected operational volume. The Free tier includes only 100MB of storage, the Pro tier expands this allowance to 1GB, and the Fund tier tops out at 10GB. While text-heavy PDF contracts and standard cap table spreadsheets consume minimal storage space, pitch decks with embedded multimedia and detailed quarterly investor reports can consume storage rapidly. Users managing multiple investments with extensive board decks must monitor their usage to avoid hitting storage ceilings unexpectedly.

A sound buyer evaluation should include uploading a sample set of legal documents, including scanned multi-page agreements and complex cap tables, to assess how the platform organizes, previews, and secures these sensitive assets. Prospective users should also verify whether the platform supports structured tagging or metadata labeling, which helps differentiate signed closing documents from preliminary draft amendments or regular quarterly founder memos.


## Deal Pipeline and Operational Task Management

Managing early-stage startup investments involves distinct operational stages that extend well beyond passive balance sheet monitoring. AngelHub incorporates an interactive Kanban-style deal pipeline board alongside task reminders to help investors track startup conversations from initial contact through due diligence and ultimate exit. This workflow is intended to prevent active investors from missing critical follow-on opportunities, board meeting deadlines, or subscription agreement submission windows.

The Kanban structure allows prospective angels to visualize their current deal flow across progressive custom stages, such as preliminary review, founder interview, technical due diligence, legal execution, and active monitoring. Pairing pipeline stages with calendar alerts creates a systematic routine for busy professionals who balance personal startup investing alongside demanding full-time operational careers. By consolidating pipeline tracking with active portfolio records, the platform reduces the friction of context switching across disparate personal productivity tools.

Prospective buyers evaluating this capability should test whether the Kanban board supports detailed deal notes and document attachments before funds are officially committed. If you evaluate twenty potential deals for every single check written, a pipeline tool must cleanly archive passed opportunities without cluttering active portfolio analytics. Testing how smoothly a prospective company card converts into a live holding upon investment closing is an essential verification step during any initial software trial.


## AI Summaries and Cost Optimization Claims

Artificial intelligence is integrated directly into AngelHub's feature set through automated investment summaries and portfolio intelligence. The publisher highlights a ninety percent cost optimization model compared to direct commercial API fees, promoting summary costs ranging from fifteen cents to two dollars and seventy cents per month. The platform provides structured monthly allowances: five AI summaries per month on the Free plan, fifteen summaries on the Pro plan, and fifty summaries with model selection between Haiku and Sonnet on the Fund plan.

These automated summaries are intended to distill lengthy founder updates, financial updates, and investor newsletters into actionable operational insights. For an angel investor backed by dozens of portfolio companies sending varied communication formats, an automated synthesis tool can quickly surface red flags, cash runway warnings, hiring milestones, and upcoming capital requirements. Fund-tier users also gain access to batch generation capabilities, allowing them to process multiple incoming founder updates simultaneously.

Prospective users should treat publisher claims regarding cost optimization as architectural positioning rather than a direct cash savings metric for the end customer, given that subscriptions are billed at fixed monthly rates. To evaluate this feature practically, a buyer should run realistic founder update letters through the summarization tool to confirm that critical qualitative context—such as customer churn explanations or burn rate acceleration—is preserved accurately without generating misleading hallucinations.


## Pricing Architecture, Tiers, and Subscription Terms

AngelHub structures its platform across three clearly defined subscription tiers: Free, Pro, and Fund. The Free tier costs nothing indefinitely and requires no credit card upon registration, permitting investors to track up to five investments with basic return metrics, an activity timeline, and CSV export capabilities. This entry point provides a risk-free environment for novice angels or investors testing the software's interface before migrating their broader historical portfolio.

The Pro tier is priced at $29 per month billed monthly, expanding tracking capacity to twenty-five investments and introducing CSV importing, PDF and Excel exports, advanced analytics, portfolio company health tracking, and the Kanban deal pipeline. For syndicates and micro-funds managing broader portfolios, the Fund tier is priced at $79 per month billed monthly. This top tier provides unlimited investment tracking, fifty AI summaries with Haiku or Sonnet model selection, API access, batch processing, and dedicated operational support. A published seventeen percent discount applies when customers select annual billing.

All paid plans are processed securely through Stripe and carry a published seven-day money-back guarantee, allowing dissatisfied subscribers to request a full refund via email. Prospective customers should note that while the Free tier operates indefinitely, upgrading is strictly necessary once a portfolio exceeds five investments or when CSV spreadsheet import capabilities are required. Evaluating whether twenty-five investments on the Pro tier provides adequate headroom is a critical planning consideration before committing to a paid plan.


## Data Portability, API Capabilities, and Exit Strategy

A primary concern for any software buyer storing proprietary financial data is long-term portability and the risk of vendor lock-in. AngelHub addresses this requirement by incorporating varied export formats across its subscription tiers. Free accounts include baseline CSV export functionality, while Pro accounts gain access to structured Excel and PDF export reports suitable for tax preparation, syndicate reporting, or personal wealth tracking. The Fund tier further introduces direct API access for teams requiring customized programmatic data integration.

Importing historical data is equally crucial for established investors who have accumulated years of transaction history across offline spreadsheets. AngelHub limits CSV import functionality to paid Pro and Fund accounts, meaning Free tier users must enter their initial five investments manually. When evaluating the platform, an active investor should prepare an export of their existing holdings to assess whether column headers, transaction date formats, and share classes align cleanly with AngelHub's automated ingestion schemas.

Maintaining a clear exit strategy is essential when adopting any specialized cloud tool. Prospective buyers should confirm that exported reports retain comprehensive transaction records, historical valuation snapshots, and file attachments rather than aggregated summary figures alone. Testing a baseline CSV and PDF export immediately after setting up a pilot account ensures that you can retrieve your complete dataset intact if your portfolio management needs change or if you choose to transition to an alternative software environment.

## Evaluating Alternatives: Spreadsheets and Legacy Platforms

Prospective buyers must weigh AngelHub against the two primary alternatives dominating the angel investing landscape: manual spreadsheets and established institutional portfolio platforms. Publisher marketing cites industry surveys suggesting that sixty to seventy percent of angel investors continue to manage their portfolios using Excel or Google Sheets. Spreadsheets provide complete customization and zero software subscription overhead, but they suffer from high human error rates, broken formulas, static valuation views, and poor handling of document storage and automated dilution tracking.

On the opposite end of the spectrum, legacy institutional platforms like Seraf cater to professional family offices and venture funds with comprehensive multi-entity accounting and compliance features. However, these enterprise systems frequently cost significantly more per month and introduce complex onboarding processes that overwhelm solo angels managing modest portfolios. AngelHub attempts to position itself directly in the middle of these extremes, offering modern interface design, automated metric calculations, and AI features at half the price of legacy enterprise tools.

To decide between these options, an investor should calculate the true administrative cost of maintaining offline spreadsheets. If you spend several hours each month updating formulas, reconciling cap table shares, and searching email threads for unread founder updates, a dedicated mid-tier application can easily justify its monthly subscription fee. Conversely, if you make one small investment every couple of years and hold straightforward common stock, the discipline of a simple personal spreadsheet may remain completely adequate for your needs.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does AngelHub.app support manual file imports from existing spreadsheets?

Yes. AngelHub.app supports CSV import functionality starting on its Pro tier, allowing users to migrate historical investment logs directly from spreadsheets. The base Free tier permits manual entry and CSV export, but users looking to bulk-import historical records must evaluate whether their initial spreadsheet columns map cleanly onto AngelHub's structure.

### How are calculations like IRR and MOIC handled inside the dashboard?

The platform calculates institutional performance metrics such as Multiple on Invested Capital (MOIC), Internal Rate of Return (IRR), and overall return on investment automatically. These calculations rely on the entry of accurate transaction dates, round valuations, check sizes, and follow-on round dilution updates entered by the investor.

### What happens if a portfolio grows beyond the limits of the Free tier?

The Free tier caps tracking at five investments and 100MB of storage. Once an investor makes a sixth investment or requires advanced capabilities like the Kanban deal pipeline or CSV importing, they must upgrade to the Pro tier at $29 per month or the Fund tier at $79 per month.

### What refund policy protects buyers evaluating paid AngelHub subscriptions?

Paid subscriptions on both the Pro and Fund plans are backed by a seven-day money-back guarantee processed through Stripe. If requested within seven days of the initial purchase, the publisher issues a complete refund without requiring ongoing service justification.
