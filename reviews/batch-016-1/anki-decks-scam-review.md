## Anki Decks SCAM Review: separate card creation from learning

Anki Decks, styled AnkiDecks by its publisher, describes an AI service for turning study material into flashcards and studying them through spaced repetition. It accepts several source formats and offers export to Anki. That combination addresses preparation work, but generating many cards is different from learning the right material accurately. A reader searching for an Anki Decks SCAM Review should evaluate source fidelity, card design, generation limits and export behavior before judging the service by its speed claims or the size of its shared library.

## AnkiDecks is not the same product as Anki

The captured pricing page explicitly says AnkiDecks is independent of, and not affiliated with or endorsed by, Anki Software LLC. That distinction is important when a buyer recognizes the Anki name and assumes a relationship. The homepage says users can study entirely within AnkiDecks without an Anki account or installation, or export a deck for use in Anki. These are separate products and separate workflows, even though the export format connects them.

Choose the intended study destination before evaluating convenience. Someone already maintaining an Anki collection may care about imported media, card types and collection organization. Someone starting in the browser may care more about session continuity and how cards are edited there. Do not assume that a subscription to the generation service includes every product or service associated with the Anki name. This review has not checked third-party application purchases or account arrangements and does not claim that the export path establishes any endorsement.


## Supported files do not guarantee complete extraction

The homepage lists PDFs, presentations, documents, spreadsheets, EPUBs, images and handwritten notes, while the pricing FAQ also mentions audio, video and YouTube links. That breadth can make the service relevant to mixed course materials. However, a supported file extension does not establish that every table, diagram, footnote or unusual scan is interpreted correctly. A lecture slide with sparse text and a dense chapter with exceptions require different forms of review after extraction.

Use a small source whose important points you already understand. Compare the generated coverage with headings, definitions and exceptions in that source. Check whether labels from a diagram remain attached to the correct concept and whether a table's row and column context survives. These are proposed acceptance checks, not observed failures. The question is not whether AI can produce plausible cards, but whether the produced cards preserve the information you actually need to recall for the selected course or task.


## Card type should follow the learning objective

AnkiDecks describes question-and-answer, cloze, multiple-choice and image-occlusion cards. Those formats support different recall tasks. A short definition may suit a focused question, while a sentence with several related terms can become ambiguous if an unhelpful word is hidden. Multiple-choice cards can test recognition while still failing to show whether a learner can explain the concept without prompts. More format options are useful only when the resulting cards match the kind of knowledge being assessed.

Review each card for a clear prompt, a sufficiently specific answer and a traceable source. Avoid combining several independent facts into one answer that is difficult to grade honestly during review. This is a recommendation about evaluating generated study material, not a claim that every card from the service has these problems. No cards were generated here. A useful pilot would compare a small set of AI-generated cards with a carefully prepared reference set and record the edits needed before study.


## Cloze generation needs enough context to remain meaningful

The homepage describes choosing Cloze rather than Basic in the generation options. A fill-in-the-blank format can be convenient for terminology and structured statements, but the hidden span should not remove so much context that several answers become reasonable. Conversely, a card that leaves an obvious grammatical clue may be easy to answer without understanding the subject. Review the sentence as a recall task rather than assuming that a correctly hidden word makes an effective card.

For technical and professional material, pay particular attention to conditions and exceptions. Removing a qualifying phrase can turn a limited statement into a misleading general rule. Keep the source reference available so uncertain wording can be corrected before repetition reinforces it. The saved pages establish the availability of a cloze option, not its accuracy for every discipline. This review does not provide medical, legal or examination guidance and does not certify a generated deck for any professional use.


## Image occlusion requires label and diagram checks

The publisher describes detecting labels and covering them automatically, with examples involving anatomy, maps and handwritten material. That is a distinct workflow from generating text-only questions. The learner needs to know which structure or region is being tested and whether nearby visual cues accidentally reveal the answer. A technically exported image can still make a confusing card if the mask covers the wrong area or the diagram lacks enough orientation to interpret the prompt.

Inspect a small set with crowded labels, small print and overlapping annotations before committing a large study collection. Compare the hidden area with the original and check that the answer reveal is legible on the intended screen. These are proposed checks, not reported test results. The pricing page also meters AI image occlusions separately from deck generations, so the study plan should consider both allowances. A single diagram-heavy course could have a different usage pattern from several text-only topics.


## Video generation should preserve source context

The homepage describes public YouTube input, transcription, language support and timestamped references. Those functions could make lecture material easier to revisit, but a transcript may include mistakes, incomplete spoken sentences or references to information shown only on screen. A card based on a short spoken passage can lose the qualification provided by the surrounding demonstration. The useful evaluation is whether the generated question can be checked against the relevant moment in the source, not merely whether a deck appears.

Use content you are permitted to process and retain the context needed to correct errors. For a tutorial, distinguish an explanation of an approach from an instruction that depends on a particular software version. For a lecture, check whether the question reflects the course's terminology rather than an invented simplification. No video was transcribed for this article. The publisher's video support does not establish permission to redistribute another creator's material or verify the factual accuracy of every public video.


## Translation can change what a learner is memorizing

The saved homepage advertises more than fifty languages and the ability to upload in one language and generate in another. This can help learners work with multilingual material, but the translation becomes part of the learning content. A fluent sentence is not sufficient if a technical term changes meaning, a grammatical distinction disappears or a name is translated incorrectly. Check both the source and the intended target-language terminology before treating the cards as authoritative.

For language study, decide whether the objective is meaning, spelling, pronunciation, usage or a particular grammatical construction. A card that tests one of these may not test the others. For subject study in a second language, preserve the terms used in the examination or workplace. These are suggested evaluation criteria rather than measured outcomes from AnkiDecks. The source set does not establish translation accuracy for any specific pair of languages, and this review has not inspected generated multilingual cards.


## Read the generation limits rather than only the monthly price

The captured pricing lists Free at four deck generations a month, Pro at fifty and Ultimate at two hundred. Document limits also differ: ten, one hundred and two hundred pages respectively, with separate character limits. Video limits are five, twenty and sixty minutes. These distinctions matter because a learner can have enough remaining deck generations while a particular source still exceeds the plan's document or video allowance. Compare the actual course material with the relevant dimensions before choosing a tier.

The displayed Pro price is $7.99 billed monthly or $60 billed annually, shown as $5 per month on the yearly view. Ultimate is displayed at $20 monthly or $144 annually, shown as $12 per month. These are captured figures, not a current quote. The lower per-month annual display should not be mistaken for monthly billing. Include image-occlusion and AI-feedback allowances in the comparison if those features are central to the study routine rather than focusing only on deck counts.


## Generation, study and subscription lifecycle are different allowances

The pricing page distinguishes generation quotas from unlimited spaced repetition and quizzes. Its FAQ says reaching a generation limit does not remove existing decks and that users can keep studying, quizzing and adding cards manually. It also describes immediate prorated upgrades, downgrades at renewal and continued access to decks after cancellation. These are useful commercial distinctions, but buyers should confirm their current account terms and the scope of any subscription change before relying on them.

For a semester-based budget, estimate the initial preparation burst separately from the ongoing review period. A learner may need more generation capacity at the start and mainly existing-deck study later. Do not assume that a paid plan is required forever to retain any useful output, or that unlimited study means unlimited AI generation. This review has not canceled, upgraded or downgraded an account. The analysis is based on the publisher's captured description of those lifecycle rules.


## Export gives portability, but the imported result still needs checking

The publisher says every plan can export an .apkg file for Anki, including the free plan. The homepage also explains that exported cards may not initially appear in the same order and gives creation-time sorting guidance. This is a useful reminder that the exported file and the receiving application's presentation are different stages. A successful download alone does not establish that the deck is organized or displayed as the learner expects on every device.

Before building an entire course around the workflow, export a small deck containing the card types and media you intend to use. Inspect the imported cards, images, answer behavior and organization in the actual destination. These are proposed checks, not an export test conducted here. Keep a recoverable copy of material you depend on and understand which study environment owns the review history. The saved pages do not resolve every synchronization or migration question between browser study and an existing Anki collection.


## Sharing, privacy and community decks require deliberate choices

The homepage describes public, private and link-only deck settings and says recipients can clone and export shared decks. That is an important distribution boundary. A link-only deck is a sharing choice, not a reason to place confidential course, workplace or personal information into material intended to circulate. Confirm the selected visibility and what a recipient can retain before sharing. Source-file privacy and the visibility of the resulting deck are related questions, but should not be assumed to be identical settings.

The publisher states that uploaded content is used for deck generation and not model training, and it displays a large community library and customer reviews. Those statements remain publisher-provided evidence here. No privacy implementation, retention process, review identity or learning result was independently verified. Community popularity does not establish that a deck matches your curriculum or contains correct answers. Inspect provenance and content rather than inventing testimonials or treating a visible rating as academic validation.
