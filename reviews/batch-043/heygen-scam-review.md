# HeyGen review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-18  
**Category:** AI video creation, avatars and localization platform  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

HeyGen is a video creation service built around AI presenters, voice and video generation, editing, and localization. Its consumer plans meter output through monthly credits and differ in video length, export quality, avatar features, and collaboration. The current monthly page lists Free at $0, Creator at $29, Pro at $49, and Business at $149, with plan-specific limits. The API is separately billed: the help center says API credits are standalone, no free API credits have been offered since February 2026, and concurrency limits depend on account type. Buyers should distinguish the free plan's noncommercial output license from paid-plan terms, review the paid-plan content license and applicable data settings, and obtain specific permission before cloning an identifiable person's voice or likeness.

## Editorial verdict

The public sources reviewed identify HeyGen as a named video platform and document plans, API billing, content rights, and voice-consent rules. They provide no source-based reason to call it a scam. This is a desk review, not a hands-on test, quality benchmark, or legal opinion. A buyer should check the exact plan and intended use before paying: Free output is limited to personal, noncommercial use under the terms reviewed; API credits are separate from studio subscriptions; and voice cloning requires documented permission. The terms also grant HeyGen a broad license over submitted content for service operation and improvement, so teams handling confidential material should review the applicable settings and contract before upload.

## HeyGen combines presenter video, editing and localization in one workflow

HeyGen's product pages describe a browser-based video workflow centered on digital presenters and generated media. A user can start with a script or a video brief, select an avatar and voice, generate a draft, then edit it in AI Studio. The pricing comparison also describes voice generation, translation, brand features, and integrations at different tiers. The platform is therefore best evaluated as a chain of steps: preparing the script, choosing a presenter, checking pronunciation, generating scenes, editing, exporting, and confirming rights for the final use.

Video Agent adds a prompt-driven planning step. HeyGen's help materials say planning and revisions to a video plan do not consume generation credits; credits are charged when the system generates media. It may combine avatar scenes, generated images or video clips, stock material, and motion graphics, so two projects of the same duration can consume different credits. A buyer should compare the estimated credit total shown before generation with the final result and the editing time still required. A polished sample does not establish how reliably the service will handle a particular script or brand.


## Studio subscription credits and API credits are separate budgets

HeyGen's monthly pricing page lists a Free plan with three watermarked videos per month, each up to one minute. Creator lists 600 credits, Pro 1,000, and Business 1,500. The paid plans differ in export resolution, maximum video duration, language features, shared workspaces and administrative controls. Those credits are not a universal count of finished minutes: a feature, model, translation mode, and generated asset mix can all change consumption. The in-product estimate before generation is more useful for planning than a simple division of credits by video length.

The API is a separate buying decision. HeyGen's API help page says API credits are purchased independently from ordinary plans and that free API credits ended in February 2026. It lists account-dependent concurrency and distinguishes older API plan credits from pay-as-you-go credits by expiry. An integration should therefore keep studio subscription, API credit balance, expiration, and concurrency as separate line items. Before estimating a campaign or production pipeline, test one representative request and record the precise endpoint, output, and debit shown in the developer account.


## Credit estimates matter more than headline video length

The Help Center describes Video Agent's standard mode as roughly 60 to 80 credits per minute and Seedance mode as about 180 to 240 credits per minute, while warning that actual use varies with duration, settings, and generated assets. It says using uploaded or stock media may reduce the number of generation steps. The same page says a user can review the estimated total before generation and see usage history afterward, but does not provide a line-by-line charge for each asset. That creates an important evaluation question: can your team forecast a batch closely enough to stay within its budget?

Run a small pilot with a short script, one localization, and one revision. Note the estimate before generation, the final credit charge, whether the output needs replacement scenes, and what happened when the script changed. Then repeat with supplied imagery rather than generated assets. Do not extrapolate from one simple clip to a long training course or a multi-language campaign. When a workflow has a fixed budget, include editing, retries, translation review, and API calls rather than counting only the first draft.


## Video length, language support and export quality change by plan

The pricing comparison lists videos of up to 30 minutes on Creator and Pro and up to 60 minutes on Business. Creator advertises 1080p export while Pro and Business list 4K export. The page also differentiates 30-plus languages on Free from 175-plus languages and dialects on paid plans, and offers additional voice, avatar and collaboration features at higher levels. A team should treat these as plan-specific ceilings, not a promise that every language pair, voice, or scene will be available with identical quality.

For localization, inspect more than the translated words. Review names, numbers, legal language, timing, captions, on-screen text, and whether the presenter remains plausible through the full clip. Ask a fluent reviewer to check a sample before publishing. For resolution, view the exported file on the screen and in the delivery channel that matters; a 4K option does not establish that every generated element has native 4K detail. Keep the original script and a human-approved version alongside each export so corrections can be traced.


## Commercial use depends on the plan and the terms attached to the output

The current Terms page distinguishes the Free Plan from Creator, Pro, and Business. It states that Free Plan output is licensed for personal, noncommercial and internal evaluation use, and says it may not be sold, monetized, used in advertising, client work, or other revenue-generating activity. For the named paid plans, the terms say users own their input and output between HeyGen and the user, subject to the agreement. A business evaluating the platform for client campaigns should not treat a free sample as cleared for paid placement just because it can be exported.

For Creator, Pro, and Business plans, the same Terms section gives HeyGen a broad license over uploaded and generated content to operate, improve, promote, provide, and develop services, including AI and machine-learning training. The page also notes that submitted content remains the user's responsibility and generated outputs may not be unique. The separate Voice Supplemental Terms say voice-specific terms do not themselves authorize general training and do not override applicable data-processing agreements or a separately agreed enterprise contract. These documents should be read together. Before uploading confidential client assets, check the exact current plan, data controls, DPA, and negotiated terms rather than relying on a headline ownership sentence.


## Voice and likeness workflows require permission, not just an upload

HeyGen's Voice Supplemental Terms say a user may reproduce their own voice or another person's voice only with prior, explicit, informed permission. The terms call for rights in the source recordings and recorded evidence that explains the intended purposes, sharing, commercial uses, and restrictions. A person must be at least 18 under those terms. Public-figure or political-candidate voice use has additional restrictions and may require HeyGen's prior written approval. Owning a recording, employing the speaker, or finding it online does not itself establish permission to clone it.

The help center also describes a consent-video requirement for video-based Digital Twins. These safeguards affect production planning: the person whose voice or likeness is used may need to participate, and a team must keep permission records and honor later restrictions or withdrawal. A buyer should assign a person to manage those records before creating avatars for a campaign or client. Review each deliverable for disclosure and context as well; permission to create a voice does not authorize a false endorsement or every possible message.


## A fair HeyGen evaluation uses a repeatable brief and a real export

Use a short script your organization is allowed to share. Write down the required presenter, tone, pronunciation, captions, visual style, language, duration, and delivery resolution. Generate one clip, inspect the result, and make one controlled revision. Record which parts required manual edits, how many credits were consumed, and whether the exported file kept its framing and audio in the intended channel. This is a proposed buyer test; Roseram has not run it or measured HeyGen output quality.

Repeat the same brief with an existing video editor or a competing avatar service if the decision is consequential. Compare the final files rather than a marketing preview. Include the time needed for script review, consent collection, export, revision, and policy approval. For API use, separately test the endpoint and asynchronous job lifecycle, then document credit consumption and concurrency. Stop before production if the workflow cannot meet your rights, disclosure, privacy, or cost requirements.


## Who may find HeyGen useful, and what should be compared?

HeyGen may suit a creator or team that repeatedly makes presenter-led explainers, onboarding material, localized announcements, or similar videos and can work within a credit budget. It is less compelling if the task depends on nuanced live performance, exact continuity across many shots, a bespoke editing pipeline, or strict limits on cloud processing of source material. Those are fit questions rather than findings of misconduct. A buyer should set the required output, rights, data-handling conditions, and expected number of revisions before comparing plans.

Compare HeyGen with conventional video editing, human-presenter production, and other avatar or localization systems using the same script and acceptance checklist. Include the API only if your workflow needs automation; it has separate credits and operational limits. Keep any conclusion specific: a tool might shorten the first draft but still require human review for names, claims, pronunciation, or consent. Choose the option whose complete workflow you can explain and budget, not the one with the most persuasive demo.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is HeyGen a scam?

The official sources reviewed identify an operating video platform and describe its plans, billing, content terms and consent requirements. This desk review found no source-based reason to call HeyGen a scam; it does not claim Roseram tested the service or verified any individual transaction.

### How much does HeyGen cost?

On the monthly pricing page checked November 18, 2026, Free was $0, Creator $29, Pro $49 and Business $149 per month. Credits and video limits differ. API credits are separately purchased, so a studio subscription is not an API allowance.

### Can I use videos made on HeyGen's free plan commercially?

The terms reviewed describe Free Plan output as licensed for personal, noncommercial and internal evaluation use. They say not to sell, monetize, use in advertising, client work or other revenue-generating activity. Check the current terms for your account before publication.

### Does HeyGen require consent for voice cloning?

The Voice Supplemental Terms require prior, explicit and informed permission to clone another person's voice, plus rights to the recordings and any requested verification. The rules also cover voices designed to imitate an identifiable person.

### Are HeyGen API credits included with a paid studio plan?

No. The API help page describes API credits as a standalone purchase, separate from standard HeyGen plans. Confirm the current API credit package, expiry, concurrency, and cost for the specific endpoint before building a production workflow.
