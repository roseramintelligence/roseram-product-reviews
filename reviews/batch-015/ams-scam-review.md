# AMS review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-25  
**Category:** Practice management and SOAP note software for small medical practices  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

AMS Software / Doctors Access provides practice-management and documentation software for chiropractors and small medical practices, including scheduling, patient records, SOAP notes, appointment reminders, billing-related workflows, patient statements and communication features. Its newer OneCare product is described as a web-based practice-management, documentation and patient-communication suite with eligibility checks, claim submission and ERA importing. AMS says pricing is customized to the practice and feature set, so buyers need a written quote covering licensing, hosting, support, integrations, migration and recurring fees.

## Editorial verdict

AMS is a specialist option for small practices that want configurable clinical documentation and administrative workflows with vendor support. Its long-running Doctors Access products and newer OneCare web suite may serve different setups, so confirm which product is being quoted and what data can move between them. Before relying on it for patient care, test note templates, backups, integrations, claims, patient messaging and record export against current clinical and regulatory requirements.

## Distinguish Doctors Access from OneCare

AMS Software’s site presents tools for chiropractors and small medical practices, including practice management, scheduling, SOAP notes and electronic records, patient statements, billing and patient communication. It separately describes OneCare as a web-based suite for practice management, documentation and communication, with patient records, voice-to-text, note shortcuts, card payments, eligibility checks, claim submission, ERA importing and two-way text messaging.

Do not assume every feature belongs to the same product, edition or migration path. Ask the salesperson to identify product name, hosting model, version, modules and supported integrations. If the practice uses an older Doctors Access installation, request a written explanation of how OneCare differs, whether both can operate together, and what is required to move patient and billing history.


## Evaluate clinical notes, scheduling and claims with real workflows

AMS describes customizable SOAP notes, a same-as-last-time workflow, scheduling, patient records and reminders. OneCare’s page describes claim workflows, eligibility checks and ERA importing. Test these against actual documentation rules, payer mix and front-desk steps. Use synthetic or otherwise permitted records; do not put identifiable patient information into a demo without an approved agreement and configuration.

Ask clinicians to complete a common visit note and an exception case. Verify authorship, timestamps, amendments and signatures. Have billing staff trace a test claim from encounter through submission, response and reconciliation. Test what happens when eligibility is unavailable or an ERA contains an unexpected adjustment. Record unresolved steps and ask whether they require manual work, an extra module or custom development.


## Request a complete custom quote

The AMS pricing page says each version is customized to practice needs and asks buyers to submit requirements for an accurate price. Request separate line items for license or subscription, cloud hosting, backups, support, updates, implementation, training, migration, integrations, messaging and any third-party clearinghouse costs.

Ask how fees change with provider count, locations, users, storage or claim volume. Confirm contract length, renewal notice, cancellation, export access after termination and costs for retrieving records. Compare the quote with the full cost of the current system over the same period, including staff time and support. Treat vendor time-saved claims as hypotheses for a pilot, not guaranteed outcomes.


## Plan migration, security and continuity

A practice-management platform may contain sensitive clinical, insurance and contact records. Review current privacy terms, security materials, backups, recovery objectives, access controls, audit history, hosting, retention and breach process with the vendor. Clarify how records, attachments and signed notes are exported and whether they remain readable without the product.

Before cutover, take a verified backup and reconcile a sample across demographics, insurance, diagnosis, visit notes, appointments and billing. Confirm reminder consent and patient communication preferences transfer correctly. Keep the former system available until clinical and billing owners approve migrated records and rollback. This desk review summarizes public information; it does not establish clinical suitability, security certification or hands-on performance.


## Features and decision criteria

- Doctors Access materials describe practice management, scheduling, SOAP notes/EHR, patient data, statements, billing and outreach
- OneCare is described as a web-based practice-management and communication suite with claims processing, eligibility checks and ERA import
- SOAP documentation includes customizable notes and a same-as-last-time workflow described by the vendor
- Pricing is customized; the vendor asks practices to request a quote based on needs and selected functionality

## Pricing considerations

AMS does not publish a standard list price on its pricing page. Request an itemized quote for each product, including setup, cloud services, support, integrations, migration, training, term, renewal and termination. Do not rely on generalized savings claims without calculating against your own baseline.
