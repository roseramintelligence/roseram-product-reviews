# Am I on AI review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-10-28  
**Category:** AI brand visibility research  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Am I on AI describes monitoring whether a business appears in AI-generated recommendations and related comparisons. The meaningful evaluation should preserve the question, observed answer and conditions of the observation. One mention is not a universal ranking, and an absence is not automatically evidence that a business needs more content or belongs in that answer.

## Am I on AI: define relevance before counting mentions

A brand appearing in an answer can be helpful, irrelevant or inaccurate. Decide which buyer questions matter and what a useful recommendation would mean in that context. Separate presence from factual accuracy and from the role the brand plays in the explanation. Counting those states together can create a metric that is easy to report but difficult to use responsibly.

The publisher describes visibility, comparison and action-oriented insight. Confirm the actual services, prompt conditions and reporting scope in the current offer. A result from one environment should not be assumed to represent what every user sees. Ask how observations are preserved and how uncertainty in interpretation is communicated to the operator.


## A prompt-to-answer review exercise

Write neutral questions representing real customer tasks and preserve the exact wording. Inspect the observed responses directly rather than relying only on a summary score. Check whether the answer actually describes the business correctly and whether any cited source supports the relevant statement. Repeat under the documented conditions to understand variability.

Include a question for which your business would not be an appropriate recommendation. This review does not report measured visibility improvements or validate a monitoring method. It proposes an evaluation that keeps the purpose of the question central rather than assuming that more mentions are always better.

A useful additional case contains an incorrect description of the brand. Ask what evidence the report preserves and which public information could clarify the confusion. Keep the observed error separate from an unproven claim about why it happened. A content change may be worth testing, but its existence alone does not establish that it caused a later change in an external answer.

Ask whether a reported recommendation would actually help the person who asked the original question. An appearance can be measurable while still being irrelevant or factually weak. Keep that qualitative review alongside mention counts. The business should improve information that serves a real audience, rather than optimize a visibility metric detached from the usefulness of the answer.
