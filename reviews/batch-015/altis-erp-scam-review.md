## Altis ERP: start with the process that crosses team boundaries

A business may lose information when sales, purchasing and field staff maintain different versions of the same request. Choose one recurring process and identify where the handoff fails today. Decide which record should become authoritative before comparing modules. A new platform can centralize disagreement without resolving the underlying definitions or responsibilities.

The publisher describes several operating functions and mobile or field-related work. Confirm the actual modules, deployment and configuration included in the proposed offer. A broad ERP description does not establish that your approval rules or exception cases are already implemented. The demonstration should follow your process rather than assume the product’s default sequence is the correct one.


## A field-change and office-reconciliation exercise

Create a fictional work request with an office owner and a field action. Introduce a change after work has begun and ask how the latest instruction reaches the people who need it. Inspect the resulting record from both sides of the handoff. The useful outcome is an understandable agreement about what was requested and what remains to be done.

Include an interrupted connection or delayed update in the evaluation discussion. Ask the publisher to explain the supported handling rather than assume every device is always online. This review does not verify synchronization, permissions or accounting behavior for an Altis ERP deployment. Those are requirements to demonstrate in the actual proposed configuration.

A useful additional case contains two similar requests that must remain separate. Ask staff to identify which completion and supporting record belong to each. This can reveal whether the process relies too heavily on memory or informal naming. A neat overview is less valuable when the organization cannot reliably connect a reported action to the original request and its approved changes.

Record which manual steps remain after the pilot and assign their owners. A partially automated process can be dependable when those responsibilities are clear, while a nominally complete suite can fail through an unowned handoff. Use the actual remaining work in the cost comparison rather than assuming that every step shown on a module diagram has disappeared from daily operations.
