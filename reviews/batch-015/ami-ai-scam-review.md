# Ami AI review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-24  
**Category:** AI sales development and outbound prospecting  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Ami is AiSDR’s AI go-to-market agent for prospect research, campaign drafting, outbound execution and performance feedback. AiSDR’s current pricing page lists pay-as-you-go at $1.50 per person prospected, with team plans quoted by volume; the product page also describes CRM integrations, suppression lists, email infrastructure and multichannel outreach. Evaluate consent and legal requirements, deliverability safeguards, data accuracy and human review before enabling campaigns.

## Editorial verdict

Ami may interest teams that want prospect research, personalization, campaign setup and response handling in one outbound workflow. Its usage-based entry point gives a buyer a way to test a bounded campaign, while team plans add support and integrations. The main risk is operational: poor targeting or overly automated outreach can damage sender reputation and annoy recipients. Start with a tightly scoped, lawful audience, enforce suppression and sending limits, and require human approval for messaging and exceptions.

## Understand Ami’s role in an outbound process

AiSDR describes Ami as an AI GTM agent that can learn a company’s positioning, identify target prospects, draft outreach, run campaign steps and suggest changes as responses arrive. Its pricing page lists prospect research, email and LinkedIn sequences, CRM synchronization, reply classification and campaign feedback as elements of the wider platform. The product is meant to connect strategy and execution rather than serve only as a text generator. The buyer should still define audience, offer, exclusions and what counts as a qualified reply.

Review each stage separately: the source of prospect data, how the system decides a person matches the ICP, which facts are used to personalize a message, how replies are classified and what actions trigger a follow-up. Ask what the system can send automatically and what requires approval. Test whether a sales rep can correct a wrong company detail, remove a lead across campaigns and inspect a full contact history without losing suppression status.


## Interpret pay-as-you-go and team pricing carefully

AiSDR’s official pricing page lists a self-serve rate of $1.50 per person prospected, charged daily, with no monthly fee. It describes separate team plans for higher monthly volumes and lists optional items such as a warmed mailbox, campaign-level assistance and a fully managed service. The page uses “prospected” as the billing unit; make sure the contract explains whether a person is charged when researched, added to a campaign, or actually contacted and how duplicates or invalid records are treated.

Model a conservative test with a fixed audience size and a hard spending cap. Include prospect charges, mailboxes or domains, CRM connections, team subscription minimums and human review time. Confirm whether data credits or prospects expire, how refunds work for inaccurate contacts and what is included in a team quote. Avoid comparing the $1.50 figure with software seat prices alone: it may include data or campaign functions that another vendor prices separately, but the total must be verified from your own setup.


## Protect sender reputation and recipient choice

Automated outbound can cause harm when targeting is irrelevant, messages misrepresent a relationship, or sending volume rises faster than recipients expect. Keep a documented lawful basis and jurisdiction-specific review for each campaign, honor opt-outs promptly, screen suppression lists before every send and avoid sensitive personal attributes. Use truthful sender identities, accurate claims and a straightforward unsubscribe mechanism. Follow the rules of the email provider and any social network used for outreach.

AiSDR lists sending limits, suppression lists, bounce checks, warmup and domain monitoring among its controls. Verify how each is configured and which safeguards can be disabled. Start slowly with a small, relevant segment; watch bounce, complaint, unsubscribe and positive-response rates. Pause automatically if hard bounces or complaints exceed your thresholds. A warmed sending domain does not make unsolicited or noncompliant outreach acceptable and does not guarantee inbox placement.


## Run a bounded pilot with measurable acceptance criteria

Before launching, approve the ICP, geography, exclusions, source data, message claims, follow-up cadence, daily cap and pause conditions. Test the workflow in draft mode with internal reviewers and a set of sample prospects. Check company names, job titles, personal references, links, opt-out handling, CRM logging and reply routing. Require approval for sensitive or ambiguous responses and make clear who owns a human follow-up after a positive reply.

Measure against a baseline: qualified positive replies, meetings actually held, inaccurate personalization, opt-outs, bounces, complaints, time spent reviewing and cost per qualified outcome. Attribute vendor success stories and aggregate benchmarks as vendor-reported, not as expected results for your campaign. This guide is based on AiSDR’s public Ami and pricing pages; it is not hands-on testing, legal advice or an endorsement of any particular outreach strategy.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does Ami AI cost?

AiSDR lists pay-as-you-go pricing at $1.50 per person prospected, with daily billing and no monthly fee; team plans are quoted based on volume. Optional mailboxes and managed services add separate costs.

### Can Ami run outbound without review?

AiSDR describes automated modes, but buyers should configure suppression lists, sending limits, approved claims and human review. Start in a draft or co-pilot workflow and authorize sending only after checking targeting and content.

### Does AiSDR guarantee booked meetings?

No. AiSDR publishes historical directional benchmarks, but outcomes depend on offer, market, targeting, data quality, deliverability and execution. Treat vendor metrics as claims to validate in a controlled pilot.
