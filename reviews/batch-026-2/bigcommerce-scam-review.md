# BigCommerce review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-18  
**Category:** Hosted ecommerce platform  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

BigCommerce is a hosted ecommerce platform with managed infrastructure and self-service Core, Growth and Scale plans plus contract-based Performance pricing. Its current US page lists Core at $39 monthly or $29 per month billed annually, Growth at $105 or $79 annually, Scale at $399 or $299 annually, and Performance starting at $1,499 per month billed annually. The 2026 pricing also applies plan thresholds and fees to some orders processed through Open Payment Providers; embedded providers are exempt from that fee. Core and Growth plans can automatically move up after trailing-12-month sales thresholds, while Scale has a monthly GMV cap and overage terms. BigCommerce offers a 15-day trial and says it does not offer refunds. These published terms describe a real platform and do not substantiate a scam allegation.

## Editorial verdict

BigCommerce is a legitimate hosted commerce product with substantial native store capabilities. The main buyer diligence is the 2026 pricing model: plan price, sales threshold, automatic plan movement, GMV calculation and the extra fee for eligible Open Payment Provider orders must be evaluated together. It may suit a store that wants managed infrastructure and embedded integrations; merchants with a preferred open gateway should model fees before migrating. This is a desk review, not a live store test.

## What BigCommerce provides

BigCommerce is a hosted commerce platform that provides a storefront, product and order administration, checkout, integrations and managed infrastructure. A merchant does not need to maintain the underlying web server as with a self-hosted installation. Its current product materials describe responsive storefronts, checkout, sales channels and multiple payment integrations across plan levels.

That managed model can make launch and operations simpler, especially for a team that wants commerce-specific tools rather than assembling a store from separate components. The trade is a platform subscription with defined plan thresholds and rules. A buyer should assess total cost and data workflows before moving a large catalog or a busy checkout.


## The 2026 plan names and public price schedule

The US page lists four current tiers: Core at $39 per month or $29 per month billed annually; Growth at $105 or $79 annually; Scale at $399 or $299 annually; and Performance, custom-priced starting at $1,499 per month billed annually. Annual price equivalents are paid for the annual term. The page’s 2026 schedule replaced earlier plan names, so old screenshots and legacy comparison pages may not match current checkout.

A store comparing an older quote should verify which plan and fee schedule now apply to its account. Record the full amount, payment cadence, plan label, tax, thresholds and included support. The subscription alone is not the complete cost: payment processor charges, any applicable Open Payment Provider fee and optional support add-ons also matter.


## How GMV thresholds and automatic plan changes work

BigCommerce says Core is for stores up to $30,000 in trailing-12-month GMV and Growth up to $100,000. Its FAQ describes automatic upgrades after the relevant threshold is exceeded, with notice and timing depending on monthly or annual billing. Scale is listed with a $33,333 monthly GMV cap and a 0.9% overage on GMV above that cap. Performance is positioned for larger, contracted operations.

The platform defines its threshold GMV using gross order value after a 10% reduction. That figure can differ from the merchant’s own gross sales report or payment-provider totals. Reconcile the dashboard definition against internal reports before a store approaches a threshold. A sales increase can be positive for the business and still trigger a larger subscription or fee, so include that step-up in forecasts.


## Open Payment Provider fees require a provider-level check

A notable part of the new pricing is the fee for orders processed through an Open Payment Provider on self-service plans. The current page lists 2.0% on Core, 1.0% on Growth and 0.6% on Scale for eligible provider orders; embedded providers are not subject to this fee. BigCommerce separately lists embedded options and states that provider classification depends on which provider actually processes each order.

Do not assume that merely offering an embedded option at checkout makes all orders fee-free. If a store uses a mix of payment providers, the applicable treatment can vary order by order. Before migrating, map each payment method to the current embedded or open classification, estimate the share of checkout volume per provider, and add both the platform fee and processor rate to the cost model. Confirm the current list with BigCommerce before making a decision.


## A 15-day trial is a useful test; the vendor says purchases are nonrefundable

BigCommerce advertises a 15-day trial. Its pricing FAQ says it does not offer refunds, making it important to check plan selection and billing before moving from evaluation to paid service. The FAQ says Core, Growth and Scale can be canceled through the control panel and that billing stops when canceled; Performance can involve a contract with negotiated terms.

Use the trial to run a representative catalog import, inventory update, shipping calculation, tax configuration and end-to-end checkout. Test the integrations the business actually needs. A storefront that looks good with sample products may behave differently with real variants, discounts, shipping zones and payment settings. Keep the trial within its dates and make the paid decision before the account converts.


## Checkout and operations matter more than a feature checklist

A commerce platform is judged at the moment a customer tries to buy. Test mobile checkout, shipping estimates, tax display, discount codes, inventory changes, order notifications and refund handling. Then test the less visible administrative tasks: importing a catalog, updating several prices, exporting orders, creating roles and correcting a product error.

BigCommerce’s feature set is broad, but the fit depends on the store’s exact workflow and integrations. Ask which systems are authoritative for products, stock, customer records and fulfillment. A clean integration diagram helps identify duplicate data, sync delays and ownership conflicts. If a feature depends on a paid extension or outside partner, include that recurring cost and support boundary in the comparison.


## Hosted infrastructure reduces server work, not every operational duty

With a hosted platform, BigCommerce manages the store’s hosting environment and platform updates. The merchant still owns product accuracy, pricing, fulfillment, customer communication, account access and the security of connected systems. Staff permissions and third-party integrations should be reviewed just as carefully as the storefront design.

Create separate user accounts for operators and restrict access to the tasks each person performs. Keep domain ownership and billing contacts under company control. Document the payment provider, tax service, shipping tool and marketing integrations in use. A managed service can remove server maintenance, but it cannot decide whether the merchant’s policies, product information or payment settings are correct.


## Support availability and contract terms vary by plan

The current comparison states that chat and email support are available on all plans, while phone support is included starting at Growth and is also included on higher tiers. BigCommerce says Core customers can add phone support under the current support terms. Performance includes a more dedicated support arrangement, subject to its contract.

A buyer who depends on phone escalation should price that access rather than assume every paid plan includes it. Review the service agreement for Performance and save the exact add-ons selected. During the trial, test how to open a support request and where to find billing information. We did not independently measure response time or support quality, so this review makes no service-level claim beyond the published plan descriptions.


## Migration from another store needs product and order validation

Moving to BigCommerce can involve importing product records, mapping categories and variants, preserving customer and order history, redirecting URLs and rebuilding checkout settings. A platform migration is not finished when products appear in an admin table. The buyer should validate images, inventory, tax, shipping, discounts, analytics and email notifications in a test environment.

Start with a representative product set that includes the most complicated variants and fulfillment rules. Keep the previous store available while verifying the new one. Prepare a URL map before launch and monitor redirects after the domain switch. For a large catalog, estimate the hours required to clean data and reconcile imports; the subscription price does not include that labor by default.


## Alternatives and total-cost comparison

Compare BigCommerce with Shopify, WooCommerce and other commerce platforms on checkout, operations, extensibility, payments, staff workflows, hosting responsibility and total fees. A managed platform can reduce maintenance; an open system may offer more control but requires the team or host to handle updates and reliability. Neither approach is automatically cheaper.

Use a common set of monthly orders, average basket, payment mix and annual sales. Model the subscription, payment processing, provider fees, support add-ons, integrations and migration time. Then run the same checkout scenarios in each platform. A difference of a few dollars in base subscription may be irrelevant next to payment fees or labor, so compare the complete operating model.


## Who may suit BigCommerce, and who should pause

BigCommerce may suit an established store seeking managed infrastructure and native commerce features, especially when its payment providers and integrations align with the business. A merchant near a plan threshold may appreciate knowing the published upgrade rules and using the plan dashboard to see relevant sales data.

Pause if the store relies on a payment provider whose additional fee changes its unit economics, if sales are near an upgrade threshold, or if a contract-based Performance tier is being considered without a full quote review. A business should also test its catalog and fulfillment integrations before committing to a migration. These are commercial fit checks, not findings of deception.


## BigCommerce SCAM review verdict: legitimate service, model the 2026 fees

Build a forecast using the current account’s GMV calculation, order mix and processors. Test a representative checkout, confirm cancellation and renewal terms, and get a written Performance quote if relevant. ## Features and decision criteria

- Hosted storefront, checkout and ecommerce administration
- Core, Growth, Scale and contracted Performance plans
- Payment-provider classifications that affect additional order fees
- Sales-based plan thresholds and support levels vary by tier

## Pricing considerations

US dollar prices and 2026 plan terms shown on BigCommerce’s official pricing page when checked November 18, 2026. Taxes are additional. GMV thresholds use BigCommerce’s defined calculation, which reduces gross order value by 10%; this may differ from a merchant’s internal sales report. Open Payment Provider fees are separate from payment processing and apply only to eligible orders processed through providers classified as Open. Verify current provider classification, threshold calculations and renewal terms in the account.
