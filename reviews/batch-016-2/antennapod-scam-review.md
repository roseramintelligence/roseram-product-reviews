# AntennaPod review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-24  
**Category:** Open-source Android podcast manager  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

AntennaPod is an open-source podcast manager for Android. It can subscribe to RSS feeds, download episodes and support offline listening; the project distributes official versions through Google Play and F-Droid and publishes its source under GPL-3.0.

## Editorial verdict

AntennaPod is a strong fit for Android listeners who want a flexible, open-source podcast app and prefer managing their own subscriptions and downloads. It is free to obtain from the project’s stated official channels, but it is not an iPhone, desktop or podcast-hosting service. Choose it for listening control, then evaluate RSS compatibility, backup behavior and Android-device support on your own library.

## Know the job the app is built for

AntennaPod is a listener’s podcast manager, not a tool for producing or hosting a show. It is designed around subscribing to feeds, managing episodes and listening on Android. That makes it relevant to someone who wants control over a listening queue and offline downloads, while a creator still needs a separate host to publish a feed.

The official project describes the app as flexible and open-source. A listener can begin with a familiar podcast RSS feed and see whether the available library and episode controls match their habits. Build a small queue, test an episode download and listen offline before moving a large subscription list. This review is based on project documentation; it is not an installed-device test.


## Download from the project’s official channels

AntennaPod’s download page says its official versions are available from Google Play and F-Droid. The project explicitly cautions that other stores may list copied versions without permission and that the maintainers do not update or support them. This makes download source an important part of the trust and update process.

The same page notes that the F-Droid repository is maintained by the F-Droid team and updates can take a few days. If your device uses managed app distribution, check which store is approved and how updates arrive. For a work phone, do not bypass company controls just to install a preferred player.


## Review the open-source license and maintenance model

The project repository identifies GPL-3.0 as the software license and links to contributing, testing and issue-reporting information. Open source gives technically capable users a way to inspect the project and its changes, but it does not guarantee that every device combination will be supported or that a feature will arrive by a particular date.

For a personal listening app, decide how much you depend on a synchronized queue, podcast discovery or cloud backup. Review the app’s current documentation for those requirements and keep a copy of any configuration you cannot easily reconstruct. A project’s public repository helps with transparency, but should not be confused with a third-party security audit.


## Check listener needs and accessibility before migrating

Before switching players, compare the controls you use each day: playback speed, skip intervals, queue ordering, downloads, sleep timer and episode cleanup. Import a few subscriptions first and listen to a complete episode through your usual Bluetooth or Android Auto setup. Confirm that new episodes appear when expected and that offline playback behaves as intended.

If you rely on transcripts, synchronized listening across devices or a desktop player, verify those needs separately because this is an Android-focused app. Keep the old app installed until subscriptions and settings have been transferred successfully. This analysis does not claim that AntennaPod supports every platform or that it was tested on a particular handset.
