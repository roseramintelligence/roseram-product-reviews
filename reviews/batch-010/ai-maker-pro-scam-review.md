## AI Maker Pro: decide what the artwork is allowed to change

Artwork can contain deliberate proportions, typography and symbolic details that should remain intact. Before generating movement, identify those elements and describe the motion you actually need. A result that looks cinematic can still be a poor adaptation if it changes the subject or introduces a misleading action.

The publisher presents an artwork-to-video workflow and community examples. Examples show possible directions, but they do not establish what your image will produce or how much correction it may require. Evaluate the current tool using an image you can legally process and a specific publication purpose. Keep generation separate from acceptance.


## Review the whole clip rather than a selected frame

Use one authorized artwork and a bounded motion brief. Inspect the beginning, transitions and end of the generated clip, including how objects and text behave across frames. View it at the size and aspect ratio required by the destination. A small preview can conceal a change that becomes obvious in the final placement.

Ask what the current workflow allows when a clip is nearly useful but needs correction. Confirm the downloadable output and the treatment of unsuccessful generations. This review does not claim that Roseram measured rendering quality, production time or retention performance for AI Maker Pro. It describes how to evaluate the finished asset.

A useful source artwork contains text or a geometric feature that should remain stable throughout motion. Review every part of the clip where that feature appears, rather than relying on a single acceptable frame. If the intended use cannot tolerate deformation, make that an explicit rejection criterion. This helps distinguish an entertaining animation from an asset suitable for the particular commercial or creative brief.
