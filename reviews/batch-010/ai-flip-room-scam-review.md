## AI Flip Room: preserve the property while changing the presentation

A staging image can become misleading if generation moves a doorway, expands a window or changes the apparent size of a room. Before testing the tool, list the fixed elements that should remain unchanged. Keep those separate from furniture and decorative choices you are intentionally exploring. This gives the reviewer a standard based on the property rather than general visual appeal.

The publisher describes interior styles and photo-based staging. A style choice does not establish an implementable design or the cost of reproducing it. Ask what corrections are possible in the specific workflow and inspect your own image rather than relying only on selected examples. A realistic surface is not proof of spatial accuracy.


## A room-photo pilot with clear rejection criteria

Use a photograph whose processing you are authorized to permit. Choose one room with visible structural details and compare the result against the original at full size. Reject outputs that obscure material defects or imply a physical alteration that has not occurred. Keep the source image and the reason for each accepted edit.

If the image will appear in a property listing, review the relevant publication requirements and explain the staged nature of the image appropriately. This article does not certify a listing or provide a legal determination. It describes the editorial and operational questions that should be resolved before a synthetic property image reaches prospective viewers.

Choose a room with an awkward but genuine feature, such as a fixed pillar or low ceiling. Ask the reviewer to verify that the generated staging has not quietly removed it. That feature may be central to a viewer’s decision even when it makes the image less attractive. Preserve the difference between improving presentation and changing the represented property when deciding which output can be published.
