## AI Image Editor AI: specify both the edit and the invariants

An editing brief should say what must change and what must stay the same. If you ask for a different background, decide whether object shape, lighting direction and existing text need to remain fixed. Without those constraints, a visually attractive result can be judged successful even though it changes the important subject.

The publisher describes several image-editing workflows and continuity-related features. These descriptions should guide a test, not be treated as a guarantee of perfect consistency across every input. Use a representative asset with details your reviewer can check. Distinguish a tool’s claimed editing category from verified performance on that asset.


## Evaluate a revision sequence rather than one output

Choose an authorized test image and request a small change. Then request a correction to that result and inspect whether earlier accepted details survive. Review the whole image, including labels, edges and secondary objects, at the intended publication size. Keep the source and each reviewed version together.

If the image contains a person or a commercial product, verify that the final representation remains appropriate and accurate for its use. This review does not establish consent, ownership or a universal right to publish generated output. Read the current terms and make those decisions using the actual inputs and destination.

A useful editing scenario changes a background while keeping a small product label readable and unchanged. Compare the result with the original rather than assuming that an untouched-looking area was preserved. This reveals the practical standard of fidelity your project needs. If the workflow cannot meet that standard consistently enough for the task, include a specialist editing or manual review step in the production plan.
