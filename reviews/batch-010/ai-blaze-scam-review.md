# AI Blaze review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-18  
**Category:** AI writing assistant and browser extension  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

AI Blaze is a Chrome extension from Blaze Today Inc that places an AI sidebar and reusable prompts in the browser. The official guide describes writing, rewriting, summarizing and translating page text; keyboard shortcuts and context-menu actions can insert or replace content in a text field. Page content is included as context by default and can be disabled for a chat or saved prompt. The Chrome Web Store listing checked November 18, 2026 showed version 1.9.34, updated October 3, a 4.9 rating from 267 ratings and 100,000 users. The listing discloses handling personally identifiable, payment, authentication and activity data. AI Blaze promotes free access, but its Terms also describe paid upgrades and the general Text Blaze pricing page does not clearly establish a distinct AI Blaze price. The official privacy policy and HIPAA guide describe provider transmission and storage differently for prompts versus live requests. This review does not establish fraud or test the extension.

## What AI Blaze is and where it runs

AI Blaze is a Chrome browser extension with an AI sidebar and reusable prompt library. The official overview frames it as a writing and editing assistant for webpages: draft a response, revise selected text, correct grammar, summarize material or translate a passage without moving to a separate AI chat. The Chrome Web Store listing also describes models including GPT-5 and Claude Sonnet. This browser placement is its core convenience: a saved prompt can be triggered in the same page where the user is writing or reading.

That convenience depends on browser access and context. It is not a general-purpose desktop coding agent or an independent foundation model. The product uses external AI providers through the Blaze service, according to its privacy and HIPAA disclosures. The Chrome Web Store listing is for a browser extension and says Add to Desktop; buyers should check current browser and operating-system support rather than assume a native app. For teams standardized on another browser, verify compatibility before making it part of a workflow.


## How the prompt sidebar and shortcuts work

The help guide describes opening the sidebar from the extension icon, asking it to draft or improve text, then choosing whether to insert the response into the active textbox, replace the original text, or copy the result. Saved prompts can be given a name and shortcut, used from the sidebar, invoked from a textbox or selected through Chrome’s context menu. The extension also supports custom prompt inputs such as text fields and dropdowns, according to its listing.

A reusable prompt can save repetitive instructions, but it does not ensure that every generated message is appropriate or correct. Review proposed text before sending, especially in customer support, employment, healthcare, financial or legal workflows. When replacement is selected, confirm which text will be overwritten. For a new team, start with a small set of prompts that clearly identify allowed context, output style and human review steps. Do not treat an automatic insertion as approval to publish or send the result.


## Page context is on by default

The getting-started guide says page content is available to AI Blaze by default, and that highlighted text or text in the current box may also be included. It shows a control to disable page context for a chat and a separate setting for saved prompts. This behavior can make a request more relevant: a summary prompt can see the current article, while a rewrite can use the text being edited. It also means the extension may handle more than the words a user typed into the prompt field.

The control is consequential on authenticated sites, internal dashboards or pages containing customer records. Before using the extension in a sensitive environment, learn how page context behaves in chats, saved prompts and form fields; turn it off if the page is not needed; and make sure team prompt settings do not silently re-enable it. A saved shortcut could be used from many sites, so set its context intentionally. The company’s own HIPAA guide says page content may contain PHI and is included by default unless the user opts out.


## What the privacy policy says it collects

AI Blaze’s privacy policy lists account information, transaction-related information, support communications, payment-related information, and data users enter into the application, such as snippets. It also says it may collect feature-usage and engagement data, including the number of snippets created or used. The Chrome Web Store disclosure lists personally identifiable, financial and payment, authentication and user-activity data as categories the extension handles. The store listing includes the developer’s declaration that data is not sold outside approved use cases or transferred for unrelated purposes.

These disclosures are useful, but they are the company’s statements and declarations rather than a Roseram audit. The privacy policy says user-provided data may be retained while an account is active and for a reasonable time afterward. The HIPAA guide separately says live request and response data is not logged or stored beyond fulfilling the request, while saved prompts are stored in Google Cloud Platform in the United States. Read these statements by data type: a saved prompt, account record, snippet and live inference request are not the same object or retention period.


## Model providers and request routing

The privacy policy says that when a user runs an AI snippet, the snippet and associated context may be sent to OpenAI, Anthropic or other model providers. The HIPAA guide says prompts are routed through Blaze servers to the designated provider and back, and identifies OpenAI, Anthropic and Google Gemini. The Chrome Web Store listing mentions GPT-5 and Claude Sonnet. Provider availability and routing may change, so confirm the actual model selected in the current product rather than relying on an older screenshot or static page.

A user’s own ChatGPT or Claude subscription is not described as the billing mechanism in the reviewed sources; the disclosures indicate Blaze routes requests using its configured provider relationships. The exact included usage allowance and model-specific caps were not made clear on the public pages reviewed. Before relying on the extension for frequent use, inspect current plan details, throttles and provider availability in the account. Avoid assuming that a free install implies unrestricted inference or that provider terms are identical across every model choice.


## AI Blaze pricing: free install, unclear standalone plan boundary

The AI Blaze landing page says it is free to get started, and the Chrome Web Store listing offers an install action. Its Terms also describe optional paid upgrades and AI Blaze Business subscriptions billed per user. However, the public AI Blaze pages reviewed do not present a distinct, current AI Blaze plan table that clearly states the standalone price, included inference volume or model limits. That is a point to resolve in the account flow before a team commits to it.

Blaze’s general Text Blaze plan page lists a Free tier, Pro at US$2.99 per month when billed annually or US$3.49 monthly, and Business at US$6.99 per user per month annually or US$8.39 monthly. That page concerns Text Blaze plans and should not be treated as a definitive AI Blaze price unless the checkout explicitly links the subscription to AI Blaze and names its entitlement. Confirm the product name, billing cadence, trial status, model availability, usage caps, tax and renewal amount before authorizing a recurring charge.


## Cancellation and refund terms

The AI Blaze Terms say paid upgrades renew automatically unless canceled before the end of the subscription period. They say an upgrade can be canceled from the Account page in the AI Blaze Dashboard or by emailing support@blaze.today, and that a refund can be requested from support. They also describe per-user billing and prorated additions for Business subscriptions. Buyers should save the checkout receipt and confirm whether the account page clearly identifies the plan and next renewal date.

The Terms do not promise that a refund request will be approved. Ask support how a refund is assessed and when cancellation takes effect if the product does not meet the need. If an organization adds seats mid-cycle, confirm how the prorated amount will appear and whether discounts apply; the Terms state that discounts do not apply to those prorated periods. These are standard subscription diligence points, not evidence of misconduct. The important decision is whether the actual paid entitlement and renewal match what the user thought they selected.


## Prompt sharing and team administration

AI Blaze’s sharing guide describes shared folders with owner, editor and viewer roles. Owners can change permissions or delete a folder, editors can add or revise prompts, and viewers can use prompts without editing them. The guide says recipients receive an invitation and changes sync to members when a shared prompt is updated. This can help a team reuse consistent answer formats and reduce repeated prompt setup.

Shared prompt libraries can also distribute outdated instructions or overly broad data requests quickly. Assign an owner to review prompt changes, restrict who can edit sensitive templates, and keep private or customer-specific information out of shared prompts unless the organization has approved that use. Test role changes and removal of a member before rolling out to a large group. The public help page explains basic folder permissions but does not by itself establish enterprise audit logging, formal change approvals or a contractual service level for the AI Blaze product.


## Healthcare and sensitive information: read the HIPAA guide carefully

The official HIPAA guide says it applies to paid AI Blaze users and that a user who wants to process protected health information needs a Business Associate Agreement. It says requests are routed to the chosen AI provider and are not logged or stored by Blaze beyond fulfilling the request. It also says saved prompts are stored in Google Cloud in the United States and advises users not to put PHI in those saved prompts. The guide describes provider-specific commitments for OpenAI and Anthropic and says Google does not train on prompts.

The same guide says AI Blaze does not independently ensure HIPAA compliance and that users are responsible for configuring and using it compliantly. Page contents can be added by default and may contain PHI; the user must opt out when those contents should not be sent. This is not a blanket guarantee that any account, prompt or workflow is compliant. A healthcare organization should obtain and review the BAA, verify its current scope and subprocessors, test settings, consult its own compliance team and confirm that the selected provider and usage path are covered before processing PHI.

## Practical risks when using an AI browser extension

An extension that helps inside webpages has to interact with the page or selected text, which creates a different decision from pasting a sentence into a standalone chat window. AI Blaze’s documentation explicitly describes page-context access and prompt insertion; its Chrome listing discloses categories of information handled. Users should install it only from the official store link, verify the publisher identity, review requested permissions at install and use browser profiles that separate personal and work accounts if policy requires it.

For a low-risk trial, use public text and a test account. Confirm that context can be disabled, check what appears in the prompt before sending, and verify whether generated text replaces or inserts into a field. Do not expose secrets, customer records or private messages without permission from the data owner. If an incident occurs, stop using the feature, preserve the account and browser details, and contact the vendor. ## Alternatives and how to compare them

For occasional rewriting, a browser’s built-in writing assistant or a model provider’s website may be sufficient. For high-volume standardized text, Text Blaze’s own snippet functions may handle repetitive templates without a model call. Other browser assistants may offer different model choices, page access controls, plan limits and team administration. Compare current policies and permissions rather than assuming products in the same category have equivalent data handling.

AI Blaze’s distinguishing proposition is the combination of a page-aware sidebar, dynamic saved prompts and shortcuts that work in text fields. A buyer should compare how many clicks each alternative needs, whether it can insert text safely, which pages it reads, what is sent to providers, and whether a team can manage prompt permissions. Also compare total cost, including a required Text Blaze subscription if the account checkout makes one necessary. A free extension can still have privacy, usage and administration costs.


## Who may benefit from AI Blaze

AI Blaze may be useful for a person who writes across web applications and repeats the same kinds of edits or responses. Saved prompts with shortcuts can reduce copying between tools, and dynamic form fields can adapt a template without making each request from scratch. A small team can share a curated library when its prompts are stable and approved. These benefits are most plausible when the work uses non-sensitive material or when the organization has reviewed the data route and controls.

It may not fit a company that cannot allow browser extensions to read webpage context, needs a guaranteed zero-retention arrangement for every data type, requires a public standalone price and exact inference cap before account creation, or cannot use external AI providers. Healthcare organizations need the BAA and controls the guide describes; sensitive-data users should disable context or avoid the tool unless their security group approves it. A buyer who wants to use personal provider subscriptions directly should verify whether that integration exists, rather than assuming it from the model names.
