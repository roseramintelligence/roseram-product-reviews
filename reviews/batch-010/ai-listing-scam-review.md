## AI Listing: product facts should constrain keyword ideas

Create a factual product sheet before generating copy. Include material, dimensions, included items and important limitations, then distinguish supported benefits from marketing preferences. A keyword that attracts interest is not appropriate if it describes a property the item does not have. The draft should help communicate the product rather than expand its apparent capabilities.

The publisher describes titles, descriptions and tags for several selling environments. Confirm the specific destination you use and inspect the generated output against that destination’s current requirements. A tool’s optimization language is not evidence of ranking, listing approval or a sale. Those outcomes depend on more than the text alone.


## A listing-review exercise using one representative item

Choose a product with a variant and one important limitation. Compare the generated title and description with the product sheet, then ask a reviewer to explain what they believe they would receive. If the answer differs from the actual offer, correct the copy before publication even when it sounds persuasive.

Look for unsupported compatibility, safety or performance claims and remove invented endorsements. Review the relationship between keywords and the person’s likely intent. This article does not report measured search rankings or conversion performance for AI Listing. It proposes a useful editorial acceptance process for generated commercial text.

Try a product whose limitation is important to the intended customer, such as a requirement for a separately supplied component. Check whether the generated copy preserves that qualification prominently enough to be understood. Omitting it can make a listing sound smoother while making the offer less accurate. Evaluate the draft by what a buyer would reasonably expect to receive, not merely by its apparent keyword coverage.


## Keep listing maintenance in the cost estimate

Confirm the current generation and revision allowances, and account for changes in product facts after the initial draft. A listing that becomes inaccurate after a stock or specification change can create more support work than the tool saved. Keep a clear owner for the final text and the underlying factual record.
