# Base44 review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-10-24  
**Category:** Full-stack AI app development  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Base44 describes app generation with backend, authentication, hosting and integrations in one platform. Its pricing separates message and integration allowances. The buying decision should connect those capabilities to a complete user journey and a realistic ongoing workload.

## Editorial verdict

The official product, documentation and pricing pages describe a concrete offer. They do not establish that a generated app has correct permissions or that every project will fit a listed allowance. No paid build, security audit or fraud finding is claimed in this review.

## An integrated stack still needs an application contract

For a client portal, write down who may create, inspect, change and delete each kind of record. Then build two harmless synthetic accounts with different roles as a proposed acceptance exercise. The question is whether the finished behavior matches your access rules. A platform supplying authentication does not by itself establish correct authorization for your app.

The official pages describe a broad integrated scope. Our analysis treats that as a reason to inspect the full journey, not just the generated screens. Include a failed submission and an unauthorized access attempt in the proposed review. These tests were not run by Roseram, and no vulnerability is alleged. They are normal checks for any builder handling private business records.


## Message credits and integration credits answer different budget questions

The plan comparison separates allowances for different activities. Estimate the building and revision work separately from repeated app actions after launch. A successful prototype with few records may not represent a busy month. Keep the published billing period visible when comparing the annual figures here with a monthly offer from another service.

Ask which external services your project calls and who pays those bills. A connected payment or messaging provider may have its own terms and costs. The amounts shown here are dated public observations, not an invoice or a negotiated offer. Do not interpret a large allowance as proof that every possible workflow will remain within budget.


## Ownership includes data, configuration and an exit path

Before committing to a substantial business tool, list the artifacts needed for a handover: the application representation, records, connection configuration, domains and operational instructions. Ask which exports and transfers are available in the plan you intend to buy. We have not exercised an export or established equivalent behavior on another host.

A useful proposed trial is to give another teammate an authorized maintenance task after the first build. Can they explain a validation rule, make a correction and identify how to recover? The result should be assessed against the business promise. Do not infer that a general no-code label removes the need for someone to own maintenance and integration decisions.

A useful additional trial distinguishes identity from application permission. Give the two synthetic users records with clearly different owners and test the permitted read and update journeys. Record both the response and the retained record after a rejected attempt. Do not assume that a hidden button establishes the underlying restriction. The desired evidence is a result consistent with the access contract, not merely a screen that appears different for each role. This is an original proposed acceptance check, not a report that we created Base44 users, inspected its enforcement or found a weakness in the platform or any customer application.
