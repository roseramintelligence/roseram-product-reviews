## What AITDK SEO Extension puts beside a page

The captured product page explains opening a website and accessing the panel from the extension icon or a keyboard shortcut. This positions the tool as a convenient place to inspect several signals, not as a replacement for every original data source. A buyer should identify whether the intended task is checking the page in front of them, investigating a competitor or diagnosing why their own search performance has changed. Those tasks require different evidence.

This AITDK SEO Extension SCAM Review uses saved publisher pages for the extension and pricing. It does not report installing the extension, granting browser permissions or comparing its results against live search data. Advertised user counts and store ratings are publisher statements rather than independently verified figures here. ## An on-page inspection is not the same as Google's indexed version

The product page describes checks for titles, descriptions, canonical URLs, headings, alternative image text and other page details. Those checks can help identify an issue in the version of a page being inspected. They do not automatically establish what a search crawler previously received or what is currently stored in a search engine's index. A recently corrected page can still appear differently in an external report until it is revisited. When using a browser inspection, record the exact URL, time and page state so the observation can be compared with the relevant server response or owner-side search report.

For a controlled evaluation, prepare a small page whose expected tags are known. Inspect its title, canonical and robots directives with the extension, then compare them with the original response and the rendered document. Include a redirect and a page that intentionally excludes indexing. These are suggested tests, not results measured for this review. The aim is to understand whether the panel is showing response headers, document markup or another derived interpretation. That distinction matters when a developer needs to identify whether a problem originates in server metadata, a client-side update, a cached deployment or a search engine's older observation.


## A GEO score is a diagnostic opinion rather than a citation guarantee

The captured page describes a zero-to-one-hundred GEO score divided into crawler access, readability, structured data, content and trust-related checks. That is the publisher's framework for organizing observations. It is not described here as an official score supplied by Google or an AI assistant. A higher number does not independently establish that a page is correct, useful or likely to be cited for a particular query. Inspect the actual checks and determine whether each recommendation addresses a concrete issue relevant to the intended audience, rather than optimizing a number without understanding its inputs.

An example makes the distinction clearer. A page can expose readable structured data while making an unsupported factual claim. Another can contain a useful answer but receive a lower tool score because a particular implementation detail is absent. This review has not evaluated AITDK's scoring method, so it does not state which recommendations it gets right or wrong. It recommends checking the evidence behind each one. Keep technical access, content quality and observed search outcomes separate in reporting. A recommendation becomes useful when the team can explain the issue, implement an appropriate change and verify the resulting behavior without promising a citation that no tool controls.


## Traffic and authority figures need source and coverage context

The extension page advertises monthly visits, engagement measures, traffic sources, domain rating and backlink information for a viewed website. The saved descriptions do not establish the collection methodology, error bounds or freshness of every figure. Do not present a competitor's displayed traffic as if it were direct access to that company's analytics account. Ask what the measure represents, how recently it was updated and which sites or regions it covers. A number with several decimal places can still be an estimate or a partial observation. Its apparent precision does not answer those questions.

For your own domain, compare a narrow metric with the corresponding original account while preserving the time period and definition. For competitor research, use directional observations and document uncertainty rather than inventing exact customer acquisition or revenue estimates. This is an interpretation recommendation, not a benchmark of AITDK's data quality. Authority scores should also be identified by their source and method instead of treated as an official search-engine assessment. A convenient side panel can reduce the work of collecting signals, while the person using it remains responsible for explaining what those signals can and cannot support.


## Keyword density and heading outlines should serve the reader's question

The product page describes phrase counts from one to five words, filtered stop words and a searchable heading outline. Those tools can make repetition and document hierarchy visible. They do not establish a target density that guarantees better search performance. A page can repeat a product name frequently and still fail to answer the reader's actual question. Start with the purpose of each section: what information should the visitor obtain, what supports it and how should the heading help them find it? Then use the outline to identify gaps or confusing repetition rather than adding keywords simply to increase a count.

A practical check can compare the visible outline with the article's navigation and section content. Confirm that one main heading identifies the page, subsections have useful names and anchor links reach the intended answer. Inspect repeated phrases for readability and factual purpose. These are proposed review steps, not a test performed with the extension. Avoid turning a density report into a reason to add generic paragraphs. The useful result is a document that communicates clearly and preserves product-specific details. Counts can help inspect that result, but they are not a substitute for editorial judgment or a measure of whether every claim has a source.


## Link, image and structured-data panels should lead to verifiable fixes

The captured feature list includes internal and external links, image alternative text, social metadata, hreflang and a structured-data tree. These views can be useful for finding a broken destination, missing descriptive text or a schema property that does not match the page. Evaluate the findings against the actual intended behavior. A decorative image and an informative product diagram do not necessarily need identical treatment. A canonical alias and a genuinely missing content page should not be fixed by the same response. The panel is a way to inspect details, not a reason to apply one blanket rule to every URL.

For structured data, verify that the declared information is also supported by visible content. Do not add a customer rating, price or review count merely because a schema field is available. For language alternatives, check that the referenced pages actually exist and correspond to the stated language. The review has not measured AITDK's ability to detect those errors. It proposes them as useful acceptance cases because they distinguish a readable report from an accurate implementation change. A successful workflow should let an operator locate the relevant source, correct it and recheck the specific issue without changing unrelated page behavior.


## Reverse AdSense results are clues, not complete ownership evidence

AITDK's page describes detecting AdSense publisher identifiers on a page and finding other sites associated with the same identifier. That can support a research lead, but it should not be converted automatically into a legal conclusion about who owns every domain. Websites can use shared advertising arrangements, historical identifiers or other configurations whose meaning requires additional evidence. The captured description does not establish the completeness or current validity of every association. Record what was observed and distinguish that from an interpretation about organizational relationships, especially before publishing claims about another business.

The same caution applies to domain information gathered through RDAP or DNS. A registration date or nameserver can help investigate a technical question without proving the identity, conduct or financial condition of a seller. Use current first-party records and appropriate corroborating sources for consequential claims. This review does not describe any specific publisher network or ownership relationship discovered through AITDK. It evaluates the advertised research function. A useful tool can surface evidence worth following while the reviewer's responsibility is to avoid turning an association into a stronger finding than the underlying records justify.


## The free plan and paid lookups have a defined separation

The saved pricing page states that core analysis tools remain free and describes credits as applying to Backlinks and Reverse AdSense lookups, at one credit per lookup. It lists a free monthly credit allowance and separately explains that generators on the website do not consume extension credits. These are publisher-described entitlements, not a verified account balance. A buyer who needs only on-page inspection should determine whether the free tools cover that task before choosing a paid plan. Someone repeatedly researching backlinks has a different usage requirement and should estimate the relevant lookup volume.

The advertised separation also means running out of lookup credits should not be interpreted as the entire extension becoming unusable. The captured FAQ says the two credit-consuming functions pause while the other tools remain available. This review has not exhausted an account's allowance or tested that behavior. During evaluation, inspect how the balance, action cost and remaining access are shown. Confirm whether repeated queries are billed each time and what input defines a lookup. A clear usage model helps a team budget research activity without confusing a free installation, a recurring credit allowance and unlimited access to every data service.


## Billing periods and expiry rules affect research capacity

The pricing capture displays Starter and Pro figures alongside annual billing amounts. It explains that a yearly purchase costs ten monthly periods and supplies the year's credits upfront. The saved annual totals are $100 for Starter and $200 for Pro, while the displayed monthly figures are $10 and $20. Confirm the selected cadence and current checkout before purchase rather than treating the discounted monthly equivalent as a one-month offer. The plan comparison also distinguishes traffic-history depth and source detail. Evaluate those differences against the actual research period your team needs.

The FAQ says monthly credits last through the billing cycle and are replaced rather than accumulated. Annual credits are described as available for twelve months, with the balance and expiry visible in settings. Those rules change the practical value of an allowance for a team with irregular research. A busy project may benefit from upfront capacity, while a light user could leave a balance unused. The review does not recommend a particular payment term without an actual usage estimate. Record the expected number of meaningful lookups and the period over which they will occur, then compare that with the stated expiry rather than only the advertised total.


## Failed lookups, cancellation and refunds should be tested within the offer

The saved pricing FAQ says failed or empty lookups are refunded automatically and that cancellation takes effect at the end of the paid period. It separately describes a seven-day refund window with conditions involving whether paid features have been used or no more than five percent of credits have been spent. Those are distinct statements. Do not translate cancel-anytime language into an unconditional right to a refund after extensive use. Review the current written offer and obtain clarification if a specific purchase depends on it. This article reports the saved language without determining an individual's contractual eligibility.

In a controlled evaluation, retain the starting balance and inspect a known unsuccessful lookup if one occurs naturally. Avoid manufacturing unwanted requests simply to consume provider resources. Confirm where account receipts, balance history and cancellation controls can be found. These are recommended operating checks, not observations from a paid AITDK account. A team should know how to identify an unresolved charge or request and which support route handles it. A refund statement is useful evidence, but the review cannot claim that a refund was requested, accepted or delivered when no such transaction has taken place.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does an AITDK GEO score prove a page will be indexed?

No. The saved product page describes a proprietary diagnostic framework. A score does not independently establish a search engine's indexing decision, citation or ranking.

### Which AITDK actions consume credits?

### Do monthly credits accumulate?

The saved FAQ says monthly credits expire with the billing cycle and are replaced at renewal, while annual credits arrive upfront and last twelve months. Check the balance and expiry shown in the account.

No. This desk review examines saved publisher pages and does not report an installation, granted browser permissions, live benchmark or completed refund.
