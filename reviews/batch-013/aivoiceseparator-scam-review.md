# AIVoiceSeparator review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-10-28  
**Category:** AI vocal and instrumental separation  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

AIVoiceSeparator advertises vocal and instrumental extraction with credits or a Patreon membership. This review examines duration-based metering, daily allowances, audio artifacts, file outputs, optional lyrics, account linking and stated retention policies.

## Editorial verdict

This desk review does not establish that AIVoiceSeparator is a scam or certify the business as safe. The cited publisher material describes an offer that can be evaluated against your requirements. Before paying, verify the seller, the current terms, any cancellation rules and the capabilities that matter to your use case. The discussion below separates publisher claims from suggested buyer checks; it does not report hands-on testing or an independent security audit.

## AIVoiceSeparator is a paid audio-separation service

AIVoiceSeparator's captured homepage describes a service that separates a recording into vocal and instrumental outputs. It offers file uploads and links from specified media platforms, together with optional lyric transcription. The main purchasing paths are prepaid credits and a Patreon-based Pro membership. That makes it important to distinguish the audio operation from the billing method. The publisher says both payment paths use the same processing engine; the membership changes the allowance rather than advertising a separate higher-quality separation tier. A buyer should evaluate the output and the usage rules independently.

This AIVoiceSeparator SCAM Review assesses saved first-party homepage and pricing material. No recording was uploaded, no membership was purchased and no separation benchmark was reproduced for this article. The search-oriented title is not a finding that the service is fraudulent. The relevant evaluation concerns the charging unit, duration limits, audible results, file handling and account connection. Public descriptions can explain what is offered, but they cannot establish whether a particular recording will produce acceptable stems. That question requires an authorized sample and a clearly defined listening or production requirement.


## The advertised song price has a duration condition

The captured pages headline a price of twenty-five cents per song, while the pricing FAQ explains that this unit covers up to seven minutes. Longer clips are described as consuming one song unit for each started seven-minute interval, with a maximum clip length of twenty minutes. Those details change the meaning of a simple per-song comparison. A short single and a long rehearsal recording can fit within the upload limit while consuming different amounts of prepaid credit. Check the current calculation before using the headline figure in a project budget.

As an illustration of the captured rule, a recording slightly over seven minutes would cross into a second started interval. That is a calculation from the published description, not a charge observed during a purchase. Build a planning list using the actual durations of the recordings to be processed, and confirm whether silence, video duration or other file properties affect metering. Ask how an aborted or failed job is treated. The saved pricing says customers pay only for successful separations, but the precise operational definition of success should be understood before processing a large batch.


## Prepaid credits and Pro solve different volume problems

The pricing capture describes prepaid packs beginning at one dollar and says purchased credits do not expire. It separately presents Pro as a thirty-dollar monthly Patreon membership with six songs per day. These are different usage patterns: credits allow an irregular workload to be paid for by the unit, while the membership has a recurring charge and a daily allowance. The publisher's monthly capacity illustration assumes repeated use across the month. It should not be mistaken for an unrestricted monthly pool that can necessarily be consumed in one session.

Estimate demand by day as well as by month. Someone who processes many recordings on a single weekend may evaluate the offers differently from someone who processes a few every day. Confirm the reset time, handling of unused allowance, treatment of longer files and relationship between Pro usage and existing credits. These details are not fully resolved by the captured summary. A sensible comparison uses the number of accepted outputs the user expects to obtain under the relevant limits, rather than dividing the monthly fee by a theoretical maximum that does not match the person's working schedule.


## Patreon adds an account-linking step to membership billing

The captured pricing FAQ says Pro is purchased through Patreon and activated by using a connection control on the service's account page. It says cancellation is managed through Patreon and that access continues for the paid period before returning to prepaid usage. That describes a two-system relationship: the billing platform records the membership and the processing service recognizes the entitlement. A successful Patreon payment is not, by itself, a check that the correct AIVoiceSeparator account has received the intended allowance. Verify the connected identity and entitlement before relying on it for a deadline.

Keep the membership receipt and check which account is signed in when connecting access. Ask what the support process is if the billing state and processing allowance disagree. For cancellation, confirm the effective date in the billing platform and review the service's displayed access state separately. This review has not tested activation, cancellation or reconciliation between the two systems. The operational point is that a recurring membership should have an understandable lifecycle, including a way to resolve mismatches without repeatedly purchasing access or assuming that signing out cancels the underlying subscription.


## Input support does not mean every recording is equally suitable

The homepage lists several audio formats, including MP3, WAV, M4A, FLAC, OGG, WebM and Opus, and also mentions MP4 or MOV uploads for video clips. It advertises limits of one hundred megabytes and twenty minutes. These are eligibility constraints, not guarantees of separation quality. A recording can meet the technical limits while containing crowd noise, reverberation, overlapping voices or instrumentation that makes the desired output difficult to isolate. Define the intended result before choosing a sample or interpreting the presence of a supported file extension as evidence of a suitable workflow.

Check the actual source encoding and preserve an original copy. A compressed recording does not regain missing detail merely because it is exported to a lossless container. If video is involved, ask whether only the audio is returned and how the user should reconnect it with the visual material. The captured pages describe audio processing rather than a full video-editing workflow. A useful pilot compares the returned stems with the original at matched listening levels and examines the passages that matter for the project. The assessment should focus on usable material rather than the ability to complete an upload.


## Listen for artifacts in the parts you intend to use

The publisher describes a three-model ensemble and names BS-Roformer, Mel-Roformer and MDX23C. It also presents a numerical separation-quality comparison against another model baseline. Those are first-party descriptions and a publisher-supplied performance claim, not results independently reproduced here. Model names and aggregate scores can explain the advertised approach, but they do not establish how a particular singer, instrument or recording will sound after separation. A buyer interested in rehearsal tracks may tolerate different artifacts from someone preparing a exposed vocal edit for a finished production.

Select listening checkpoints before processing. Include quiet entrances, dense choruses, sustained notes and moments where vocals overlap with similar-frequency instruments. Check for residual accompaniment in the vocal stem, missing vocal components and audible texture changes. These are proposed evaluation criteria, not defects observed in AIVoiceSeparator. If a comparison with another service is needed, use the same authorized input, comparable output settings and a consistent listening procedure. Avoid treating a single attractive example or a published benchmark number as a universal result across musical styles and recording conditions.


## Loudness normalization is separate from source separation

The captured material advertises EBU-R128 loudness normalization alongside the separation pipeline. That is a processing description, not proof that a returned stem will automatically fit every mix or playback context. Separating the components of a recording and changing their loudness are different operations. A user should compare output levels carefully so that a louder result is not mistaken for a more accurate separation. Ask whether the available outputs have undergone normalization and whether any settings or documentation explain how the original dynamics are treated.

For production use, inspect the stem's level and dynamics in the software used for the next stage. Keep headroom for later mixing and compare important passages with the source. The review does not establish a particular peak level, mastering specification or clipping behavior for the service. It recommends checking the delivered file rather than inferring those properties from the normalization label. A rehearsal workflow, a remix preparation task and an archival extraction can have different requirements. The appropriate acceptance standard should reflect the actual destination of the audio instead of assuming that one processing choice answers every use case.


## Output formats should match the next editing step

The homepage and pricing pages describe MP3, WAV and FLAC output options and distinguish a default compressed format from lossless choices. The pricing also refers to three files per song: vocals, instrumental and the original. Confirm the current output package before purchasing around a particular downstream requirement. A producer who needs separate components for editing should check exactly which stems are delivered; a two-component vocal and instrumental split is different from an arrangement offering independent drums, bass and other instruments. The captured interface explicitly discusses a two-stem mode in connection with transcription.

Download a small authorized job and verify file names, duration, synchronization and import behavior in the intended editor. Check whether the original and separated files align without manual trimming. This article has not performed that import test or measured timing differences. Keep a clear folder structure so the source, processed stems and later edits are not confused. Lossless delivery can preserve the output as encoded, but it does not establish that the separation itself is lossless in the musical sense. The practical question is whether the available files support the next task without additional assumptions or avoidable conversion steps.


## Lyrics transcription adds a separate accuracy question

The publisher says an optional lyrics control runs Whisper on the isolated vocal stem and returns SRT, LRC and plain-text files. It describes a modest additional processing time and notes a two-stem restriction. These are useful workflow details, but lyric transcription and music separation should be assessed independently. A recognizable vocal stem can still contain difficult diction, harmonies, background voices or repeated phrases that create transcription errors. The existence of subtitle formats does not establish that the words or their timing have been checked by a human.

Use a short section whose words are known and compare the transcript with the authorized reference. Inspect timing around line changes and repeated choruses, then review how punctuation and non-vocal passages are represented. These are suggested checks, not transcription results from this service. If the files will be published, make editorial review part of the workflow rather than treating an automatic transcript as ready for release. A generated subtitle file can save formatting effort while still needing substantial correction. Budget for that correction when deciding whether the optional feature improves the actual project.


## Link ingestion does not grant rights to reuse the audio

The captured interface supports YouTube and SoundCloud links and directs users of some other platforms toward video uploads. It explicitly tells users to process only material they have rights to use. That distinction is central to evaluation: a service's ability to retrieve or transform a file does not establish the user's permission to publish, remix or redistribute the result. For a business workflow, identify the origin of the recording and the approved purpose before creating separated derivatives. This review does not provide a rights determination for any recording or platform.

Prefer an owned recording or a clearly authorized sample when evaluating the service. Keep the permission record associated with the project and avoid interpreting a successful download as a license. If an external link fails, confirm the supported access conditions rather than repeatedly submitting unrelated versions or assuming every public page is supported. The sources do not establish universal platform access or a guarantee that links remain usable. Separating technical compatibility from permitted use makes it easier to choose an appropriate test and prevents the processing tool from being mistaken for a source of reuse authorization.


## Retention statements should be evaluated as service policies

The homepage says input and output files are automatically deleted after seven days and describes an account-based period for downloading them again. It also says audio is processed on a private GPU in Thailand, is not used for model training and is not shared between users. Those are publisher statements. This review has not inspected the infrastructure, verified deletion or tested access controls. The phrase about local GPU processing should not be confused with processing entirely on the user's own computer; the described workflow still involves sending a file to the service.

Before uploading sensitive or unreleased material, review the current terms and ask about the details that matter to the project, including earlier deletion, backups, access handling and any contractual confidentiality requirements. The captured text's reference to an opaque job identifier does not independently prove isolation between accounts. For ordinary use, download accepted outputs promptly and maintain the user's own project copies. A published retention window can guide operational planning, but it is not a substitute for verifying that the provider's handling is appropriate for the particular recording and the obligations attached to it.
