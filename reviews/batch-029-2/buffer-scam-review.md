# Buffer review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-10-23  
**Category:** Social media publishing  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Buffer offers social content planning and publishing. Its paid comparison is priced per channel, making the number of connected profiles a central part of the buying decision.

## Editorial verdict

The official pricing and publishing pages describe a concrete service. We did not connect accounts, measure delivery reliability or verify audience growth. A buyer should evaluate the exact channels, approval needs and total bill; the reviewed material does not establish fraud.

## Count profiles before estimating the bill

The most useful comparison starts with an inventory of the accounts you actually manage. A creator with one professional profile has a different requirement from an agency managing several brands. Count each destination, assign an owner and distinguish a required channel from an experiment. Then use the selected billing period consistently. A per-channel rate should never be presented to a client as the full cost of an entire multi-brand account.

Our buying recommendation is to compare the planned quarter rather than only the first day. Ask what happens when a client leaves, a second brand joins or another team member needs approval access. Record the applicable cancellation and renewal terms from checkout. The figures in this review are observations from a public comparison; they are not an invoice, negotiated offer or promise about the amount a particular account will pay.


## A useful trial tests the editorial handoff

A scheduler can move a post through a calendar without improving the quality of the post itself. Write down who drafts, verifies claims, checks the destination link and approves release. For an affiliate campaign, include the disclosure in that checklist. Inspect the final link on each channel rather than assuming that a copied caption preserves the intended referral parameters and presentation.

Our proposed trial uses one benign post, a deliberate correction and a canceled draft. Confirm the timezone, account identity and scheduled destination before any release. Verify the resulting post in the original social service when authorized. Save a record of the outcome and any platform-specific differences. We did not perform this trial, and no delivery-rate or time-saving figure is implied by recommending it.


## Separate publication, attention and commercial outcomes

The analysis should distinguish a draft, a queued item, a published post and a customer action. Those stages answer different questions. If an agency reports results to a client, agree in advance which source establishes publication and which establishes a sale. A post appearing in a calendar is not evidence that an audience saw it, and a click does not by itself establish a commission.

Treat analytics as inputs for a specific editorial decision. Ask whether a clearer offer, better landing page or different topic would help the audience. Avoid interpreting a small change in attention as proof that the scheduler caused a revenue increase. Keep account permissions under review and revoke access when a person or contractor leaves. These are operating controls we recommend, not findings about a Buffer security incident.

## Features and decision criteria

- Content planning and scheduling
- Multiple social-network connections
- Plan-dependent analytics
- Team approval options in the paid comparison

## Pricing considerations

Observed 23 October 2026 with yearly billing displayed. These figures are per channel, not a flat account-wide monthly quote. Check channel count, monthly-billing options, trial conditions, taxes and the checkout total.
