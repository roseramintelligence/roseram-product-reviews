# Clientjoy review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-24  
**Category:** Agency CRM and client portal  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Clientjoy combines lead tracking, proposals, e-signatures, invoices, booking pages, email sequences and a branded client portal for agencies and service businesses. Its current public pricing page lists US$19 per user monthly or US$199 per user annually, with a 14-day trial that starts billing automatically unless canceled.

## Editorial verdict

Clientjoy may suit a small agency that wants its lead pipeline, proposals, invoices and client-facing portal in one workspace. Its per-user annual price can be easier to compare than a collection of separate tools, but the advertised 14-day trial converts to a paid plan unless canceled. Review email limits, storage, payment workflow and migration before committing customer records to a new system.

## Review the whole client journey

Clientjoy positions itself as an agency workspace that follows a client relationship from lead capture through proposal, invoice and portal. That bundle can reduce context switching for a small service business. It still matters whether the client portal reflects how you actually deliver work and whether your accounting process can use the invoices and payment records it produces.

Map one real journey: a lead submits a form, a team member qualifies the request, a proposal is sent, an agreement is signed and an invoice is issued. Identify which data is entered once and which steps require a separate system. A feature list does not show whether the handoff is smooth for your customers.


## Understand per-user pricing and trial conversion

Clientjoy’s current pricing page lists US$19 per user monthly or US$199 per user annually. It advertises a full-featured 14-day trial and says the paid plan starts automatically when the trial ends unless canceled. The page says a reminder arrives three days before that date.

Count every team member who needs a login and multiply by the chosen term. Add tax, any external payment processing and tools you keep. Put the trial end date on a calendar and confirm the exact renewal price in account settings rather than relying on a reminder email.


## Check the email and storage allowances

The pricing page lists 5 GB of file storage per workspace and 1,000 emails per month per workspace. Those limits can matter for an agency with several employees, large client attachments or a regular sequence. Ask how overages or send restrictions work before relying on the platform for time-sensitive follow-up.

Review sender authentication, consent records, unsubscribes and delivery logs. An email-sequence feature should be used with permission-based contacts and clear opt-out handling, not as a reason to add scraped addresses. Test an internal sequence before connecting a customer list.


## Compare a unified suite with individual tools

Clientjoy’s pricing page frames the product as a replacement for separate CRM, proposal, scheduling and invoicing subscriptions. Compare those costs using your actual team size and existing tools. Include the value of integrations, accounting exports, client access and the cost of changing a mature process.

A unified product can simplify administration, but a gap in one critical area may require keeping another service. Check proposal templates, e-signature status, invoice numbering, taxes, payment gateways and how records export. Do not assume that replacing several subscriptions automatically creates the savings shown in a vendor calculator.


## Pilot with a low-risk client workflow

Create a test client and run through booking, proposal, signature, invoice and portal access. Verify what a client can see, how an invoice status changes and whether a closed project can be archived. Test on a phone and ask a first-time user to complete the steps without coaching.

Before importing active clients, confirm privacy terms, data export, account closure and cancellation details. Keep trial and renewal dates visible to the billing owner. This review is an analysis of the official pages, not a claim that Roseram tested Clientjoy or measured agency savings.


## Features and decision criteria

- Lead and opportunity pipeline
- Proposals, e-signatures and invoices
- Branded client portal, meeting booking and email sequences
- Workspace limits include storage and monthly email capacity

## Pricing considerations

Clientjoy lists a 5 GB file-storage allowance and 1,000 emails per month per workspace, with free portal access for clients. It says sales tax may apply and the trial sends a reminder three days before ending. Confirm seat count, limits, trial date, renewal amount and cancellation in the account flow.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does Clientjoy cost?

The official page lists US$19 per user monthly or US$199 per user annually, with sales tax potentially additional.

### Does Clientjoy’s free trial automatically renew?

Yes. The pricing page says the plan begins automatically after the 14-day trial unless the user cancels before then.

### Are clients counted as paid users?

Clientjoy says team members are paid users and clients can use the portal without a separate client seat charge.
