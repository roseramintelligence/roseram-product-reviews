# Close CRM review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-24  
**Category:** Sales CRM and communications  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Close combines lead and pipeline management with calling, email, SMS and sales workflows. Its current pricing page lists Solo at US$19 monthly or US$9 per user monthly billed annually, Essentials at US$49 monthly or US$35 annually, Growth at US$109 or US$99, and Scale at US$149 or US$139; call and SMS usage is billed separately.

## Editorial verdict

Close may fit a small sales team that wants customer records, pipeline activity and communication in one place. Its seat price is only part of the bill because calling and SMS are usage-based, and advanced features vary by plan. Evaluate consent controls, import quality, mobile workflow and usage costs before moving a contact database. A CRM can organize follow-up; it cannot make an unsolicited message welcome or guarantee sales.

## Map the sales work you want the CRM to hold

Close combines a lead database and opportunity pipeline with email, calling, SMS and workflow tools. It may be useful when sales representatives currently switch between records and communication tools to understand the next step with a prospect. Start by mapping how a lead enters the system, who owns it and what useful follow-up looks like.

A CRM should make a legitimate customer conversation easier to manage. Define the source of each contact and the permission basis for each channel. Do not treat an imported address or phone number as consent to send marketing messages. Keep opt-outs and suppression status visible to every connected workflow.


## Compare seat prices with communications usage

Close’s current public pricing shows monthly and annual rates for Solo, Essentials, Growth and Scale. The annual display lowers the effective monthly seat amount but requires an annual commitment. Calling and SMS are charged based on usage, so a high-volume team can spend more than the seat price alone suggests.

Estimate users, leads, call minutes, phone lines, SMS volume and add-ons using your own expected activity. The pricing page gives approximate usage examples, not a fixed bill. For US messaging, Close notes that A2P 10DLC registration is required. Confirm registration, destination fees, taxes, trial credits and usage limits before forecasting the monthly total.


## Choose a plan for team visibility and permissions

The feature matrix varies by plan in areas such as lead visibility, role-based access, predictive dialing and call-recording retention. A solo seller may not need every control, while a team handling many accounts may need clearer ownership and separation of customer records. Compare the exact permissions available on the plan you intend to use.

Before importing a full database, test a small set of records. Verify field mapping, duplicate handling, source attribution, unsubscribe flags and how activity history appears. Confirm how an employee’s access is removed when they leave and whether assigned leads are reassigned safely.


## Keep outreach useful and permission-based

Built-in sequences can make follow-up consistent, but automation also makes it easier to send irrelevant or repeated messages. Use a clear reason for each contact, respect channel-specific consent and opt-out requirements, and cap the number and frequency of messages. Review a sample of every sequence before it starts.

Segment customers by an actual business need rather than by a vague keyword match. Include a way to stop future marketing communications and ensure the stop request reaches every automation and connected list. This review does not provide legal advice or endorse unsolicited bulk outreach.


## Run a measurable pilot

Choose one sales process and track response time, qualified conversations, follow-up completion, unsubscribes and cost per useful outcome. Compare the result with the existing workflow, including staff setup time and data-cleaning effort. Do not infer a sales increase from a feature list or vendor case study.

Review the trial period, money-back terms and cancellation timing before inviting a team. Close’s current page describes a 14-day trial and plan-specific refund details; check the account flow for the terms that apply. This article is based on public product and pricing information, not a Close account test.


## Features and decision criteria

- Lead and opportunity records with sales pipelines
- Email, calling and SMS in CRM workflows
- Call and message usage billed separately from seats
- Plan limits and access controls differ by tier

## Pricing considerations

Close’s pricing page states calling and SMS are usage-based and passed through at cost; its public estimate is around US$0.02 per outbound calling minute and phone numbers around US$1 per month. US SMS sending requires A2P 10DLC registration. Verify taxes, billing period, included AI credits, usage and renewal terms before signup.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does Close CRM cost?

The current pricing page lists Solo from US$19 per user monthly or US$9 per user monthly on annual billing; other plans have higher rates.

### Are calling and SMS included in the seat price?

Close says calling and SMS are usage-based and passed through at cost, so usage adds to the subscription.

### Does a CRM guarantee more sales?

No. It organizes customer records and follow-up. Results depend on the offer, audience, consent, process and execution.
