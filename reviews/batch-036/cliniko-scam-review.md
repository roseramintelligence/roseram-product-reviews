# Cliniko review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-24  
**Category:** Allied health practice management software  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Cliniko is practice management software for allied health clinics, with appointment and patient management and a monthly price based on practitioner count. Its US pricing page lists tiers from US$45 per month for one practitioner to US$395 for 26–200; a 30-day trial is available without payment details, and SMS credits cost US$0.10 per message.

## Editorial verdict

Cliniko is designed for clinics that want scheduling, patient records and practice operations in a single service. Its published pricing is unusually explicit: the monthly subscription scales by practitioner count, while the company lists unlimited admin and reception users, locations, storage and patients across subscriptions. Clinics should still check local compliance, migration scope, telehealth requirements and third-party integration fees before moving patient workflows.

## Practice operations for allied health clinics

Cliniko describes itself as practice management software for allied health practitioners. Its pricing structure is based on practitioner count, and the company lists every Cliniko feature in each subscription. That is a straightforward model for comparing a solo practice with a multi-practitioner clinic, although clinics should verify that their specific appointment, billing and patient workflows are supported before migrating.

The pricing page says subscriptions include unlimited admin users, reception users, locations, file storage and patients, plus support, monitoring and backups. That can simplify account sizing for a clinic that has non-practitioner staff or several locations. The practice should still confirm user permissions, access logs, backup restoration and data export needs with the vendor.


## Compare the price by practitioner count

Cliniko’s published US monthly rates are US$45 for one practitioner, US$95 for two to five, US$145 for six to eight, US$195 for nine to twelve, US$295 for thirteen to twenty-five and US$395 for twenty-six to two hundred practitioners. The company says features do not incur separate upgrade fees, while optional SMS costs US$0.10 per message.

Cliniko says customers can switch to annual billing in settings and receive one month free each year after the initial trial. Third-party integrations may carry separate charges, so a clinic should list the external apps it depends on and add those costs to its estimate. Confirm the pricing region because the cited figures are displayed in US dollars.


## Use the trial and migration support to test fit

The company offers a 30-day trial without entering payment details. Its pricing page notes that some features, including telehealth, may require card details before they are unlocked, and that an account with a saved card is billed automatically at the end of the trial unless cancelled. Mark the trial end date and verify which card-dependent features you plan to evaluate.

Cliniko says it can help migrate data from many other systems at no charge. Before starting a transfer, ask for the supported source formats, records included, timeline, validation process and rollback plan. Compare a sample of patient, appointment and billing records after migration; never assume a free transfer means every data type or custom field is included.


## Review privacy, patient access and connected apps

A practice system contains sensitive information, so evaluate data processing, account roles, access to patient records, auditability and the provider’s security documentation. Cliniko lists 24/7 systems and security monitoring and constant backups as subscription inclusions; clinics should ask how backups are retained and restored and what contractual terms apply to their jurisdiction.

Connected apps can expand workflow options but may introduce separate fees and data-sharing arrangements. Inventory which services will receive patient or appointment information and check consent, access and retention requirements for each. Ask how an integration can be disconnected and whether the clinic can export the underlying data.


## Who should shortlist Cliniko?

Cliniko deserves consideration by allied health clinics that want a practice-management subscription priced by practitioner count and a trial before migration. Its explicit monthly tiers make the basic software cost easy to estimate. Clinics with unusual regulatory, telehealth, billing or interoperability needs should verify those details during the trial and obtain written answers before committing.

This guide is based on Cliniko’s public pricing and FAQ pages, not a hands-on clinical workflow test. It is not a compliance assessment. Confirm local privacy requirements, contract terms and migration acceptance with your own practice and legal advisors.
