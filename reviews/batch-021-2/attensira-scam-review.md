# Attensira review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-10-29  
**Category:** AI search visibility and generative engine optimization  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Attensira describes monitoring buyer questions across AI engines and coordinating research, content, outreach and website changes under user approval. This review examines sampling, billing, permissions and outcome measurement.

## Editorial verdict

This desk review does not establish that Attensira is a scam or certify the business as safe. The cited publisher material describes an offer that can be evaluated against your requirements. Before paying, verify the seller, the current terms, any cancellation rules and the capabilities that matter to your use case. The discussion below separates publisher claims from suggested buyer checks; it does not report hands-on testing or an independent security audit.

## Scope of this Attensira review

Attensira describes software for monitoring how a brand appears in AI-generated answers and organizing work intended to improve that visibility. Its captured pages describe prompt tracking across answer engines, research into cited pages, content and website changes, outreach drafts, approval steps, credit billing and before-and-after measurement. This review examines that product proposition and the questions a buyer should check. It does not establish that Attensira is a scam, guarantee that it is safe or report a hands-on account test.

The sources are unusually explicit about operating boundaries: the company says some material is illustrative, that drafts wait for approval, that repository changes can be submitted for review, that LinkedIn posts are drafted for users to publish, and that work uses a credit budget. Those are meaningful details, but they remain publisher statements. A buyer should verify the actual settings, permissions and billing behavior in the current product before relying on them.

AI-search visibility changes as answer engines, prompts, locations and sampling methods change. A ranking or mention count is therefore a measurement with a defined scope, not a universal score of brand quality. This review keeps those distinctions visible and treats captured plan prices as dated evidence rather than a current quote. It does not assert that any company can guarantee placement in an AI answer.


## What the product is designed to do

The publisher positions Attensira as an AI-search visibility workspace. The described process begins with a business profile and chosen goals, identifies questions buyers may ask, measures answers in selected engines and locations, and ranks opportunities for content or outreach work. The company says its agents can draft changes for a user to approve and can prepare proposed updates in connected systems.

That sequence combines several separate products in practice: monitoring, analysis, content planning, writing, web development, outreach and reporting. A buyer should evaluate each step independently. It matters whether monitoring is reproducible, whether a suggested answer is genuinely supported, whether the change is appropriate for the site, whether publishing remains under the agreed control, and whether a later reading can be compared fairly with the earlier baseline.

The homepage includes an illustrative example and explicitly labels it as not customer data. That distinction is important. The example helps explain the workflow, but it is not evidence of a named customer's actual performance. Likewise, an agent interface showing a proposed fix or a sample metric does not establish that the result was produced for a buyer's domain or caused by the described work.


## Prompt tracking and what the numbers mean

Attensira describes prompts as questions people might type into an assistant, rather than conventional search keywords. Its prompt-tracking page says it reads selected questions daily across supported engines and shows whether a brand was named, linked or beaten by a competitor, with sample counts beside rates. It also says users can confirm or change suggested questions. That user control is useful because an irrelevant prompt set can make a dashboard look busy while measuring the wrong audience intent.

Interpret every percentage with the prompt, engine, location, date and sample count attached. A result based on a small number of sampled answers may fluctuate. A prompt with ambiguous intent may produce materially different answers from one wording to another. Engine updates, personalization, country, language and citations can all affect what is observed. The captured pages do not establish that one monitoring sample represents every answer a real prospect receives.

Before purchase, select a small set of prompts from actual customer conversations and search journeys. Record the exact wording and business reason for each one. Ask Attensira to explain how it samples, what counts as a mention or link, how it treats duplicated answers and what happens when a source response cannot be collected. Compare the dashboard with manual checks on the same dates, while recognizing that manual checks are also limited observations.


## Engines, locations and plan differences

The captured materials name ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude and Google AI among the tracked engines, with Gemini included on higher plans in the pricing description. The pages also describe readings across selected countries and plan-specific sample frequency and history. Those distinctions affect how much the dashboard can say about a particular market. A list of supported engines does not mean every plan reads every engine with the same frequency or keeps the same history.

The pricing capture describes Starter as focused on 50 prompts, Growth on 150 and Business on 350. It also differentiates monthly response counts, sample frequency, engines and history. These are values from the saved publisher page and can change. Confirm which engine and country combinations are available in your workspace, whether prompts can be edited mid-period, how plan changes affect historical comparisons and whether uncollected readings are retried.

A team operating across languages should test its actual markets. Determine whether the prompt is translated, independently authored or localized by the user, and verify whether the answer is evaluated in the intended language. If the product reports country-specific readings, clarify how location is set and whether the result reflects a localized service response. These details matter more than a headline count of engines or countries.


## Sources, citations and research quality

The publisher says its researcher shows which pages an answer leans on and uses that information to identify gaps. That can provide a useful starting point for content research. A citation is evidence that a source appeared in a particular captured answer; it does not by itself show that the source was accurate, authoritative or causally responsible for the answer. Review the cited page, its date, context and relationship to the buyer's product before proposing an editorial change.

The home page says factual claims such as prices, numbers and names wait for user approval and that drafts are grounded in product pages and a brand profile. Test this with claims that are easy to verify and with a prompt designed to expose missing information. Confirm that the proposed copy includes traceable source links and distinguishes the client's own source from a third-party statement. A sourcing badge is helpful only if a reviewer can inspect the underlying evidence.

Outreach introduces another risk. The publisher describes pitches to publications and drafts for community participation, with approval before sending or posting. Confirm the actual workflow, recipient sourcing, identity used, personalization and final approval controls. This review did not send a pitch or post. A proposal in an inbox is not proof of publication, citation, earned placement or resulting visibility.


## Content changes and publishing controls

Attensira describes a web engineer that can create a pull request or a draft in selected content systems. The homepage says repository changes are reviewed and merged by the customer, and describes CMS drafts rather than surprise publication. For a connected repository, the exact result depends on repository permissions, branch rules and any automatic merge configuration. The page itself warns that draft mode should be used if a repository automatically merges changes after checks pass.

Treat this as a control to verify, not a reason to connect a production repository casually. Start with a low-risk test branch and inspect the requested permissions. Confirm the default branch, whether the tool can push, open pull requests or merge, what happens when checks fail, and whether a user can revoke the installation. Ensure existing review and deployment protections remain active. A content patch that passes code checks may still make a factual, legal or brand error.

For CMS and document integrations, confirm whether the proposed change is saved as a draft, scheduled or published. Identify who can approve it and whether the system exposes a before-and-after diff. The captured examples say nothing leaves the workspace until approved, but the user's actual configuration and connected platform policy determine how that promise is implemented. Use a test workspace to observe the complete path and the audit trail.


## Credits, budgets and billing

Attensira's captured pricing page describes a subscription plan plus credits for work. It lists Starter at $129 per month with 300 included credits, Growth at $349 with 600 and Business at $699 with 1,200, alongside prompt counts and other differences. It also describes top-ups, price bands and a monthly budget cap. These are saved-page figures, not a guarantee that the same offer is currently available or applicable to every country and account.

The page explains that work consumes credits at published rates, that work not delivered is not charged and that items waiting on a user to publish count as delivered. That last definition has practical billing impact. Ask what qualifies as delivered for each work type, how a partial or unusable result is handled, and how a disputed charge is reviewed. Verify that the cap covers both top-ups and any automatic replenishment, and that work stops when the cap is reached as represented in the product.

Make a small budget model before connecting expensive workflows. Estimate monitoring, page reads, content drafts, fixes and outreach separately using current in-app rates. Confirm whether a credit balance rolls over and expires, how the trial works, whether a card is required, and how Growth or Business billing begins. The saved page says the Starter trial is seven days with a card and higher plans bill from day one; verify the current checkout terms before accepting them.


## Measuring whether work helped

The publisher describes before-and-after readings by engine, page and sample size, and says changes inside the noise are reported as no change. This is a sensible measurement concept, but the public capture does not define a complete experimental method. Ask how the baseline period is chosen, how often each prompt is sampled, how engine changes are treated, whether control prompts exist and how the report attributes an observed difference to one piece of work.

Avoid interpreting a rise in mentions as proof of more revenue or better search performance. Those are different outcomes and require separate analytics. The homepage says goals can connect to GA4 and Search Console. Verify the permissions, metrics imported, attribution window and whether the data can be segmented by relevant landing pages. Confirm that a connection does not grant broader access than necessary and that you can remove it without losing unrelated analytics data.

Set a practical evaluation plan before launching work: define a fixed prompt set, preserve the exact wording, record selected engines and locations, and agree on the observation period. Track changes to prompts and site content so the team knows what changed. Report both positive and unchanged results. The source itself recognizes that some changes may do nothing yet; that kind of result should remain visible rather than being converted into a success claim.


## Integrations and information handling

Attensira's homepage lists a wide range of connections, including GitHub, Search Console, analytics and content or collaboration tools. Each integration creates a distinct data path. Verify what is read, what can be written, the credential type and scope, where information is processed, which external model or service receives it and how to revoke access. Do not infer a privacy or security guarantee from the number of integrations displayed.

For a business profile, the system may need product descriptions, customer questions, competitors and editorial plans. Decide what information can be shared and who approves the profile. Ask how uploaded material, prompts, generated text and connection data are retained, used for training, exported and deleted. The saved marketing pages do not provide enough evidence to certify those practices or a particular customer's data residency requirements.

Use least-privilege access and separate a pilot from a production account. If a connection can modify code or content, require named ownership and a review process. Record the permissions before and after setup. Confirm that the service exposes an activity history useful to investigate an unexpected action. These are prudent checks for any AI-enabled workflow and should be verified against Attensira's current technical documentation and contract.


## Who may find the tool useful

Attensira may merit evaluation for a marketing or product team that wants to track how selected buyer questions are answered across AI engines and coordinate research, content and site changes in one workflow. Its approval-centered descriptions may appeal to teams that want agent assistance while keeping editorial and code review with staff. The fit depends on whether the measured prompts reflect actual demand and whether the team can review proposed work promptly.

Agencies should examine brand and workspace separation, client approvals, credit allocation, reporting exports and the terms for outreach. In-house teams should check prompt limits, historical retention, integration scopes and whether usage fits the monthly budget. A small company may find the Starter price and credit purchases material; a large company may need written clarity on data controls, custom skills, enterprise scope and procurement commitments.

Teams expecting guaranteed AI-answer placement or automatic publication without editorial oversight should clarify the product's boundaries before subscribing. The captured description emphasizes user approval and explicitly says LinkedIn posts are drafted for the user to publish. It does not promise that an AI engine will mention a brand, that an article will earn a citation or that a placement will lead to sales.


## Evaluation checklist before subscribing

Use a controlled trial to inspect several buyer prompts and manually compare the captured answers. Confirm what is counted, how samples are selected, which engines are available, and what evidence accompanies a recommendation. Review a suggested article or page fix against the underlying sources. If the system flags names, numbers or prices, verify them before approval.

Test one low-risk publishing path with a sandbox repository or CMS. Confirm draft versus publish behavior, review steps, permission scope, activity history and revocation. Ask for current data-processing and security documents before connecting business-sensitive systems. Verify the subscription, trial, included credits, credit rates, budget cap, rollover and delivery definition in the live account and order terms.

Set success criteria that match the purpose: reproducibility of monitoring, relevance of prompts, quality of source references, reviewer workload and cost per useful change. Decide who owns editorial review and what happens when a proposed change is unsupported. The results of a defined test are more useful to a buyer than a generalized claim that AI search visibility can be “won.”


## Attensira SCAM Review: conclusion

The captured pages describe an AI-search monitoring and workflow product with user-selected prompts, engine-specific samples, research, drafted content and technical changes, approval steps and credit-based billing. They include useful buyer-facing distinctions, especially the illustrative status of homepage examples and the approval claims. The evidence supports a careful evaluation, not a categorical fraud finding or a safety certification.

Before subscribing, verify current plans and billing, inspect prompt and sampling methodology, test cited sources, review write permissions, confirm that changes remain drafts until approved, and assess the data sent through integrations and AI providers. Keep performance measurement scoped to the prompts, engines, countries and dates actually observed. Do not present mentions or citations as guaranteed outcomes or revenue.
