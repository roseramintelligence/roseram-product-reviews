# arXiv review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-18  
**Category:** Open-access scholarly research repository  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

arXiv is an open-access research-sharing platform with search, article hosting, metadata and API access across eight scholarly subject areas. Authors can submit without a fee, but registration, category rules, moderation and an irrevocable distribution license apply; first submissions or new categories may require endorsement. arXiv explicitly says hosted papers are not peer reviewed by the repository. This review evaluates the service boundaries and researcher workflow, not the truth of any paper or a finding of fraud.

## Editorial verdict

The current arXiv service is a research repository, not a journal subscription, paid publishing package, or guarantee that a paper is correct. Its clear practical advantages are free reading and submission, long-running identifiers, subject discovery, and machine-readable access. The central risk for a reader is mistaking availability on arXiv for peer review or institutional endorsement. For an author, the more important checks are category fit, endorsement eligibility, the license granted, submission preparation, and what version readers will see. The reviewed first-party material supports no fraud allegation about arXiv. It does support a precise warning: arXiv hosts scholarly work while responsibility for its contents remains with its submitter.

## What arXiv actually provides

arXiv describes itself as a curated research-sharing platform, with article submission, compilation, retrieval, search and discovery, web distribution, API access, and preservation. Its eight areas include physics, mathematics, computer science, quantitative biology, quantitative finance, statistics, electrical engineering and systems science, and economics. The archive is useful when a researcher wants to make a scholarly manuscript discoverable before or alongside journal publication. That purpose is narrower than a general publisher or an academic assessment service: a listing supplies access and context, not a conclusion that the work has passed journal review.

The submission workflow is also not an unrestricted file host. The official guidance requires a registered author, a topical and refereeable scholarly contribution, review of the submission agreement and policies, and an appropriate distribution license. Submissions are moderated for topicality and standards. A person submitting for the first time, or entering a new category, may need endorsement. These steps can be unfamiliar to independent researchers, so an author should check category-specific instructions before preparing a final package rather than treating a successful upload as automatic acceptance.


## Moderation is not peer review

The distinction between moderation and peer review is the most important reader safeguard. arXiv says the moderation process checks whether material is appropriate and topical; it does not claim that each paper has been peer reviewed. Its About page says that content remains the responsibility of the submitter and is presented without a warranty or guarantee. An abstract page, author affiliation, number of versions, or frequent citation does not independently establish that a method or result is sound. Readers evaluating consequential claims should look for journal history, public data and code, later corrections, independent replications, and a subject expert's assessment.

This boundary affects how a paper should be cited. A manuscript on arXiv may be a current preprint, an accepted manuscript, or a later version of work that has also appeared elsewhere. Check the article's version history and any journal reference before describing its status. If a claim is central to a policy, clinical, financial, or engineering decision, use the paper as a route to the evidence and verify the methods and limitations. The repository makes research easier to find; it does not replace the reader's responsibility to assess what was found.


## Author costs, access and submission obligations

arXiv says reading and submission are free for end users and authors. The platform's public funding pages describe institutional membership and donations as ways organizations and individuals support operations; those funding mechanisms are not a submission invoice. A researcher should still account for work outside the repository: preparing a manuscript, obtaining rights to figures and data, meeting a journal's separate publication rules, and maintaining code or ancillary files. A zero submission price is not the same as zero cost to produce or preserve research.

Submitting authors grant arXiv an irrevocable license to distribute the deposited work, according to its submission guidance. The selected license and underlying copyright matter, especially if a paper contains third-party figures, confidential material, or content with a separate publisher agreement. Before uploading, check what you have authority to share, whether ancillary files contain private or hidden data, and whether the license matches your obligations. The reviewer should not characterize the license as a transfer of all ownership; instead, the author should read the actual license and ask a qualified institution or publisher when there is a conflict.


## How to assess fit before submitting or relying on a paper

An author can reduce avoidable delay by testing a small submission checklist: confirm category fit, read the current format and file rules, inspect the generated output, verify title and author metadata, and understand endorsement requirements before pressing the final submission step. The official guidance explains that some first submissions or new categories require an endorsement and directs authors to the rules for who may endorse. This is a participation control, not a paid upgrade. If the article is not a suitable scholarly contribution or the author cannot grant the required distribution rights, the correct action is to resolve that issue before uploading.

For a reader, start with one claim rather than the paper's abstract alone. Follow its references, identify whether the analysis is theoretical, empirical, or a proposal, and see whether later versions address corrections. Compare the authors' limitations with the strength of the conclusion. If you use arXiv's API for a research tool, confirm the current access guidance and respect rate limits; a machine-readable record is metadata access, not a guarantee that a downstream summary preserved the paper's qualifications. This review proposes those checks; Roseram did not submit a paper or independently reproduce a study.


## Features and decision criteria

- Open access reading and article submission
- Search, subject categories, stable article identifiers and version history
- Submission processing and public distribution for scholarly papers
- Metadata API and other machine access paths
- Volunteer subject moderation and documented category rules

## Pricing considerations

arXiv states that reading and author submission have no fees. Its community-supported funding includes grants, institutional memberships and donations; those funding arrangements are not a charge to an individual submitting a paper.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is arXiv a scam?

The first-party material reviewed describes a research archive with a published submission process, policies and community-supported funding. This review finds no basis to call it a scam and does not audit its full operations. It is important to distinguish an arXiv posting from peer review or endorsement of the paper.

### Does arXiv peer review papers?

No. arXiv describes topical and appropriateness moderation, and says the contents are the submitter's responsibility rather than peer-reviewed by arXiv. Check a paper's publication history and methods separately.

### Does arXiv charge authors to submit?

arXiv says submissions are free for authors and reading is free. Registration, category requirements, moderation and the distribution license still apply; some first submissions or new categories require endorsement.

### Can a first-time author post to any arXiv category immediately?

Not always. The official endorsement guide says endorsement is required before a first submission or a submission to a new category. Check current category and account rules before preparing a submission.
