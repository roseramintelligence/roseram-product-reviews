Aside describes a desktop assistant for call preparation, live guidance, recording, transcription and follow-up. Its central interface is a small prompt near the camera rather than a bot joining the meeting. That design may appeal to people handling sales calls, interviews or recurring customer conversations, but it raises several separate evaluation questions. ## No meeting bot does not mean no recording

Aside says it captures microphone audio and computer sound without adding another participant to the call. That is an interface and capture-method distinction, not evidence that recording permission is unnecessary. Its own pricing FAQ explicitly tells users to inform people that they are recording. A business evaluating the product should establish its recording practice and the applicable organizational requirements before enabling capture. The absence of a bot notification should not become a reason to leave participants unaware of what is happening.

Use a clear notice and an appropriate alternative when recording is not suitable. The exact requirements depend on the circumstances and are not determined by this review. No legal assessment or consent flow was tested here. The practical point follows the publisher's own disclosure: a quieter interface can reduce screen clutter while the user still remains responsible for an understandable recording arrangement and for handling the resulting information appropriately.


## Local audio and synced text are different data flows

The homepage says recordings stay on the computer and that transcripts, summaries, notes and plans sync to the account. The feature page also explains that text can be sent to AI when needed by a feature. Those are materially different boundaries. A statement about local audio should not be interpreted as a promise that every derived piece of information remains only on the device. A transcript can contain the same sensitive business content as the recording from which it was produced.

Map what is captured, stored, processed and shared for the intended workflow. Review who can access the account and any connected AI application before discussing confidential customer or company information. These are proposed evaluation steps, not an audit of Aside's data handling. The publisher also says content is not used for model training, but this review has not inspected provider agreements or technical enforcement. Keep that attributed claim separate from the unresolved questions of access, retention and operational policy.


## Transcription has local and provider-assisted routes

The captured feature page describes an on-device speech model for English and twenty-four European languages, with an initial download of about 650 megabytes. It also says other languages, or computers too slow to keep up, use a speech-to-text provider. That qualification matters when assessing the local-first description. The actual processing route can depend on both language and device capability, not only on the user's preferred privacy setting or the fact that the application is installed locally.

Evaluate a representative call language and device before promising a particular processing boundary. Ask how the application indicates the active route and where the relevant current privacy details are documented. These are proposed checks, not a transcription experiment performed here. The review has not measured recognition accuracy, latency or hardware performance. A successful English demonstration on one machine cannot establish that every user in a multilingual team receives the same on-device behavior.


## Prepared answers need an accountable owner

Aside describes placing answers from a plan or playbook near the camera when a related question arises. Its homepage demonstrates this with a security-related sales question. The usefulness of that feature depends on the prepared answer being accurate and current. A confidently displayed response about pricing, compliance or delivery timing can create a business commitment if a person repeats it without checking. Decide who owns approved answers and how outdated statements are corrected before relying on them during customer conversations.

Use examples with conditions, uncertainty and exceptions, not just a single straightforward product fact. Check whether the suggested wording preserves those qualifications. These are proposed acceptance checks rather than observed behavior. This review has not asked Aside to generate or retrieve an answer. The tool may help make information available in the moment, but its presence does not make an unsupported statement true or authorize a salesperson to promise something the business cannot provide.


## Live coaching should support the conversation rather than replace judgment

The feature page describes goal indicators, a suggested next move and answers requested through the interface or keyboard shortcuts. These can help someone remember planned topics, but a conversation is not a checklist alone. A suggested question may be inappropriate after the other person has already declined, changed the subject or explained a constraint. The user should remain responsible for deciding whether a prompt fits the actual interaction and whether it adds value for the other participant.

During evaluation, include a call where the planned agenda changes and inspect whether the guidance remains helpful or distracting. Consider whether reading the prompt causes the user to miss important information. These are suggested checks, not a measured usability result. The captured pages do not establish conversion improvements or better interview outcomes. A feature that makes a prompt available is distinct from evidence that following it improves every conversation.


## Call preparation relies on identity and source accuracy

Aside says it can research people from external calendar meetings before the call and present a brief with suggested questions. That can reduce preparation effort, but names, companies and roles can be ambiguous or outdated. Verify the identity and important facts before using them in a conversation. A mistaken profile can be more damaging than a missing profile if the user treats it as confirmed knowledge about the person they are meeting.

Keep the difference between sourced background, prior conversation notes and generated advice visible. These are proposed review criteria, not a claim about a specific Aside interface failure. No calendar was connected or person researched here. The saved feature description establishes the intended preparation workflow, but it does not independently validate a particular brief. Sensitive or consequential claims should be checked against an appropriate source rather than repeated because the assistant displayed them shortly before the meeting.


## Speaker labels and timed notes need correction paths

The publisher describes notes placed at the moment they were started and speaker names inferred from calendar context or clues in the conversation. It also says the reason for a name can be inspected and the assignment undone. That correction path is important because an attribution error can change the meaning of a promise or objection. Review whether the right person is attached to statements that will later appear in a follow-up or account history.

A pilot should include more than two speakers, interruptions and similar voices if those conditions are common in the intended use. Check a short, known segment against the transcript rather than judging only the summary's fluency. These are proposed tests, not recordings analyzed here. The tool's ability to label speakers does not establish perfect identification, and a timestamped personal note should remain distinguishable from words actually spoken by a participant.


## Summaries and promises should remain drafts until reviewed

Aside describes write-ups containing summaries, next steps, questions, objections and promises, as well as a follow-up email. Those outputs can help organize a meeting, but they should not silently become an authoritative contract or an email sent on the user's behalf. A tentative idea, a conditional offer and a firm commitment are different. Review the transcript context and the responsible person's intent before assigning a due date or telling another participant that an action was agreed.

The saved pages describe composing a follow-up rather than establish that every generated email is automatically dispatched. Keep generation, review, approval and sending as separate states in the workflow. This review has not generated or sent a message. A useful evaluation would inspect whether a summary preserves uncertainty and whether an incorrectly inferred promise can be corrected before it propagates to another tool or a customer-facing communication.


## Free recording and paid guidance have different limits

The captured pricing describes unlimited recording, notes and transcripts on Free, with live guidance and write-ups for two calls per week. It describes a fourteen-day Pro trial without a card, after which users move to Free unless they choose a plan. That is different from saying every feature remains unlimited without payment. Identify whether the primary need is a recording archive, occasional coaching or guidance on most calls before comparing plans.

The saved Pro offer is displayed at $15 billed monthly or $144 yearly, shown as $12 per month. Confirm the actual payable amount and renewal terms at purchase. A lower annual monthly equivalent does not mean monthly billing. This review has not started or canceled a trial. Its recommendation is to estimate the calls that need the paid functions and distinguish that count from the number of recordings the user wants to retain.


## Planned team features should not be treated as released capabilities

The pricing page labels Team as coming soon and gives planned pricing and features such as shared plans, a team meeting library and consolidated billing. Those descriptions are useful for understanding the roadmap, but they are not evidence that a customer can purchase and use those functions now. A buyer needing shared governance should verify release status rather than commit a team workflow based on a future-feature card.

The captured page provides a waiting-list path for Team. That reinforces the distinction between an available individual product and an anticipated organizational edition. This review has not checked current launch status, so the historical wording should be reconfirmed. Do not report a roadmap feature as implemented merely because its planned price appears alongside active plans. An individual account's useful capabilities may still leave an organization's access, administration and ownership requirements unresolved.


## Desktop requirements and screen-sharing behavior need a real-device check

The captured FAQ lists Apple silicon with macOS 14.2 or later and supported Windows versions, while the homepage describes use across call applications because capture occurs through computer audio. That is broader than a meeting-platform integration list, but it still depends on the device and permissions. Confirm the actual operating system, audio setup, headset and call environment used by the intended participant. A phone call routed through the computer and a meeting held entirely on another device are different scenarios.

The publisher also says its guidance interface is hidden from screen sharing. Verify that claim in the actual sharing mode and application if it is important to the workflow. These are proposed device checks, not a screen-sharing test conducted here. Even if the interface is hidden as described, the user still needs the recording disclosure discussed earlier. Interface visibility and participant awareness are separate issues.


## Connected AI access extends the account's information boundary

Aside says AI applications such as Claude or ChatGPT can read past calls after connection. That can make follow-up preparation more convenient, but the connected service becomes another route through which transcript and account information is used. Review the permissions, data categories and purpose before connecting an account containing confidential conversations. A local recording architecture does not eliminate the significance of synced text being available through an authorized integration.

Ask how access is revoked and what remains available after disconnection or account changes, using the current policy and documentation. The saved marketing pages do not answer every lifecycle detail. This review has not connected an AI application or inspected token handling. The appropriate evaluation considers both convenience and the scope of information being made available, with the user remaining responsible for which conversations and notes belong in that workflow.
