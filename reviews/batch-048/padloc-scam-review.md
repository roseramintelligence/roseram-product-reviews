# Padloc review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-24  
**Category:** Password and shared-secret management  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Padloc presents an open-source, end-to-end encrypted service for passwords, notes, files and shared secrets. Its official site lists desktop, mobile and browser application paths and sharing for groups. Security positioning is attributed to the provider and has not been independently certified by Roseram.

## Editorial verdict

Padloc is relevant when a person or group needs more than a list of passwords and wants a structured sharing routine. Test a dummy item from creation through access changes and export. Sharing convenience should be evaluated together with ownership and recovery responsibilities.

## A shared item needs an owner

A team can solve an immediate access problem by sharing a credential and still leave future responsibility unclear. Decide who maintains the item, who should use it and who can change its audience. Start the trial with dummy information so these roles can be explored safely.

Include an update and a removal of access, not only the first invitation. The important outcome is an understandable lifecycle for the shared material. A polished vault screen is only one part of that lifecycle.


## Different kinds of secrets need useful structure

The official product covers notes and files as well as passwords. Those items can have different uses, owners and review needs. Give the collection enough organization to make the right item easy to identify without storing unnecessary sensitive material simply because the tool accepts it.

Use an expendable sample reflecting the intended workflow. Check search and retrieval after several similar items exist. This review did not inspect a real vault or determine the suitability of the service for a regulated dataset.


## Recovery must work when the ordinary device is absent

Read the current documented recovery requirements and preserve any needed material independently. A person should understand the difference between recovering account access, restoring an item and obtaining access through another device.

Test only with dummy data and follow the applicable official instructions. Roseram did not execute recovery or audit the encryption implementation. Provider privacy language should be evaluated alongside practical account and device controls.


## The group plan should match the actual collaboration

List the participants, devices and storage needs before choosing a subscription. Confirm the current feature boundaries and what happens when group membership changes. A broader plan is useful when it supports a concrete responsibility rather than a hypothetical future feature.

Compare alternatives using the same shared-item lifecycle. This source-based review does not establish a security guarantee, paid entitlement or completed acceptance test. It explains the documented product scope and the operating decisions a trial should resolve.


## Features and decision criteria

- Password, note and file storage
- Provider-described end-to-end encryption
- Desktop, mobile and browser paths
- Group sharing workflows

## Pricing considerations

The official site links pricing for the service. Check current individual and group limits, storage, billing and support terms; no current plan amount or unlimited entitlement is established here.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Padloc limited to passwords?

The official overview also describes notes, files and other sensitive information.

### Does sharing an item establish a complete offboarding process?

No. Define who owns access, how it is changed and what other actions are needed when a person leaves.

No. The review attributes them to the provider and does not constitute an audit.
