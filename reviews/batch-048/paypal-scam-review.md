# PayPal review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-16  
**Category:** Online payments and merchant services  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

PayPal offers consumer and merchant payment services, including online checkout, invoicing and card processing. The cost and protection terms depend on the payment method, market and transaction.

## Editorial verdict

PayPal publishes current United States fee schedules and Seller Protection terms. They support a practical pricing and eligibility review, not a blanket promise that payments are risk-free or that every transaction is protected.

## PayPal review: what a merchant actually gets

A **PayPal business account** can bring PayPal checkout, card acceptance and other payment methods into an online sales flow. The purchase decision is less about the familiar button than the complete path around it: which methods appear for each customer, what the seller pays, how refunds are recorded and where a disputed order is handled.

PayPal’s official checkout material describes a choice of payment experiences, including PayPal, Venmo, Pay Later and cards, with options depending on the checkout product. Treat that as a feature menu to verify in your own account. A payment method appearing on a product page does not by itself show that it is enabled for your business or available to every customer.


## PayPal merchant fees in the United States

The US merchant fee page, last updated November 4, 2026, lists PayPal Checkout for domestic commercial transactions at **3.49% plus $0.49** and standard card payments at **2.99% plus $0.49**. It also lists an additional percentage for international commercial transactions. These are market-specific rates; a different checkout product, payment type or country can produce a different bill.

Run the figures against your real average order value and expected mix of card, PayPal and international orders. Include refunds, conversion to another currency and any separate platform or chargeback costs. The *fee table is a starting point*, not a complete margin calculation. Save the terms and rate page that apply to the account before you plan a launch.


## PayPal Seller Protection: read the eligibility conditions

PayPal’s Seller Protection policy says an eligible claim may allow a seller to retain the purchase amount, subject to PayPal’s decision and the program requirements. The policy identifies conditions around the account market, transaction details and evidence. For shipped goods, the seller needs to use the shipping address in the transaction details and retain valid proof of shipment and delivery. Some goods and transactions are excluded.

Do not translate “Seller Protection” into “every sale is covered.” Check whether the actual transaction is marked eligible, whether the product type qualifies and which documents the policy requires. A service business, a digital delivery, an in-person collection and a shipped physical item may have different evidence needs. This distinction matters before you choose how to fulfill an order.


## PayPal checkout, reconciliation and a practical test

Use a low-value test order to follow the complete record from checkout through capture, refund and the accounting export. Confirm which transaction identifier appears in the shop, payment provider and bank reconciliation. Then ask someone who handles bookkeeping to match the records without help from the person who set up the integration.

Test what the customer sees when a payment fails or a refund is issued. Write down which party answers a question about a product, a card charge or an account restriction. Checkout is only one part of support, and the seller remains responsible for describing delivery, cancellations and its own customer service clearly.


## PayPal alternatives and who should compare them

Compare PayPal with a card processor, a marketplace payment service or a checkout already included in your commerce platform. Use the same order value, countries, payment types and refund example. Examine not just the headline rate but the settlement record, supported currencies, dispute workflow, recurring payment needs and account administration.

PayPal may suit a merchant whose customers already use its wallet or who wants several payment methods under one provider. A business with unusual fulfillment or compliance requirements should read the applicable agreement and ask the provider about its exact case. This review summarizes published terms; it does not claim a hands-on payment test or guarantee a particular approval outcome.


## Features and decision criteria

- PayPal and card payment acceptance
- Online checkout and invoicing
- Transaction details and conditional Seller Protection

## Pricing considerations

Rates are from PayPal’s US merchant fee page, last updated November 4, 2026. This is not a quote for every PayPal product or market. Payment channel, transaction type, currency, international status, disputes and later policy changes can affect the final amount; check the current fee page and the account checkout terms.
