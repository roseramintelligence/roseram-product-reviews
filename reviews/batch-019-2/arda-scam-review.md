## Visibility measurement needs an explicit query population

The homepage describes tracking buying-intent queries and showing how often a brand is named across Google and several AI services. That can be useful competitive information, but the result depends on the queries selected. A brand appearing for a small favorable set does not establish broad market visibility, and a missing mention does not prove that every potential buyer receives the same answer. Define which questions matter and how they connect to the actual product and audience.

Keep the exact query set and measurement scope with the report. Review whether the sample includes discovery, comparison and practical purchase questions rather than only the brand name itself. These are proposed evaluation steps, not queries run here. The saved pages do not establish a complete sampling method or universal coverage. A useful score should describe the monitored population and its limitations, not silently become a claim about every search or answer generated anywhere.


## AI-answer tracking and traditional rankings are different observations

Arda discusses both search-engine optimization and answer-engine visibility. The underlying observations need to remain distinct. A traditional search position, a brand mention in a generated answer and a linked citation can represent different kinds of exposure. Do not combine them into one reassuring number without explaining what is counted. A report should let the team see the original observation and understand why it matters to the chosen business question.

Record the relevant service, time and response context when evaluating a monitored result. These are proposed measurement requirements, not a live comparison conducted here. The source's platform list establishes intended coverage, while the pricing distinguishes some model access by plan. Confirm the actual plan and current capabilities before assuming every named answer engine is included. This review does not verify ranking accuracy or reproduce an AI-answer result.


## Drafting a fix is different from proving its effect

The homepage describes technical audits, content refreshes, comparison pages and structured-data work. Those are concrete production categories, but each needs a diagnosed problem and an acceptance criterion. A new page should answer a real question with relevant evidence; a technical fix should address an identified issue. A completed draft is not proof that the search system has crawled it, understood it or chosen to display it.

For a pilot, choose a small issue that can be checked before and after the proposed change. Keep the source evidence and the observed technical result separate from later visibility outcomes. These are proposed checks, not a fix applied here. The saved pages describe a workflow that moves beyond reporting, but the ability to publish does not establish that every suggested change is correct or commercially useful for the site receiving it.


## Approval before publishing is a central product claim

Arda repeatedly says the team approves changes before they ship, with before-and-after review and an enterprise audit trail. That is more specific than simply saying a human remains in control. A buyer should test the actual authority boundary: who can approve, which exact revision is approved and whether a later agent edit requires renewed review. A visible approval button is useful only if it governs the change that is ultimately published.

Evaluate a rejected draft, a revised draft and an approved change in an appropriate non-production environment. These are proposed acceptance scenarios, not approvals exercised here. Confirm how the system handles a CMS error or uncertain publish result so a retry does not create confusing duplicates. The review has not validated the enforcement mechanism. It treats approval-before-publishing as a substantive publisher claim to investigate rather than as a completed assurance for an unknown account.


## CMS connections create a real write-access boundary

The source lists WordPress, Webflow, Shopify, Framer, Sanity and GitHub among supported routes. These platforms have different content models and release practices. Confirm the actual target, requested permissions and scope of the intended integration before giving an agent access. Editing a draft entry, changing a live template and committing to a repository can have different consequences even when all are described as publishing a fix.

Use the minimum suitable access and a representative staging workflow for evaluation. These are proposed implementation practices, not credentials or integrations configured here. The homepage says access can be revoked, which should be checked for the actual connection and account owner. The saved material does not establish every token, retention or repository-protection behavior. A no-migration claim should not be interpreted as a promise that no implementation or governance work remains.


## Content generation needs source and commercial-claim review

Arda describes drafting comparisons, new pages and content refreshes. A marketing team should review factual statements, dates, product identity and the distinction between its own analysis and third-party claims. A comparison should not invent a competitor's price or customer experience, and a generated page should not imply hands-on testing that did not occur. Publishing speed does not reduce the need for those checks.

Create a short evidence record for important claims and verify the actual draft before approval. These are proposed editorial practices, not generated content examined here. No page was created through Arda for this review. The source describes production capability, but it does not independently establish the accuracy or originality of every output. The buyer remains accountable for what the brand publishes, including the context of any call to action or commercial recommendation.


## Schema and technical changes should reflect the actual page

The homepage mentions structured data and on-page fixes. Those can help communicate a site's content, but the representation should match the real information available to users. A technical change is not a justification to add invented ratings, unsupported testimonials or a claim that the page represents a reviewed product experience. Evaluate the markup and visible page together rather than treating a passing technical check as editorial validation.

For a pilot, inspect the proposed change against the actual route, canonical destination and page content. These are proposed acceptance checks, not a technical audit performed here. The saved pages do not guarantee index inclusion or enhanced search display. Keep deployability, crawlability and the search provider's indexing decision separate. A valid implementation can be necessary for a particular goal without making that goal automatic.


## Trial credits meter work rather than outcomes

The captured pricing offers a free trial with one hundred agent credits and says agent actions consume credits. Examples include crawling, drafting, citation checks and monitoring across models. Those are different operations, so a credit allowance should not be interpreted as a fixed number of accepted pages or successful improvements. Ask what a representative workflow costs, including revisions and repeated checks, rather than assume the headline count maps directly to output volume.

The FAQ says work pauses after all trial credits are used and directs users to discuss more capacity or an ongoing plan. That is a concrete operational limit. This review has not consumed credits or verified the pause behavior. A useful pilot should keep a usage record linked to completed, reviewed work so the buyer understands both the resource cost and the actual result before estimating a larger campaign.


## Enterprise terms need a scope-specific quotation

The enterprise offer is custom-priced, with a custom credit allowance and additional governance, integrations and service support. The captured pages do not provide a universal monthly figure. Confirm the query population, models, sites, content scope and human assistance included in the proposed agreement. A small trial and an agency operating across several clients can have very different needs even if both use the same platform name.

The trial is described as requiring no credit card, but this review has not enrolled or inspected a contract. No price, automatic conversion rule or refund policy is invented where the sources do not establish one. ## Analytics access does not establish attribution by itself

Arda describes reading Google Analytics and Search Console and connecting visibility reports with marketing work. Those systems can provide useful observations, but an increase after publishing is not automatically caused by the agent's change. Seasonality, other campaigns, site changes and measurement differences can affect the result. Keep the time period, source and business event explicit when evaluating performance rather than treating every positive movement as a validated effect.

Define outcomes such as qualified inquiries or completed purchases separately from impressions and mentions. These are proposed measurement practices, not analytics inspected here. No conversion rate or revenue uplift was verified. The source's promise to improve visibility should be treated as a product objective and marketing claim, while the actual commercial effect requires evidence from the site's own operating context and a suitable comparison.


## Alerts and repeated optimization need change discipline

The homepage describes visibility-drop alerts and repeated cycles that use prior observations to inform later work. That can support ongoing maintenance, but a fluctuation should not automatically trigger a large unreviewed rewrite. Determine whether the signal is persistent, meaningful and connected to an actionable problem. A system observing change needs a defined response, including when the right decision is to investigate rather than publish immediately.

Keep a history of approved changes and their intended purpose so the next cycle can distinguish a deliberate trade-off from an accidental regression. These are proposed operating practices, not repeated runs tested here. The saved source does not independently establish that each cycle improves results. An iterative workflow becomes useful when it preserves evidence and human judgment rather than increasing the volume of changes whose effects nobody can explain.

A link count alone does not establish useful audience exposure or a durable commercial relationship. Likewise, a mention on a source associated with AI answers does not guarantee that an answer engine will cite or recommend the brand.

The saved pages do not provide a complete placement process, endorsement arrangement or guaranteed result, and this review does not invent one. No outreach or external publication occurred here. Ask for concrete, reviewable deliverables and current terms before authorizing work under those labels. The broader lesson is to assess the substance of a proposed reference or relationship rather than treat every additional link as evidence that the underlying content deserves visibility.
