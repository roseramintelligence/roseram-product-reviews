# GetResponse review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-16  
**Category:** Email marketing, automation and content monetization  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

GetResponse combines email campaigns, automation, signup pages and marketing features, with higher tiers adding commerce and creator tools. Its published US pricing starts at $19 per month for Starter and varies with the selected contact band and billing period.

## Editorial verdict

GetResponse’s pricing page makes subscriber count, automation depth, add-ons and billing cadence central plan checks. Treat the 14-day paid-feature trial and the reduced free account afterward as separate states, and confirm what happens to each workflow when the trial ends.

## GetResponse review: email campaigns and bundled marketing tools

GetResponse brings email marketing, signup forms, landing pages and automation into one service. The plan comparison also lists capabilities for ecommerce, web push, webinars, courses and paid newsletters on higher tiers. The breadth may suit a team that wants one system for audience capture and follow-up; a business already using a specialist CRM should check whether the additional modules create overlap.

Start by mapping the intended customer path from a form to a confirmation message and a follow-up. Identify the first moment when a marketing email is allowed, how consent is recorded and which activity ends the automation. Then test each handoff to the website, checkout or course area before moving a live list.


## GetResponse pricing, subscriber bands and billing terms

The US pricing page displays Starter at $19 monthly or $15.58 per month when paid yearly, Marketer at $59 monthly or $48.38 yearly, and Creator at $69 monthly or $56.58 yearly. The displayed list prices are based on an entry package; GetResponse also offers multiple list-size bands and enterprise quotes. The page states an 18% discount for yearly billing, so the annual equivalent requires a longer commitment than paying month to month.

A help article describes the monthly cost as beginning at $19 for up to 1,000 subscribers, while the pricing page provides package selectors. Check whether dormant, unsubscribed or duplicate records affect the account’s billable subscriber count. Recalculate the quote using your actual list and expected growth, and compare the total annual charge rather than multiplying a discounted monthly equivalent by twelve without checking the billing screen.


## GetResponse trial, Free version and workflow limits

GetResponse describes a 14-day trial in which verified accounts can test paid features. Its pricing guidance says that, after the trial, premium features become unavailable unless the customer upgrades, while a reduced free version remains accessible. Starter includes one custom automation workflow; Marketer and Creator list unlimited workflows. The plans also differ in audience segmentation, ecommerce tools and content products.

Before building around a trial feature, identify which features remain after day fourteen and what is disabled. Export the automation logic and note any landing page or domain that relies on a paid tier. A team should test downgrade behavior before using a trial to build a time-sensitive public funnel, since login access is not the same as continued access to every paid capability.


## GetResponse creators, ecommerce and operational fit

The comparison positions Creator for courses, webinars and premium newsletters, while Marketer adds sales-oriented features such as abandoned-cart recovery and revenue reporting. Those products can be convenient when the same team owns email and digital content. They also introduce separate customer records, purchases and content access rules that need monitoring.

For a store or membership business, test a refund, a failed payment, a duplicate contact and a change of email address. Confirm that a buyer loses access only when the business’s policy says so and that marketing preferences remain separate from transactional notices. Document which records synchronize to the ecommerce system and which reports are authoritative for accounting.


## GetResponse alternatives and the SCAM Review question

Compare GetResponse with MailerLite or Campaign Monitor for simpler newsletter needs, and with a dedicated CRM automation platform if sales records and account ownership dominate the workflow. Use the same contact band, number of automations, required landing pages, team seats and annual billing assumption. Verify add-on availability by country because the official pricing guidance notes that some accounts may not be able to enable or purchase every add-on.

The official plan pages reviewed here describe a marketing platform and its tier limits; they do not establish that GetResponse is a scam. This is a source-based desk review, not a hands-on test of deliverability, support response or course delivery. The buyer should confirm the live package, post-trial feature state and renewal amount before attaching a production funnel.


## Features and decision criteria

- Email campaigns, signup forms and landing pages
- One or unlimited automation workflows depending on plan
- Higher-tier webinars, courses, ecommerce and newsletter tools

## Pricing considerations

Prices checked on GetResponse’s US pricing page on 2026-11-16 and correspond to the displayed entry selection; the plan is priced by list-size package. Annual billing is presented with an 18% discount. Verify contact band, add-ons, currency, local tax and renewal rate in checkout. A 14-day trial of paid features is distinct from the reduced free version available afterward.
