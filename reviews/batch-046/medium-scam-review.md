# Medium review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-18  
**Category:** Publishing platform and reader membership service  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Medium is a reading and publishing platform with free accounts, a paid reader membership and a Partner Program for eligible writers. Membership is listed at $5 per month or $50 per year. Partner Program enrollment requires an active membership, a published story, a complete profile, age 18 or older and an eligible bank and tax residence. Earnings depend on member activity and story performance; a $10 payout threshold, Stripe setup and verified tax information apply. Writers should treat revenue as variable, check content rules and retain copies of their work.

## Editorial verdict

The reviewed sources do not substantiate a scam allegation. Medium documents membership pricing, Partner Program eligibility, its engagement-based earnings model, the $10 payout threshold and content restrictions. The central risk is confusing access to a publishing platform or a paywall with predictable income: earnings vary with qualifying member engagement, policy compliance and eligibility, and Medium can change program terms. Treat it as one distribution channel and keep an independent archive and audience plan.

## Medium SCAM review: understand the label and the service being evaluated

“SCAM Review” is the label used by this directory; it is not a finding of fraud or a claim that Medium has been independently audited. This article examines Medium’s public product and program terms: what readers pay for, how writers publish, what it takes to enroll in the Partner Program, how earnings are calculated and what conditions govern payout. No membership was purchased and no creator account, story or payout was tested for this desk review.

Medium can serve as a place to read, publish and discover essays, commentary and other writing. It also sells a reader membership and offers eligible writers a share of membership revenue based on the performance of locked stories. Those are separate value propositions. A writer may use Medium without joining its Partner Program, while membership does not mean a reader is automatically enrolled as a paying writer.


## Medium membership price and reader benefits

Medium’s Help Center lists membership at $5 per month or $50 per year in USD. The listed benefits include unlimited, ad-free access to stories, text-to-speech listening, offline reading lists on iOS and Android, and support for writers through the Partner Program. The company says a portion of membership fees is allocated to authors when members read members-only stories. The exact commercial value depends on what an individual wants to read and how much they use those features.

The same guidance says a membership can be canceled from account settings, or through Apple’s App Store or Google Play when it was purchased in those apps. Benefits continue to the end of the billing cycle after cancellation. That distinction matters: canceling in the wrong billing channel may not stop a renewal. Buyers should identify where the subscription was purchased and check the account’s renewal date, taxes and regional price before treating the US help-page amount as a final quote.


## Publishing on Medium does not require a promise of earnings

A free Medium account can publish stories, while a paid reader membership unlocks member-only reading benefits. The Partner Program is an additional monetization path for writers who meet its requirements. These functions should not be collapsed into the claim that Medium “pays anyone to write.” A public story may reach readers without being paywalled and without generating Partner Program revenue.

A writer should first decide whether the platform’s editor, distribution, reader community and publication model fit the work. Then consider whether to make a particular story eligible for earnings. Keep a source copy and maintain an audience relationship outside one platform. This protects the writer if a story is unlisted, the program changes, the account is suspended or a future export does not preserve the intended presentation.


## Partner Program eligibility: several conditions must all be met

Medium’s current eligibility page says an enrolling writer must have an active Medium membership, at least one published story and a complete profile; be at least 18; have a bank account and tax residence in a supported country; and agree to the Partner Program Terms and the content-eligible-for-paywall policy. The supported-country list is extensive but not universal, and the help page says outlying territories are not eligible.

The enrollment guide says there is no separate application review once all requirements are met, but this does not mean monetization is automatic for every account or every story. Writers still need to maintain program eligibility and comply with content rules. Check the live list for the relevant country and payout method before paying for membership solely to pursue monetization. Eligibility and terms can change.


## How Medium describes Partner Program earnings

Medium’s earnings help article says payouts for locked stories are calculated using member reading or listening time, engagement points from member claps, highlights and replies, bonuses for Boosted stories, and an adjustment based on the ratio of member reads to total member views. The page says a qualifying member read requires at least 30 seconds, and idle time is excluded from reading-time calculations. These measures are combined into a model that Medium may update.

This makes the income variable rather than a fixed rate per view. A large number of page loads from non-members does not equal a guaranteed payout, and a title that attracts clicks but does not hold member attention may receive a lower adjustment. Earnings are updated daily and may take up to 48 hours to finalize, according to the Help Center. Writers should use their dashboard as the record of actual program performance rather than online anecdotes or a hypothetical calculator.


## Medium’s $10 payout threshold, Stripe and tax details

Medium says earnings must reach $10 USD before a payout is sent to the writer’s Stripe account. If the monthly total is below the threshold, the balance rolls over. To receive payment, the writer needs a valid connected Stripe account and verified taxpayer information. The payout guide says payments are typically initiated by the 10th business day of the month for the previous month and can take another five to seven business days to appear.

The enrollment guide warns that when an account reaches $10 while payout settings remain incomplete for 30 consecutive days, Partner Program enrollment can become inactive and stories can be removed from the paywall. The exact state and deadlines should be checked in the writer’s dashboard. Keep legal identity and tax information consistent with the payout processor’s requirements, and do not treat a displayed balance as money already received.


## Paywall rules and AI-generated writing restrictions

Medium’s current paywall eligibility policy says stories primarily about Medium itself, the Partner Program or the mechanics of the platform are not eligible to earn. It also excludes stories where most of the work is generated by an AI writing tool with little human editing, fact-checking or original contribution. The policy distinguishes that from using AI for outlining, spell-checking, grammar support or fact verification. The Partner Program terms also require the writer to have sufficient commercial rights to every element of a locked story.

This review is not a recommendation to disguise automated writing as human work or to put material behind a paywall without adding original analysis. Anyone publishing on Medium should read the current AI and content rules and accurately represent authorship. A policy violation can make a story ineligible and, under Medium’s rules, can lead to removal from monetization. Copyrighted images, quotations, audio and other embedded content need separate rights consideration.


## Discoverability and the limits of platform distribution

Medium offers publication pages, topic discovery, member recommendations and external sharing, but it does not promise a particular audience size for a new article. Distribution depends on the story, the reader’s interests and history, editorial curation and platform systems. Search traffic and social sharing can contribute to reach, but they are not a stable substitute for an owned mailing list or a direct relationship with readers.

A sensible publishing plan uses Medium as one channel among several. Keep a canonical copy of the work, link to an author site where appropriate, and make sure the excerpt and headline accurately describe the story. If the objective is a business newsletter, compare whether an email service or a website gives better control over subscriber export, segmentation and branding. Medium can help with discovery, but the publisher still owns the distribution strategy.


## Alternatives and a fair comparison for writers

Compare Medium with a self-hosted blog, a newsletter platform and other creator marketplaces using the same goals: ownership of the audience, publication layout, portability, recommendation reach, analytics, paywall controls, fee structure, payout eligibility, tax setup and moderation rules. A hosted platform reduces setup and maintenance work; a self-managed site can offer more control but requires hosting, security, payments and audience development.

Medium’s reader membership is a clear paid product, while the Partner Program offers variable earnings rather than a guaranteed revenue share per subscriber or per read. A newsletter subscription may give a writer a more direct recurring relationship but introduces separate payment and list-management costs. Choose based on the workflow and audience that actually exist, not on an assumed income projection.


## Account access, moderation and work portability

Publishing platforms can change rules, product features and distribution at any time. Medium’s Partner Program terms reserve the right to modify, suspend or discontinue the program, and say a locked story may be unlocked if a writer is removed. A writer also has obligations around originality, rights and program standing. Those terms matter even when day-to-day publishing appears simple.

Retain local copies of drafts, final text, images, links and publication metadata. Keep an independent list of subscribers or readers when permission has been given to maintain it. Review account and publication permissions, and understand how a story’s paywall setting affects access. Portability is a basic business continuity practice, not a prediction that any particular platform will close.


## Who Medium may suit and where it may fall short

Medium may suit writers who value a low-friction editor, a built-in reading community and access to a paid membership audience. Readers who consume many member-only articles may find the flat membership more useful than purchasing stories one at a time. Writers with a complete profile, a steady publishing practice and a supported payout location can evaluate the Partner Program after reading its terms.

It may be a poor fit for a creator who needs fixed, predictable earnings; full control over the customer relationship; custom commerce or membership tiers; guaranteed distribution; or monetization from every kind of content. Eligibility requirements also exclude some countries and territories. A writer should not pay for an annual membership based only on an income promise from a third party.


## Medium buyer checklist before paying or enabling a paywall

For readers, compare the $5 monthly and $50 annual options against actual reading habits and confirm the billing channel. For writers, verify the active-membership and published-story requirements, supported country, profile status, tax setup, Stripe connection and current paywall policy. Estimate costs independently of any expected Partner Program payment.

Before locking a story, confirm that the work is original, accurately attributed, eligible under the AI policy and cleared for commercial use. Read the current Partner Program terms, understand the $10 payout floor and timing, and keep copies of all published material. Track actual member reads and earnings over time; do not equate page views, claps or a popular post with a guaranteed return.


## Medium SCAM review verdict: useful publishing reach, variable creator income

The official sources reviewed describe a functioning reader membership and a defined Partner Program, with published eligibility, earnings and payout rules. They do not substantiate a scam allegation. The main caution is financial: paying for membership is a platform cost, not an investment that guarantees earnings. The Partner Program depends on qualifying member activity, a writer’s ongoing eligibility, content rules, valid payout setup and Medium’s program calculations.

Medium is easiest to assess as a publishing and reading service with optional monetization, not as a promised income system. Decide whether its audience and editor are useful even if Partner Program earnings are zero. This dated desk review uses public first-party sources and does not include a live account, payment or moderation test. Membership prices, payout rules and program terms were checked November 18, 2026; confirm current details before subscribing or publishing.


## Features and decision criteria

- Free reading and publishing account with optional paid membership
- Member-only stories and listening or offline reading benefits
- Partner Program revenue based on eligible story performance among members
- Writer analytics, publications, comments and distribution tools

## Pricing considerations

Medium’s public Help Center displayed these membership prices and Partner Program conditions when checked November 18, 2026. Regional prices, taxes, app-store billing, eligibility and product terms may differ. Partner Program revenue is not guaranteed income; the $10 payout minimum and a valid Stripe account and verified tax information are required for payouts.
