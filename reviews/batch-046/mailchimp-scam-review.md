# Mailchimp review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-16  
**Category:** Email marketing and audience management  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Mailchimp offers email marketing plans whose price and send allowance depend on audience size and tier. List quality, consent and billing limits should be evaluated before importing subscribers.

## Editorial verdict

Mailchimp publishes plan limits and trial terms. Prices vary with contacts, region, promotions and selected features. This review does not recommend unsolicited outreach or make claims about campaign performance.

## Mailchimp review: audience quality matters more than list size

An email service is only useful when the sender has permission to contact the audience and can respect each person’s choices. Before importing contacts, identify how each address was collected, what the person agreed to receive and how an unsubscribe is recorded. Separate opted-in subscribers from records with uncertain provenance. Do not use a purchased or scraped list simply because a platform offers an import button; provider terms, law and recipient expectations still apply.

Start with a small, clearly permissioned group and a message relevant to the signup. Check the sender identity, reply path, unsubscribe link and rendering on common devices. Monitor complaints and bounces, and pause if a source or consent record cannot be explained. Mailchimp’s features do not create consent or guarantee inbox placement.


## Mailchimp contact limits, sends and total price

Mailchimp’s US pricing page showed Free at $0 with a 250-contact limit, Essentials starting at $13 per month and Standard at $20 per month when checked on 2026-11-16. The page explains that price depends on contact count and plan limits, and that overages may apply when contact or send limits are exceeded. Treat the displayed starting prices as a dated indication, not a quote for a particular audience. Non-US pricing and promotions can differ.

Before choosing a tier, count billable contacts using the provider’s current rules and estimate monthly sends, not just active subscribers. Remove duplicates and honor suppression records before import. Model seasonal growth and a month with more campaigns than usual. Confirm what happens when a limit is reached and whether the service pauses sending or charges for extra volume. Save the checkout summary with the chosen contact tier and renewal date.


## Mailchimp forms, automation and message review

Build a test signup form that clearly states what the subscriber will receive and how often. Submit it yourself, verify the confirmation flow, then test an unsubscribe and a preference change. Check that these choices propagate to every audience segment and connected tool. A consent record should not be overwritten when a contact is imported from another source.

If you plan to use automation, begin with one simple welcome sequence and a test address. Confirm that triggers fire only for the intended signup, that a person can leave the sequence and that an updated preference stops future messages. Review every automated message before it is sent to a real audience. Add a named owner and pause procedure so a broken trigger does not continue contacting people.


## Mailchimp migration, data and deliverability controls

Prepare a copy of the current list and map each field before migration. Retain source, consent date and suppression state where the service permits. Import a small sample, compare counts and investigate unexpected changes rather than assuming all records were accepted. Keep the original provider available until historical reports and opt-out behavior have been reconciled.

Configure sender authentication and follow current provider instructions for the sending domain. Start with expected, permission-based messages rather than a sudden high volume. Watch bounce and complaint patterns; an attractive template cannot compensate for a list people did not expect to hear from. Follow the law and provider policy applicable to your audience and business.


## Mailchimp alternatives: compare the same consented workflow

Compare Mailchimp with another email platform using the same subscriber count, monthly sends, signup form and automation. Include contact billing rules, overage terms, segmentation, exportability and support. Evaluate ease of honoring opt-outs at least as carefully as campaign design. A service with a lower entry price may cost more at your audience size, while a service with more automation may add complexity the team does not need.

Ask each provider how to export subscribers and suppression records, what happens when a plan is cancelled and which features are included in the selected tier. Run a small test before transferring an active audience. Make the decision from the complete workflow and the actual billing estimate, not an isolated promotional headline.


## Who should consider Mailchimp?

Mailchimp may suit an organization that wants campaign, audience and automation tools and can maintain clear permission records. Verify contact and sending limits for the selected plan, configure sender authentication and test signup, unsubscribe and export paths before sending to the full audience. Use only contacts whose expectations and consent you can document.

Mailchimp’s current plan page supports a source-based description of pricing tiers and usage limits. It does not establish that a campaign will perform or that any particular list is lawful to contact. This buyer review is not a fraud finding. Confirm terms and local requirements before import, and keep outreach aligned with the permission each subscriber gave.


## Features and decision criteria

- Email campaigns and audience records
- Plan-based monthly send allowances
- Forms, scheduling and automation features by tier
