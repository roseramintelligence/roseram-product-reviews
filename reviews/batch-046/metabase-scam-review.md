# Metabase review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-16  
**Category:** Business intelligence and analytics dashboards  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Metabase helps teams answer questions about data and share dashboards. Assess how you will define a metric and protect its underlying records before selecting a plan.

## Editorial verdict

Metabase documents how collections and data access interact. It is a practical candidate for internal reporting when the team can name reliable metrics and prove that access works for ordinary users.

## Metabase dashboards that survive a disputed number

Sales says it closed twelve deals. Finance counts nine. Before opening the dashboard builder, sit with both teams and define which date counts, what happens to a refund and where the source records live. Give the analyst a small dataset with one of each disputed case. If two people still get different answers, fix the definition before investing in more charts.

Make the **Metabase dashboard** explain that definition where people need it. Store the date range and units alongside the query. Ask a colleague to reproduce the result from the same sample and find the refund that changed the total. A useful dashboard gives the person who is late to the meeting enough context to join the conversation.


## Metabase collection vs data permissions

Metabase documents different controls for dashboards and the data they use. Check both. For an internal pilot, create a sample public team report and a restricted payroll dataset with fictitious names. Sign in as an ordinary user and work through the dashboard, drill-through and download paths. Repeat with an account belonging to more than one group so you can see the effective permissions.

The documentation identifies some data-viewing and result-download controls as paid features. Compare the edition against the *permission boundary* you actually need. A pilot with a single administrator cannot establish what a customer or a colleague should see. Ask the person responsible for data access to approve the same test they will run after an upgrade.


## Metabase Open Source vs Pro and Apache Superset

The price comparison starts with the Open Source edition and runs through hosted and paid plans. Budget for the warehouse or database, the operating work and any particular controls the team requires. Ask for the price at your expected reporting audience size. Check whether added users, embedding and hosting affect the bill for that exact setup.

An engineering-heavy analytics group can compare Apache Superset using one dashboard and an agreed access test. A team that depends on colleagues without SQL experience should give those colleagues the same question in both tools. Choose the one that lets the people responsible for the decision retrieve an agreed number and explain it without having to ask the original analyst every week.


## Features and decision criteria

- Visual queries and SQL reporting
- Saved questions and dashboards
- Collection permissions and plan-specific data controls
