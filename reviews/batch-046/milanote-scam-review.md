# Milanote review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-24  
**Category:** Visual planning for creative projects  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Milanote organizes creative project material on visual boards that combine text, images, video, sketches, links and tasks. Its pricing page distinguishes a capped free plan from individual and team plans with unlimited content. Evaluate whether the visual workspace remains manageable as a board grows.

## Editorial verdict

Milanote is a good candidate for moodboards, storyboards and research collections where seeing references together matters. The free edition limits content and uploads, so test a realistic project before committing to a larger archive or inviting collaborators.

## Build a board around one creative deliverable

Collect references, a short brief, rough sketches and the next few tasks for one real project. Check whether the board lets the team see both the big picture and the details without excessive zooming or scrolling. A gallery is most useful when each item has a clear reason to be there.

Use one project with a collaborator and ask them to locate the current brief and next action. The official site describes view, comment and edit participation, but this review did not create a board or test permissions.


## Set a limit before the inspiration pile grows

Visual tools make it easy to save another reference. Agree on how the team will name boards, archive completed work and mark the current version. Without these conventions, a board can collect more material than anyone can review.

Try finding one image and one decision after a few days. If the team must remember where an item was placed, add a simple label or structure rather than accumulating more boards.


## Compare content caps with a real project

The free plan lists 100 notes, images or links and 10 file uploads. Count what a representative storyboard or campaign board actually needs, including files added by collaborators. The cap applies even when the number of boards themselves is unlimited.

Paid individual and team plans list unlimited content, but their billing structures differ. Estimate the appropriate seats and compare annual commitment with the project’s expected duration.


## Use the visual space where it adds context

A board can make relationships between references easier to discuss than a folder of disconnected files. Routine deadlines, detailed status reports or complex permissions may need another tool alongside it. Define which system holds the authoritative task and due date.

This review is based on Milanote’s product and pricing pages. It does not evaluate privacy settings, export completeness, mobile behavior or a particular team’s collaboration. Confirm those questions with a small project trial.


## Features and decision criteria

- Boards with notes, images, video and sketches
- Web research collection
- Tasks and visual project organization
- Collaborator view, comment and edit roles

## Pricing considerations

The page also offers trials for paid plans. Confirm team seat count, billing cycle and current terms before checkout.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does free mean unlimited content?

No. The current plan page lists limits of 100 notes, images or links and 10 file uploads.

### Can a board be shared with different access levels?

The product page describes edit, comment and view collaboration; test the actual permissions before sharing confidential work.

### Is it best suited to a text-heavy task database?

Its documented emphasis is visual creative organization; compare task filtering and reporting needs separately.
