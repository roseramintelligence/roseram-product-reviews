# Miro review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-16  
**Category:** Visual collaboration and whiteboarding  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Miro provides collaborative visual boards and structured canvas formats. The Free plan limits editable boards, while paid plans add collaboration and capacity; verify seat, access and AI-credit details.

## Editorial verdict

Miro’s official pricing page describes board limits, seat billing and monthly AI credits. This review reports publisher claims and makes no hands-on performance or customer-outcome claims.

## Miro review: test a board that helps people decide and act

A whiteboard is most useful when it turns discussion into a shared result. Pick a real workshop with a clear question and define what participants should leave with: a decision, an ordered set of priorities or an assigned next step. Create a small board with only the needed frames and prompts. Invite one participant who has not seen the setup, then observe whether they can contribute and find the conclusion without a tour.

After the session, see whether the board remains understandable to someone who missed the meeting. Add a concise decision summary, owner and date. If the only record is a crowded canvas, the exercise may have generated ideas without producing a useful handoff. Miro’s formats and templates can support collaboration, but the facilitator still needs a clear objective and a way to close the discussion.


## Miro plans, editable boards and member seats

Miro’s official pricing page described Free with three editable boards; it noted that other boards can remain viewable while only the three most recent are editable. Starter and Business list unlimited boards and add access and collaboration capabilities. The page showed $8 per member monthly for Starter and $20 for Business with yearly billing when checked on 2026-11-16. Miro prices by seat on those plans; annual billing was described as 20% less than monthly. Confirm current terms, guests and member counts before inviting a full team.

Miro distinguishes members, guests and visitors, and the free plan’s sharing model differs from paid plans. Test the exact audience you intend to invite: an internal collaborator, a client and someone with a view-only link. Verify what each person can see and change. A seat budget should count people who need to create boards, not only those who start a project.


## Miro AI credits and integrations

Miro’s pricing material describes a monthly allocation of AI credits on paid plans, with amounts varying by tier and additional packages available. If a workshop depends on AI summaries or generated diagrams, test that task and check how credits are counted. Do not assume “AI included” means unlimited use. Record which outputs need human review and avoid placing confidential material into an AI workflow until access and data handling have been reviewed.

List the integrations the team expects to use, then test one with a non-sensitive board. Check permissions, what information travels in each direction and how duplicate or stale records are handled. Miro lists more than 250 integrations on its pricing page. The count does not establish that each integration fits your setup or is included in every plan.


## Miro workshop operations and accessibility

Run a short rehearsal with the devices and connection conditions participants will use. Check that text is readable at ordinary zoom, that the board can be navigated without relying only on color and that participants know where to add material. Assign a facilitator to help people who have not used a canvas before. For important decisions, produce a text summary that can be read without interpreting a dense diagram.

Use a consistent naming pattern and archive old boards deliberately. Search for the latest board and check whether a newcomer can distinguish it from a duplicate. If boards contain customer or employee information, define who can share public links and how quickly access should be revoked after the event.


## Miro alternatives: compare facilitation and follow-through

Compare Miro with the whiteboard or diagramming tools your team already has. Use one identical workshop agenda, with a warm-up, prioritization exercise and final action list. Evaluate participation, board clarity, exports, sharing control and the effort required to turn the result into tracked work. A visual tool may be excellent for exploration while a project system may be better for long-term ownership; your workflow might require both.

Compare plan costs using the actual number of paid members and external collaborators. Check how visitors and guests are handled, what AI allowance applies and how much ongoing administration is required. Ask vendors to demonstrate your specific sharing scenario rather than relying on a static feature matrix.


## Miro account safety and board retention

Review privacy and security materials against your organization’s needs. Assign workspace administrators, restrict public sharing where appropriate and test removal of a collaborator. Keep a written record of where important decisions are stored and how they are exported. This review does not certify Miro for a particular regulated environment; obtain specialist assessment when required.

Export a sample board and confirm the copy remains legible and useful outside the account. Decide who will preserve the board if the facilitator leaves and what should be retained after a project closes. Clear retention and access practices make a collaborative board easier to govern.


## Who should consider Miro?

Miro is a candidate when teams need a shared visual canvas for workshops, diagrams or planning and can manage the associated member and access model. Start with one session, verify that a decision and next steps remain readable afterward, and confirm the plan’s editable-board, seat and credit limits before wider adoption.

Official materials support the described plan structure and collaboration features; they do not prove that a particular workshop will be effective. Use the same agenda and participants to compare alternatives. This “SCAM Review” is buyer diligence, not a conclusion that Miro is fraudulent.


## Features and decision criteria

- Collaborative visual boards
- Docs, tables, timelines, Kanban and slides on canvas
- Plan-dependent private boards, member roles and AI credits

## Pricing considerations

Published amounts observed on Miro’s official pricing page on 2026-11-16. Plans are seat-based; annual billing was shown as saving 20% over monthly. AI credits and feature limits differ by plan. Check current billing and guest rules before purchase.
