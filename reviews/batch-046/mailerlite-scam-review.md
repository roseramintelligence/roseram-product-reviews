# MailerLite review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-18  
**Category:** Email marketing and automation software  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

MailerLite is an email campaign, audience, form and automation service with Free, Comfort, Power and Enterprise options. Plans scale by subscriber count and email usage, while paid annual billing is prepaid and its terms narrowly limit refunds. The service also documents AI-agent access through MCP and a multi-year inactive-account deletion policy.

## Editorial verdict

MailerLite publishes current pricing, privacy and terms-of-service documents. The sources reviewed do not substantiate a scam allegation. Buyers should verify subscriber and sending tiers, understand the refund and renewal terms, review MCP permissions and export their list before account closure or long inactivity.

## MailerLite SCAM review: check the list size and the exit terms

MailerLite provides email campaigns, subscriber management, automations, signup forms and landing pages. Its current pricing page describes a Free plan and paid Comfort and Power plans that scale with subscriber count and sending needs, plus a custom Enterprise option. The Terms of Service and Privacy Policy describe cancellation, refunds, AI-agent connections and retention. This review is based on those first-party sources rather than a live campaign or account test.

“SCAM Review” is the directory’s label and does not mean a fraud finding. The sources reviewed do not substantiate that accusation. The main buying questions are whether the selected tier fits both the subscriber list and send volume, how an annual charge is handled, whether a refund is available after purchase, what an AI agent connected through MCP can change, and how data is retained after account closure.


## MailerLite Free plan and its starting limits

The pricing page reviewed lists Free with up to 250 subscribers and 2,500 monthly emails, two user seats, community support, three automations, one website and landing page, one digital product or booking and three signup forms. It says the Free plan does not require a credit card and shows basic campaign functionality. These allowances let a small creator try a newsletter workflow without beginning a paid subscription.

Map each form and automation to the audience and frequency it will serve. A few thousand emails can be consumed quickly if a list receives multiple campaigns and automated follow-ups. Decide whether the limited websites, forms and support are enough for the launch. If the audience is larger, remove duplicates and suppress people who should not be emailed before estimating the paid tier.


## Comfort and Power change the email, automation and team limits

The pricing page describes Comfort for regular newsletters and automations, with three user seats, email support, 50 automations, ten websites or landing pages and ten signup forms. Power is positioned for frequent senders, teams and AI agents, with unlimited monthly emails, seats, automations, landing pages and forms, plus live chat and email support. The page says pricing changes according to the subscriber count and the selected billing term.

The words “unlimited” on the pricing page should be read with the plan’s acceptable-use rules, sending policy and subscriber tier in view. Confirm the current volume estimate and the actual checkout amount for the business’s list size. Check whether each teammate needs an individual seat and which integrations are required. If the team mostly sends a monthly newsletter, a plan intended for frequent sending may be more than it needs.


## Subscriber count and sending volume both influence price

MailerLite’s pricing presentation asks for the number of subscribers and distinguishes monthly and yearly billing. It advertises a 10 percent saving with yearly billing, and notes that taxes may apply. Contact size and email use are related but not identical: a company may have a large list with infrequent sends or a smaller list with several automations and product announcements.

Calculate the monthly audience and send calendar before selecting a tier. Include welcome messages, re-engagement and transactional messages if the plan covers them. Save the final checkout quote, not just the headline price. Revisit the tier after a list import or campaign launch so subscriber growth does not trigger an unexpected plan change or force a downgrade during a busy period.


## Annual plans are paid upfront and cancellation does not guarantee refunds

MailerLite’s pricing page offers yearly billing, while the Terms explain that subscription cancellation and refunds are separate questions. The terms state that unused or partially used monthly or yearly plans are not refunded when the customer forgets to cancel, cancels after renewal or chooses the wrong plan. They state that a refund is generally required only when MailerLite terminates service without cause before the end of a prepaid year, subject to the terms and applicable law.

Before choosing annual billing, confirm that the list size and features have been tested and that the organization expects to use the service for the full term. Add the renewal date to a finance calendar and preserve the contract version. If the company no longer needs the plan, cancel before the next billing date and check the account’s effective date. Do not assume a cancellation request automatically reverses a charge already processed.


## Account review can pause sending and landing-page publishing

MailerLite’s terms describe an account review for suspected non-compliance, unusual activity or security concerns. During review, the provider may pause operational functions such as email sending and landing-page publishing while it requests information. It says an account may be terminated if the user does not respond to an inquiry within 30 days. This is a service-integrity control with potential business consequences for a sender who relies on the platform.

Keep the account contact email monitored and maintain evidence of signup source and permission. If the provider requests clarification, respond promptly through the account owner and preserve the support thread. Do not launch a campaign with a newly imported or questionable list before the approval process is complete. Maintain a backup channel for critical updates so an account review does not eliminate the only way to reach customers.


## AI features and MCP connections can access and modify account data

MailerLite’s Terms include AI-powered features and specifically say that connecting an AI Agent through MCP authorizes that agent to access, read and potentially modify data in the MailerLite account according to its functionality and configuration. The listed data can include subscriber lists and personal information, campaign content, templates, automation workflows and forms. That is a broader permission than asking an AI tool to draft text in a separate document.

Review the permissions before connecting an MCP client. Use an account with the minimum appropriate role, keep the connection under a monitored business identity and revoke access when the experiment ends. Test with a small audience or duplicate campaign before allowing changes to live flows. Ask whether the integration can send messages, edit consent settings or export records. Do not connect a personal AI agent to a production account without approval from the data owner.


## Privacy policy roles and contact-data responsibilities

MailerLite’s Privacy Policy distinguishes customer account data from subscriber contact lists that the customer uploads. It says the customer is responsible for its contacts and that the service processes list content for the customer. It lists account information and technical usage data, and describes legal obligations that can require retention of certain billing or abuse records after a deletion request.

Keep a record of where each subscriber came from, what message they agreed to receive and how their preference can be changed. Avoid uploading sensitive categories of information unless the provider’s terms and the organization’s legal basis permit it. Review the current data-processing terms and subprocessor list before a cross-border or regulated use. A privacy policy does not turn a purchased or scraped list into an opted-in audience.


## Data retention and inactive-account rules

MailerLite’s Privacy Policy says an eligible account-deletion request results in personal data being deleted or anonymized within 30 days. It also says terminated or inactive accounts can be permanently deleted after three years, with email notices and an additional 60-day window to request a manual export after access is revoked. Some billing and legal records may be retained for seven years to meet legal, dispute or abuse-prevention needs.

Treat the dashboard as an operating system, not as the only copy of a business mailing list. Export subscriber statuses, suppression records, campaign templates and analytics before deleting the account or letting it remain inactive. Keep the account owner’s inbox current so inactivity notices arrive. Verify which exports include consent evidence and ensure that an import to another service preserves unsubscribe status.


## Signup forms, automation and consent need a complete test

MailerLite’s paid tiers add forms, websites, landing pages and automations. A subscriber’s path might begin with a signup form, continue through confirmation and a welcome series, then change when the person unsubscribes or purchases. Each transition should preserve the person’s preference and avoid repeated or unintended messages. A feature list alone does not prove that a form’s consent wording or automation timing meets a business’s requirements.

Test the whole lifecycle with internal addresses: signup, confirmation, edit preferences, unsubscribe, re-subscribe and deletion. Inspect every branch for a duplicated send and confirm that suppressed users do not re-enter a campaign after an import. Use clear disclosure next to the form and keep a version of the wording shown at signup. Build a pause control that staff can use if a campaign begins behaving unexpectedly.


## Email delivery and sending reputation remain a shared responsibility

MailerLite supplies campaign tools and infrastructure, but inbox placement depends on sender authentication, permission, bounce and complaint rates, engagement, content and sending history. Its terms include an anti-spam policy and permit review or suspension for suspected violations. Account suspension can affect sending and published landing pages, so list management is an operational requirement, not just a compliance detail.

Authenticate the sending domain and begin with recent, permission-based subscribers. Do not send to purchased or scraped contacts. Monitor bounces, unsubscribes and complaints after each volume change, and maintain a process for responding to provider reviews. If the business has been inactive for months, reconfirm permission as required by the current policy rather than resuming a dormant list at full scale.


## MCP access should be piloted with a least-privilege account

MailerLite’s AI terms say that connecting an AI Agent through MCP may permit access to subscriber lists, campaign content, templates, automations, forms and other account data, and may allow changes. That means a connection intended to draft a message could have broader access depending on the agent and its configuration. The terms treat the connection as an explicit authorization from the account holder.

Create a test workspace or limit the connected user to the smallest supported role. Try read-only research before granting write actions, review every proposed automation change and keep an approval step before publishing. Revoke the token after the pilot if the business does not intend to use it continuously. Do not treat an AI integration as a harmless browser extension when it can access personal subscriber records.


## Migrate subscribers without losing consent and suppression history

A list export can include an email address without preserving the context that makes it usable: signup source, consent time, preference, bounce or complaint state and the version of the notice shown. MailerLite’s terms and privacy policy place important responsibilities on the sender and distinguish the account owner’s data from subscriber information processed for campaigns.

Before importing a list, map each source field and test a small sample. Confirm that unsubscribed or complained contacts remain suppressed and that custom fields retain their meaning. Compare total active subscribers with the destination’s billing count and remove duplicates only when that does not erase needed compliance evidence. Keep the original export in a restricted archive until the new platform’s reports and preferences have been verified.


## Generated email copy and automations need editorial and compliance review

AI-assisted copy can speed up a subject line or draft, but generated text may invent product details, overstate a discount or omit a required disclosure. A campaign automation can repeat that error across an audience. The organization remains responsible for the accuracy of its offers, unsubscribe links, timing and audience selection, regardless of which tool wrote the initial draft.

Review every generated message against the current product facts, promotion terms and brand policy. Send a test to several inboxes and inspect mobile rendering, merge fields, tracking links and footer details. Confirm the intended segment and suppression rules before scheduling. For claims about health, finance or measurable results, require a qualified human reviewer before release.


## MailerLite alternatives and an equivalent price comparison

Alternatives include newsletter-focused services, ecommerce marketing tools, transactional email providers and all-in-one CRM platforms. Compare active subscriber definitions, monthly send allowances, automations, seats, forms, AI access, support, overages, refund rules and how data exports are handled. A low-cost service can become expensive if a large list is billed at a higher tier, while a broader marketing suite may include capabilities the team does not use.

Use one sample audience, one welcome flow and one campaign to compare finalists. Price the same subscriber and sending volume under monthly and annual billing. Test an export and verify unsubscribe states. Ask whether AI integrations can modify live lists or trigger sends, and whether the lowest tier provides the support the business needs. Decide based on the complete workflow and exit cost, not only the base price.


## MailerLite SCAM review verdict: favorable entry tier, review billing and AI permissions

MailerLite publishes a free starting tier, paid subscriber-based plans, account-retention rules and terms explaining refunds, cancellation, AI functionality and MCP access. The Free plan has meaningful but limited audience and sending allowances. Annual plans can be prepaid, unused time is generally non-refundable under the terms, and connected AI agents may receive permission to read or modify account material.

The public sources reviewed do not substantiate a scam allegation. Before buying, verify the subscriber tier and billing term, calendar the renewal, test the consent and unsubscribe lifecycle, review MCP permissions and export data before closure or extended inactivity.
