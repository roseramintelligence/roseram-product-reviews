## AI Resume REview: product-management claims need concrete evidence

Product-management applications often describe outcomes produced by several people. A resume should explain the applicant’s actual contribution without implying sole ownership of every result. Before requesting feedback, distinguish the decision you made, the work you coordinated and the result observed by the organization. That context helps you reject a rewrite that sounds impressive but changes the facts.

The publisher describes reviewing structure, clarity and alignment with a job description. Those are different tasks from verifying employment history or predicting selection. A suggestion can improve readability while being wrong for a specific role. Compare it with the vacancy and your own evidence, rather than accepting it because it uses familiar product-management language.


## A feedback review with an intentionally weak bullet

Prepare a harmless sample containing one clear achievement and one vague statement. Write what evidence is available for each before requesting analysis. Inspect whether the proposed feedback asks for missing context or simply invents specificity. A useful revision should make the real contribution easier to understand without creating unsupported metrics or responsibilities.

Compare the revised document with the actual job requirements. Remove keywords that imply experience you do not have, even if they appear prominently in the vacancy. This review does not verify the feedback model or report hiring outcomes from NextSprints. It proposes a way to evaluate whether the advice improves an honest application.

Ask another person to identify the applicant’s role in a cross-functional result from the final wording. If that interpretation differs from the actual contribution, revise it again. This is particularly useful for claims about revenue, launch results or customer adoption, where a short bullet can easily obscure the difference between participation and direct responsibility.

Keep the rejected suggestions as well as the accepted edits during a small evaluation. This reveals whether the review process repeatedly encourages the same unsupported claim. A useful service should reduce ambiguity about the candidate’s actual contribution, not merely make every bullet sound more senior. The applicant remains responsible for the final account of experience.
