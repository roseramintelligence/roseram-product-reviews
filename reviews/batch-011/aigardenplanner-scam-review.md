# AIGardenPlanner review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-25  
**Category:** AI garden design and landscape planning software  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

AIGardenPlanner combines a free browser-based 3D garden editor with paid AI design tools. Its public materials describe photo redesign, garden styles, plant identification and advice, image enhancement, video animation and 3D tours, with usage controlled by monthly credits and project limits. Professional and Business plans advertise commercial-use rights, while Starter is described as personal use. Buyers should check the annual upfront charge, credit cost per feature, image-upload terms and the limits of AI-generated garden concepts before planning a real installation.

## Editorial verdict

AIGardenPlanner is worth evaluating for homeowners who want visual garden concepts and for landscape professionals who need client-facing design material. The free 3D editor and credit-based plan structure make it possible to separate manual layout work from AI-generated features. Treat generated images as concept references, not planting, drainage, construction or horticultural plans; verify species, climate suitability, measurements and site constraints with qualified local sources before purchasing materials or beginning work.

## Separate the free 3D editor from credit-based AI features

AIGardenPlanner’s official product pages describe two different ways to work. The browser-based 3D editor lets a user arrange plants, paths and walls, and the pricing page says the editor itself is free with one saved project. Paid plans add AI photo redesign, style transfers and other generation tools. This distinction matters: someone who needs a simple layout may be able to start without a subscription, while repeated image generation or client work can require a paid tier.

Before starting, identify the outcome you need. A rough planting layout, a visual style exploration and a client presentation are different jobs. Test the free editor with one small area, then decide whether photo redesign or a 3D tour solves a real limitation. Keep your own site measurements, sun exposure, drainage, access and plant list alongside any generated image; the image is a visualization and should not be treated as a construction specification.


## Estimate credits and recurring cost before creating designs

The pricing page lists a credit for a standard garden design, 10 for a video animation, five for Plant Advisor, five for a quick 3D tour and 30 for a full-detail tour. Ultra Realistic Pro designs use four credits each. Because a visually polished result may invite repeated prompts and revisions, estimate the number of versions you expect before choosing a plan. Check whether failed generations or variations also consume credits and whether unused credits roll over; do not assume either behavior unless the current terms say so.

When checked, the page displayed Starter from $9 per month with an annual amount of $99, Professional from $21 per month with $249 annual billing, and Business from $42 per month with $499 annual billing. The page also showed annual promotional messaging. Compare the exact checkout amount with the renewal price, credit allowance, project cap and required license. A monthly equivalent shown beside a yearly charge is not the same as paying month to month.


## Check commercial rights and uploaded-image terms

AIGardenPlanner lists the Starter plan for personal use and says Professional and Business include commercial usage rights. If you create concepts for a customer, ask whether the license covers client presentations, marketing, editing, resale and delivery of source files. The terms say rights depend on the subscription plan and that generated images may not be unique. Keep a copy of the plan and terms that apply when you create deliverables.

The terms also say users retain ownership of uploaded content while granting the service a worldwide, royalty-free license to use, reproduce and modify it to provide and improve the service; they describe possible use of images in aggregated and anonymized form to improve models. They further say data associated with an account may be deleted after cancellation and recommend backups. Before uploading a yard photo, consider whether it shows a person, a neighboring home, a license plate or other sensitive detail, and check current retention and deletion controls.


## Use generated concepts as a starting point, not a site plan

AI visualizations can help compare styles or communicate an idea, but they do not establish that a plant is suitable for a local climate, that a retaining wall is structurally sound, or that grading and drainage will work. Verify plant names, mature size, toxicity, water needs and local restrictions separately. For work involving utilities, structures, slopes or drainage, consult the relevant local professional before acting on a rendered image.

A practical pilot is to upload a non-sensitive photo of a small garden area, request one style, and compare the result against known boundaries and measurements. Record which details remain wrong and how much editing or credit use was needed. Then test a second workflow only if it addresses a real need. This desk review summarizes the publisher’s public pages and terms; it does not claim hands-on testing, horticultural validation or guaranteed design accuracy.
