# Motion review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-24  
**Category:** AI task and calendar planning  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Motion brings together task and project planning, calendars, meeting notes, documents and workflows. Its pricing page lists monthly AI credits for each seat and different limits by plan. Buyers should test how the schedule handles changes instead of assuming automated prioritization always matches personal judgment.

## Editorial verdict

Motion is useful to evaluate when an individual or team wants tasks and calendar time planned together. It is a paid subscription after a free trial, and AI credits can affect the operating cost. Keep a human review step for deadlines and schedule changes.

## Test the plan with a messy real week

Add a few tasks with different durations, deadlines and dependencies alongside existing calendar events. Then change one meeting and see how the suggested plan responds. The output should make tradeoffs visible enough that the person can correct an unrealistic assumption.

A task scheduler cannot know whether a deadline is negotiable unless the user records that context. Review the proposed changes before allowing a schedule to become the team’s operating promise. This review did not run Motion or measure its scheduling quality.


## Check what a seat includes

The current page lists Pro AI and Business AI per-seat amounts with different features and monthly credits. Multiply by the number of people and include annual or monthly billing. Teams should also decide who administers workspaces, permissions and connected calendars.

If AI is part of the intended workflow, observe credit usage during a representative trial. A feature list alone does not estimate how quickly a team will use its included allocation.


## Keep integrations and calendar scope clear

Connect only the calendar and work sources needed for the trial. Verify which events and tasks appear, which source remains authoritative and how conflicts are handled. A consolidated view is useful when it stays clear where an event was originally created.

Meeting summaries and project documents can contain sensitive information. Review account access and the provider’s current privacy terms before connecting real company data. This review does not audit integrations or data handling.


## Treat productivity claims as a hypothesis

Motion’s site makes broad performance claims. Measure the user’s own task completion, missed commitments and time spent maintaining the schedule before drawing conclusions. A tool can rearrange work, but a more packed calendar is not automatically a better result.

This source-based review identifies published features, plans and credits and recommends a bounded evaluation. It does not verify productivity percentages, AI accuracy, cancellation behavior or compatibility with a particular organization.


## Features and decision criteria

- AI task and project planning
- Calendar and meeting features
- Docs, notes and integrations
- Seat-based plans with monthly AI credits

## Pricing considerations

The page shows annual billing with savings and separate credit top-up rates. Confirm trial length, billing cycle and actual AI consumption at checkout.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does Motion provide unlimited AI usage?

No. The current pricing page lists a monthly credit allowance per seat and a separate price for additional credits.

### Can a user override an automatically planned day?

Check the current product behavior in a trial and keep control of deadlines and calendar commitments.

### Is the published price a total team cost?

No. The listed amounts are per seat; calculate the total for all users and any additional credits.
