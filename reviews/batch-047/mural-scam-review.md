# Mural review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-16  
**Category:** Visual collaboration and online whiteboard software  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Mural is a collaborative visual workspace for workshops, diagrams and team planning. The free plan limits how many murals are editable at one time, while paid plans expand collaboration, rooms and administration.

## Editorial verdict

Mural is designed for structured group work on a shared canvas, with useful facilitation tools and external-collaboration options. Buyers should test the workshop workflow, guest model and active-board limits before using it as the archive for ongoing project decisions.

## Mural review: visual collaboration for workshops

Mural provides shared canvases for workshops, mapping exercises, diagrams and planning sessions. Its template library and facilitation features are intended to help a group move through a structured activity rather than simply place notes on an empty board. That can be useful for distributed teams that need a visible way to collect ideas and agree on a next step.

The quality of the outcome depends on preparation and follow-through. A workshop board can capture many opinions without showing who made the decision or what action followed. Start with one actual meeting, assign a facilitator, and decide how notes become owners, dates and project records afterward. Review how participants with different roles join and whether the session can be understood later by someone who did not attend.


## Mural plan limits, members and external guests

Mural's current pricing page lists a Free plan with unlimited murals but only three editable murals at a time, plus unlimited members and view-only visitors. Team+ is listed at $9.99 per member per month billed annually or $12 monthly. Business is listed at $17.99 per member per month billed annually and includes SAML single sign-on and unlimited external guests. Enterprise adds controls such as multiple workspaces, SCIM and audit logs.

The most relevant capacity question is not how many boards have ever been created; it is how many need to remain editable and available to the team. Map core members, one-time visitors and ongoing external guests to the plan definitions. If clients will edit boards over several months, confirm whether that collaboration requires a paid guest or Business access. Put the renewal date and member count in the project budget.


## Mural facilitation, templates and decision records

Mural advertises a large template collection, integrations and facilitation tools designed for workshops. Templates can give a session a useful structure, but a board does not automatically produce a decision record. Teams should standardize how they label assumptions, open questions, decisions and follow-up work so the canvas remains useful after the call.

Try a planning or retrospective template with the people who will use it regularly. Check keyboard access, zoom, performance on a smaller screen and whether collaborators can participate without extra friction. After the exercise, transfer decisions to the system used to track commitments. Archive obsolete canvases and label the canonical version; otherwise, a library of visually appealing boards can become difficult to search.


## Mural security, access and retention

Mural's pricing page places identity and administration features such as SAML SSO, SCIM, audit logs and data residency at higher tiers. Its trust materials describe security and compliance information for enterprise buyers. These controls may be important when boards include unreleased product plans, customer information or internal strategy, and their availability should be checked against the exact subscription.

Review link-sharing defaults, external guest access and the organization's workspace settings before inviting a client. Use separate workspaces where teams require different access boundaries. Decide who may export a board, invite participants or delete content, and confirm retention and data-location terms with procurement. A workshop tool can still contain consequential business information even if individual sticky notes look informal.


## Mural alternatives and the SCAM Review question

Compare Mural with Miro, FigJam and a whiteboard tool already included in the team's collaboration suite. Use the same number of editable boards, outside participants, facilitation sessions, integrations and security requirements. Consider whether an external guest needs recurring access and how workshop output is archived. The best fit is the one that supports participation and makes decisions traceable after the meeting.

Mural's official pricing and support pages describe a visual-collaboration service and plan boundaries; they do not establish that Mural is a scam. This desk review did not run a paid workshop or audit a tenant. Confirm the current membership and guest rules, access controls, export and retention behavior before placing client or strategy work on shared boards.


## Features and decision criteria

- Shared visual canvases with templates and facilitation tools
- Free and paid plan differences for editable murals, rooms and guests
- Enterprise identity, administration and data-residency controls

## Pricing considerations

Prices are the current public USD listing. Confirm how members, guests and visitors are defined for the intended workspace and whether the plan is purchased monthly or annually.
