# ActivTrak review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-18  
**Category:** Workforce analytics and employee activity monitoring software  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

ActivTrak is a workforce analytics and activity-monitoring platform that uses an installed agent to summarize how work devices are used. The vendor describes reports for application and website activity, active and idle time, schedules, productivity trends and workforce planning. Its default privacy controls hide more detailed data; screenshots, screen views, title bars, detailed URLs and some alarm actions are available through the Screen Details add-on. ActivTrak says it does not use keystroke logging, camera access, video recording or email monitoring. Its pricing page checked November 18, 2026 listed Free at $0 for up to three workers, Work Activity Tracking at $10 per worker/month, Workforce Management at $15 and Productivity Optimization at $17. The paid figures are displayed per worker per month, but the vendor says plans are billed on annual contracts. A 14-day trial includes a higher-tier bundle and then downgrades to Free. Capterra’s ActivTrak profile showed 4.5/5 from 592 user reviews at the time checked, with praise around usability and visibility alongside recurring concerns about price, plan restrictions and administration complexity. Public evidence describes an established product; it does not establish fraud or prove that the monitoring approach suits a particular workplace. Roseram did not install the agent, test collection, audit the platform or assess legal compliance.

## Editorial verdict

The evidence does not substantiate calling ActivTrak a scam. It describes a real workforce analytics product with useful privacy controls, but the product still collects activity data from work devices and can expose more detailed browsing and screen information when an organization buys and enables optional features. The central buying decision is therefore as much about workplace policy and employee trust as dashboard quality. The vendor’s limits on keystroke, camera, video and email collection are meaningful product boundaries, not a guarantee that a particular employer’s configuration will be proportionate or transparent. A small team can test a limited Free plan; organizations needing longer history, reporting, user controls or detailed screen features should model the annual license commitment and add-ons. Before deployment, document exactly what is collected, who can see it, when collection runs, how long each data type remains, and how workers can view or challenge interpretations. The review is based on public vendor material and a Capterra review snapshot; it is not a hands-on agent test, privacy audit, employment-law opinion or endorsement.

## ActivTrak review: workforce analytics with an employee-privacy decision attached

ActivTrak sits in a sensitive category. It is sold to organizations that want to understand how people use work devices, but its reports are built from activity data connected to employees. The vendor increasingly calls the product workforce intelligence and analytics; buyers and workers may still experience it as employee monitoring. Both descriptions can be true. The important question is how an organization configures the platform, explains it and uses its reports—not only whether a dashboard looks polished.

The public record describes a product with configurable privacy defaults, optional screen-level detail, several plan tiers, a free offer and data-retention limits. It also provides help-center explanations, legal agreements and user feedback on Capterra. None of those public documents proves that an individual deployment is transparent, proportionate or well interpreted. ## What ActivTrak does for managers and operations teams

The product collects activity from work devices and organizes it into reports about applications, websites, time patterns, schedules and team activity. Its commercial packages now span work-activity tracking, workforce management and productivity optimization. The pricing page’s comparison matrix shows features such as automatic time tracking, activity summaries, trends, schedule adherence, real-time visibility and role-based access. ActivTrak also presents newer workflow and AI-related analysis within the broader platform.

This can help an organization locate workload imbalances, understand which tools teams rely on or investigate a device-use question. It can also turn a rough proxy—time in an app, an active/idle label or a categorized website—into a judgment about performance. That is a management choice, not an objective conclusion produced by the software. Reading the product as an analytics aid rather than an automatic employee scorecard is an important distinction for implementation.


## Default privacy settings and what the vendor says it does not collect

ActivTrak’s help center says privacy protections are enabled by default and says the standard package does not include screenshots, screen views or certain alarm actions. The company also says its platform does not support keystroke logging, camera access, video recording, email reading or email counting. It describes privacy controls that can hide detailed title bars, URLs and screen images from reporting. Those boundaries distinguish it from products built around continuous video or keylogging, but they remain statements from the vendor rather than measurements made in this review.

A buyer should ask which endpoints, operating systems and user sessions are covered, what counts as an activity event, whether personal accounts or non-business browsing are excluded, and whether all reports inherit the same privacy settings. A worker should be able to learn what the agent is installed on, what information reaches managers, when tracking runs and how a personal-use exception is handled. ‘Privacy-first’ is a product posture; the configured data path and written workplace policy determine what it means in practice.


## Screen Details: optional screenshots, screen views and detailed URLs

Screen Details is a paid add-on for more granular activity. ActivTrak’s help page lists screenshots, real-time screen views, pop-up alarms, application termination, full URLs and title bars. The page says screen views are disabled by default and that screenshots are created through alarms rather than captured as an always-on video stream. For accounts created after January 24, 2023, the support page listed Screen Details at $2 per user/month and says the add-on license count must match the account’s ActivTrak licenses.

The add-on changes the privacy profile materially. A screenshot can contain customer records, private messages, credentials or personal material even when the tool does not collect keystrokes or video. Before purchasing it, define a narrow purpose, configure exclusions and triggers, restrict who can inspect images, document retention, and decide how staff can ask for a review. The vendor says its standard privacy controls and Screen Details are mutually exclusive for newer accounts, so administrators should understand the precise mode before enabling the feature.


## How to interpret activity, idle time and productivity labels

Activity analytics need context. A person may spend time reading, sketching, speaking with a customer, working in a physical environment or thinking through a difficult problem without producing a long sequence of mouse and keyboard events. A website can be useful for one job and irrelevant for another. Even ActivTrak’s activity-classification help material recognizes that the same application or site may be productive for one role and not another, which is why customer-specific rules and categories matter.

Use the reports to ask better questions, not to infer intent from a color-coded chart. Compare related work outputs, role expectations, schedules and employee explanations. Avoid ranking people by a single activity metric or treating low device activity as proof that no work occurred. A pilot should include time for employees and managers to inspect classifications and correct errors. That process is not a workaround for weak data; it is necessary because digital traces only represent part of work.


## ActivTrak pricing: free limit, paid tiers and annual commitment

The pricing page checked November 18, 2026 showed a Free plan for up to three workers with 30 days of activity history. Work Activity Tracking was listed at $10 per worker/month with six months of history; Workforce Management at $15 with six months; Productivity Optimization at $17 with one year. These are the page’s displayed monthly equivalents. The same page explicitly says the vendor currently offers annual plans only, so they should not be read as month-to-month subscriptions. A buyer should calculate the annual worker count, add-ons, support and applicable taxes before comparing budgets.

The optional Screen Details add-on was listed at $2 per user/month. The Data History add-on was listed at $1 per worker/month and can extend history in yearly increments up to three years. ActivConnect API access and several integrations are also paid add-ons. A 14-day trial includes a Workforce Management and Productivity Optimization bundle, with Screen Details available for selected use cases, then downgrades automatically to Free. Confirm whether the trial automatically starts at signup, what details are required and which data remains available after downgrade.


## Data history and account-data retention

ActivTrak’s data-history help article says that, from May 1, 2026, the retention policy applies to free and paid accounts regardless of creation date. It gives rolling windows of 30 days for Free, six months for Work Activity Tracking and Workforce Management, and one year for Productivity Optimization and its bundle. Paid customers can purchase additional history in one-year increments to a maximum of three years. Older activity data becomes unavailable as the rolling period advances, so teams needing long-term trends should plan exports and retention before the oldest records expire.

The same article distinguishes activity data from configuration information. It describes activity as agent-collected items such as websites, applications, durations, screenshots or alarms, and says account configuration data—settings, classifications, user/group permissions and audit logs—is retained indefinitely. That distinction is important for a deletion request: a finite activity window does not mean every account record disappears on the same schedule. Ask the company how account closure, backups, subprocessor deletion and any legal retention requirements fit the published policy.


## Security documentation and what a SOC 2 claim can tell you

ActivTrak’s security materials say customer data is hosted on Google Cloud Platform and describe encryption, role controls, audit logs and cryptographic integrity checks. The company’s help center states that it maintains SOC 2 Type 1 and Type 2 reports and says customers can contact the vendor to review the report. The pricing page also lists SOC 2 Type 2 among its plan features. These are useful signals for procurement because they point to a formal control framework and a process for requesting evidence.

A SOC 2 report is not a guarantee that software cannot be breached, that every add-on has the same controls, or that a customer’s configuration is safe. Request the current report and scope, confirm the covered service and audit period, and review the DPA and subprocessor list available on the legal page. For a risk assessment, include the installed agent, admin access, screenshots if enabled, identity integrations, export paths and account deletion. Roseram has not independently validated the vendor’s controls or certifications.

## A practical comparison with alternatives

For basic team time reporting, a lighter time tracker may cost less and collect less device detail. For workforce capacity and schedule planning, a broader workforce-management suite may be a closer comparison. For incident investigations and deep endpoint control, specialized security products may offer a different feature set and a different privacy burden. ActivTrak’s own package ladder ranges from capped activity visibility to workforce-management and productivity-optimization capabilities, with screen detail and longer history sold separately.

Before evaluating a competitor, write down the narrow job: scheduling, workload analysis, software adoption, time capture or security investigation. Compare user count, retention, role permissions, on-device data, screenshots, alerts, add-ons, integration fees, support and the annual minimum. Capterra’s comparison results include alternatives such as DeskTime, Monitask, Connecteam, Hubstaff and Insightful; the right shortlist depends on whether the organization needs employee monitoring, timekeeping or workforce planning.


## Privacy and employee trust are implementation requirements

Installing an activity agent without a clear explanation can damage trust even if the default settings hide detailed data. Workers should know which devices are covered, which activities are recorded, what the organization wants to learn, who can access individual-level data, whether managers see reports, how long information remains and how to raise a concern. Employees should also be able to distinguish the product’s private self-insight features from employer-facing reports. A monitoring notice should describe the actual configuration rather than repeat general marketing language.

Governance should be set before collection begins. Select a limited purpose, identify the least granular configuration that can answer it, restrict admin access, decide how exceptions and personal use are handled, and set a review date for the data. If screenshots or full URLs are enabled, perform a more specific assessment of what can appear in those records. Seek jurisdiction-appropriate professional advice where required; this article does not determine what an employer is legally allowed to collect.


## Potential fit for small teams and larger employers

A small organization can use the capped Free plan to understand basic activity reporting for up to three workers. That may be enough to evaluate the interface and decide whether a paid use case exists. A larger organization may value the six- or twelve-month history, schedule tools, group management, single sign-on, reporting and API options. The product’s breadth makes it more appealing when a team can name several recurring questions that the dashboards will help answer.

A company that needs only a simple timer, uses little computer-based work, or cannot explain why individual activity records are needed may be better served by a lower-detail solution. The software is also a poor choice for a workplace that plans to interpret activity as a standalone measure of output. People with roles centered on meetings, customer visits, hands-on tasks or deep reading can be misrepresented by device-centered measures unless managers build context into the process.


## What ActivTrak does not prove about productivity

An activity report can show that a device interacted with an application or site. It cannot by itself tell whether a customer was helped, a design improved, a case resolved, a safety procedure followed or a decision made well. Product pages and customer stories may describe better efficiency or return on investment, but those outcomes depend on the organization, baseline, measurement method and management response. The presence of an AI insight feature does not turn a proxy into ground truth.

A fair pilot starts with a question and an outcome measure independent of the dashboard. For example, compare whether a process bottleneck was reduced while also gathering worker feedback and checking quality. Share findings with the people whose data is included, and retire measures that do not improve a decision. Do not use a single activity score as automatic evidence of misconduct or poor performance.


## Plan and add-on questions to resolve before purchase

Ask sales to confirm the annual price in writing, the minimum worker count, how part-time or inactive accounts are billed, and what happens when a team grows past its license allowance. Confirm whether support packages are required for your seat count—the pricing page says paid support is required for purchases involving more than 50 licenses. Verify which reports are included in each tier, which items require ActivConnect or HRIS add-ons, and whether the displayed rate includes taxes.

For data governance, request the current DPA and subprocessor list, report scope, retention behavior after downgrade, deletion process and screenshot configuration. Confirm whether an inactive Free account can be deleted after the inactivity notice—the pricing page says no login for 30 days triggers notice and a further 30 days to upgrade before deletion. Keep the quote, trial settings, administrator list and cancellation steps with the organization’s procurement records.

## Final verdict: capable analytics, conditional fit

ActivTrak offers a broad set of workforce-activity and analytics tools, including default privacy controls and optional screen-level features. Its free plan gives a small team a low-cost way to inspect basic reporting, while paid plans add retention, management, planning and integration capabilities at annual-contract prices. Independent Capterra feedback is generally positive on usability and visibility, but highlights price and package complexity. These indicators make the product worth comparing when an organization has a defined, transparent use case.

The risk is less a hidden scam than a bad deployment: collecting more detail than the question requires, explaining the tool poorly, misreading activity as output, or selecting a plan whose annual commitment and add-ons are a poor fit. Set policy and access controls before installing, keep employee-level conclusions in context, and test whether the data improves a real decision.
