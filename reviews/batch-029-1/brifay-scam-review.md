# Brifay review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-10-27  
**Category:** Computerized Maintenance Management System (CMMS)  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Brifay is a cloud-based maintenance and facility management platform designed to streamline repair requests and asset tracking. Developed for mobile field teams, it integrates voice-to-text input, automatic triage, and asset recognition powered by European AI models. The software organizes work orders across list, Kanban, and calendar formats while managing scheduled preventive tasks. With native applications for mobile operating systems, an entry-level free tier, and per-seat subscription plans, Brifay aims to replace manual notes and spreadsheets with digital records, automated equipment metrics, and inventory oversight.

## Target Audience and Core Computerized Maintenance Scope

Brifay positions itself as a full AI-native Computerized Maintenance Management System and facility management tool tailored for small-to-medium enterprises, mid-market organizations, and local public entities. The platform aims to eliminate the chaotic fragmentation of maintenance requests scattered across mobile messaging apps, emails, and physical sticky notes. By centralizing ticket intake into a single digital ledger, the software attempts to provide operational transparency to maintenance managers who must track asset health and defend maintenance budgets before executive committees.

The software serves distinct user personas within an industrial or commercial setting. Field technicians represent the primary mobile users, tasked with executing daily repairs, conducting equipment inspections, and updating ticket statuses from their phones. Facility managers and maintenance leads operate as administrative controllers who assign incoming jobs, monitor labor hours, set preventive intervals, and review performance indicators. Meanwhile, general staff members act as requesters who merely report broken equipment or environmental issues without needing administrative oversight.

Prospective buyers must assess whether their internal organizational scale aligns with this intended operational scope. Operations managing hundreds of distributed machines across regional warehouses have vastly different data collection demands than a single small workshop. Brifay addresses multi-site setups by incorporating localized hierarchies and location trees, but organizations must examine whether their specific technicians will embrace mobile data logging or whether legacy physical procedures remain too deeply entrenched to replace without extensive change management.


## Voice-to-Text Ticketing and Field Data Capture Workflows

The core technical differentiator highlighted in Brifay documentation is its voice-driven work order intake. Rather than forcing technicians to type extensive diagnostic summaries on cramped mobile keyboards while wearing work gloves, the application captures spoken descriptions. The embedded AI assistant, named Brio, transcribes the voice audio into written text, extracts key descriptive attributes, estimates the relative urgency of the problem, and links the ticket to the specific asset involved.

In a documented field workflow, a technician encounters an overheating pump, opens the mobile app, and dictates a quick verbal account of the unusual noise, fluid leak, and observed temperature. The system processes the audio through European speech-to-text models powered by Mistral AI, formatting the spoken words into a coherent ticket record. Supplementary metadata can be appended immediately through the device's hardware, including camera photographs of the defect and integrated location coordinates to confirm physical presence on site.

While hands-free voice logging sounds promising in promotional videos, buyers must rigorously verify its real-world performance under plant conditions. Acoustic interference from heavy machinery, high ambient decibel levels, variable regional accents, and specialized technical terminology can severely impair automated transcription accuracy. A buyer evaluation should specifically test whether Brio correctly transcribes obscure part serial numbers and hydraulic terms in noisy operating bays before relying on voice dictation for critical safety interventions.


## Asset Hierarchy, QR Code Tagging, and Inventory Controls

Asset traceability in Brifay relies on structured parent-child equipment relationships and digital labeling. The software organizes capital assets using hierarchical location trees, accommodating physical sites, specific buildings, individual rooms, parent machinery, and modular sub-equipment. Technicians can scan attached QR codes using native iOS or Android cameras, instantly calling up the complete operational history, safety guidelines, and active maintenance logs tied to that specific mechanical asset.

Beyond individual machine records, higher subscription tiers introduce structured inventory controls to prevent maintenance delays caused by missing components. Documented stock modules track spare parts, component categories, supplier directories, and minimum stock alert thresholds. The system logs stock movements, supports bill of materials listings, and enables First-Expired, First-Out lot management for consumable maintenance goods across multi-depot storage facilities, connecting part usage directly to work order expenses.

Teams should evaluate the practical labor required to construct and maintain this digital catalog. Populating thousands of legacy assets, uploading supplier references, and printing physical QR tags represents a substantial initial investment of time. A buyer must verify how smoothly equipment datasets move into the platform via provided spreadsheet import templates, and whether field technicians consistently scan asset codes during hectic emergency repair runs.


## Preventive Maintenance Scheduling and Audit Readiness

Reactive firefighting often dominates under-resourced maintenance departments, leading to premature equipment failure. Brifay provides a preventive maintenance module designed to transition teams into scheduled upkeep. Administrators can define customized inspection frequencies across daily, weekly, monthly, or annual intervals. Once programmed, the system automatically generates work orders at the designated calendar dates, assign tasks to qualified technicians, and tracks completion against mandatory checklists.

Audit compliance forms a major operational justification for this structured scheduling. When external regulators or insurance adjusters conduct inspections under frameworks like ISO or GMP guidelines, maintenance teams must demonstrate verifiable records of routine servicing. Brifay logs timestamped records of interventions, technician notes, and safety compliance sign-offs, producing exportable reports intended to replace the frantic scramble of reconstructing maintenance histories from disparate spreadsheets.

However, automated scheduling is only as dependable as the human accountability enforcing it. Buyers must evaluate how the software handles overdue preventative tasks and whether notification rules effectively escalate neglected safety checks to senior managers. A practical evaluation test should involve scheduling recurring multi-point lubrication and electrical checks across several test machines to observe how the platform handles missed deadlines and technician workload balancing.


## Subscription Tiers, Hidden Minimums, and Operational Quotas

Brifay markets four distinct pricing tiers, starting with a permanent Free plan that includes up to three active users, five requesters, ten pieces of equipment, and twenty-five monthly work orders. The paid ladder begins with the Starter tier at 9 euros per active user per month when billed monthly, or 7.50 euros on an annual term. However, the Starter tier omits the Brio AI voice assistant, Kanban boards, and preventative maintenance, restricting small teams to basic ticketing and three dashboard metrics.

Unlocking the platform's signature features requires moving to the Pro plan at 29 euros per user monthly, or the Business plan at 59 euros per user monthly, both of which enforce a mandatory minimum of three active users. This minimum sets the effective monthly baseline at 87 euros for Pro and 177 euros for Business prior to annual discounts. Notably, paid tiers do not charge for requesters—personnel who merely submit alerts—allowing organizations to distribute issue-reporting capabilities widely without paying per-seat licensing penalties for casual staff.

Buyers must also monitor operational resource quotas embedded within the AI capabilities. Higher tiers allocate shared pools of AI credits—ranging from 500,000 to 20 million Brio units—and cap voice-to-text processing between 300 and 2,000 monthly minutes per organization. Teams with intensive daily dictation or continuous photo analysis could potentially exhaust these allowances, making it essential to audit monthly technician usage rates against published package ceilings.


## Data Sovereignty, Privacy Boundaries, and System Limits

In an era of stringent industrial data regulations, the technical infrastructure hosting operational records is critical. Brifay states that its data architecture is hosted within France and the European Union, operating under GDPR compliance standards by design. User data remains proprietary to the subscribing enterprise, with actions timestamped for audit tracking. Furthermore, the embedded Brio assistant relies on models provided by Mistral AI, emphasizing European hosting over cross-border data transfers to foreign hyperscalers.

Despite these published protections, buyers must understand the architectural boundaries of the software. Brifay is delivered as a software-as-a-service application relying on native iOS and Android clients alongside web browsers. A crucial area for technical validation is the system's offline resiliency. Field technicians frequently operate in reinforced concrete basements, remote pumping stations, or shielded industrial plants where cellular coverage drops. Buyers must verify whether the mobile app caches work orders and voice notes locally when disconnected.

Another operational boundary involves data retention and migration policies. While publisher documentation affirms that customer data remains exportable to spreadsheet formats at any time, organizations must confirm the depth of these exports. An enterprise needs to verify whether raw historical voice recordings, attached high-resolution defect photographs, and complete audit trail logs can be extracted in bulk if the company decides to migrate to an alternate system in the future.

This controlled scenario allows the company to measure practical return on investment before executing a widespread rollout. Simultaneously, the test highlights practical roadblocks, such as whether technicians remember to log their actual repair minutes and whether the automated mean-time-between-failures calculations reflect operational reality.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between active users and requesters in Brifay?

Brifay bills organizations exclusively for active users, such as administrators, maintenance managers, and field technicians handling tasks. Requesters—personnel who solely submit maintenance tickets or issue alerts—are free and unrestricted on paid subscription tiers, though capped at five on the free tier.

### How does the AI assistant function during ticket creation?

Brifay deploys an assistant called Brio, hosted on European infrastructure and utilizing Mistral models. In documented workflows, it converts audio notes into text, classifies the operational urgency, detects duplicate issues, and pairs the logged ticket with identified machinery or facilities.

### Are there minimum seat requirements on Brifay paid plans?

Yes. While the entry-level Starter plan allows single-seat subscriptions starting at 9 euros per month, the Pro and Business tiers enforce a mandatory minimum of three active user seats, establishing starting entry costs of 87 euros and 177 euros monthly before annual discounts.

### What happens if a team exceeds the limits of the Free plan?

Publisher documentation indicates that exceeding Free plan allocations does not trigger an abrupt operational cutoff. Organizations retain stored data while administrators are prompted to transition into a paid tier accommodating their volume of users, equipment records, and monthly requests.
