# Brevo review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-18  
**Category:** Email, SMS, messaging automation and customer relationship platform  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Brevo is a customer engagement platform offering email, SMS, WhatsApp, chat, sales tools and marketing automation. Its help center currently lists a Free plan at US$0 with 300 email sends per day and storage for up to 100,000 contacts; Free and Starter automation is limited to 2,000 unique contacts. Paid tiers are priced by sending volume and contact tier, with the official guide listing Starter from US$9 per month, Standard from US$18, Professional from US$499 and Enterprise at a custom price. Monthly-plan email allowances expire at the end of the billing period, while separate prepaid email credits are described as not expiring. Brevo says unused Free daily sends do not roll over. Free accounts without activity for four months can be deleted with reminders, unless they have automatic activity such as an automation or transactional email. Account cancellation and data removal are separate decisions, and the operator’s DPA and current privacy materials should be reviewed before importing contacts. The evidence reviewed supports a real communications platform and does not substantiate a scam allegation.

## Editorial verdict

Brevo documents its plans, daily and monthly sending limits, contact-storage tiers, add-ons and inactivity policy. The free plan is useful for low-volume evaluation, but 300 daily sends and 2,000 automation contacts are operational caps; paid tiers and add-ons change the total. Buyers should distinguish expiring monthly plan sends from prepaid credits that do not expire, monitor account inactivity, test deliverability and preserve consent and suppression data during a migration. Nothing reviewed substantiates calling Brevo a scam. This is a public-source desk review, not an account trial or deliverability test.

## Brevo SCAM review: multichannel marketing with clearly tiered quotas

Brevo is a hosted customer-engagement platform with email marketing, transactional messages, SMS, WhatsApp, chat and sales features. Its help center describes a set of plans from a permanent Free tier through Starter, Standard, Professional and Enterprise. Pricing depends on send volume and contact storage, while other features such as seats, automation and channel add-ons can change the total.

This article uses “SCAM Review” as the review-page label; it is not a finding that Brevo has committed fraud. The company publishes current plan documentation, quotas, billing guidance and data-processing materials. Those sources support identifying an operating product. They do not establish inbox placement for a specific sender or prove that every customer’s contact collection complies with local rules. The practical evaluation is about limits, billing behavior, account activity and list governance.


## Free plan: contact storage does not mean unlimited sending

Brevo’s official pricing guide says Free costs US$0 per month, supports up to 100,000 stored contacts and allows 300 email sends per day. Unused daily sends do not carry forward. The plan also has a one-seat marketing allowance and free features that can support a small pilot. A large stored-contact ceiling can look generous, but it is not the same as permission to send an email to every contact or a guarantee that a campaign will be delivered.

Plan campaigns around the daily cap. If an audience is larger than the allowance, splitting sends across days can change timing and message context; upgrading or buying credits may be more appropriate. Confirm whether a transactional message is subject to the same account quota and what retry behavior applies. The official help center documents a retry queue for transactional emails, which should not be confused with campaign sending. Keep a local send plan so operational limits do not surprise the team on launch day.


## Paid plans combine send volume and contact tiers

Brevo’s pricing help currently lists Starter from US$9 per month, Standard from US$18, Professional from US$499 and Enterprise at a custom price. For Starter and Standard, the tier sets monthly email volume and the number of contacts that can be stored. Professional supports larger volumes and up to two million contacts; Enterprise is customized. These amounts are vendor-published starting points and can change with the selected tier, add-ons, billing term or location.

Before migrating a list, match the existing contact count and the expected monthly send volume to a tier. The send volume should include transactional notifications and recurring automation, not only newsletters. If a business has many stored but infrequently contacted records, ask whether the tier charges by sends or contacts and whether deleted contacts free capacity immediately. Save the live checkout estimate and review it again when adding SMS, WhatsApp, seats or sales tools.


## Automation has its own active-contact quotas

The official quota article says Free and Starter automation supports up to 2,000 unique contacts in active automations, while Standard and higher tiers allow unlimited contacts. Other automation limits include processing rates and stored workflow counts. That means a user can have many contacts in the account but still face a smaller automation limit depending on plan.

Inventory which contacts enter each active workflow and how long they remain enrolled. A person who never exits a long nurture sequence can occupy a limit even if the journey is dormant. Review re-entry rules, stop conditions and unresponsive contacts. Test automations with a small internal segment, then inspect the live contact count before importing a full database. Do not assume that contact storage capacity equals automation capacity.


## Monthly sends expire; prepaid credits follow different rules

The Brevo help center says monthly email allowances must be used within the billing period and do not roll over. It separately describes prepaid email credits for occasional use as never expiring, with the number of credits and price depending on the pack. These are different products with different budgeting behavior. SMS and WhatsApp credits have their own pricing based on recipient country and message category.

Choose the purchase type that matches actual demand. A monthly plan may be economical for a recurring program but leave unused volume at month end; prepaid credits may be better for sporadic sends, though they still require careful recipient and sender management. Track whether an add-on renews or is a one-time pack, whether it applies to marketing and transactional messages, and which channels it covers. The official guide says monthly email credits expire; do not carry that assumption over to prepaid credits or vice versa.


## Billing changes can affect contact access and invoice timing

Brevo documents that adding emails to a plan can trigger a prorated charge from the purchase date through the next billing date, then the usual recurring amount. Removing email volume takes effect on the next billing date, with current credits available until then. If the organization has more contacts than a lower tier allows, it may need to delete contacts before selecting the smaller plan.

Review the billing summary before increasing volume, and set a reminder for recurring changes that are no longer needed. A plan downgrade can require cleanup of the contact database, so export a verified backup and confirm which records are safe to delete. Keep opt-outs and suppression lists in a separate, protected state so capacity cleanup does not erase compliance evidence or permit a later re-import.


## Inactive Free accounts can be deleted after four months

Brevo’s help article says Free-plan accounts unused for four months can be automatically deleted and all data permanently lost. It describes four reminder emails before deletion, with notices at 30, 15, 7 and 1 day. A Free account with automatic activity such as an automation or transactional email is considered active under the article; paid accounts are not deleted for inactivity in the same way.

Treat Free as a real hosted account with a lifecycle, not as a guaranteed archive. Sign in periodically if it is only being used to retain contacts, or maintain an approved export and backup. Make sure the account owner’s email can receive the deletion notices and is still monitored. If the organization has stopped using the platform, export data and request deletion rather than keeping a dormant account indefinitely.


## Cancellation, retention and deletion are related but separate

Brevo’s marketing page says a customer can cancel through the account and that data may be stored for later use or deleted upon request. The account plan, data-processing agreement and privacy materials determine the exact handling. Canceling a paid plan should not be treated as equivalent to deleting every contact record, event, message or backup.

Before ending a subscription, export campaigns, templates, contact fields, consent history, suppression states and transactional logs that the business is entitled to retain. Then identify a deletion owner, confirm what is removed and what is retained for legal obligations, and keep the written request or confirmation. For a migration, stage the destination before closing the source account and verify the count of unsubscribed and bounced recipients.


## Account administration and campaign ownership

The plan guide lists seat limits and paid add-ons, which makes account ownership important for teams. Use individual access for the people who build or approve campaigns rather than sharing one administrator password. Decide who can change billing, import contacts, edit templates and publish sends. Keep a second administrator who can access the account if the original owner leaves.

Before migration, clarify who owns the sending domain, templates, transactional integrations and historical contact records. If an agency manages the account, ensure the business has its own administrator and can revoke vendor access without interrupting transactional messages. Review which roles can export data or start a campaign. A tool can provide seats and permissions, but the organization still needs a clear approval process for high-impact messages.


## Privacy, consent and contact-source responsibilities

Brevo offers forms and consent-recording features and publishes GDPR information and a DPA. The platform can help document a subscription form, but the business still has to collect contacts lawfully, explain the intended messages and honor unsubscribe or access requests. A contact imported from another CRM does not automatically become an opted-in subscriber to every Brevo channel.

Keep evidence of where each address came from, what terms were shown, when consent occurred and which channels were selected. Separate transactional notices from marketing campaigns. Honor a suppression across lists and automations, and avoid uploading bought or scraped data. Review the DPA and applicable privacy notice for the organization’s region and integrations before sending personal or sensitive information.


## Sender reputation and deliverability still need a managed rollout

The plan documents describe message volume and functionality; they do not promise that every campaign will be accepted by receiving providers. Inbox placement depends on authentication, list quality, content, recipient engagement, sending history and complaint rates. Moving a domain or a large database into a new platform can change sending patterns and should be planned carefully.

Authenticate the sending domain, confirm the from-address, check the unsubscribe experience and start with recently engaged contacts. Monitor hard bounces, complaints, unsubscribes and replies after each volume step. Do not send the entire list on day one solely because the account can technically store it. If deliverability matters to a business-critical program, test a small segment and define a rollback or pause procedure.


## Keep transactional and promotional messages operationally distinct

Brevo describes both marketing campaigns and transactional email. They serve different purposes: a receipt or password reset is expected as part of a customer action, while a promotion invites a commercial response. Do not use the transactional label as a way to bypass marketing consent or unsubscribe requirements. Keep templates, sender identities and internal approval rules clear enough that staff can tell which category a message belongs to.

Test delivery and failure handling for each type separately. A marketing campaign should honor suppression and preference settings; a transactional message should contain only the information needed to complete the request. Verify which account limits apply, how retries work and who can change the sending configuration. These distinctions help a business maintain trust and troubleshoot a problem without accidentally resending a promotional blast to people who opted out.


## Where Brevo may fit and where its limits may be binding

Brevo may suit a small business that needs email and transactional messaging, wants a simple path into SMS or WhatsApp, and can operate within a daily Free cap or a predictable monthly tier. The free plan’s contact storage can be generous for evaluation. A larger marketing team may value the broader automation, segmentation and support options in paid tiers.

It may be a poor fit for a high-volume sender that needs custom governance but cannot justify the Professional starting tier, or for a company that needs deep analytics, advanced permissions or a long-term data archive at a lower tier. A team that only sends sporadic campaigns should compare prepaid credits with a recurring plan. Choose based on the number of messages actually sent, not the number of channels shown in the menu.


## Alternatives and comparison checklist

Alternatives include newsletter services, dedicated transactional email providers, CRM marketing suites and customer-data platforms. They differ in contact-based versus send-based pricing, automation flexibility, SMTP/API behavior, consent features and data export. A simple newsletter service may be easier for a weekly update; a transactional provider may be more appropriate for receipts and password resets; a larger marketing platform may be needed for complex journeys.

Compare equivalent contact and send volumes, automation contacts, overages, non-expiring prepaid credits, seats, deliverability support, account deletion policy and the time required to migrate. Test a campaign and a transactional message separately. Evaluate unsubscribe, bounce handling and deletion behavior with a test contact before importing a production list.


## Brevo SCAM review verdict: broad platform, mind the quotas and account lifecycle

Brevo’s official materials describe an email and multichannel platform with Free, Starter, Standard, Professional and Enterprise plans. They disclose the Free plan’s 300 daily email sends, 100,000 stored contacts and limited automation audience; paid tiers vary by send volume and contact tier. The documentation also explains expiring monthly sends, non-expiring prepaid credits, and a four-month inactivity deletion policy for unused Free accounts.

The evidence reviewed does not substantiate calling Brevo a scam. Buyers should calculate email and contact tiers, plan automation entry volume, distinguish monthly sends from prepaid credits, maintain consent and suppression data, and export records before cancellation or inactivity deletion.
