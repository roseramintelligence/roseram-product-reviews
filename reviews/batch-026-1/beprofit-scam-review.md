# BeProfit review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-24  
**Category:** Ecommerce profit and financial analytics  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

BeProfit is an ecommerce analytics app listed in the Shopify App Store. Its listing describes contribution profit by order and product, expense tracking, ROAS and POAS comparisons, shipping-cost analysis, reports, AI query features and integrations. The listing shows a free plan and paid tiers at $29, $99 and $149 per month, with Enterprise from $249 per month; order limits, extra-order charges, history and features differ by tier.

## Editorial verdict

BeProfit may help a Shopify merchant reconcile advertising, product costs, refunds, shipping and transaction fees into a more useful profit view than revenue-only dashboards. Its usefulness depends on accurate cost inputs and connected data sources. Before relying on its margin or campaign reports, reconcile a sample against accounting and ad-platform records, check how refunds and multi-store overhead are allocated, and verify the app permissions requested for customer, order and payment data.

## Look beyond revenue to the costs behind each order

BeProfit’s Shopify App Store listing describes analytics intended to show contribution profit after costs such as product costs, advertising, shipping, transaction fees and refunds. It also highlights profit on ad spend alongside return on ad spend. That distinction can help a store avoid scaling a campaign that generates sales but loses money after fulfillment and fees. The result is only as good as the cost data and attribution that feed it.

Before connecting an account, list where each cost comes from: supplier or product costs, carrier charges, advertising platforms, payment processing, discounts, refunds, taxes and shared operating expenses. Ask how each one is imported, whether it is allocated per order or at a summary level, and how often it updates. Do not assume a profit dashboard replaces bookkeeping or tax reporting.


## Compare plans against your order volume and history needs

The Shopify App Store listing showed a free tier for one shop and up to 150 orders per month, Basic at $29 for up to 450 orders, Pro at $99 for up to 900 orders and Scale at $149 for up to 1,700 orders. It also displayed a $0.30 charge per extra order, capped at $500 per month, on paid tiers, plus Enterprise from $249. Features, AI credits and data history differ by plan; recheck the listing before committing.

Estimate order volume for a seasonal peak, not just an average month. Confirm how the app counts canceled orders, refunds, test transactions, subscriptions and multiple stores. The listing states recurring and usage charges are billed every 30 days. Compare monthly and annual options where offered and include the cost of any additional store, integration or required plan upgrade.


## Reconcile analytics before making budget decisions

Install only in a store where you have permission to connect the required data, then compare a small sample with your accounting records and ad platforms. Pick orders with different shipping methods, discounts, refunds and product costs. Check the raw source values, report currency, time zone, attribution window and whether a refunded order reverses the original marketing expense. Follow through on any discrepancies before treating the dashboard as a source of truth.

Use a month-end comparison to identify missing fees and allocation assumptions. Multi-store merchants should test whether shared costs can be assigned consistently rather than counted twice or left out. A campaign report can help prioritize investigation, but it should not be treated as causal proof that an advertisement produced profit without checking attribution rules and actual cash settlement.


## Review permissions and data handling

The Shopify App Store permission section shows that BeProfit requests access to customer, product and order information and Shopify Payments data, along with store-owner details. Those permissions support the app’s financial calculations but are sensitive. Review the exact authorization screen before installation and confirm which team members can view reports, download exports or connect advertising accounts.

BeProfit’s privacy policy explains how the company describes its data handling roles and practices. Read the current policy, data-retention terms, subprocessors and deletion process, especially if customer information or payment reporting is involved. Limit access to staff who need it, use individual accounts where available and revoke the Shopify app connection if the business stops using the service.


## Who should evaluate BeProfit?

Shopify merchants with multiple cost sources, paid-ad spend or enough order volume to make manual profit reporting burdensome may find BeProfit worth testing. A very small store with simple costs may be served by spreadsheets or its accounting tools. The decision should be based on whether the reports reconcile, whether the plan fits seasonal order volume and whether the app’s permissions are proportionate to the value it provides.
