# Streamlabs review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-24  
**Category:** Live streaming software and creator tools  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Streamlabs provides free creator tools for desktop, mobile and browser workflows, with Streamlabs Ultra and Ultra Plus bundles adding multistreaming, overlays, automation limits, storage and access to companion products. Its current public pricing page shows Ultra starting at US$15.75 per month, while plan selectors also expose monthly and annual billing.

## Editorial verdict

Streamlabs is a practical option for a creator who wants to start broadcasting with a free toolset and add production features as the workflow grows. Free limits include restricted themes, storage and some cross-product capabilities; Ultra bundles more tools, but the monthly cost varies by offer and billing term. Compare a single Streamlabs product against the bundle, and confirm which free features continue if you cancel Ultra.

## Start with the free workflow

Streamlabs says creators can use free versions of several products, including Desktop, Mobile, Web Suite, Video Editor, Cross Clip and Podcast Editor. That lets a new streamer test a basic scene, alert and recording flow without immediately buying the suite. Identify the one feature that blocks your next step before considering an upgrade.

Start with an unlisted test broadcast. Check audio levels, screen capture, alerts, scene changes and the appearance on the destination platform. Then review what the free tier limits, including themes, storage, guest capacity or the number of active automations. The vendor’s feature table is the source for these limits; this article does not claim to have streamed through the service.


## Decide whether the suite replaces separate tools

Ultra bundles premium access across multiple Streamlabs tools, including desktop, mobile, console, browser studio, clipping and editing products. That can simplify billing if you actively use several of them. If you only need a single editor or multistream capability, compare that standalone service with the bundle rather than assuming the package is automatically cheaper.

Streamlabs currently describes Ultra as starting at US$15.75 per month; its plan selector also displays US$27 monthly and US$179 annually. These figures may reflect a discount or billing view. Save the exact checkout offer, note its renewal amount and check if the annual option is charged up front.


## Understand production limits and data storage

The plan comparison lists 10GB of cloud storage and 10 active automations for Ultra, with higher limits on Ultra Plus. Free Starter includes a smaller storage allocation and fewer active automation slots. If you keep recordings or assets in the service, work out how quickly the quota fills and where you will keep an independent copy.

A creator who uses scene automations should test what happens when a trigger fails or a game event is misread. Keep a manual way to switch scenes and review automated behavior before broadcasting to a large audience. AI co-host and production features can change, so confirm the feature allowance on the plan page before designing a show around a specific quota.


## Plan what happens if you cancel

Streamlabs says access to free versions of its products continues after an Ultra subscription ends. That is useful, but paid overlay libraries, storage, multistreaming and premium editing features may no longer be available. Before subscribing, identify the assets or workflows that depend on Ultra and make an export plan for files that matter.

Use a test month or the shortest appropriate period to confirm that the premium features save enough effort to justify the fee. Read the cancellation and renewal terms, keep a copy of the receipt and verify access after any plan change. Streamlabs is a toolkit, not a promise of audience growth or income; results still depend on the creator’s content and distribution.
