# Substack review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-18  
**Category:** Newsletter publishing and paid subscription platform  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Substack is a newsletter and publishing platform where writers can publish for free or charge readers for monthly, annual or founding memberships. Free publishing has no platform fee based on subscriber count. Paid subscriptions incur a 10% Substack platform fee plus Stripe processing and recurring-billing charges; the creator sets prices. A custom domain is listed as a one-time $50 fee. Writers should model net revenue, app-store pricing, taxes, refunds and payment fees, and keep an export and audience-continuity plan.

## Editorial verdict

The reviewed official sources do not substantiate a scam allegation. Substack publicly explains its 10% platform fee, Stripe charges, free publishing model, reader pricing and payment controls. The key buyer concern is net revenue: the platform percentage is not the only deduction, and app purchases, taxes, discounts or local methods may affect what the writer receives. Use the Stripe dashboard and Substack earnings breakdown to reconcile real payouts; do not equate gross subscriber prices with creator income.

## Substack SCAM review: distinguish a directory label from a finding

“SCAM Review” is the page label used by this directory; it is not evidence that Substack is fraudulent. This article examines Substack’s published product and fee structure for readers and writers. It does not claim to be a customer testimonial, and no paid publication, payout or support case was tested. The conclusions below are limited to the company’s official help, payment and terms pages.

Substack combines a web publication, email newsletter distribution and an optional paid-membership model. A writer can publish free material, set a price for paid access or use both formats. Readers subscribe to individual writers rather than buying one universal content catalog. That simple entry point has a trade-off: when paid subscriptions are enabled, the writer must account for platform, payment-processing and recurring-billing costs.


## Free publishing and paid newsletter subscriptions

Substack’s fee guide says publishing is free regardless of subscriber count when a publication remains free. If a writer enables paid subscriptions, Substack keeps 10% of the subscription revenue and Stripe charges processing fees. The writer chooses monthly, annual and founding-member prices. This makes it possible to test a free newsletter without a platform subscription charge, but paid revenue introduces several deductions before funds reach the creator.

A publication can choose to offer a mix of free and paid posts. The writer should make the value and cadence of paid access explicit and avoid implying that free followers automatically become paying readers. The audience’s willingness to pay is not guaranteed by using Substack’s checkout, and the platform does not provide a universal earnings promise. Estimate net proceeds using the actual price, payment mix and billing period.


## Substack and Stripe fees: calculate the amount retained

The official fee article lists Substack’s 10% share and gives a US card-processing example of 2.9% plus $0.30 per payment, along with a 0.7% Stripe Billing charge for recurring payments. Local payment methods and Stripe pricing vary by country. The figures are examples from the official article, not a guaranteed net rate for every account. A one-time custom domain is listed at $50.

For an illustrative $10 monthly web subscription paid by a US card, the published percentages and fixed card charge would reduce gross proceeds before taxes, refunds or other adjustments. The exact calculation depends on whether the payment is new or recurring, how the fee base is applied and the creator’s Stripe country. Use the payment breakdown shown for a real transaction rather than estimating every payout from the headline price.


## Reader prices, app billing and taxes

Substack says it does not add a fee to web prices set by creators. It notes that sales tax or VAT may be added by some writers, and subscriptions bought in its iOS app may cost more to cover Apple’s fees. Its guidance says writers can decide whether to adjust app prices or keep them the same as the web price. Payment methods depend on reader location and can include card wallets or local bank methods.

A writer should compare the public web price, any app-store price and the amount ultimately settled to Stripe. A reader should check the displayed renewal price in the channel where the purchase is made. Terms and tax handling can change by location and date; as of the terms page reviewed, it stated that local VAT, GST, sales or use tax may be added to paid-subscription purchases beginning December 1, 2026. Confirm the current checkout disclosure.


## Payouts, Stripe records and revenue reconciliation

Substack’s payout guide says paid subscription payments are handled through the creator’s Stripe account. It directs writers to inspect Transactions and Payouts in Stripe for payment details and says Substack’s earnings view shows net revenue and a breakdown after applicable Substack and Stripe fees. The guide says payments usually arrive in a bank account within 48 hours of each transaction, though timing can depend on payment processing and account status.

Do not reconcile a payout by multiplying subscriber count by the listed monthly price. Discounts, failed payments, refunds, recurring billing, app-store transactions, card fees and tax treatment all affect the net. Check the transaction ledger, payout status and fee breakdown for a sample month. If the Stripe account is used for several businesses, isolate publication transactions before comparing dashboard totals.


## Discounts and subscription pricing need transparent controls

Substack’s payout guide notes that Boost can present discounts to readers, including a 20% discount in examples, which changes the amount paid by a new subscriber. A discount can help a reader convert, but the writer should model what it does to renewal expectations and net proceeds. Any offer should state the renewal price and duration clearly where the checkout flow permits it.

Before opening paid subscriptions, decide on a sustainable monthly and annual price, the benefits offered and how a cancellation or refund request is handled. Avoid assuming every annual reader renews or that a founding-member tier creates recurring support. Keep a record of price changes and notify readers as required by the terms and applicable rules.


## Custom domains, publication identity and control

Substack lists a custom domain as an optional one-time $50 charge. A custom domain can make a publication easier to recognize and share, but the publication still runs within Substack’s hosted service and terms. Consider what content, subscriber data, payment records and branding can be exported and how links behave if the publication later moves elsewhere.

Use a domain the organization controls, keep registrar access secure and document DNS records. Do not treat a branded URL as full ownership of the publishing stack. Maintain copies of the archive and an independent contact system only for subscribers who have consented to those communications. Test any export process before the publication depends on it.


## Audience portability and newsletter continuity

An email publication creates an ongoing relationship, but audience access is subject to the platform’s product, policies and account state. A writer should review the available subscriber export, understand which fields and subscription states are included, and keep consent and suppression information intact. Payment credentials and billing histories are not the same as a list of email addresses and should not be assumed portable.

Set up a continuity plan: retain source manuscripts, images, links, publication settings, mailing preferences and a record of which content is free or paid. If migrating, notify readers in a clear and permission-respecting way. Do not re-add an exported address to an unrelated marketing list or ignore unsubscribes. Portability is a practical safeguard for a creator business, not a claim that Substack will fail.


## Substack alternatives and fee comparison

Compare Substack with hosted newsletter services, membership platforms and a self-hosted publication. Use the same number of free and paid readers, subscription prices, payment countries, publication cadence and feature needs. Include each platform’s revenue share, payment processing, recurring billing, domain cost, tax handling, reader app fees, export capabilities and deliverability controls.

Substack’s no-platform-fee free publishing can be attractive when the creator is building an audience and does not need a complex site. A different platform may be preferable where the business needs custom branding, detailed automation, direct control over audience data or a different fee model. A self-hosted option can reduce a platform revenue share but shifts hosting, updates, payment integrations and security onto the publisher.


## Who Substack may suit, and who should compare other tools

Substack can suit an individual writer who wants to begin publishing without a subscriber-count platform fee and may later charge for access. Its built-in publication and reader experience can reduce setup work, and writers choose their own paid prices. A reader who wants newsletters and posts from several writers may also value a single reading account.

It may not suit a publisher that needs full control over design, deeply segmented lifecycle marketing, custom subscription logic or low per-payment fees at scale. The platform’s 10% share applies to paid subscription revenue, in addition to processor charges. An organization with a large audience should compare the total cost and portability trade-offs at realistic revenue levels, not only the zero-cost entry point.


## Substack buyer checklist before turning on paid subscriptions

Write down the web monthly and annual prices, any founding tier, the promised content cadence and the cancellation terms. Calculate example net receipts after the 10% platform share and the applicable Stripe card, recurring-billing or local-method fee. Check what readers see in the web and app purchase flows and determine whether tax is included or added. Decide how discounts are used and how renewals are communicated.

Connect the correct Stripe account, confirm access and payout details, test a low-value transaction if appropriate, and reconcile it in both Substack and Stripe. Keep an archive and a subscriber export plan. Review the current terms and fee page before launch because processor rates, platform fees, tax treatment and app policies can change.


## Substack SCAM review verdict: straightforward publishing, fees to model

Substack’s official sources describe free publishing, creator-set prices, a 10% fee on paid subscriptions and Stripe charges. They do not substantiate a scam allegation. The practical caution is to separate gross subscriber payments from net creator revenue: card processing, recurring billing, discounts, taxes, regional methods and app purchases can all affect the result. Payout details should be verified in Stripe and the publication’s earnings view.

Substack may be a simple way to begin a newsletter when its reader experience and fee model fit the business. It is not a guarantee of audience growth or recurring income. This dated desk review did not open an account or process a payment; fee examples and terms were checked November 18, 2026. Confirm current charges, payout settings and the latest terms before accepting subscriptions.


## Features and decision criteria

- Free newsletter publishing with optional paid subscriptions
- Writer-defined monthly, annual and founding-member prices
- Email delivery, publication site, subscriber analytics and reader app
- Stripe-powered payments, with platform and processing fees on paid subscriptions

## Pricing considerations

Substack’s official Help Center article, updated October 29, 2026, lists the 10% platform fee and the example Stripe fees for US card payments. Readers pay the price the writer sets; tax, local payment-method fees, discounts and iOS in-app purchase pricing can change the net or displayed amount. Confirm Stripe’s current country-specific rate and the live terms before launching.
