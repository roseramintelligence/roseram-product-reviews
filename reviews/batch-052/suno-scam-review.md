# Suno review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-18  
**Category:** AI music creation and editing platform  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Suno is an AI music platform for generating, sharing, editing and downloading songs. Its current plans distinguish daily free credits from monthly paid credits, song download allowances, model access, stem tools and commercial-use rights. The Free plan is advertised at $0 with daily credits but no monthly song downloads or commercial rights; paid plans add credits, downloads and commercial use under the stated terms. Uploaded audio and voice features require permission for the material supplied. Suno's terms do not promise copyright protection for generated songs, so creators should check source rights, subscription status, download eligibility and local law before using tracks commercially.

## Editorial verdict

This source-based review does not call Suno a scam and does not make a claim about the copyright status or musical quality of any particular output. Suno publishes current plan details, help articles about downloads and commercial rights, and terms covering uploaded submissions and voice models. Those sources support a buyer's review of billing and usage conditions, not independent testing. The most important checks are whether your plan permits the intended use, whether the song was made while the qualifying subscription was active, and whether you have rights to every uploaded recording or voice.

## Suno is a song-generation workflow with editing and publishing decisions

Suno turns a user's prompt and permitted source material into generated music, then offers ways to share, edit, extend or download the result. The current plan comparison includes different models, remix tools, section editing, stem separation, voice features and Suno Studio at higher tiers. That makes it more than a one-click song demo, but it also means the choice is about the whole process: drafting, selecting a version, editing, exporting and deciding where the finished track can be used.

The output that matters is a track you can actually publish for the purpose you have in mind. A catchy preview does not answer whether the chorus is consistent, whether a vocal sounds right in the mix, whether the lyrics fit a client brief or whether you may use the song in a paid campaign. Plan a test around a real but low-risk project. Save the prompt, source material, generation date, plan and download details so you can evaluate both creative quality and the rights attached to that result.


## The free plan has daily song credits but no commercial use or monthly downloads

Suno's pricing page showed a $0 Free plan with 50 song credits per day when checked on November 18, 2026. It also listed no monthly song downloads and no commercial rights for that tier. A separate help page says free-plan songs are intended for personal, non-commercial use. These conditions make the free option suitable for learning the interface and sharing through Suno, but it is not equivalent to a paid publishing license or a recurring export allowance.

The free allowance is a daily generation budget, not proof that a particular song can be monetized. If you intend to distribute a track, confirm how it was created, whether you have a qualifying license and what the current rules say about downloading it. Suno's help materials distinguish trial downloads and free uses from paid subscription rights. Do not assume that upgrading later automatically changes the rights on older songs; the publisher says retroactive commercial rights are not granted by default.


## Paid tiers combine song credits, download quotas and different tools

The pricing page listed Pro, Premier and Platinum with different monthly song-credit and download allocations. Pro included 2,500 song credits and 20 monthly downloads; Premier listed 10,000 credits and 60 downloads; Platinum listed 15,000 song credits, visual credits and 120 downloads. Advanced models, editing, stem separation, voice tools and Suno Studio also vary by plan. A buyer should compare the combination needed for the actual project instead of treating all credits or downloads as interchangeable.

The prices shown included monthly and discounted annual billing, with annual totals displayed for paid tiers. Paid plans auto-renew and taxes are calculated at checkout. Confirm whether the amount shown is a monthly payment or a monthly equivalent billed for a year, and note the renewal date before starting. If you create frequently but rarely export, a large download quota may not matter. If you need stems or studio editing, the plan's feature gates may matter more than its credit count.


## A generation credit is not the same thing as a downloadable song

Suno tracks song credits and downloads separately. The Free plan can generate songs with daily credits but currently lists no monthly song downloads. Paid plans include a finite number of downloads per month. The help center says Pro subscribers receive 20 downloads and Premier receives 60; Free users receive zero download credits under its current download guidance. This distinction matters if your workflow produces many drafts and only a few final exports, or if several versions need to leave the service.

Map the path from first prompt to usable asset. Count the generation attempts, remixes, final versions and formats you expect to download. Check whether downloading the same song again or downloading stems consumes a quota under the current rule. Decide how a team will track which track has a commercial-use grant and which remains a private draft. A generation budget can look generous while a download cap constrains the final production handoff.


## Commercial-use rights are conditional and do not settle copyright ownership

Suno's plan page distinguishes Free, which does not include commercial rights, from paid plans that list commercial use. The help page explains that songs downloaded while subscribed receive commercial-use rights, and its guidance says songs made on a free tier do not become commercial by default simply because someone subscribes later. That makes the timing of creation and the plan active at that time important records. The specific agreement and current terms should be checked for the exact track and release.

The publisher also cautions that granting commercial use does not guarantee copyright protection. Those are separate questions: a platform may give permission under its own contract while the law in a particular country determines whether a human or other rights holder can claim copyright in the work. Do not advertise an AI-generated track as automatically copyright-protected. Keep the relevant plan receipt, date, prompt and source licenses, then obtain legal advice for high-value distribution or a dispute.


## Uploaded audio and voice features require rights at the source

Suno's Terms of Service tell users to upload only submissions they have the rights, licenses and permissions to use. The terms also state that a Voice Model may resemble only the user's own voice and prohibit creating or attempting to create a model of someone else. A file being available on the internet is not permission to upload it, train a model on it or build a commercial song from it. A team's upload process should identify the recording owner and the agreed scope of use before any file enters the workspace.

For client work, keep the recording license and performer consent with the project. Ask whether the permission covers editing, model creation, distribution and a generated voice likeness. Remove third-party speech from test data unless you have explicit authorization. If a brief depends on a recognizable voice, obtain legal review and ask the publisher to clarify any ambiguity. This is especially important because Suno's terms place responsibility for submitted materials and generated output on the user.


## A practical buyer test measures accepted tracks and revision effort

Prepare a short brief with the intended use, duration, genre, mood, structure and any required lyric or instrumental details. Generate a small number of versions, then listen on headphones, a phone speaker and the target playback setup. Check the opening, ending, lyrics, transitions and consistency. If a client will approve the track, involve that reviewer before producing a large batch. Record which settings and edits yielded a usable version, as well as the amount of human cleanup needed.

Measure the cost per accepted track, not just the cost per generated draft. Include song credits, download quota, paid plan, editing time and any required rights review. Add a correction test: request a specific change and determine whether it preserves the part you liked. This is a proposed evaluation, not a claim that Roseram generated, listened to or benchmarked Suno songs. A personal test can answer whether the workflow suits your project without relying on a polished sample from the vendor.


## Who should consider Suno, and what alternatives belong in the comparison?

Suno may be useful for creators exploring complete-song drafts, rapid musical concepts or editing generated material in one browser workflow. It may not fit a production that requires a fully documented chain of human-authored composition, a fixed per-export cost, control over every instrument track or a rights guarantee beyond the platform's terms. A free experiment can still be useful for ideation, but the intended use should be decided before moving a draft into a monetized channel.

Compare music tools with the same brief, length, vocal or instrumental requirement, export format and acceptance rubric. Include traditional production, a licensed music library or a human composer if those options better satisfy your rights or quality requirements. Estimate revisions and downloads under each plan. Choose the approach that gives you a track you can use confidently, rather than a result that only sounds promising in the creation interface.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Suno a scam?

This review found no source-based reason to label Suno a scam. It reviews the publisher's music-generation service, plan information and usage terms. That does not guarantee a song's quality, commercial success or copyright protection.

### Can I use Suno songs commercially?

The current plan page says Free does not include commercial rights and paid plans do. Suno's help page describes commercial-use rights for songs downloaded while subscribed and cautions that those rights do not guarantee copyright protection. Check the exact plan, song creation date and current terms.

### Do Suno credits roll over?

The current pricing page says subscription credits do not carry over day to day or month to month. It says purchased top-up credits do not expire but require an active subscription to use.

### Can I upload another person's voice to make a Suno voice model?

Suno's current terms say a Voice Model may resemble only the user's own voice and prohibit creating a model of another person or uploading someone else's voice recording for that purpose. Follow the current terms and obtain rights for other uploaded material.

### Does paid access guarantee copyright in an AI song?

No. Suno explicitly says that commercial-use rights do not guarantee copyright protection. Copyright eligibility and protection depend on the applicable jurisdiction and facts about the work.
