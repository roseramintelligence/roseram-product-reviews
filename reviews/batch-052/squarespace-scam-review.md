# Squarespace review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-18  
**Category:** Website builder and commerce platform  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Squarespace is a hosted website platform with design tools, managed hosting and commerce features. Its current US pricing lists Basic, Core, Plus and Advanced at $19, $29, $49 and $99 per month when billed annually, or $25, $39, $65 and $139 monthly. The page lists a 14-day trial and no permanent free plan. Basic has a 2% online-store transaction fee; Core and higher remove that fee, while card-processing rates vary by plan. Squarespace says annual website subscriptions may qualify for a full refund when canceled within 14 days of the first payment; monthly subscriptions and renewals are not refundable under that policy. Site export is partial and omits important commerce and design content. The reviewed sources do not substantiate a scam allegation.

## Editorial verdict

Squarespace is a legitimate hosted builder whose design workflow and included hosting can suit portfolio, service and commerce sites. Buyers should calculate subscription and transaction costs and plan around an incomplete site export. It is a better fit when a team accepts the hosted model than when full portability is required. This desk review uses public materials, not a live account, checkout or support test.

## What the Squarespace bundle is built to do

Squarespace combines a visual editor, design templates, managed hosting and business tools. Its official plan page describes selling features across paid tiers, with more capabilities and lower listed card rates at higher levels. That setup can help a small team concentrate on publishing instead of server maintenance.

A hosted platform also ties a site’s operation to the provider’s environment. Include the exit path in the evaluation: what happens if the account owner changes, a store outgrows the current plan, or the business moves providers? A straightforward editor can still leave substantial migration work when a site includes many products or customized layouts.


## The 14-day trial is not a permanent free plan

Squarespace describes a 14-day website trial and says it does not offer an ongoing free plan. The trial can be used to test editing and a representative customer flow, but the site will need a paid plan to continue after the trial. Before starting, note the trial end date, the plan likely to be needed and the first paid amount shown in the account.

Build a homepage, one service or product page, a contact form and the primary conversion path during the evaluation. Review the public pages on a phone, not only inside the editor. Ask the person who will maintain the site to make a routine change. That will expose workflow friction that a template gallery cannot show.


## Squarespace pricing: annual savings versus monthly flexibility

The official US page currently lists Basic at $19 per month annually or $25 monthly; Core at $29 annually or $39 monthly; Plus at $49 annually or $65 monthly; and Advanced at $99 annually or $139 monthly. Annual subscriptions include one eligible custom domain year, and that domain renews at the standard rate after the introductory year. Taxes and regional availability may affect the final total.

Annual rates are monthly equivalents for an annual commitment, not twelve monthly payments. Compare cash flow, tax, domain renewal and any additional services against expected revenue. Monthly billing may be preferable while the business is uncertain, whereas an established site may value the annual discount. Save the actual checkout amount and term.


## Store transaction fees and card processing are separate costs

The US plan page lists a 2% online-store transaction fee on Basic and no store transaction fee on Core, Plus or Advanced. It separately lists card-processing rates from 2.9% plus $0.30 on Basic and Core, 2.7% plus $0.30 on Plus, and 2.5% plus $0.30 on Advanced. These figures are a dated reference, not a promise that all countries or payment methods use identical terms.

Estimate total costs with realistic order values and monthly volume. Compare the higher subscription price against the difference in processing and store fees. Do not count the platform transaction fee as if it were the card fee; they are separate line items. Confirm which payment services are supported in the merchant’s country and what settlement rules apply.


## How to choose between Basic, Core, Plus and Advanced

Basic lists two contributors and a store transaction fee, while Core increases the contributor allowance and removes that transaction fee. Plus and Advanced add capabilities and lower listed card rates. Squarespace’s full comparison has more detail than the plan names, so use it against the site’s actual job rather than automatically choosing a highlighted tier.

Write down needs for editors, product types, discounts, abandoned-cart recovery, shipping, taxes, analytics, memberships and expected payment volume. Confirm each requirement in the current plan matrix. If a feature is only available at a higher tier, include the added subscription cost. If not, a lower plan could be more economical even after its transaction fee.


## Exports are partial; a store may need a rebuild to leave

Squarespace documents an XML export that can move selected content toward WordPress. It lists layout pages, one blog and certain text and image blocks as exportable. The same guide excludes store pages, product blocks, audio and video blocks, custom CSS, style settings, drafts and several page types. It warns that the export is not a full site transfer.

This is a portability limitation, not proof of a scam. A site with a substantial store or customized design may need manual recreation. Keep original copy, product data and media, test the XML export early, and make a list of elements that must be rebuilt. If moving the entire site later is a firm requirement, compare export behavior before you scale.


## Keep domain control separate from the website plan

Squarespace’s help center says a domain registered there does not automatically expire when the website subscription is canceled. An annual plan may include one eligible domain year, after which the domain renews at its standard price. That separation can preserve a brand’s address, but the owner still needs to know where it is registered and billed.

Keep the domain in a business-controlled account and maintain valid recovery contacts. Before changing platforms, review DNS, email routing, expiration and renewal. A canceled site and a live domain are separate states. Record every connected service instead of assuming that one cancellation ends all charges.


## Cancellation and refunds depend on timing and billing cadence

Squarespace’s current website refund policy says annual website subscriptions canceled within 14 days of the first payment can qualify for a full refund through a request process. It does not offer refunds under that policy for monthly subscriptions, annual plans canceled after the initial 14 days or renewal payments. The policy notes the cost of a free domain may be deducted from an eligible refund.

Keep the purchase date, plan term and renewal date with the invoice. If evaluating an annual commitment, make the decision inside the stated window and follow the official request route. For a later cancellation, act before renewal and verify the subscription state in billing. Removing a page, disconnecting a domain or closing a browser does not itself confirm that a subscription has ended.


## Privacy and access controls are part of running the site

Squarespace’s privacy policy describes information collected about customer accounts and site visitors, including technical and interaction information. A business owner should decide what forms collect, which analytics are enabled, what integrations receive data, and what notices or consent controls visitors need. The platform privacy policy is useful context, but it does not replace the site owner’s own privacy notice or legal responsibilities.

Give named editors only the access they need, remove access when roles change and protect account recovery. Before enabling an integration, check which data it receives and how to disconnect it. For a store, distinguish permissions to edit pages from access to orders, customer records and payments. These steps support clear ownership on any platform.


## SEO controls do not guarantee search visibility

Squarespace lists built-in SEO and mobile optimization among its plan features. Those tools can help configure a site, but rankings depend on the usefulness and quality of the pages, competition, search-engine systems and other factors. A website provider cannot promise a particular position or indexing schedule simply by enabling a setting.

A responsible launch includes unique titles and descriptions, a clear page structure, useful original content, working mobile layouts and a way to monitor search and conversion results. Publish only pages with a clear purpose. If a URL changes, map a redirect and check internal links. Treat impressions as observations to investigate, not proof of qualified demand.


## Compare alternatives using the same site brief

Compare Squarespace with Wix, Shopify, WordPress-based systems and other hosted builders according to the site’s job. A portfolio may prioritize design consistency; a store may prioritize catalog operations; a publication may care more about export and URL control. Compare total ownership cost, including processing, transaction fees, add-ons, staff time and potential migration.

Use one sample page, a real customer journey and an export test. Ask a nontechnical editor to change a price and publish a page, then inspect the export to see what is missing. The fastest platform to start is not necessarily the cheapest to run, and the lowest subscription can cost more if fees apply to routine sales volume.


## Who should consider Squarespace and who should pause

Squarespace may suit a business that values an integrated editor, managed hosting and a clear progression from a simple site to paid selling tools. Core and higher tiers remove the listed store transaction fee; the lower plan can still fit a small site if its costs and checkout features work for the business.

Pause if full site portability is essential, a store depends on content types that do not export, or the business needs infrastructure beyond the hosted system. A merchant should calculate the annual commitment and fees against realistic sales rather than relying on a headline price. Those are buyer-fit questions, not evidence of deceptive conduct.


## Squarespace SCAM review verdict: polished hosted design, partial portability

The official materials reviewed describe a real website and commerce platform with a 14-day trial, four paid tiers, plan-specific transaction and card fees, published cancellation rules and a partial content export. They do not substantiate a scam allegation. Buyers should focus on concrete terms: annual plans are billed for the term, refunds depend on cadence and timing, and exports will not carry every store, style or media element.

For a business that wants a polished hosted site and accepts these tradeoffs, Squarespace may be reasonable. For a store or publication that must migrate completely, run an export test and compare alternatives before building at scale. Confirm rates and payment eligibility in the right region, keep domain control clear, and set a renewal reminder. This is a dated public-source review, not a live purchase or support test.
