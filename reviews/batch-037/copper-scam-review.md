# Copper review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-16  
**Category:** Google Workspace CRM and relationship management  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Copper is a sales CRM that integrates with Google Workspace and organizes contacts, pipelines, tasks and projects. Its plans differ in contact limits, automation, reporting and per-seat annual pricing.

## Editorial verdict

Copper’s pricing page lays out contact ceilings and features by tier, with a 14-day trial and lower annual-billing rates. Teams should match their actual contact count, workflow requirements and Google data-sync needs to the plan and confirm data handling before import.

## Copper CRM review: relationships inside Google Workspace

Copper presents itself as a CRM built for Google Workspace, with contact and company records, pipelines, tasks, projects and interactions associated with Gmail and Calendar. Its plan comparison lists features such as email sync, calendar synchronization, mobile apps and a Chrome extension. Teams already centered on Google tools may value this context, while the same integration makes account permissions and field ownership important to review.

Start with a small group and confirm what messages, files and calendar events are synced. Decide which information should become a CRM record and whether every user may view or export it. Test an email thread, a duplicate contact and a calendar event involving an external customer. The goal is a reliable shared history, not a blanket copy of every personal message into the company database.


## Copper plan pricing and contact caps

Copper’s US pricing page lists Basic at $29 per seat per month, or an annual equivalent of $23, with a 2,500-contact limit. Professional is $69 monthly or $59 annually equivalent, with a 15,000-contact cap and features such as workflow automation, bulk email and reporting. Business is $134 monthly or $99 on the annual equivalent, with unlimited contacts and additional capabilities. The page says prices exclude applicable taxes and fees.

The gap between tiers may matter more than the initial subscription amount: an organization close to its contact ceiling should forecast growth and determine how active, inactive and duplicate records count. Multiply the selected price by the full number of seats and compare monthly versus annual total commitment. The 14-day trial does not remove the need to check which plan features remain after the trial.


## Copper pipelines, automation and reporting

The pricing comparison describes customizable sales and project pipelines, deal management, recommendations, task automation, workflow automation, bulk email and custom reporting at different levels. A CRM workflow should help representatives understand the next action without encouraging premature stage changes or unsupported revenue forecasts.

Define a few consistent pipeline stages and require only fields that are useful for qualification, forecasting or handoff. Test an automation that creates a task and sends a reminder, then check how duplicates or changed owners are handled. Review who can modify reports and export data. If the sales process uses multiple currencies, verify the tier and settings that support those values before using reports for financial decisions.


## Copper security, data import and exit planning

Copper’s public pricing page lists data import and export, team permissions and sign-in options that vary by plan. Data portability matters because contact records can include email history, customer notes and project details. Before a migration, map each field from the prior CRM and establish which system is the source of truth for consent, deal status and customer identity.

Test exports on a small dataset and confirm that relationships among people, companies, opportunities and tasks survive. Use individual accounts and review connected apps before broad rollout. Document how an employee’s access is removed and how customer data is retained or deleted when the company changes providers.


## Copper alternatives and the SCAM Review question

Compare Copper with Pipedrive, HubSpot and a CRM that does not depend on a particular productivity suite. Use the same seat count, contact total, pipeline structure, email tracking, project needs and annual-billing assumption. Copper may suit teams that work primarily in Google Workspace; a company with substantial sales-service routing or complex admin requirements may need a broader CRM.

Copper’s official feature and pricing pages describe a commercial CRM; they do not establish that Copper is a scam. This desk review did not connect a mailbox or test a sales workflow. Validate the data-sync scope, contact ceiling, user permissions and renewal price before importing customer records.


## Features and decision criteria

- Contact and company records linked to sales opportunities
- Google Workspace integrations with Gmail and Calendar
- Tier-dependent pipelines, automation, bulk email and reporting

## Pricing considerations

Copper’s public pricing page showed monthly and annual prices on 2026-11-16. Annual plans display lower monthly equivalents; prices exclude applicable taxes and fees. A 14-day trial is offered without a card. Verify that the current tier supports your contact volume and integrations before checkout.
