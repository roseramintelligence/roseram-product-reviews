# Competitors App review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-24  
**Category:** Competitive intelligence monitoring  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Competitors App monitors changes in competitor websites, marketing channels and online mentions. Its main public page currently lists pricing from US$19.90 per competitor per month, while a separate pricing page contains duplicate plan blocks and placeholder text, so buyers should request a clear current quote before purchase.

## Editorial verdict

Competitors App may help a marketer collect competitor updates across channels without checking each source manually. Its pricing presentation needs careful verification: the main page shows US$19.90 per competitor monthly, but a separate page includes repeated tiers, inconsistent annual and monthly labels, and visible placeholder copy. Compare the exact monitored channels, limits and add-ons in writing before budgeting or relying on the data for a launch decision.

## Use competitor monitoring to answer a specific question

Competitors App describes a service for collecting changes in competitors’ websites, marketing channels, social posts and other public activity. It can help a team notice a new page or campaign without visiting every competitor site manually. It cannot establish why a competitor made a change or whether that move is profitable.

Start with a short list of direct competitors and the signals you need: pricing changes, new product pages, email announcements or social cadence. Too many monitored brands can create more alerts than a small team can review. Assign an owner who will turn relevant changes into a decision rather than treating the alert feed as a strategy.


## Resolve the public pricing inconsistency

The main Competitors App page currently displays a starting price of US$19.90 per competitor per month. Its separate “New Pricing” page contains repeated Business and Business Plus blocks, annual and monthly labels that do not align cleanly, duplicate feature names, a custom plan displayed as “$??”, and the placeholder line “Lorem Ipsum”.

That makes it difficult to determine which amount is current or which channels and reports each plan actually includes. Before purchasing, ask the company for a written quote with competitor count, billing interval, channel coverage, add-ons, user seats, activity limits, tax and cancellation terms. Do not use a visible discount amount as a reliable budget until this is clarified.


## Check channel coverage and collection limits

The product pages describe monitoring across websites, social platforms, email and reviews, but the pricing page indicates that specific channels, LinkedIn, review tracking, data export and AI reports vary by plan or add-on. Compare the list of sources you need against the current account configuration, not only the headline description.

Run a small pilot on one competitor whose public changes you can independently verify. Check how often updates arrive, how duplicate alerts are handled and whether a source stops updating. Avoid assuming that every social platform or private email is observable. The tool should monitor public or authorized information within the platform’s stated scope.


## Turn alerts into a useful research process

A monitoring feed is most valuable when it supports a decision such as adjusting a product comparison, reviewing a pricing page or preparing sales staff for a public launch. Keep the original source link with each finding and record when the observation was made. A third-party summary should not replace checking the competitor’s current page.

Do not copy competitor marketing claims into your own site without attribution or fact-checking. Treat any automated AI analysis as a prompt for human review, particularly when it interprets sentiment or assigns strategic intent.


## Protect the work from false certainty

Public web pages can change temporarily, run experiments or show different content by region. A screenshot or alert may capture an A/B test rather than a permanent product change. Confirm material observations on the source site and note the date and country where relevant.

This review highlights an official pricing-page quality issue and describes the advertised product scope; it does not allege fraud. Ask the vendor to correct or explain the inconsistent plan display before subscribing. The analysis is based on publicly readable pages, not a paid account test.


## Features and decision criteria

- Website and marketing-channel monitoring
- Change alerts and email summaries described by vendor
- Plan pricing varies with number of competitors and features
- Agency and custom plans are marketed separately

## Pricing considerations

The pricing page also visibly contains “Lorem Ipsum” placeholder text and duplicated feature labels. Because the public pages conflict, do not rely on the displayed discount or one price line; ask the vendor to confirm currency, term, number of competitors, channels, add-ons, trial and cancellation in a written quote.
