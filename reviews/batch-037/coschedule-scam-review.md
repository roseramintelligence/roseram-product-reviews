# CoSchedule review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-24  
**Category:** Marketing calendar and social publishing  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

CoSchedule combines a marketing calendar with social publishing, campaign planning and team workflow features. Its pricing page lists a free calendar for one social profile and up to 15 scheduled messages, Starter at US$29 per month billed annually or US$39 monthly, and Professional at US$49 annually billed monthly equivalent or US$59 monthly.

## Editorial verdict

CoSchedule may help a solo marketer or team plan social posts and campaigns on a shared calendar. The free plan has a clear one-profile, 15-message cap, while X/Twitter is an extra charge on paid plans according to the pricing page. Choose it for a calendar and publishing workflow you will use, and verify each social network’s account requirements before replacing your current scheduler.

## Decide whether a marketing calendar solves your problem

CoSchedule combines campaign planning and social publishing around a calendar. That can help a marketer see upcoming posts and project work in one place. It is most useful when multiple planned activities need coordination; a person who publishes occasionally to one channel may prefer the free tier or a simpler workflow.

Write down the channels, number of profiles, people and approval steps you need. Then check that the supported account types match your actual social pages. For example, social platforms can restrict publishing by third-party apps to certain account types, so a connection test matters before migrating a campaign calendar.


## Understand the free limit and paid add-ons

CoSchedule’s free calendar allows one social profile and up to 15 scheduled messages at a time. The pricing page lists Starter at US$29 monthly on annual billing or US$39 month to month, and Professional at US$49 on annual billing or US$59 monthly. User and profile allowances differ by tier.

The same pricing page lists X/Twitter at US$16 per profile per month as an extra charge. Add that to your plan estimate along with additional social profiles and users. Count scheduled drafts and failed posts when considering the free limit; CoSchedule says they can count toward the 15-message cap until published or removed.


## Check trial billing and profile requirements

Starter and Professional include a 14-day trial, and CoSchedule says a card is required at signup. It says the user is not charged until the trial ends if they cancel before then. Note the exact trial end and cancellation method when starting, particularly if several people can manage billing.

Review the network list and connect a test profile before scheduling business-critical posts. Confirm permissions, account type, image and video support, and any publishing limitations set by the social network. Do not assume that “11 networks” means every profile or post format is supported in every region.


## Make approvals and responsible publishing part of the workflow

A shared calendar can improve visibility, but scheduled content still needs review. Assign an owner for each post, check links and dates, and require approval for claims that affect customers or regulated topics. If a product changes, pause scheduled content that has become inaccurate.

Use social tools to plan useful content, not to automate repetitive comments or unsolicited messages. Review platform rules and provide a clear correction path if a scheduled post is wrong. A well-organized calendar cannot guarantee engagement or sales.


## Run a small editorial pilot

Plan one week of content for a single profile. Test drafting, media upload, best-time scheduling, approval and changes to a post that is not yet published. Confirm that native posts appear in the calendar as expected and that the social network allows the planned format.

After the pilot, compare the setup time and publishing reliability with your current process. Review how team seats, profiles and add-ons change the total price. This review summarizes official feature and billing pages; it is not a CoSchedule account test or a measured marketing result.


## Features and decision criteria

- Marketing calendar for campaigns, projects and social messages
- Free plan includes one profile and up to 15 scheduled messages
- Paid plans include unlimited scheduled posts but differ by seats and profiles
- X/Twitter is billed as a separate paid profile add-on

## Pricing considerations

The pricing page lists a 14-day trial for Starter and Professional, requiring a payment method and charging only after the trial unless canceled. X/Twitter profiles are listed at US$16 per profile monthly as an add-on. Confirm included networks, social account types, add-ons and renewal terms in checkout.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does CoSchedule have a free plan?

Yes. Its support page says the free plan supports one social profile and up to 15 scheduled messages at a time.

### Is X included in a CoSchedule paid plan?

The current pricing page lists X/Twitter as a separate US$16 per profile monthly add-on.

### Does CoSchedule publish to every account type?

No. The support page lists supported account types by network, and some platforms restrict external publishing.
