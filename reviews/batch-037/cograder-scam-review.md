# CoGrader review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-24  
**Category:** AI-assisted grading for educators  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

CoGrader is an AI grading assistant for educators that uses rubrics to help assess written assignments and provide feedback. Its official pricing page lists a free Starter tier, Standard at US$15 per month billed annually and custom school plans; the help center currently describes a 90-day, 1,000-grading new-account trial followed by 150 gradings per month on Starter.

## Editorial verdict

CoGrader is designed to help teachers process assignments and draft rubric-based feedback, not replace educator judgment. The free and paid allowances make it possible to evaluate a real class workflow, but the public pricing page and help article currently show different free-submission counts. Schools should clarify the live allowance, student-data handling, originality checks and LMS integration before deployment, and review every grade before returning it to a student.

## Rubric-based feedback support for teachers

CoGrader describes itself as an AI grading assistant for writing and quizzes. A teacher can use rubrics and student work to draft scores and feedback, then review the output. The product lists integrations with Google Classroom and, in school-oriented plans, Canvas and Schoology. It is intended to reduce repetitive grading steps while keeping the teacher responsible for the assessment.

For a meaningful pilot, choose one assignment with a clear rubric and a representative set of submissions. Compare the AI’s suggested scores and comments with the teacher’s normal process, note disagreements and revise the rubric or instructions where needed. Do not return machine-generated comments without checking accuracy, tone and relevance to the student’s actual work.


## Resolve the plan and trial allowance discrepancy

CoGrader’s pricing page currently lists Starter at 100 submissions per month, while a more recently updated help-center article says that after trial, Starter includes 150 gradings per month. The help article describes a new-account trial of 90 days or 1,000 gradings, while the pricing page lists 90 days of premium access. Because the public materials differ, the exact current allowance should be checked in the product’s Plan and Billing screen.

The Standard plan is listed at US$15 per month when billed annually on the pricing page. The help article also lists a US$19 month-to-month option and US$15 per month annually. School and district plans are quote-based and describe unlimited grading. Confirm current limits, whether trial usage counts toward paid quotas and what happens when a class exceeds its allowance.


## Check integrations and educator controls

The pricing page lists Google Classroom integration on Starter and names Canvas and Schoology integrations for schools and districts. Higher tiers add shared rubric libraries, user administration, analytics, onboarding and support. A school should confirm its learning-management system, student roster sync, account roles and reporting permissions before rollout.

Use an assignment with a known rubric and compare feedback for different writing styles and accessibility needs. Test how the system treats missing submissions, accommodations, handwritten work and multi-language responses. Keep the original student submission and the educator’s rubric available so every suggestion can be reviewed in context.


## Treat student work as sensitive information

CoGrader’s AI transparency note describes assignment content and rubrics being sent to an AI service provider with additional instructions. Schools should read the current data policy and determine which student information is processed, which providers may receive it, how long it is retained and whether the institution’s consent and retention rules are satisfied. Do not include unnecessary identifiers in a pilot.

The pricing page advertises FERPA compliance and lists originality checks on paid plans. Verify what those claims mean for the specific school contract and whether a data-processing agreement, student notice or district approval is required. AI-detection and plagiarism indicators should be treated as signals for human review, not proof of misconduct.


## Who should evaluate CoGrader?

CoGrader may be worth evaluating for teachers who spend substantial time producing rubric-based feedback and can review each suggestion. Its integrations and school tiers may fit a larger workflow, but grading is a high-impact decision and needs educator oversight. Start with a small, low-stakes assignment and compare time saved against correction effort and feedback quality.

This guide summarizes CoGrader’s public pricing, plan help and AI transparency information. Roseram did not test grading accuracy or review a school contract. Confirm current limits, data handling and subscription terms before using it with student work.

The help article says newly created accounts receive a 90-day trial with 1,000 gradings; the marketing page lists 90 days of premium access.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a teacher use CoGrader for free?

CoGrader lists a free Starter plan, but its public pricing page and help article currently state different monthly submission limits; check the account billing screen.

### How much is CoGrader Standard?

The official pricing page lists US$15 monthly billed annually; the help article also lists US$19 month-to-month.

### Should AI grading decide a student’s final grade?

No. Use AI suggestions as a draft, verify the rubric application and review feedback before returning an assessment.
