# Cometly review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-24  
**Category:** Marketing attribution analytics  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Cometly offers marketing attribution for connecting advertising interactions with leads, pipeline and revenue. Its current pricing is based on monthly pageview volume, and the public page does not publish a single fixed price; it directs buyers to a quote call. The company says there is no free trial.

## Editorial verdict

Cometly may suit a B2B SaaS team that needs a view of how paid campaigns relate to pipeline and closed revenue. The tool depends on accurate tracking, CRM setup and a reliable definition of attribution. Since price is tied to pageview tiers and no public flat rate is shown, a buyer should obtain a written quote and map data flows before connecting ad accounts or production customer information.

## Know what attribution is intended to answer

Cometly is designed to connect marketing interactions with leads, sales pipeline and revenue. A team might use it to compare campaigns or see how multiple touchpoints appear in an account journey. Attribution is a model built from captured events; it is not the same as proving that a single ad caused a sale.

Before evaluating it, define the question your team cannot answer today. Specify the conversion event, CRM source of truth, sales stages and revenue fields. If your event naming or CRM records are inconsistent, a new dashboard may make the inconsistency more visible without resolving it.


## Clarify pageview pricing before wiring the stack

Cometly’s pricing page says plans are tailored to monthly pageview volume. Its FAQ defines a pageview as a tracked page load through the pixel or server-side event, and says the platform does not throttle attribution when a tier is exceeded. The company says it notifies customers at 90% and automatically moves them to a higher tier at the start of the next period.

Ask for a quote that lists pageview bands, how server-side events are counted, what happens during a traffic spike, and the price of the next tier. Compare monthly and annual billing; the page says annual plans save 20%. Since no free trial is offered, determine what onboarding and demonstration are available before providing production data.


## Audit event definitions and data ownership

An attribution system is only useful when the team understands which events it receives and how they are joined to CRM records. Map pageview, signup, qualified lead, opportunity and closed-won fields. Check identity resolution, deduplication, consent settings and any server-side event implementation with your technical and privacy teams.

Use a test environment or a limited rollout first. Compare event counts with the analytics and CRM systems already used. Investigate discrepancies before making budget decisions from a new dashboard. Ask what data can be exported and how long it remains available after cancellation.


## Treat models as decision support

Marketing attribution can organize evidence, but different attribution windows and models can assign credit differently. Document the model used and compare it with holdout tests, cohort performance or other incrementality methods where appropriate. Avoid treating a last-click or multi-touch chart as a causal experiment.

Set a review cadence where finance and marketing reconcile spend and revenue definitions. Note refunds, delayed sales, offline events and cross-device limits. These checks help prevent a precise-looking chart from becoming an unsupported claim about return on ad spend.


## Check access and integration requirements

The pricing page describes ad-platform connections and identifies additional warehouse and API features for Enterprise. Confirm which integrations are included on the quoted plan, what permissions each needs and whether credentials can be restricted. Avoid connecting accounts you do not own or lack permission to use.

Review onboarding, support, data-processing terms and cancellation. Keep a record of your tracking configuration outside the platform so your organization can understand its own measurement design. This review is based on Cometly’s public pages; it is not a live integration test or a measurement of customer revenue.


## Features and decision criteria

- Pixel and server-side tracking
- Multi-touch attribution, account journeys and dashboards
- Connections to major advertising platforms listed in pricing FAQ
- Enterprise adds warehouse, API and custom integration options

## Pricing considerations

The official page defines a pageview as a tracked website load and says it will notify customers at 90% of a tier and move them to the next tier at the following billing period. It advertises a 20% annual-billing discount. Request the exact tier, included pageviews, overage handling, onboarding fee and renewal terms in writing.
