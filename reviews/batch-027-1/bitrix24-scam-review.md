# Bitrix24 review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-24  
**Category:** CRM, project management and business collaboration software  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Bitrix24 combines customer relationship management, sales pipelines, tasks and projects, communication, calendars, websites and other business tools. Its cloud pricing uses organization-level tiers with user caps rather than a simple per-seat price. The official pricing page shows a free option for one or two users, paid Essentials, Basic, Standard and Professional plans, and a separate Vibe+ lineup with AI and app-building capabilities. Feature availability, storage and pricing depend on region, billing term and plan; confirm the current comparison before migrating a team.

## Editorial verdict

Bitrix24 may suit teams that want CRM and collaboration tools in one workspace and prefer organization-level pricing with a defined user limit. Its broad feature set can simplify vendor management, but it also makes plan comparison, setup and user adoption important. Start with the CRM and task flows the team will actually use, verify the user cap and storage, and test exports, permissions, integrations and billing before moving operational data.

## Map the CRM and collaboration work you need

Bitrix24’s official product pages present a broad business workspace. CRM records and sales pipelines sit alongside tasks and projects, calendars, chat and video calls, storage, forms and additional tools that depend on the subscription tier. This breadth may appeal to a company trying to reduce disconnected systems, but a long feature list does not show whether the everyday workflow will be simple for a particular team.

Write down one customer journey before configuration: how a new inquiry arrives, who owns it, how follow-up is recorded, what changes a deal stage and how a manager sees the next action. Then map tasks, permissions and reporting around that path. Ask which capabilities require a paid plan, a marketplace app, a particular region, an administrator or outside implementation. Keep the first rollout focused enough that staff can explain where a record lives and how to correct it.


## Understand organization pricing, user caps and plan changes

When checked, Bitrix24’s official US pricing page listed Free at $0 for one to two users, Basic at $69 monthly or $49 per month billed annually for up to five users, Standard at $144 monthly or $99 billed annually for up to 50 users, and Professional at $289 monthly or $199 billed annually for up to 100 users. It also showed a separate Vibe+ lineup with different features and prices. These are dated observations from the vendor’s page, not a promise that every region or account sees the same offer.

An organization-level price can be economical when a team fits within a tier, but the user limit creates a threshold to plan around. Count administrators, contractors and occasional users as the vendor defines them. Compare monthly billing with the full annual prepayment, check the renewal price and cancellation process, and verify whether changing plans affects storage, automation, history or API access. Include implementation, data cleanup, training and paid apps in the project budget.


## Test permissions, data portability and integrations

A CRM often contains contact details, notes, sales history and internal comments. Before importing real records, test roles with separate accounts and verify who can view, export, edit and delete each type of information. Review access logs, backups, retention, data-region options and administrator controls against the organization’s requirements. Confirm how a departed employee’s access is removed and how customer requests are handled.

Inventory each integration the team depends on, including email, telephony, payments, web forms and reporting. Check which direction each field syncs, how duplicates are handled, what happens when an API connection expires and how failures are surfaced. Export a representative set of records and attachments in a format the team can retain. A demo that shows data entering the CRM is not enough; also test edits, deletion, re-import and a failed integration.


## Run a small pilot before rolling it out broadly

Use a limited group and a sample pipeline with realistic, non-sensitive data. Ask users to complete a repeatable set of actions: add a contact, log an interaction, create a task, move a deal, find an overdue follow-up and build a basic report. Record where people hesitate, which fields they skip and what requires administrator help. Compare the outcome with the current process rather than relying on the number of modules available.

Before migrating, decide which system is authoritative, how old records are cleaned, which fields map, and how rollback will work. Schedule training around the team’s actual customer process and name an owner for permissions, automations and data quality. This buyer guide summarizes public vendor materials and observed US pricing; it is not an independent security audit, usability test or guarantee of savings.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does Bitrix24 have a free plan?

The official US pricing page listed a free tier for one or two users with 5 GB of storage when checked. Confirm the current feature and regional limits before choosing it.

### How does Bitrix24 price its cloud plans?

Its public cloud plans are priced per organization with user limits, rather than a simple price for each added user. The official page showed monthly and annual prices for several tiers; verify current pricing and included limits.

### Is Bitrix24 only a CRM?

No. The vendor positions it as a broader workspace with CRM, tasks and projects, communication, calendars, files and other plan-dependent tools.
