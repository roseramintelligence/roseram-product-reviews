# 123Tenders.co.uk review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-17  
**Category:** UK tender discovery and bid preparation software  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

123 Tenders is a UK business platform that aggregates public procurement notices and adds opportunity matching, tender analysis, bid-answer drafting, alerts and business administration tools. Its free tier allows contract browsing and a limited Tender X-Ray; paid Pro and Business plans add higher analysis and drafting allowances, while a full Bid Pack is a separate one-off purchase. The service also offers adjacent products such as business toolkits, Growth & Visibility and an AI receptionist. Pricing and plan details were reviewed on November 17, 2026; the buyer should re-check the live price page because this is an actively changing service.

## Editorial verdict

The reviewed official pages identify 123 Tenders Ltd, publish product terms and prices, describe limits on generated bid drafts, explain the service’s data role and disclose that the company does not yet hold formal ISO 27001 or Cyber Essentials certification. This evidence does not support calling it a scam. The material buying risks are reliance on aggregated notices that may be delayed or incomplete, treating AI drafts or match scores as authoritative, confusing subscription allowances with a separately priced full bid draft, and accepting supplier security statements as independently audited facts. No paid account, tender submission, bid outcome or security control was independently tested.

## 123Tenders.co.uk review: tender search plus a set of bid-preparation tools

123 Tenders presents itself as a UK business hub for finding public work and preparing to bid. Its tender workflow brings notices from four named public-sector sources into one searchable interface, then layers in region and sector filters, a procurement profile, an AI match score, Tender X-Ray, a recorded Bid / No-Bid view, saved answers and drafting. The intended customer is a small business that can deliver the work but may not have a dedicated bid team. The product is therefore best assessed as a notice-discovery and decision-support system rather than as a bid-writing consultancy or a buyer portal.

The site also groups other services under one account: calculators and templates, business administration toolkits, hiring and homeowner quote workflows, Growth & Visibility and the 123 Answer AI receptionist. These are related business tools, but their charges and terms do not all sit inside the tender subscription. A buyer should identify the particular workflow being purchased instead of assuming that the broad “business growth hub” description means every module is included.


## What the notice and analysis workflow can—and cannot—tell a bidder

The company says it republishes public notices from Find a Tender, Contracts Finder, Public Contracts Scotland and Sell2Wales, and lets users filter by region, sector, value and closing date. Match Score compares a notice against details recorded in a procurement profile; Tender X-Ray extracts the scope, dates, requirements and evaluation information; Bid / No-Bid records a structured view of fit. These functions can make initial triage faster, especially when a small team is scanning a large notice list. The underlying notices remain the buyer’s documents, however, and the service directs users to verify the original before relying on a field or deadline.

A score is not a procurement decision, and a plain-language summary is not a substitute for reading the full specification, eligibility conditions, pricing schedule, attachments and portal instructions. An apparently strong match can still miss a mandatory certificate, geographic condition or delivery requirement. A sensible pilot is to compare several summaries with their original notices, record omissions, and decide what must always be checked by a person before any bid decision is made.


## AI bid drafting, answer tools and award expectations

The plan page distinguishes answer-level drafting from a complete bid response. Pro lists up to eight monthly Bid Co-Pilot drafts and Business lists up to 30; the same page states that a full AI bid draft is not included in those subscriptions. Bid Pack is listed separately at £349 for one first draft. That difference matters for budgeting: a feature described as drafting support does not automatically equal a complete, submission-ready response. The Answer Library lets a business keep and adapt its own content, while the Word export provides an editable handoff rather than evidence that the generated text is correct.

The terms state that no plan or generated document guarantees a shortlist or contract award. Users are responsible for checking accuracy, supplying evidence, meeting deadlines and finalizing the submission. Treat the AI output as a starting document: confirm every factual statement against company records, map each answer to the buyer’s scoring criteria, remove unsupported claims, verify legal and technical commitments, and use the buyer’s own portal as the source of truth. Nothing in the reviewed materials provides independently verified win-rate data or proof that a particular user secured a contract because of the service.


## Current plans, separate products and value for money

The live pricing page checked on November 17, 2026 lists Free at £0, Pro at £89 per month or £799 per year, and Business at £199 per month or £1,990 per year. The Free tier includes browsing, access to original notices and one Tender X-Ray taster. Pro raises the analysis limits and adds answer-level drafting and other workflow tools; Business raises those limits again and includes readiness features and 100 calls of 123 Answer. Bid Pack is £349 one-off. The adjacent Growth & Visibility service is separately priced at £299 annually or £29.99 monthly. The prices are in pounds and the page notes that VAT may apply.

Those figures should be compared with the frequency and type of work a business actually bids for. A company that only wants to browse public notices may be adequately served by the free tier and official portals. Regular bidders may value consolidated search and time-saving analysis, while a business needing a full response must account for the separate Bid Pack or professional bid support. Business Toolkits and document downloads also have their own limits outside the main tender feature table. Before checkout, check the live page for current caps, billing interval, VAT treatment and whether the module being considered is included, an add-on or a separate purchase.


## Data quality, source licensing and operational checks

The provider says its notice data is aggregated from public sources under the Open Government Licence and warns that contract information can be incomplete, delayed or out of date. That is an important limitation for a product that surfaces values and deadlines: a buyer should open the official notice and any amendment before spending money or committing resources. The Open Government Licence is a reuse framework for qualifying public-sector information, but it does not make every notice field complete or remove the need to follow the licence’s attribution and exceptions. Verify that a specific source document carries the licence claimed for it.

Alerts and reminders are described as best-effort, and the terms place deadline and final-submission responsibility on the user. For a live opportunity, keep the original notice, buyer portal, amendment history and a calendar reminder under your own control. Do not rely on one email digest or a third-party record as the only trigger for a high-value deadline. The service can organize information; it cannot ensure that a buyer’s portal remains available or that a notice has not changed.


## Privacy, security and company disclosures

The privacy policy says the company handles account and business-profile details, tender text and analysis results, saved tool records, payment/order data, usage limits, referral codes and support correspondence. It says it does not store card or bank details itself. For records a customer enters about staff, clients or contacts, the policy and terms describe the customer as controller and 123 Tenders as processor. The terms identify named subprocessors for particular functions, including hosting, AI, database, email and payment providers; the separate 123 Answer terms list call-processing vendors and explain that call data is processed in the United States with transfer safeguards. Organizations should read the current notices against the types of information they plan to enter.

The security page says traffic uses HTTPS/TLS, saved data is held in an encrypted PostgreSQL database, and row-level security separates accounts. These are provider statements, not independently verified audit findings. The same page explicitly says the company does not yet hold formal certifications such as ISO 27001 or Cyber Essentials. That disclosure is useful for an early-stage buyer, but it may be a procurement blocker for an organization that requires certified controls, a specific assurance report or a completed supplier security assessment. Ask for the current Data Processing Agreement and evaluate subprocessor, retention, access, incident and deletion details before storing sensitive bid material.


## Terms, cancellation and buyer responsibilities

The terms say plans can be cancelled through the account and access continues until the paid period ends. Refund eligibility depends on whether the paid feature has already been used: the terms say an unused paid feature can be refundable on request, whereas a delivered feature such as a generated Bid Pack or paid download is non-refundable. This is more specific than assuming either that all subscriptions are non-refundable or that every unsuccessful bid can be refunded. Read the full current terms and save the checkout description before paying for a separately delivered item.

The company also disclaims professional legal, tax, accounting and bid-writing advice, sets a liability cap tied to fees paid in the previous 12 months subject to legal exceptions, and says it does not promise contract awards. These provisions are meaningful because bid preparation has a high consequence of error. The user remains responsible for truthful statements, eligibility, required evidence, pricing, compliance and timely submission. A bid writer or procurement adviser may still be appropriate for a complex or high-value response.


## Who may find it useful, and when to pause

A small UK supplier that searches across several official notice portals and wants a single workflow for filtering, note-taking and early analysis may find the free tier a low-friction way to evaluate the interface. Pro or Business may be more relevant if the team actually needs the larger analysis limits, alert regions, answer drafting or readiness tools. The separate packages make it possible to buy only one job-specific draft or an adjacent business function, though each should be compared with alternatives on its own terms.

Pause before committing if the business expects an automated draft to be submission-ready, wants a guarantee of wins, depends on the listed security features being independently certified, or cannot maintain a manual check against original notices and buyer portals. Ask whether the current package covers the use case, what data the team must provide, which subprocessors apply, what the exact cap is, and what happens to drafts and account data on closure. These checks are not signs of misconduct; they are proportionate procurement diligence.


## Is 123Tenders.co.uk a scam? Evidence-based conclusion

The official pages provide a named UK company, company number and registered office, a detailed feature and price table, dated terms, a privacy policy and a security disclosure. They openly state that bid drafts do not guarantee an award, that public notices can be out of date, that the user must verify and submit the final response, and that the company does not yet have formal ISO 27001 or Cyber Essentials certification. The reviewed evidence does not substantiate calling 123 Tenders a scam. The meaningful risks are product-fit, data-quality, AI-draft reliance, separate billing and the level of security assurance a buyer requires.

This is a source-based desk review, not a paid subscription, vendor audit, test of a live bid workflow or evaluation of customer outcomes. Prices and plan limits were checked on November 17, 2026 and can change. Before buying, verify the amount and renewal interval at checkout, use a free account to compare a few notices with their official source documents, check current security and privacy terms, and independently review any generated answer before submission.


## Features and decision criteria

- Search and filters for public-sector notices aggregated from Find a Tender, Contracts Finder, Public Contracts Scotland and Sell2Wales
- Procurement profiles, AI Match Scores, Tender X-Ray analysis and structured Bid / No-Bid notes
- Bid Co-Pilot answer drafts, a reusable Answer Library and editable Word exports
- A separate one-off Bid Pack draft for an individual tender, with no award guarantee
- Adjacent UK business utilities including admin toolkits, calculators, hiring and homeowner quote workflows
- Optional 123 Answer AI receptionist product with published call allowances and overage charges
