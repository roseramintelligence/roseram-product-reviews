# 1min.AI review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-17  
**Category:** All-in-one AI workspace  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

1min.AI is a multi-tool AI workspace that brings chat, writing, document, image, audio, video and social-agent features into one account with a shared usage-credit wallet. Its public pricing page displayed Free at $0, Pro at $6.50/month, Business at $10/month and Enterprise at $7/member/month, with annual-billing savings advertised at 20%; top-up rates and storage periods differ by plan. The Terms page still carries an August 2024 date and names Hoan Tran IT, while the July 2026 Privacy Policy names 1minAI. Its refund help article says direct purchases can be submitted within 30 days, with the refund rate affected by credits already used. Treat provider availability, credit cost per model, retention and legal entity as checkout questions rather than assuming one flat unlimited allowance.

## Editorial verdict

The official product material reviewed does not substantiate calling 1min.AI a scam. It identifies a live multi-feature service, a free tier, paid plans, a public API, support and published privacy and refund pages. The substantial buyer diligence is about the way an all-in-one interface meters usage, the current model list and provider routing, the duration of stored assets, how uploaded content reaches underlying model providers, and whether the published legal terms reflect the current contracting entity. 1min.AI’s own trust article describes encryption, control processes and a SOC 2 Type II audit in progress; that is a vendor statement, not an independently verified audit report. No account, API call, generation, billing event, data deletion or security assessment was performed for this desk review.

## 1min.AI review: the appeal is the shared workspace

1min.AI is presented as an all-in-one workspace rather than a single model or a narrow writing tool. Its public product catalogue groups chat, writing, document work, image creation and editing, audio, video, social content agents and an API under one account. That makes comparison convenient: a user can explore several kinds of AI work from one interface instead of opening a separate product for every task. The trade-off is that the product name alone does not tell a buyer which underlying model powers a particular action, how much it costs in credits, or how long its output and uploaded inputs remain available.

The homepage’s “trusted by millions” and privacy claims are marketing statements. The site also publishes policies, plan tables, help articles and developer documentation that make a more useful buyer review possible. This assessment uses those public materials and does not pretend to be a customer testimonial: no account was opened, no response quality was benchmarked and no purchase was made. Because the model roster and promotional prices can change, this review separates the visible plan structure from claims that need to be checked inside the current product.


## What is included, and what the bundle does not settle

The public feature list spans multi-model chat, writing aids, document translation and presentation creation, image tools, speech and audio generation, video tasks, and social agents for several networks. Its API documentation separately describes image-to-prompt, DALL·E 3 image generation and video analysis endpoints. This breadth is useful for a freelancer or small team that wants one place to draft, transform and organize different kinds of media. It is less useful if the main requirement is a tightly controlled production pipeline with one fixed model, a documented service-level agreement, or a specific integration that is not listed in the current API reference.

A catalogue that names familiar model families should not be read as a guarantee of permanent access, identical provider features, or the same output as each model’s own first-party application. Model versions, context windows, image sizes, generation rules and provider restrictions can differ. Before migrating a team workflow, identify the exact model and operation in the live UI, save a representative prompt set, and compare output, latency, file export and credit charge against the existing tool. The public API overview is a useful starting point, but production integration still requires checking the endpoint reference, authentication, rate limits, retention and error handling.


## Credits, rollover and top-ups: plan price is only half the budget

1min.AI uses a shared wallet rather than a simple promise of unlimited model calls. The public plan page shows monthly bonus-credit amounts for paid tiers and additional-credit rates; the product also advertises rollover for unused credits. A published credit explainer says costs depend on the action and the chosen model. That is a sensible structure for a broad catalogue, but it makes a typical workload hard to price by looking only at the headline subscription fee. A short chat, long-context document analysis, image generation and video work can consume very different balances.

The buying decision should be based on the work you actually expect to run. Test a small, non-sensitive sample for each important task and record the model, input size, output size, credit change and result quality. Check whether promotional credits, monthly bonus credits, purchased top-ups and rolled-over balances expire under the current account rules; the public marketing page says rollover and the credit page says purchased credits do not expire, but the in-product ledger is the place to verify which bucket a charge used. If a campaign or team depends on recurring output, set a low spending limit and review the bill after the first usage cycle.


## Public plan prices and storage periods

On November 17, 2026, the public page displayed the Free plan at $0, Pro at $6.50 per month, Business at $10 per month and Enterprise at $7 per member per month. It advertised a 20% saving with annual billing. Free was described as having limited AI features and three days of storage. Pro listed three months of storage and one million monthly bonus credits; Business listed 12 months of storage, two million credits, collaboration and up to ten members; Enterprise listed unlimited storage and members, two million credits per member, priority support and feature requests. The page displayed top-ups at $1.34 per million credits on Free and $0.67 per million on paid tiers.

These are page-displayed values, not a quote: the public page uses dollar signs without establishing every user’s checkout currency, and annual billing changes the effective price. A credit-per-million rate also is not the same as the cost of a useful finished result; the feature and model determine how many credits an action uses. Confirm the selected plan, billing period, taxes, included credits, top-up rate and renewal amount in the final checkout. Storage length matters too: a low subscription tier may not be appropriate for a team expecting the workspace to serve as a long-term archive.


## Privacy, uploads and the trust claims

The July 31, 2026 Privacy Policy lists account details and usage data such as IP address, browser, pages visited, timestamps and device identifiers. It says face data uploaded to the service is used to process user inquiries, retained for no longer than one year and not shared with third parties. It separately says other uploads may include documents and voices, and it allows sharing personal data with service providers, affiliates and business partners in specified circumstances. The policy also allows international processing and gives a one-year maximum for personal data subject to legal, dispute and agreement exceptions; usage data has separate, less definite retention language. These distinctions are material for users uploading client documents, personal images or voice recordings.

The homepage says “No AI Training,” and a vendor-authored security explainer describes encryption, operational controls, claimed HIPAA and GDPR status, and a SOC 2 Type II audit that was still in progress with an expected January 2027 completion. Those are the provider’s own statements, not proof that every model vendor, app feature or account type has the same protection. The Privacy Policy’s general sharing language also means an organization should not infer a blanket no-subprocessor rule from a short marketing badge. Ask for the current data-processing agreement, subprocessor list, retention/deletion behavior for prompts and generated assets, applicable business associate agreement if handling protected health information, and the precise scope of any compliance claim. Do not upload regulated or confidential material until that review is complete.


## Refund requests, cancellation and the contracting entity

The public Terms page displays the date August 31, 2024, names Hoan Tran IT as the company, and identifies California, United States as the country. The Privacy Policy, dated July 31, 2026, names 1minAI as the company. That may reflect a brand and operating entity relationship, but the pages do not explain it clearly; check the legal seller shown on the payment receipt and ask support which entity is the contractual counterparty. The Terms contain broad warranty disclaimers and limit liability to amounts paid or US$100 if no purchase has been made. This is contract language to review in context, not a conclusion about enforceability in any particular jurisdiction.

The Terms give a short 30-day refund promise. The more detailed Help Center explains that direct purchases can be submitted within 30 days by sending a receipt, that the refund rate varies with how many credits have already been used, that authorized-partner purchases must be refunded by that partner, and that Apple handles App Store refund requests. The refund route therefore depends on where the purchase occurred and how much of the credit balance has been consumed. Save the receipt, identify the seller before checkout, and ask support how usage is calculated before relying on the guarantee. The public materials reviewed did not establish a single universal cancellation workflow for every sales channel.


## Who should compare 1min.AI with alternatives

The service is a plausible shortlist candidate for an individual creator who wants to test multiple media and writing tools, or a small team willing to manage a shared usage wallet. The plan ladder, bonus credits and common interface can be simpler than juggling many separate subscriptions, particularly when work is exploratory. The strongest fit is a task where the user can compare several models, accept a credit-metered workflow and keep a record of which provider and generation settings produced a usable result.

Compare it with direct model subscriptions if you need the first-party product’s newest features or a known allowance; with an API marketplace if you need routing and developer controls; with a specialist image, audio or video service if output consistency and production controls dominate; and with enterprise platforms that can provide a contract, audit materials, access controls and support commitments matching your procurement requirements. Do not compare only the number of tools in a catalogue. Compare the cost of a repeatable finished task, data terms, available export formats, team permissions, support response and the consequences of a provider or model disappearing from the bundle.


## 1min.AI scam review verdict: evidence supports a real service, with diligence still needed

The official material reviewed describes a working multi-category AI product, publishes pricing and credit information, supplies API documentation, provides a support channel and sets out privacy and refund pages. This evidence does not substantiate calling 1min.AI a scam. It does not independently establish model quality, uninterrupted access, data security performance, certification scope, or that a user will receive a particular result for a given credit charge. The right buyer conclusion is measured: the product has a broad feature set and a low displayed entry price, while costs, model availability, data handling and legal terms need a closer look before sensitive or high-volume use.

This is a source-based desk review, not a hands-on product test, payment audit, penetration test, legal opinion or endorsement. Prices and product claims were checked November 17, 2026; the Privacy Policy states July 31, 2026, while the public Terms page still displays August 31, 2024. Start with a low-risk task, inspect credit deductions, export your work, verify the receipt’s legal seller and obtain current written answers on data retention and subprocessors. Revisit these checks if the plans, policies or model providers change.

- Social content agents and collaborative workspaces advertised for selected plans
- Shared credit wallet, bonus monthly credits on paid plans, rollover claims and optional credit top-ups
- Developer API documentation for image-to-prompt, image generation and video analysis use cases
