# 1CRM review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-17  
**Category:** Customer relationship and business management software  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

1CRM positions itself as a broad customer relationship and business-management platform spanning sales, marketing, orders, projects and customer support. Its public pricing page lists tier-specific user and record limits as well as cloud and on-premise options, so the relevant comparison is the total fit and operating responsibility of the selected edition.

## Editorial verdict

The official materials reviewed describe an established CRM product with published editions and implementation documentation; they do not establish that 1CRM is a scam. Check the current quote, edition ceilings, hosting location, update/support terms and migration plan against your team’s actual workflow.

## 1CRM review: a broad CRM aimed at connected business operations

1CRM describes itself as an all-in-one CRM and business-management system. Its feature pages and edition comparison cover sales opportunities, marketing, orders, inventory, projects, tasks and customer service, alongside configurable reports and integrations. The appeal is consolidation: a team can evaluate whether customer records and the work around them belong in one system instead of scattered spreadsheets and separate apps. That breadth can be useful when sales, fulfillment and support need to see the same account history.

Breadth also makes implementation more consequential. Before a **1CRM review** turns into a purchase decision, map one real process from lead capture through a closed sale, delivery and support. Identify who owns each record, which fields are mandatory, what happens when a deal is lost, and which teams may see customer data. The system should be evaluated against those responsibilities rather than against a feature checklist alone. A menu item for a module does not by itself show that the team’s specific workflow is configured correctly.


## 1CRM pricing and edition limits

The public editions page lists four subscription editions in US dollars and shows both annual and month-to-month rates. As displayed when reviewed, Startup is listed at $13 per user each month on annual billing or $15 month-to-month, with up to three users, 300 accounts and 750 leads, contacts or targets. Startup+ lists $20 annually or $23 month-to-month, with ceilings of ten users, 600 accounts and 1,500 leads, contacts or targets. Professional and Enterprise show higher per-user rates and no stated user or record ceilings on that comparison. The same page describes a 30-day trial without a credit card.

These figures are vendor-published, may change and do not include every implementation, migration or operating cost. The page also distinguishes cloud subscriptions from on-premise licenses and describes support and update terms differently for those offers. Confirm the exact price, billing interval, taxes, storage, user count, record caps, renewal and support scope in a current written quote. A **1CRM pricing** comparison should account for the point at which a small Startup account reaches its limits, not just the initial per-seat number.


## 1CRM cloud or on-premise: choose who will operate the system

1CRM offers a hosted service as well as on-premise editions. A hosted deployment reduces the work of installing and maintaining the application server, while the vendor page says cloud customers can choose a host country. An on-premise installation gives the organization more direct responsibility for the server, updates, backups, availability and recovery. The page also describes a free on-premise Startup edition but notes that this version cannot receive product updates as it evolves; that limitation should be weighed before putting live customer data into it.

Ask the administrator to demonstrate a backup and restore using a test system before migration. Confirm how a move between cloud and on-premise is handled, what assistance costs, where each production and backup copy resides, and how version updates will be applied. The cheapest license can become a poor deal if nobody owns patching or a restore is untested. Conversely, a cloud plan may be worthwhile when predictable operations and included support matter more than direct server control.


## CRM data migration, fields and day-to-day administration

A CRM implementation starts with the quality of the records being imported. Deduplicate contacts, agree on account ownership, normalize lifecycle stages and decide how historical activities will be carried forward. 1CRM’s implementation material describes importing and exporting information and configuring company-specific settings, while the knowledge base provides guides for administrators and users. That is a useful starting point, but a migration still needs a field map and a repeatable reconciliation between the source system and the imported records.

Run a small pilot with representative records, including a company with multiple contacts, a stale lead, an open opportunity and an existing support case. Have staff verify that the right relationships, notes and permissions survived. Then compare record totals and high-value fields before cutover. If a vendor or consultant is quoting implementation work, request a written list of included imports, custom fields, training sessions, integrations and post-launch support so the project does not expand invisibly.


## 1CRM customization, roles and integrations

The feature matrix describes customization and configuration, access through roles and teams, workflow automation, scheduled reports and a REST API in its editions. Those capabilities may let an organization align the system with its own terminology and handoffs. They also increase the number of decisions a system administrator must document. A highly customized CRM can be difficult to upgrade or maintain when the original implementer leaves unless the organization keeps a clear record of why each field, automation and integration exists.

Test the permission model with actual user roles before connecting the full staff. Verify which people can export a contact list, edit customer ownership, view financial records or trigger a mass email. For every integration, document what data leaves the CRM, whose credentials authorize it and how access can be revoked. Confirm the edition includes each connection you need; a row in a comparison table or an integration name should not be mistaken for confirmation that a specific third-party configuration is included in your quote.


## 1CRM privacy and customer-data responsibilities

The 1CRM privacy policy distinguishes information about account users from customer information stored by a client. It states that the client is responsible for giving its own customers notice about the purpose and handling of their records, and describes service providers, cookies, analytics, deletion requests and transfers among several hosting regions. The publicly available policy page is dated May 1, 2018, so a buyer should ask whether a newer contractual privacy or data-processing document applies to the proposed service and whether the published policy has been superseded.

Location matters for a company with legal or contractual residency requirements. The privacy page describes hosting across the United States, Canada, Singapore, Australia and the European Union, with EU customer databases in the Netherlands, while also noting that service providers may process data in other regions. Ask for the current subprocessor list, hosting location, retention and deletion behavior, breach-notification commitments and any available data-processing agreement. Do not infer that choosing a database region alone keeps every integration or support process within that jurisdiction.


## 1CRM support, backups and continuity planning

The pricing page includes support and updates for subscription editions and specifies a first-year support and update requirement for the listed perpetual licenses, with optional support afterward. The product also provides backup management and vendor guidance for storing copies in locations such as a controlled remote server. The useful buyer question is whether the team understands the restore process and can meet its recovery-time needs, rather than whether a backup setting merely exists.

Ask support to clarify response targets for the plan under consideration, how urgent service issues are handled, what migration assistance costs and how data is returned if the contract ends. Keep a backup under an organization-controlled account and test restoring it on an isolated instance. If backups depend on a Dropbox or other external integration, verify the correct organization account is connected and that access remains available when an employee departs.


## Who should compare 1CRM with other CRM systems

1CRM may be worth evaluating for a small or midsize company that wants sales records and operational modules together, or for a business that has a genuine reason to self-host. It may be a less natural fit for a team that wants a very simple sales pipeline, has strict cloud requirements that are not covered by the available plan, or depends on a specialized ecosystem whose integrations need to be verified individually. Migration work and administration should be included in any cost comparison.

Compare the same sample process in 1CRM and at least two alternatives. Count the steps needed to qualify a lead, create a quote, assign fulfillment and resolve a support request. Check exports, permissions, mobile workflows, API limits, support and the effort required to reverse a configuration decision. A side-by-side trial with real staff is more useful than an “all-in-one” claim or a vendor’s static feature matrix.


## 1CRM scam review verdict: verify the edition and deployment obligations

The official site describes a substantive CRM product, publishes an editions-and-pricing comparison, and provides implementation, privacy, terms and backup materials. The available sources show deployment choices and record limits that a buyer can verify directly. They do not establish that **1CRM is a scam**. The most significant diligence points are the edition’s user and record caps, the difference between hosted and self-managed operation, the age of the posted privacy policy, and the practical cost of configuration and data migration.

This is a source-based desk review, not a hands-on product trial, security audit or endorsement. Confirm current prices and support terms in writing, test a representative workflow, obtain current privacy documents, and prove a restore before making 1CRM the system of record. If those checks match your requirements, the platform may merit a controlled pilot; if they do not, compare another CRM before migrating customer data.
