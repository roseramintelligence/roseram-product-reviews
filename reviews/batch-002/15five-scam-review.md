# 15Five review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-17  
**Category:** Employee engagement and performance management  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

15Five is a subscription platform for employee engagement, performance management, goals, feedback and manager development. Its public sources describe tiered plans and AI features that can process workplace feedback and review material.

## Editorial verdict

The reviewed primary sources describe a real HR software service and do not establish that 15Five is a scam. The decision deserves careful review because employee feedback, performance records and AI-assisted summaries can affect trust and sensitive workplace decisions.

## 15Five scam review: what the evidence can and cannot answer

A search for “15Five scam review” can begin with a fair but important distinction: a product being expensive, difficult to implement, unpopular with an employee, or unsuitable for a company is not by itself evidence of fraud. This review checks the service’s own product, pricing, privacy, security and AI disclosures. Those materials describe a commercial platform for employee engagement and performance management, along with paid plan levels and support documentation. They do not establish that 15Five is a scam.

The word “scam” is still useful as a buyer’s question when it is translated into verifiable checks. Is there an identifiable vendor? Are the advertised features and prices described? What data is collected? What commitments bind the employer and service provider? Can an administrator export or remove data? The sources reviewed here provide answers to several of those questions, while leaving account-specific details to a proposal, contract, DPA and implementation plan.

This is an independent desk review of publicly available material, not a paid 15Five account, product trial, employee survey, security audit or legal opinion. It does not measure customer outcomes or validate vendor marketing claims. Pricing and product capabilities can change. The most useful conclusion is practical: examine the terms and data flows for the exact plan before introducing performance software into a workplace, and do not treat a marketing score or AI summary as a decision about an employee.


## What 15Five is designed to do for an organization

15Five describes its platform as a set of tools for engagement, performance and manager effectiveness. The public overview presents employee check-ins and 1-on-1 agendas, feedback and recognition, AI-assisted reviews, goals, engagement surveys and manager learning. The pricing page groups capabilities into Engage, Perform and Total Platform. Engage focuses on surveys and engagement analysis; Perform adds performance management features; Total Platform combines the two and includes manager training microlearning. The exact matrix should be checked at purchase time because plan boundaries matter.

The proposed operating loop is familiar to HR teams: gather employee signals, make them visible to managers, schedule conversations, set objectives, request feedback and revisit progress. A recurring check-in can help a manager notice a problem sooner than an annual review would. But software does not make a manager attentive, create a fair evaluation rubric or guarantee that staff feel safe enough to answer honestly. Those outcomes depend on policies, training and how leaders act after information is collected.

The vendor says its platform is used by more than 3,000 companies on the product page. Treat that as a company-published statement, not an independently verified adoption statistic or proof that a particular customer had a successful deployment. For a buying committee, the more informative evidence is whether the workflow matches the organization’s review calendar, whether employees understand who sees each response, and whether reporting helps leaders act without turning context-dependent feedback into a simplistic ranking.


## 15Five pricing: annual per-user list prices and what to confirm

On the public pricing page checked November 17, 2026, 15Five lists Engage at $4 per user per month, Perform at $11, and Total Platform at $16; each is described as billed annually. The page presents compensation as a separate product area with its own pricing, so the three figures above should not be read as a complete quote for every HR product or add-on. The public list makes a useful starting comparison, but it is not a substitute for the order form.

Before approving a budget, ask whether the displayed per-user rate applies to every seat, what minimum annual commitment is required, which employees count as billable, and whether implementation, support or integration fees are quoted separately. Confirm whether the selected plan includes the exact survey, review, goal, analytics and manager features the organization intends to use. Ask how seat additions and removals affect the term and whether the stated price is promotional or tied to a particular contract length.

An annual, per-user platform can be poor value when rollout is limited to a small pilot but the contract covers the entire workforce. Conversely, using only a low-tier survey plan while separately buying overlapping performance or learning tools can obscure total cost. Build a one-year and renewal-year model with expected headcount, plan mix, add-ons, service fees and the internal time required to administer the workflow. Obtain a written renewal and cancellation schedule rather than inferring it from a pricing card.


## AI-assisted reviews need a separate employee-data assessment

15Five’s AI handling article says AI is used in features including Kona Notetaker, AI-Assisted Reviews, Amaya and 15Five Agents. Its article about data submitted to a sub-processor says review resources such as High Fives, wins and challenges, self-reviews and peer reviews may be sent to an AI model and summarized into Strengths and Opportunities. If a manager asks the model to draft or improve an open-text response, the article says objectives and 1-on-1 data are also included. That is sensitive employment context, not generic text.

The same article says data is sent from 15Five’s application server to the model sub-processor within the United States, the sub-processor does not retain data after responding, and customer data is not used to train the model. The broader AI handling page says 15Five does not permit model providers to train on customer data, and identifies Anthropic, OpenAI and Pinecone as sub-processors in the July 2026 listing. These are vendor commitments; an organization should confirm the current sub-processor list, applicable contract language and enabled feature settings for its own account.

AI-generated review language can make an incomplete record sound more certain than it is. A manager should verify every statement against documented work, remove irrelevant personal detail and let the employee correct factual errors. Do not use an automatically synthesized strength, engagement theme or coaching tip as the sole basis for compensation, promotion, discipline or termination. Ask how generated content is labeled, who can edit it, whether it is stored in a formal employee record and how an employee can challenge a mistaken summary.


## Privacy, retention and access boundaries in HR workflows

The 15Five privacy policy says the service is designed for employee engagement and performance management and applies to its web and mobile materials. Its AI information page distinguishes retention by feature: Kona transcripts are said to be retained for 30 days and then deleted; 1-on-1 notes are kept for the account lifetime unless the account is sanitized after deactivation or a participant’s account is PII-deleted; platform records such as reviews, check-ins, objectives and surveys are governed by the DPA. Those different rules make a generic “how long is our data kept?” answer insufficient.

Ask for a data inventory that maps each workflow to the relevant record, its visibility, retention period and deletion path. Engagement surveys may include open text that identifies an employee even when a dashboard presents aggregate results. Review comments may contain health, family or accommodation details that were never intended for broad circulation. Define who can see raw responses, when aggregate thresholds apply, which managers can access notes and how former employees’ records are handled. The customer’s configuration and internal policy are part of the privacy outcome.

A people platform can support better decisions, but measuring engagement can also change behavior. If employees believe a survey is anonymous when it is attributable, trust can be damaged. If a response may be used for performance decisions, explain that clearly before collection. Have HR and privacy owners review the exact configuration and employee notice. For cross-border teams, identify processing locations and transfer terms in the DPA and confirm the service arrangement meets the company’s obligations.


## Security documentation and what a buyer should request

The security page describes TLS protection for communications, policy-level encryption for stored data, hashed passwords and need-to-know employee access. It also says payment card data is handled by Stripe rather than stored or transmitted by 15Five, and that the company uses data centers in the United States. The page describes independent SOC 2 framework auditing, vulnerability scans and periodic external penetration testing; the current AI handling article says the 2026 SOC 2 Type II and SOC 3 scope includes AI functionality. These are vendor statements, not a substitute for the actual assessment report.

A prospective customer can request access to the SOC 2 report through 15Five’s Trust Center, according to its security help page. Ask for the report’s period, auditor, scope, exceptions and subservice organization treatment. Compare the report to the features and region in the proposed agreement. Review the DPA for breach notification, subprocessors, deletion, assistance obligations and transfer mechanisms. If a requirement is not addressed by public sources, put it in the procurement questionnaire and get a written answer before employee data is loaded.

Security controls do not prevent every account compromise or misuse of legitimate access. Decide how single sign-on, two-step authentication, admin roles, user provisioning and offboarding will work. Use a test account to check how access changes when someone moves teams or leaves the company. Limit bulk exports, record who can retrieve identifiable employee information and set a review cycle for administrator privileges. The vendor’s described security program is relevant evidence; the employer still has responsibility for identity, device security, configuration and response.


## Implementation, integrations and the manager workload

Implementation should begin with the question the company wants the platform to help answer. A team might need a better 1-on-1 rhythm, a consistent performance review, a way to follow up on engagement feedback or manager training. Select one workflow, define its owner and pilot it with a representative team. Document the existing process first so the organization can see whether the software reduces missed follow-ups or merely transfers work into another dashboard.

The public pricing comparison mentions HRIS integrations and multi-channel notifications in plan tables, but does not establish that every connector is available in every package. Ask which HRIS, identity, collaboration and payroll integrations are supported for the proposed account and whether any require additional fees or configuration. Confirm what data moves in each direction, whether field mappings are editable and how a failed sync is surfaced. Test a transfer with a small set of non-sensitive records before importing the full employee directory.

Manager adoption is the hidden operating cost. Employees may receive more surveys, check-ins and review requests than leaders have time to address. Set a cadence that matches the organization’s capacity to respond. Train managers to write behavior-based feedback, explain access and avoid using the platform as a substitute for difficult conversations. Track completion and follow-through, not just how many forms were submitted. If a manager is rewarded for response rate alone, people can learn to optimize the metric instead of improving the workplace.


## Alternatives and a fair way to compare HR software

Alternatives include HR suites with built-in performance modules, dedicated performance and engagement platforms, and simpler combinations of forms, survey software and a documented manager process. The right comparison depends on whether a company needs an integrated employee record, frequent feedback, compensation workflows, succession planning, learning content or a lightweight review cycle. 15Five’s own pricing and product pages emphasize engagement and performance features; buyers should compare those workflows to actual requirements rather than count every item in a feature matrix.

Use a scripted evaluation with the same sample team, review cycle and privacy scenario for each vendor. Ask each product to show how an employee completes a self-review, how a manager requests peer feedback, how HR views a trend without exposing an individual response, how an AI draft is identified, and how records are exported or deleted. Have an employee representative and an HR operations owner participate. Record what the vendor demonstrates, what is only promised, and what must be configured or purchased separately.

Compare the full lifecycle cost: per-user license, implementation, integrations, admin time, manager training and the effort to migrate historical reviews. Ask for references from organizations with similar size, geography and review maturity, then verify that the reference describes the same product and plan. A mature suite can be worth the cost when it replaces several disconnected processes. A smaller team may be better served by a less formal tool until it has the managerial capacity and policies to use a performance system responsibly.


## 15Five scam review: buyer conclusion and evidence limits

The reviewed primary sources identify 15Five as an employee engagement and performance management provider, publish annual per-user plan prices, describe product capabilities, and provide privacy, security, DPA and AI data handling material. The evidence checked here does not establish that 15Five is a scam. It does show why the purchase deserves careful scrutiny: the platform can handle identifiable workplace feedback and review content, and some AI features can process performance-related information. The buyer should evaluate those flows as part of HR governance, not as a routine productivity app rollout.

This conclusion is a source-based desk review, not a firsthand trial, independent audit, labor-law analysis or measurement of employee outcomes. Public pricing was checked on November 17, 2026; the listed prices are annual per-user rates and the final quote may depend on scope, headcount, add-ons and negotiated terms. Vendor descriptions of security, AI protections and customer adoption should be checked against the current contract, Trust Center evidence and implementation configuration.

For a company that wants structured check-ins, performance reviews, engagement surveys and manager enablement, 15Five may merit a controlled evaluation. Proceed only after confirming the plan, renewal terms, records and AI data flows; assigning ownership for access and retention; and agreeing how employees can understand and challenge the use of their feedback. If leadership cannot explain who sees a response, how an AI summary is reviewed or what happens when an employee leaves, pause the rollout until those policies are clear.


## Features and decision criteria

- Employee engagement surveys and action planning
- Performance reviews, goals and continuous feedback
- AI-assisted reviews and manager support features

Public list prices observed on November 17, 2026. Confirm currency, minimum seats, included features, add-ons, implementation fees and renewal terms in the current order form; annual billing is stated on the pricing page.
