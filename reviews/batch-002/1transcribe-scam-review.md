# 1Transcribe review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-17  
**Category:** Speech transcription software  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

1Transcribe combines audio and video transcription, meeting recording, desktop dictation and file-based text extraction. Its official pages describe a five-minute free preview, paid plans, a 14-day source-file retention period and persistent saved transcripts, but the free export wording differs between pricing and help pages.

## Editorial verdict

The current product and legal pages describe a real subscription transcription service with explicit recording-consent obligations and useful data-retention details. Buyers should clarify the free export inconsistency, verify the processor and billing flow, and treat machine transcripts as drafts that require checking against the recording.

## 1Transcribe review: transcription, meeting capture and dictation in one account

1Transcribe describes a service that turns uploaded audio and video into text, extracts text from PDFs and images, records meetings and supports dictation across desktop and mobile workflows. The pricing page presents one subscription for the file, meeting and dictation features, with speaker labels, AI summaries, quizzes and common document or subtitle exports on paid plans. That scope may be convenient for a journalist, researcher, student or team that handles several kinds of spoken material.

The right **1Transcribe review** must distinguish a broad workflow from a guarantee that the transcript is accurate. The service's own help page warns that overlapping speech, background noise, proper names and technical language can produce errors. A speaker label can be assigned to the wrong person, a number can be misheard and a summary can omit a qualification. Use generated text as a draft and verify important quotations, decisions, deadlines and legal or medical details against the recording.


## Free limits and conflicting export descriptions

The official pricing page currently lists the first five minutes of every file and ten free dictations, with a paid upgrade for full-length jobs. It includes export formats in the free-plan bullet. The help page, however, says a free user can read the transcript on the page, while copying, downloading and transcribing the rest of a longer file are part of a paid plan. The site's dedicated audio page also describes the first five minutes as a free preview. Those descriptions do not clearly agree about free export access.

This is a practical point to resolve before relying on the free tier for a deliverable. Upload a short, non-sensitive sample and check whether the expected format is actually available without payment; if not, ask support to confirm the current plan behavior. Do not assume the more generous wording applies to every platform or region. The discrepancy is a documentation issue to clarify, not evidence by itself of a deceptive charge. Review the checkout screen for the selected plan and retain the displayed terms.


## File limits, pricing and automatic renewal

The pricing page lists the paid monthly plan at US$19.99 and the yearly plan at US$49.99, with the same core features and a claimed saving against monthly billing. It describes paid uploads up to five gigabytes and ten hours per file, plus unlimited transcription minutes. The free option permits a short preview and a fixed number of dictations. The page says these are standard US dollar prices and that regional offers may differ, with the checkout confirming the amount and billing terms.

Paid plans renew automatically until canceled. The legal page asks subscribers to cancel at least 24 hours before renewal and says no refund is issued for an uncanceled subscription; the help page says cancellation happens through the billing provider and does not itself refund a past payment. Check whether Apple, Google, the website or another provider handles the transaction, because the cancellation steps and refund rules may depend on that channel. Save the receipt, renewal date and plan length at purchase.


## What happens to recordings, transcripts and account data

The legal page says saved audio and video uploads are removed from 1Transcribe's servers after fourteen days, while the resulting transcription text remains in the account until the user deletes it. It describes real-time and Chrome dictation as ephemeral rather than saved as account transcription records, and says PDF and image uploads follow the same general retention policy as audio and video. The help page summarizes the source-file and transcript distinction in similar terms. These distinctions matter: deleting a source recording automatically does not mean the transcript also disappears.

The privacy policy lists account identifiers, uploaded content, dictation, saved transcript text, preferences and technical usage information. It identifies AWS hosting in the US region, Cloudflare for website delivery and Firebase among service providers, while referring more generally to transcription partners. Account deletion removes the main profile, transcripts and uploaded files, but the policy says limited creation metadata—including the account creation date, the creation IP address and a user ID—is retained. An organization handling sensitive interviews should confirm which processor handles its content, how to request deletion and what evidence confirms the deletion.


## Third-party transcription processing and model-training statements

1Transcribe says uploaded recordings and selected content are processed through transcription partners to produce the requested text. The legal page says those partners receive necessary content and are contractually bound to confidentiality and data-protection obligations. Its marketing pages state that recordings are processed to create the user's text rather than improve another model. Those are useful disclosures, but the public policy materials reviewed do not identify each transcription partner by name or describe every provider's model-retention setting in detail.

Before submitting confidential audio, ask for the current subprocessor list, data-processing agreement, geographic processing locations and the deletion schedule applied by each partner. A meeting recording can include names, voices, negotiations and information about people who never opened an account. Remove unnecessary content, limit access to the saved transcript and use the service only when the organization has a valid basis to record and process that conversation. Do not infer that a 14-day server-file deletion rule automatically covers all derived data or third-party logs unless the contract says so.


## Meeting recording consent is the user's responsibility

The meeting feature is designed to start and stop recording from the application, including meetings already in progress. The Terms place responsibility on the user to obtain all required permissions and comply with recording and consent laws. They also include an indemnity related to unauthorized recording and disclaim liability for consequences arising from use of the feature. The help page likewise tells users to ask participants for permission before recording. A tool's ability to capture system audio does not provide legal consent or tell participants that recording is underway.

Set a consistent meeting practice before recording: announce the purpose, obtain consent where required, respect a participant's refusal, and make the recording indicator and retention period clear. For interviews or regulated information, confirm internal policy and the jurisdiction's rules. Store the transcript only with the people who need it and remove it when the retention purpose ends. A recorder without a visible meeting bot may be convenient, but it can also make disclosure and consent especially important because other attendees may not see an extra participant in the call.


## Transcript quality, speaker labels and responsible use

The service advertises speaker labels, summaries, quizzes and transcription across multiple file types. These can reduce manual effort, but they do not remove review work. Speaker diarization may confuse similar voices or interruptions; a summary may flatten a disagreement; subtitles may drift from the spoken timing; an OCR pass over a PDF or image may miss layout context. The product's own help materials recommend checking the transcript, particularly names, technical terms and overlapping conversation.

For a transcript that will be quoted, publish only text checked against the source. For an action list, confirm who owns each action and whether the meeting actually assigned a deadline. For research, preserve the original file until the project's retention policy permits deletion and record any corrections. For subtitles, review timing and spelling before release. These are ordinary quality controls for automated transcription, and they prevent a plausible-sounding output from being mistaken for a verbatim record.


## Refund boundaries and account support

The Terms say subscription fees are nonrefundable except where a technical issue prevents the application from working as intended and cannot be resolved within a reasonable time. They also say accidental purchases and unused portions after cancellation are not refunded. The help page directs customers to the billing provider for cancellation and says cancellation stops future renewals but does not automatically refund an earlier charge. The public pricing page notes that the checkout confirms regional billing details.

That means a user should test the free workflow before selecting an annual plan, verify which account or app-store payment route is used and save evidence of a technical problem promptly. If a plan was bought through an app store, the platform's refund process may apply; if purchased on the website, contact the listed support channel under the service terms. Clarify whether a file rejected for size, language or format is considered a technical failure under the refund terms. The policy is strict, but it is visible before purchase and can be evaluated in advance.


## Who benefits from 1Transcribe and who should compare alternatives

The product may fit an individual who needs file transcription and dictation across several devices, has manageable audio volume and can review the result. A short free preview provides a way to check a representative recording before subscribing. The annual price is substantially below twelve monthly payments as displayed, but it requires committing to a full year and confidence that the workflow will be used. A buyer should compare language support, speaker labeling, export options, processing location, retention and mobile or desktop behavior against other services.

A team with strict confidentiality needs, formal audit requirements or a need to retain recordings inside its own infrastructure should ask for more detail before adopting a hosted transcription service. Anyone who needs a fully human-verified transcript should budget for review or a professional transcription service rather than rely on automation alone. The tool's user should also ensure that a subscription is appropriate for the actual time saved and not simply an attractive 'unlimited' headline; file-size caps, language support and review time still shape the total cost.


## 1Transcribe verdict: a useful workflow with important retention and billing details

1Transcribe's official pages describe a multi-device transcription and dictation product with a free short-file preview, transparent monthly and yearly prices, clear fourteen-day source-file deletion and persistent user-controlled transcript storage. The legal documents explain that third-party processing occurs and place recording-consent responsibility on the user. The help page candidly notes that machine output needs correction, which is the right way to set expectations for consequential content.

The buyer should clarify the free export inconsistency, understand auto-renewal and strict refund rules, identify the transcription partners and separate source-file deletion from transcript deletion. This is a source-based desk review, not a hands-on accuracy benchmark, legal opinion or security audit. The available evidence does not establish that 1Transcribe is a scam. Test with non-sensitive audio, check the transcript against the original, secure consent before recording and confirm the payment platform and data terms before putting confidential material into the service.

## Pricing considerations

The official pricing page lists the first five minutes of each file and ten dictations free, a monthly plan at US$19.99 and a yearly plan at US$49.99. Paid features include unlimited transcription minutes and files up to 5 GB or ten hours. Plans auto-renew until canceled; regional checkout prices may differ. The free export entitlement is inconsistent between pricing and help pages and should be confirmed before use.
