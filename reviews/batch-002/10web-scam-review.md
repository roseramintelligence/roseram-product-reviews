# 10Web review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-17  
**Category:** AI website builder and managed WordPress platform  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

10Web offers an AI-assisted platform for creating and managing WordPress websites, stores and apps, with managed hosting, an editor, SEO tools, project and team management, and agency plans. Its current pricing page offers a free tier and paid tiers with project limits, monthly AI-credit allowances, hosting resources and transaction-fee differences for its own payment service. The platform is broad enough that a buyer should evaluate the builder, hosting, AI actions and ecommerce features separately. Pricing and plan details were checked on November 17, 2026; feature caps, local prices and promotions may change.

## Editorial verdict

The reviewed pages identify TenWeb, Inc., explain the product and plans, and publish terms and privacy disclosures. The available evidence does not support calling 10Web a scam. Material diligence points are monthly AI-credit limits and non-rollover, ecommerce transaction fees in addition to payment processing, possible project deletion after trial, inactivity or cancellation, plan-specific refund conditions, and the age and breadth of the published privacy and user-content terms. 10Web’s terms explicitly warn that generated code and agent actions may contain errors or security vulnerabilities and can change live projects. No account, website migration, checkout, AI generation or security control was independently tested.

## 10Web review: an AI builder attached to WordPress hosting

10Web combines a website-building interface with a hosted WordPress environment. Its current pages describe AI generation and editing for websites, online stores and apps, followed by a visual editor, a WordPress backend and hosting. The same product family includes SEO, forms, booking, store management, migrations and agency-oriented project administration. A buyer is therefore choosing more than an AI page generator: the ongoing hosting and account lifecycle are part of the decision.

The distinction matters when comparing alternatives. A prompt-based prototype can be quick to create, but a production site still needs content review, domain and email setup, payment configuration, accessibility work, backups and ongoing WordPress maintenance. 10Web’s product may reduce setup work for a team comfortable with WordPress; it does not make the team’s operational obligations disappear.


## AI credits, editing workflow and generated-code responsibility

The pricing page assigns a monthly AI-credit allowance to paid plans and explains that generating or changing a project consumes credits. It lists 15 credits on Free, 100 on AI Starter, 200 on AI Premium and 300 on AI Ultimate, with separate top-ups and no rollover of unused monthly credits. The precise cost of an action can vary, so a buyer should watch the credit balance while trying multiple page generations or substantial redesigns rather than treating an allowance as a count of finished websites.

The Terms of Service say AI agents may edit pages, install or configure plugins, connect integrations, migrate content or generate code at a user’s request. They warn generated code can contain errors, security vulnerabilities or incompatibilities, that some agent actions may affect a live project and may not be reversible, and that output is supplied as-is. Use a staging copy, take a verified backup, review code and plugin changes, and test payment, authentication, forms and responsive behavior before publishing. Those are the provider’s stated limits, not a finding that a specific generated site is defective.


## Hosting, WordPress, ecommerce and plan fit

The published plans vary by number of projects, AI credits, storage and visitor allowances. The page lists WordPress as the backend across plans, and it describes managed hosting, SSL, backups and project tools. Higher tiers introduce larger resources or additional services such as CDN coverage, agency migration support and branded dashboards. The pricing grid is the right place to confirm current limits for a specific workload; marketing phrases such as “faster” or “enterprise-grade” are vendor claims and should not be read as a measured performance guarantee for a particular website.

For stores, the platform advertises product and order management, checkout and 10Web Payments. The same price page shows a 7% fee on 10Web Payments at Free and 2% on AI Starter, with 0% on Premium and Ultimate; Stripe processing fees still apply. Do a real fee comparison against a direct payment processor and account for plan price, transaction volume, hosting and any paid extras. A lower platform percentage can be outweighed by the subscription cost for a small store, while higher-volume sellers should model costs at their own sales level.


## Free plan, subscription renewal and refund details

The pricing page currently advertises a free plan at $0 with one project, 15 AI credits and a 10web.site address. It says no card is needed. Paid prices captured on November 17, 2026 with annual billing selected were $10 per month for AI Starter, $15 for AI Premium and $22.50 for AI Ultimate; the page also displays Agency Starter at $42.50 and Agency Core at $80 per month. These are annual-plan monthly equivalents, not a statement that monthly billing costs the same. The page says local prices can vary by country and billing interval.

The pricing page advertises a 30-day money-back guarantee and says it ends when the included domain is claimed. The terms frame subscription refunds as plan-specific and otherwise generally final, so preserve the checkout plan terms and verify the refund route before purchase. There is also a documentation mismatch: the current pricing page describes Free as forever with no card, while an older Help Center article still describes a three-day trial whose site is deleted without an upgrade. Separately, the terms reserve the right to deactivate or delete free projects considered inactive or abandoned. Confirm the plan and project status shown in the live dashboard, and keep an independent backup.


## Privacy and security disclosures a business should review

The privacy policy describes account and profile data, purchase history, site URLs and content used by certain services, support records and third-party infrastructure. It says payment-card data is sent to Stripe rather than stored directly by 10Web. For the AI co-pilot, it describes possible recordings of website changes for troubleshooting and service improvement, with a stated one-month retention period and an option to opt out before use. The published policy page itself says it was last updated March 21, 2024, so an organization evaluating current AI, hosting or migration functions should check for a newer policy and current processor list instead of assuming that the page fully reflects every later product change.

The Terms of Service place responsibility on the user for credentials and third-party connections. They also contain broad wording about user submissions and grant a wide licence to content supplied through the platform. That wording deserves a careful read before entering confidential customer information, proprietary designs or client assets. This review does not determine the clause’s legal scope or enforceability. Ask for the current data-processing agreement and verify retention, deletion, subprocessors, project exports and recovery windows against the organization’s requirements.


## Migration, portability and alternatives

A migration claim should be tested on a non-production copy. Check theme and plugin compatibility, media libraries, permalinks, forms, payment settings, redirects, user accounts and email delivery. Export a WordPress backup and confirm that it can be restored outside the managed dashboard. A builder may accelerate the move, but the important business question is whether the resulting site remains portable and maintainable when the subscription ends or a plan changes.

Alternatives include self-hosted WordPress with a separate hosting provider, managed WordPress hosts, and hosted builders such as Wix or Squarespace. Compare control of the underlying site, access to plugins and code, backup restoration, domains, e-commerce fees, AI-credit budgets, team permissions, support, and the cost of maintaining the site after the initial build. The best fit depends on whether the buyer prioritizes control, ease of use, integrated hosting or agency operations.


## Who should consider 10Web and what to confirm first

10Web may suit a freelancer or small business that wants AI-assisted creation, a WordPress backend and managed hosting under one account. An agency may value project management, migrations and white-label tools. It may be a poorer fit for teams that need a fully self-hosted installation, do not want a WordPress dependency, require a formal security certification that the vendor cannot provide, or cannot accept a credit-metered AI workflow.

Before subscribing, build one representative page, track the credits used, test a form and store flow, restore a backup, and confirm that the site can be exported in the way your team needs. Verify billing interval and transaction fee at checkout. For a client or regulated site, review the current privacy policy, data-processing terms and security evidence with the owner of the data.


## Is 10Web a scam? Evidence-based conclusion

The official material reviewed identifies TenWeb, Inc., describes a functioning suite of WordPress and AI-builder products, publishes current plan details, and discloses important limits around credits, project handling, generated code and privacy. The evidence does not substantiate calling 10Web a scam. The realistic questions are whether its AI and WordPress workflow fits the buyer, whether the total cost and limits are clear, and whether its contractual, privacy and project-retention terms satisfy the customer’s needs.

This is a source-based desk review, not a paid account, code audit, benchmark, migration or checkout test. Prices and product limits were checked on November 17, 2026 and may change. Confirm the current billing schedule and refund eligibility, keep independent backups, review any generated code before release, and verify current data terms before moving client information into the platform.

- 10Web Payments checkout with plan-specific platform transaction fees plus processor fees
