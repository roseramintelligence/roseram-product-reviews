## 17hats features: lead capture, projects, documents and follow-up

The product combines a contact database with a project-centered workflow. The help center describes a project as the hub for client documents, forms, quotes, invoices, contracts, emails, files, events and tasks. Workflows are reusable sequences of to-dos, pauses and actions; actions may send a quote, contract, invoice, questionnaire or email, or start another workflow. A service provider can therefore map a repeatable path from inquiry to booking and onboarding without rebuilding each email manually.

The combined-document feature can present a quote, contract and invoice in a single client sequence. The help article says the client sees the quote first, accepts it, then signs the attached contract before the invoice becomes available. It also warns that editing attached contract or invoice details after a quote is accepted can require the client to accept the quote again and re-sign. That sequence is useful for reducing back-and-forth, but it makes template review and change control important. Test the complete client-facing flow before sending a live contract.

The client portal can let a customer view and complete sent quotes, contracts, questionnaires and invoices, subject to the feature being included in the account plan. Email correspondence remains in the internal project page rather than automatically appearing in that portal. This boundary matters when staff assume that “everything is in the portal.” Decide which documents clients should see, which remain internal, and how uploaded materials differ from 17hats-created documents. Use one sample project to confirm the actual view before inviting customers.


## 17hats pricing: trial, annual commitments and add-on costs

The public pricing page checked November 17, 2026 lists $60 per month, $600 per year or $800 for two years, and states that prices are in U.S. dollars. It advertises a seven-day free trial and a promotion to skip the trial for a first-year discount. The page also says Bank Connect costs an additional $5 per month and that extra users and brands may be purchased. These are the amounts displayed on the public page, not a guaranteed quote or proof that every customer qualifies for a promotion.

A separate feature section on the pricing page describes several packages and different document limits, so buyers should confirm which current plan is actually selected at checkout and whether the feature matrix applies to their account. Verify whether document caps, additional-user access, extra brands, workflow automation and client portal access are included. A small operator could find the advertised total changes if the business needs multiple brands, extra logins, bank feeds or more documents than its tier allows.

Compare annual savings with the risk of paying before the service is integrated into the business. Use the trial to send a test quote, contract and invoice to a personal test contact, check mobile rendering, collect a test payment if available, and confirm that the payment processor and bank integration behave as expected. Record the renewal date and the path for cancellation. Do not let a “save” badge replace a written total-cost estimate that includes tax, add-ons and any payment-processing fees.


## Cancellation, renewals and keeping access to business records

The terms say subscriptions renew automatically at the end of the selected monthly, annual, biannual or other billing cycle and direct members to account billing information for the renewal date. The help-center cancellation article says a phone call, email or direct chat request does not itself count as cancellation; the account owner must use the cancel-account control in Billing Info. It also says cancellation before the paid term ends does not create a prorated refund, while the account remains active until that term expires.

The privacy policy and cancellation article say content may be retained for at least one month after access ends and that, after 45 days, 17hats may delete content at its discretion. They also say content is no longer accessible after the active subscription ends and recommend exporting reports, contact lists and important documents beforehand. That has direct operational consequences for a freelancer who keeps their only copy of client contracts, invoices or tax records inside the service.

Before canceling, export client and project records, invoices, reports, templates, contact lists and files; then check that the exports open and include the information you need. Save copies in a controlled system with appropriate access and backup. The terms say data deleted inside an active account becomes immediately inaccessible and cannot be restored. For an orderly exit, assign a person to check the export, reconcile outstanding invoices and preserve records needed for bookkeeping before the billing term ends.


## Privacy and financial integrations require a close read

The privacy policy describes collection of account details, device and browser information, site activity, cookies and email interaction signals. It names providers for hosting and metrics, payment processing, messaging, bank integrations, customer support and analytics. For bank connections, the policy says a third-party provider may access and transmit personal and financial information from the relevant financial institution under that provider’s own privacy policy. A user should understand the exact connection vendor, requested permissions and account data fields before linking a business bank account.

The policy says the database, including stored content, is encrypted with AES-256 and describes a TLS 1.2 connection. It also lists the possibility of financial, customer, invoice, contract and communication information in the platform. The terms include a broad statement that technical processing and transmission of content may involve transfers over networks and may be unencrypted. Those texts should be reconciled with the current implementation and contract before sensitive client records are placed in the system. Ask 17hats whether that general transmission clause is current boilerplate and what encryption controls apply to documents in transit and at rest.

The privacy policy’s listed subprocessors and technical details show a last-update date of October 31, 2024. Because vendors and configurations change, request the current subprocessor list and current security documentation rather than assuming the old list is complete. Verify how client data is separated, who can access it for support, how bank credentials or access tokens are handled, what happens after deletion and whether a data processing addendum applies. The public statements are useful starting points, but they do not replace answers for the account being purchased.


## Workflow automation: convenience without accidental client messages

A workflow can automate repetitive steps, but it can also send a wrong invoice or contract at scale if a template or trigger is misconfigured. The help center distinguishes internal to-do tasks from action items, and says actions can be completed automatically or after review. For a first rollout, keep external actions behind a human review. Use a staging contact, vary the service type and test edge cases such as a declined quote, rescheduled event, partial payment or client who submits incomplete information.

Map each trigger to a real business event and a responsible owner. A lead form might create a project and start an intake sequence, while a manually activated workflow could be appropriate for a custom client. Decide what should happen if a client responds outside the expected sequence or requests a change after signing. Confirm which team member is notified and whether the dashboard shows overdue tasks. Automation reduces repetitive work only when someone monitors exceptions and owns the next action.

The help article warns that workflow and lifecycle features may not be available on every plan. Build the demo around the exact account tier and staff roles instead of relying on a generic marketing video. Ask how workflow templates can be copied, edited and archived, and whether changes affect existing client projects. Keep a record of approved templates and contract versions. A live process should have a rollback path so the owner can pause automation if a message, document or payment request goes to the wrong recipient.


## Security and service terms: vendor statements are not an audit

The privacy page describes encryption and a vulnerability reporting path, while the terms say the service is provided as-is and does not guarantee uninterrupted access, accuracy, security or error-free operation. The terms also describe broad limitations around data loss and service availability. These clauses are common risk-allocation topics in software contracts, but a small business should still decide what level of outage it can tolerate and whether it has an independent copy of essential records.

Ask for security materials that cover authentication, multifactor options, role-based permissions, audit history, backup frequency, restore testing, incident notification and data deletion. Confirm how the platform supports separate team logins and whether a staff member can be removed without disrupting ownership of client projects. Review the bank connection flow on a non-critical account before connecting primary business finances. The privacy page’s list of payment and banking providers should be matched to the provider shown during setup.

A cloud CRM may hold personal information belonging to the business’s clients. The business remains accountable for choosing what it stores, who can view it and how it is retained. Use least-privilege logins, keep account owners current, avoid shared passwords, and require a second person to review high-value invoices or changed payment instructions. If a client asks for deletion or access, know where the relevant records live and what the platform can remove. These controls reduce the chance that a convenient all-in-one workspace becomes a single point of failure.


## Alternatives and a practical small-business evaluation

Alternatives include other service-business CRMs such as HoneyBook and Dubsado, broader suites such as Zoho, and a smaller stack built from a calendar, e-signature tool, invoicing product and documented email templates. Compare the client experience and the data exit path as carefully as the feature count. A photographer, consultant, designer and home-services business may all need quotes and scheduling, yet differ in contract structure, client volume, payment timing and team permissions.

Run the same test scenario through each product: a new lead completes a form; the business qualifies the client; a quote and contract are sent; the client signs and pays; a reminder fires; the project is completed; and the business exports the final record. Record how many steps require manual review, whether the customer can use a phone, how a change is handled and how invoices are reconciled. Do not interpret a streamlined demo as proof that every local tax, payment or contract requirement is handled automatically.

Ask a bookkeeper or accountant to review how invoices, payments and bank-feed data fit the business’s records. Confirm which system remains the accounting source of truth and how duplicate transactions are avoided. Compare implementation time and the cost of migration, not just monthly subscription price. A well-integrated platform can help a solo business keep commitments visible; a simpler dedicated invoicing tool may be safer if the owner does not need workflow automation or does not want to transfer client communications into a single vendor system.


## 17hats scam review: conclusion and buyer checklist

The official pages reviewed identify 17hats as a client and business-management service, publish pricing in U.S. dollars, advertise a seven-day trial, and document projects, forms, quotes, contracts, invoices, scheduling and workflows. The sources do not establish that 17hats is a scam. They do reveal decisions a buyer should make before relying on the service: automatic renewal, the in-account cancellation path, what happens to access and data after a subscription ends, and how financial integrations receive personal and banking information.

This is a source-based desk review, not a paid-account test, accounting opinion or independent security review. Public pricing was checked November 17, 2026. The privacy policy’s last-update date is October 31, 2024, so its provider list and technical statements should be confirmed against current documentation. The terms and privacy notice contain language about data transport that should be clarified for sensitive customer documents. A buyer should get the current answer in writing and keep independent exports.

17hats may fit a freelancer or service business that wants to combine client documents and repeatable follow-up in one project workflow. It may be a poor fit for a business whose staff will not maintain the templates, whose records cannot become inaccessible after cancellation, or whose security requirements are not addressed by the vendor’s current materials. Use the trial to test the entire path, export records before renewal or cancellation, and avoid treating “all-in-one” as a reason to skip accounting, privacy or contract review.
