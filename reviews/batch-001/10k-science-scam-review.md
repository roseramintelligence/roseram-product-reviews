# 10k Science review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-17  
**Category:** Immersive science learning for web and VR  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

10k Science, made by Dynamoid, combines interactive 3D science experiences for web and Meta Quest with AI Guides and an educator dashboard. Public lessons include CRISPR and sickle-cell biology, plant disease, food chemistry and pain pathways; teachers can use a browser without providing every student with a headset. The company publishes small pilot and survey findings, but these are not a substitute for a large independent efficacy evaluation. Its privacy policy says voice recordings may be sent to transcription providers and transcripts/responses may be processed by AI providers and stored with learning activity; it says the service is not intended for children under 13. Current school pricing is routed through a JavaScript pricing page that did not expose a dollar amount in this review, so verify the live quote, trial scope and school/student data agreement before adoption.

## Editorial verdict

The reviewed materials identify 10k Science as a real educational product built by Dynamoid, with a current web viewer, Meta Quest listing, content library, educator tools, documented lesson topics, a public privacy policy and a knowledge base. They do not substantiate calling it a scam. The educational proposition is attractive for abstract science that benefits from spatial exploration, but the claims need context: company-published pilot findings are promising rather than conclusive, current full-library pricing was not accessible as plain text, and the privacy policy is not intended for children under 13 despite the platform’s classroom use cases. A school should confirm age eligibility, consent/contract terms for student data, available content, device coverage, accessibility and the scope of any learning evidence before procurement. No classroom, headset, browser lesson, AI guide, assessment or administrator workflow was tested for this desk review.

## 10k Science review: learn by moving through a scientific system

10k Science is an immersive science-learning product from Dynamoid. Instead of treating a lesson as a video or static 3D object, the company describes browser and Meta Quest experiences where learners move through a scientific environment, inspect structures and cross scale—from a body system to cells and molecules, or from a familiar object to the material it contains. Current listed content includes CRISPR and sickle-cell biology, grapevine disease, food chemistry, pain signaling and other in-development topics.

This format is a plausible fit for concepts that are difficult to see directly. A learner can explore a cell or molecular interaction spatially, then explain what is happening. The product also includes AI Guides and an educator dashboard, so the intended classroom workflow is not only immersion: it combines exploration, prompts, verbal explanations and teacher review. Whether that teaching sequence fits a particular grade, curriculum, headset fleet or lesson period still needs to be established by the school.


## Web access helps with headset coverage, but check classroom operations

The knowledge base says 10k Science is available through both a web viewer and Meta Quest, and that a browser experience can be used for individual work, group activity or station rotations. That flexibility matters for schools that do not have one headset per student. The vendor’s FAQ describes AI Guides as audibly orienting students, prompting them to explain what they observe and offering real-time feedback. The Educator Dashboard is described as summarizing student responses and progress so an educator can see where a learner may need support.

A browser option does not automatically make a product suitable for every device or accessible to every student. Before a purchase, verify supported browsers, school network requirements, headset management, shared-device sign-in, audio/microphone needs, accessibility settings, language options and whether students can complete the same learning objective without VR. Test the teacher workflow: assign a lesson, get students in, review a response, reset a device and export or delete learning records. This desk review did not test those workflows.


## Content and curriculum: examine the actual lessons, not only the catalogue

The published library covers several areas of science, including molecular biology, genetics, plant systems, food chemistry, pain and pharmacology, environmental health, respiratory and circulatory systems, chemistry, evolution and sound. For example, the CRISPR & Sickle Cell experience is described as moving from the human body through blood, cells, DNA and protein synthesis; Trouble in Sunshine Creek connects grapevine systems and bacterial disease; Snack Shack uses soda and food to introduce molecules; Pain & Painkillers explores nerves and sodium channels. Some topics are marked in development or coming soon in the library documentation.

These descriptions are useful for deciding whether a lesson fits a unit, but they do not prove that every listed experience is currently available to every subscription. The library documentation explicitly says availability can change. Some learning standards connections are described as best fit and depend on grade and instructional framing. Teachers should inspect the current lesson, check scientific wording against the curriculum, and plan a debrief that distinguishes a simplified visualization from a complete scientific model. A VR representation can clarify scale and mechanism, but it also needs careful context.


## AI Guides and formative feedback are support tools

10k Science positions the AI Guide as an instructional companion: it can orient students, call attention to a structure, prompt an explanation and respond to spoken input. The company says the dashboard provides visibility into progress and student thinking and characterizes this as formative assessment rather than high-stakes testing. In a well-run lesson, that could help an educator notice which students are describing a structure accurately and which need a follow-up question.

Schools should clarify how the feedback is generated, which student responses are retained, whether teachers can inspect the evidence behind a score or summary, and how automated feedback can be corrected. The product’s FAQ says the teacher remains responsible for context, discussion, feedback and curriculum choices. Treat the AI response as a prompt for teaching rather than as a formal grade or a stand-alone measure of a child’s understanding. This review did not evaluate scoring accuracy or compare AI feedback with educator judgments.


## Evidence is promising, but the published pilot is small

Dynamoid publishes an early study summary describing 137 students at Oakland School for the Arts randomly assigned to use a VR experience or watch a video containing the same lesson material. Its summary says 22 of 28 interviewed students preferred the VR experience to reading or video, and reports higher interest in the VR condition. The FAQ also summarizes pilot and survey signals, including a content-knowledge increase and engagement measures, as well as a separate Stevens Institute student pilot. These are worth examining because they point to questions the product is trying to solve.

The evidence available in the sources reviewed remains limited for a school procurement claim. The public student-response article is written by the provider and describes a small qualitative interview subset within a pilot, not a large independent multi-school trial that establishes durable learning gains across subjects, ages or devices. A comparison with video also cannot automatically answer whether the platform outperforms teacher-led discussion, hands-on labs or other interactive software. Ask for the full study protocol, assessment instruments, sample characteristics, analysis and any independent report before using numerical claims in a district-wide adoption case.


## Student voice data and the under-13 policy boundary

The privacy policy updated November 11, 2026 says Dynamoid provides 10k Science services and may process names, email, organization and account details, device and session data, learning activity, student responses, audio and transcripts. It says voice recordings can be sent to transcription providers; transcripts and related responses may be processed by AI providers to generate instruction, feedback and assessments, and may be stored as learning records. The policy lists infrastructure, analytics, payment, transcription and AI providers and says processing may occur in the United States and other regions where vendors operate.

The same policy states that 10k Science is not intended for children under 13. That deserves a direct school-level answer because the product is described for classrooms, and younger students may be present in some educational settings. Before a school uses the platform with students, verify age eligibility, school-directed account setup, parental or school authorization, the applicable data-processing agreement, vendor retention and deletion timelines, and who can access student responses. Confirm whether a district’s privacy or security review is required, and do not send identifiable student voice recordings until those conditions are settled.


## Plans and alternatives: request a quote that matches the learning design

The Meta Quest app is advertised as free to download, while the full content library requires a subscription. The FAQ says educators can start a web-platform trial and directs institutions to the live pricing page for the current plans, trials and organizational licensing. That pricing page requires JavaScript and did not expose a price in the review environment, so this review does not quote an amount. Ask for the current per-student or per-school rate, included experiences, trial scope, headset and browser seats, dashboard access, onboarding and renewal terms.

Compare the product with other VR science applications, web-based interactive 3D content, physical labs, teacher-built visualizations and high-quality diagrams or simulations. The right comparison is the specific learning objective, not whether a headset looks impressive. Consider device availability, time to launch, teacher preparation, accessibility, student-data terms, content updates, standards fit and how assessment evidence is used. If a classroom can meet its goal through a simpler web simulation or teacher-led lab, that may cost less and involve less device management.


## 10k Science scam review verdict: a real product; validate the school fit

The official product and documentation identify 10k Science as a real web and VR platform by Dynamoid, describe named learning experiences and educator tools, provide a current privacy policy and offer a school demo and trial route. This evidence does not substantiate calling it a scam. Its most distinctive promise—spatial exploration of hard-to-see science—has a credible instructional use case, while the available impact findings are early provider-published evidence rather than proof of broad efficacy. The most important procurement gate is student privacy and age eligibility, followed by current pricing, actual content availability, device support and the teacher’s ability to supervise and interpret AI-generated feedback.

This is a source-based desk review, not a classroom trial, headset evaluation, curriculum audit, independent study or privacy assessment. Product material and policies were checked November 17, 2026. Before purchasing, view the live experiences, request the current school quote and data agreement, confirm whether students under 13 can use the service, map voice/transcript processing to district rules, and pilot one lesson with a teacher reviewing every automated feedback step.


## Features and decision criteria

- Immersive 3D science experiences designed for web browsers and Meta Quest VR headsets
- Content that moves across scales, including cells, molecules, organs, ecosystems and environmental processes
- AI Guides that orient students, prompt observations, listen to spoken explanations and provide formative feedback
- Educator Dashboard for reviewing progress and student responses during experiences
- Web-based activities and classroom rotations for schools that do not have a headset for each learner
- Creator and researcher workflows intended to turn data into interactive 3D science communication

## Pricing considerations

The product’s knowledge base directs buyers to the live pricing page for current subscriptions, trials and organizational licensing, but the accessible page returned only a JavaScript requirement. No dollar amount is inferred here. Verify the final scope and quote directly with Dynamoid before procurement.
