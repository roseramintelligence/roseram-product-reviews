# 100Plus review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-17  
**Category:** Remote patient monitoring and chronic care management  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

100Plus offers a provider-oriented remote patient monitoring and chronic care management program that combines cellular-connected home devices, patient onboarding and engagement, reporting, and monitoring services. It should be assessed as a clinical workflow and vendor relationship, not as an emergency-response device or a guarantee of Medicare reimbursement.

## Editorial verdict

The company publishes a clear description of its platform, device categories, provider workflow, privacy policy and website terms, and it states that the service is not for emergencies or conditions needing immediate action. Public evidence reviewed does not support a scam accusation. The key diligence is operational and clinical: validate device and network fit, response escalation, data responsibilities, current payer requirements and the separate service contract before enrolling patients.

## 100Plus review: a managed remote patient monitoring program

100Plus is a remote-care platform aimed at medical practices and their patients. Its public materials describe remote patient monitoring (RPM), chronic care management (CCM), cellular-connected home devices, patient onboarding and engagement, provider reporting, and services that support practice workflows. Rather than selling only a consumer gadget, the company presents a combined technology-and-service model in which information collected at home is routed to a care team for review.

That distinction sets the right frame for a **100Plus review**. The buyer is often a practice deciding whether a vendor can fit its care model, staffing, records systems and billing operations. A patient needs to understand what device is being provided, what information is sent, who sees it, and how quickly anyone responds. The service is not a substitute for clinical assessment or urgent care, and the product page’s broad outcomes language should not replace a practice-specific evaluation.


## 100Plus devices and the patient experience

The company lists blood pressure, glucose, pulse oximetry and weight-monitoring devices. It says its equipment uses a regular cellular connection, so a patient does not need to pair a smartphone, Wi-Fi or Bluetooth for readings to reach the provider. That can reduce setup hurdles for people who do not use smartphone apps or home networking. The patient resources and device guides are useful places to check instructions and support routes before an enrollment decision.

Cellular-only operation is also a deployment dependency: the practice should confirm network coverage where each patient will use the device and have a plan for missed or delayed transmissions. A device guide should be reviewed for the exact model supplied, including calibration, intended use, measurement limitations and when to contact a clinician. No device was tested for this review, and no claim is made about measurement accuracy or compatibility with an individual patient.


## Provider workflows, monitoring and patient engagement

The 100Plus pages describe enrollment and consent, device shipping, patient education, reminders from the Esper SMS assistant, incoming data monitoring, notifications and reporting. The monitoring page also promotes a clinical team and routine patient check-ins. If delivered as described, this combination could reduce the administrative work of launching an RPM program and give a practice a repeatable path from identifying a patient to reviewing transmitted readings.

The key procurement question is who owns each action after a reading arrives. Ask which alerts are monitored, what thresholds trigger contact, who follows up after a missed reading, how quickly critical-looking data reach the practice, and what staffing or clinical supervision the vendor provides. Confirm which tasks remain with the practice and which are included in the service agreement. A dashboard and text reminders can support a care program, but they do not by themselves constitute continuous observation or guarantee a clinical response.


## Safety limits and Medicare RPM requirements

100Plus explicitly says its RPM platform is not intended for hospital contexts, conditions requiring timely or immediate clinical action, or active patient monitoring. That boundary should be visible to patients and staff during enrollment. If a patient’s condition changes urgently, the team needs an established clinical escalation path outside routine device review; the platform should not be presented as an emergency response service.

CMS describes RPM as a sequence of patient education and setup, connected-device data transmission, and provider treatment or management. Its current page states general conditions including an acute or chronic condition requiring monitoring, an internet-connected device meeting the FDA definition of a medical device, and data transmitted on at least two days in a 30-day period. Actual claims depend on current Medicare rules, the service furnished, documentation and payer policy. 100Plus itself advises practices to have qualified billing personnel review claims. Do not treat a vendor FAQ or a projected reimbursement example as approval that a particular patient or service is billable.


## 100Plus pricing and reimbursement claims need independent validation

The public provider material gives approximate reimbursement and practice-economics examples. Those figures are marketing context, not a contract price, guaranteed payment or prediction of a specific clinic’s net return. Payment can depend on payer, geographic rate, patient eligibility, enrollment and consent, device requirements, reading frequency, documentation, staff time, claims edits and denials. The practice should calculate its own economics from current payer rules and its actual service agreement.

Ask 100Plus for a written fee schedule covering devices, shipping, platform access, monitoring staff, onboarding, integrations, training, minimums, contract term and termination. Then compare it with running RPM in-house and with another managed provider using the same assumptions. Include the cost of clinical review, compliance, EHR configuration, patient acquisition and support. A projected reimbursement total is not profit unless all these costs and responsibilities are accounted for.


## 100Plus privacy, EHR integration and contract terms

Health measurements and care communications are sensitive data. The company says its services include provider-facing reporting and EHR integration, and its privacy materials identify ConnectAmerica as the operator of the 100Plus website. The website terms state that they govern website use only; separate agreements may govern the purchase and delivery of the actual services. A clinic should obtain those operational agreements and determine which entity is responsible for each data flow.

Before launch, request the applicable business associate agreement if required, the list of subprocessors and device partners, retention and deletion periods, breach notice terms, access controls, audit logs, data export and a diagram of how readings travel from the device to the practice. Confirm that the EHR integration is available for the clinic’s specific system and that staff can correct patient identity or device assignment errors. This review did not inspect a live dashboard, security assessment, BAA or production integration.


## Who should consider 100Plus and what to verify

100Plus may be worth a structured evaluation for a practice that has an appropriate outpatient RPM program, patients who could use cellular devices, capacity to respond to alerts and a need for onboarding or monitoring support. It is less suitable when patients require immediate supervision, cellular coverage is unreliable, or a clinic expects devices alone to deliver a complete clinical program. The product’s success depends on the surrounding care team and workflows as much as on its interface.

A practical pilot should begin with a small, carefully selected group and written measures: successful enrollment, device delivery and connectivity, readings received, missed-reading follow-up, alert response time, staff workload, patient questions, EHR reconciliation and claim outcomes. Review these against the practice’s existing process before expanding. Keep clinical decisions with qualified care professionals and verify each patient’s needs and payer rules independently.


## Is 100Plus a scam? Evidence-based conclusion

The materials reviewed identify ConnectAmerica as the organization behind the 100Plus website and publish product information, provider and patient resources, contact details, privacy information and terms. The platform describes a real category of remote-care service and publishes a meaningful safety limitation against emergency and active monitoring use. No evidence reviewed substantiates calling 100Plus a scam.

This is a source-based desk review, not a clinical outcome study, device accuracy assessment, HIPAA audit, Medicare coverage determination or customer reference check. Before enrolling patients, verify the current device models, service contract, payer rules, data protections, staffing, escalation protocol and integration with the practice’s records. The responsible decision is to judge the full care workflow and contract, not to rely on optimistic reimbursement headlines or a directory label.
