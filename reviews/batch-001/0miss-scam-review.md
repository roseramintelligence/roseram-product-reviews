# 0miss review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-18  
**Category:** AI receptionist and appointment booking  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

0miss is a Spanish-market AI phone receptionist for local appointment businesses. It uses conditional call forwarding on an existing number, handles calls a business misses, can book against Google Calendar, and sends lead details through WhatsApp. The publisher lists Starter at €49/month for 240 minutes, Pro at €99 for 540 minutes, and Max at €199 for 1,200 minutes, with VAT included. There is a material terms question: the English product page advertises a seven-day free Starter trial, while the Spanish terms say there is no free plan or automatic hidden trial and that trials may be granted at the operator’s discretion. The pages also assign the business responsibility for its callers’ notice and recording consent. This source-based buyer review does not claim that Roseram tested calls, bookings, carriers or data controls. The reviewed evidence does not establish fraud.

## Editorial verdict

0miss addresses a concrete problem: a missed phone call can mean a missed appointment, and voicemail alone does not book a time. Its offer combines conditional call forwarding, a voice assistant, calendar-aware appointments and WhatsApp lead cards instead of asking a business to change its public number. The strongest apparent fit is a local, appointment-based operator in Spain that already uses Google Calendar and wants missed calls covered without running a staffed phone desk. The largest due-diligence issue is not hidden in the feature list. The accessible English product page markets a seven-day free Starter trial, yet the Spanish terms state that there is no free plan or automatic trial and that the operator may grant a trial at its discretion. Ask which terms apply at signup and inspect the checkout before entering a payment method. Price and included call minutes also need to be assessed together: the entry plan is €49 for 240 minutes, and the terms say the assistant switches to message-taking after its quota rather than continuing a full conversation. That fallback can control cost, but a caller may not receive the full booking service when the allowance runs out. The privacy policy gives useful detail about recording and transcript retention, Google Calendar use, EU hosting, subprocessors and possible transfers outside the EU. Those are publisher disclosures, not an independent audit; the business still owns its caller notice, consent and data-policy decisions. Roseram did not create an account, route a phone line, test a language, verify a booking or inspect a DPA. The sources reviewed support a careful product evaluation and do not substantiate fraud.

## What 0miss sells

0miss is a phone-based virtual receptionist and reservation system aimed at local businesses. The product page describes a flow in which a customer calls the business’s usual number, conditional forwarding sends an unanswered call to the assistant, and the system can take a message or arrange an appointment. It also advertises a lead card through WhatsApp and a two-way connection with Google Calendar. The business keeps its phone number and SIM according to the publisher’s setup description.

That puts 0miss in a different category from a website chatbot or a general-purpose voice API. The key purchasing outcome is whether a real caller can reach the right service, understand the assistant, find a valid appointment time and receive a confirmation. The publisher’s examples describe that workflow; this review has not placed a call or verified a live calendar integration.


## The offer is designed around missed calls

The product is meant to answer when staff are busy, the line is occupied or the phone has no coverage. The product page says calls answered directly by the business remain unchanged and forwarding only activates under the configured conditions. This narrow handoff is central to the pitch: an owner can retain the existing number and use the assistant as overflow rather than replacing every human call with automation.

A buyer should confirm the carrier-specific forwarding code and the conditions under which it triggers. The page says the setup works with Spanish carriers, which points to a geographic and telecom assumption worth checking before a business outside Spain subscribes. Ask how forwarding behaves for no-answer, busy and unreachable states on the exact carrier and line you plan to use, and how an employee can turn it off quickly.


## Calendar booking is the feature to validate first

0miss says it reads occupied time from connected Google Calendars and creates or updates events for appointments. The page describes availability by professional, time off and breaks, and says the assistant offers existing open slots instead of inventing a time. If accurate for the account configuration, that can prevent a receptionist from promising an appointment that conflicts with a calendar entry.

The privacy policy says the service stores only busy-time blocks from connected calendars, without event titles, descriptions or attendees, and uses the connection to read availability and create or update appointment events. It says Google credentials are encrypted, that calendar data is not used with the service’s AI models, and that access can be revoked from 0miss or Google settings. Verify these details against the DPA and actual Google OAuth permissions shown during setup.


## Prices are bundled with call-minute limits

The English pricing page accessible for this review lists Starter at €49 per month for 240 minutes, Pro at €99 for 540 minutes, and Max at €199 for 1,200 minutes. It estimates those totals as roughly 80, 180 and 400 calls respectively. The page says VAT is included and lists custom Enterprise pricing for businesses with more than three locations. It also labels Starter as a seven-day free offer.

Treat the approximate call counts as the vendor’s estimate, not a guarantee: call length changes how quickly an allowance is consumed. Convert your real call volume and average duration into minutes, then model a busy month. Check whether voicemail-style message-taking after the quota still meets your service promise, and whether any plan has additional limits per call. The terms say each tier includes monthly voice minutes and a call-duration limit.


## The free-trial language needs confirmation

There is a clear mismatch between two first-party pages. The English product page shows “7 days free” on Starter. The Spanish Terms of Service say there is no free plan and no automatic hidden trial, while noting that the operator can grant trials at its discretion. Those statements may refer to different signup flows or a changed promotion, but the available evidence does not explain the difference.

Before entering payment information, ask 0miss to confirm whether the seven-day trial is currently available to your business, whether payment details are required, when the subscription begins, and how cancellation works during the trial. Save the terms presented in the actual checkout. This discrepancy deserves a clear answer; it is not, by itself, evidence of fraud.


## What happens when the allowance runs out

The English FAQ says the assistant keeps answering and moves to message-taking when the monthly minutes are used, with no automatic charge for the extra conversation. The Spanish terms also say that after the quota, the service changes to a lower-cost message-taking mode rather than carrying on with the full dialogue. This is a useful budget boundary, but it changes what the caller experiences.

Ask what the fallback message includes, where it is delivered, and how the business learns that the minutes are nearly exhausted. If missed calls are time-sensitive, test the notification path and set an internal usage check before the monthly allowance is depleted. Do not assume a quota safeguard means that every caller can still book an appointment after the full receptionist feature switches modes.


## The language and location fit may be specific

The publisher describes Spanish and English calls, language switching during a conversation, and answering in the caller’s language. The marketing page also says conditional forwarding works with Spanish carriers and gives local Spanish business examples. Together, those details suggest a product primarily positioned for Spanish local-service operators, even though the assistant is presented as multilingual.

A business elsewhere should verify its country, phone provider, number type, forwarding conditions, supported languages and local caller-disclosure rules. A multilingual conversation is not the same as support for every national telecom network or every regional accent. Ask for a demonstration using the language mix your callers actually use, including a case where the assistant cannot safely answer and should take a message.


## Caller disclosure and call recording are business responsibilities

0miss says that the assistant identifies itself as AI at the beginning of each call. Its privacy policy says callers are informed when recording is active. The terms assign the business responsibility for informing customers that an AI assistant may handle the call and for obtaining recording consent when applicable law requires it. This makes the configuration and the business’s own customer notices part of the deployment, not merely a vendor feature.

Do not treat a vendor’s statement that a notice satisfies a law as a legal conclusion for every country, industry or call type. Confirm the notice wording, recording behavior, retention and any consent step against the rules that apply to your business. For health, financial or other sensitive conversations, ask whether those use cases are supported and review the DPA before forwarding real calls.


## Retention details are unusually concrete

The privacy policy says audio recordings, when the business enables recording and informs the caller, are kept for 30 days. Call transcripts are listed as retained for 90 days in Spain, after which the policy says they are automatically deleted. It also distinguishes caller data from the business account’s data and describes the business as controller for information about its own callers while 0miss acts as processor under the accepted DPA.

Those statements give a buyer useful questions to resolve: whether the same periods apply to every account and country, whether the customer can disable recordings, how exports work, what happens to backups, and which records remain after subscription cancellation. The policy says account and business data are deleted after cancellation except billing and tax records retained as required by Spanish law. Confirm details in the agreement for the actual account.


## The service depends on several processors

The 0miss privacy policy lists Hetzner infrastructure in Germany and Finland, Cloudflare, Telnyx for telephony and SMS, ElevenLabs for conversational voice processing, OpenAI for after-call text processing such as summaries and translation, Meta WhatsApp, Stripe, Resend, Google Calendar when connected, and Sentry in the European region. The policy says some providers, including voice, AI and messaging providers, are established or may process information outside the EU, with transfers relying on Standard Contractual Clauses and additional safeguards.

That is a useful subprocessor list, and it also means the service is not a single-host data flow. Ask for the current DPA and subprocessor list, confirm which providers see audio versus transcripts or summaries, and determine whether your contract requires notice before providers change. For sensitive calls, trace where the recording, transcript, calendar details and WhatsApp message travel, rather than relying only on the phrase “data hosted in the EU.”


## The business keeps responsibility for its catalog

The product page says the assistant answers from the business’s configured services, prices and hours, and should take a message when it does not know something. That design is preferable to improvising a price or availability, but it depends on the business entering and maintaining a correct catalog. The terms also place responsibility for the accuracy of prices, services and availability on the business.

Before activation, load a short, verified service list and test common questions, price changes, cancellations, special requests and unavailable appointment slots. Give staff a simple way to correct the source information. A receptionist can only be as reliable as the data and rules it receives; the business should review transcripts and booking records during the initial rollout rather than assuming the model interprets every instruction correctly.


## Cancellation, renewal and refunds should be read together

The marketing page says there is no lock-in and that the owner can cancel from the dashboard. The terms say subscriptions renew monthly, cancellation takes effect at the end of the current period, and fees are non-refundable except where the law requires otherwise. The terms also explain that the service may be suspended for nonpayment after a notice or grace period. These clauses are more useful for planning than a broad “cancel anytime” label.

At signup, record the billing date, the exact cancellation path, and when forwarding codes should be disabled. Confirm whether call data remains exportable after cancellation and how a Google Calendar connection is revoked. The privacy policy describes deleting synced calendar data immediately when the connection is removed, but the customer should still verify what account-level data is available before the subscription ends.


## Compare it with a receptionist, a booking page and a voice-agent build

A practical comparison should include a part-time human receptionist or answering service, an existing online booking page, and a configurable voice-agent product if the business has the staff to manage one. Keep the same test cases: missed-call coverage, calendar conflict prevention, caller notice, supported languages, handoff when the system is unsure, and total monthly cost at your actual minutes. A voice product with a low sticker price can still require considerable setup or monitoring.

0miss’s differentiator is the packaged link between conditional phone forwarding, appointment booking and WhatsApp lead cards. That convenience matters if the business wants a managed workflow. If it already has a booking tool or needs a phone system outside the stated Spanish carrier focus, compare integration effort and failure handling before paying for overlapping features. No third-party performance or price comparison is asserted here.


## A trial checklist for a local business

Use a secondary line or a controlled test window first. Verify that a directly answered call remains untouched, that a missed call reaches 0miss, and that disabling the forwarding code restores the prior route. Test one appointment with a real calendar conflict, one change or cancellation, one unsupported question and one language switch. Confirm the calendar event, caller confirmation and WhatsApp summary all contain the expected details.

Then run a quota and privacy check. Ask how call minutes are counted, what happens as the plan approaches its limit, and whether message-taking still notifies the owner. Confirm the AI notice, recording notice and retention controls, and inspect the DPA and payment terms. This is a proposed evaluation plan; Roseram did not run these tests or verify the product’s booking accuracy.


## Who may benefit and who should pause

0miss may suit a salon, clinic, repair shop or other appointment business that misses calls while serving customers, uses Google Calendar, and wants calls routed through its existing number. A small team may value the bundled calendar and WhatsApp workflow more than a large feature catalog. The product’s message-taking fallback and monthly minute tiers provide a clear starting point for a low-volume pilot.

Pause if the business operates outside supported carrier coverage, has high or unpredictable call volume, cannot accept message-only handling after its minutes run out, or needs a data residency or recording commitment not described in the DPA. Also resolve the trial-page contradiction before checkout. Regulated or sensitive services should review the full processing path and disclosure requirements with their privacy or legal adviser.

## The evidence-based verdict

0miss presents a focused answer to a familiar local-business problem: cover calls that staff cannot answer, book into a calendar and send the owner a usable lead summary. The package is easiest to evaluate when measured in monthly minutes, forwarding reliability and the quality of the fallback after the allowance is used. Its published entry price is not the whole decision; the phone carrier, caller experience and privacy flow determine whether the system belongs in a real business.

The most immediate step is to resolve the trial language in writing and confirm the terms shown at checkout. Next, check the exact carrier and country, inspect the DPA and subprocessors, and run a controlled call-and-booking pilot with non-sensitive information. If these checks fit the workflow and budget, expand gradually. The available evidence supports a cautious evaluation of a legitimate AI receptionist product; it does not support a fraud accusation or a guarantee of recovered revenue.
