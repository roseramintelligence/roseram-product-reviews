# 0miss review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-18  
**Category:** AI receptionist and appointment booking service  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

0miss is a subscription AI receptionist and booking service aimed at local appointment businesses in Spain. Its English product page says it forwards unanswered, busy or out-of-coverage calls from a business’s existing number, identifies itself to callers as AI, books into a calendar and sends lead details through WhatsApp. The page lists Starter at €49 per month for 240 minutes, Pro at €99 for 540 minutes, Max at €199 for 1,200 minutes, and custom Enterprise pricing; it advertises a seven-day free trial on Starter and says VAT is included. The service terms, however, state there is no free plan or automatic trial and that trial access may be granted at the operator’s discretion. The privacy policy names telephony, voice, AI-summary, WhatsApp, hosting and error-monitoring providers; it says enabled call recordings are kept 30 days and transcripts 90 days in Spain, while some processing may occur outside the EU. The site’s loss calculator is an illustrative estimate, not measured customer results. Public materials establish a described commercial service, but do not prove each feature, compliance claim, call outcome or revenue estimate. This source-based review does not establish fraud, test live calls or give legal advice.

## Editorial verdict

The reviewed evidence does not substantiate calling 0miss a scam. The service has an identifiable product site, dated privacy and terms pages, feature descriptions and a public price schedule for a narrow use case: handling calls a Spanish local business does not answer and converting some of them into appointments or messages. The offer merits careful operational and privacy diligence because it sits directly in the call path and can handle caller names, numbers, bookings, transcripts and optional recordings. Before forwarding a live business number, ask for the DPA and subprocessor list, test escalation and failure behavior, confirm the carrier’s call-forwarding support and fees, and reconcile the seven-day Starter trial advertised on the product page with terms saying there is no automatic trial. Check whether the published minutes and locations suit your usage and whether the calling notice and recording setup meet your obligations. ## What 0miss does for a local business

0miss describes itself as an AI phone receptionist and booking system for businesses that rely on appointments. The English page says a business can keep its current number and turn on conditional call forwarding. If a call is unanswered, the line is busy or the phone has no coverage, the caller is routed to the assistant; calls the owner answers normally are intended to stay with the owner. The assistant is presented as AI at the start of a call, gathers what the caller wants and can book an available appointment or take a message.

The workflow is meant to reduce the gap between a missed call and a usable follow-up. According to the product page, the caller can receive a booking confirmation and the business can receive a lead card through WhatsApp. The service also offers an online booking link, customer appointment history and a team calendar. This is a telephony and workflow product, not simply a voice chatbot: a buyer must configure the call route, business catalogue, hours, professionals, calendar and message channel for the assistant to act on live enquiries.


## Call forwarding, existing numbers and carrier checks

The promise of keeping a familiar business number is central to the product. The site shows conditional call-forwarding codes and says the service works with Spanish mobile carriers. That can lower setup friction for a single-location business, but call forwarding is controlled by the phone carrier and the handset’s service plan. Before activation, ask the carrier which no-answer, busy and unreachable forwarding conditions are supported, whether a code is available on a fixed line or mobile plan, and whether forwarding creates call charges or affects voicemail.

Do not assume that a Spanish deployment description means every international carrier is supported. The English product page refers to Spanish carriers, prices in euros and VAT, and the terms specify Spanish law and courts. If a business operates in another country, confirm the number type, forwarding code, telephony provider, caller ID behavior, emergency handling and local requirements in writing. A technically successful transfer also needs an operational fallback: staff should know how to disable forwarding, how after-hours rules work, and what happens if the AI or its upstream telephony service is unavailable.


## AI receptionist features and booking controls

The Starter plan is described as including one professional, instant reservations, Google Calendar synchronization, WhatsApp lead cards and an online booking page. Pro adds unlimited professionals, a team calendar and WhatsApp appointment reminders. Max is for up to three locations and advertises custom integrations on request; Enterprise pricing is negotiated for larger chains. The product page says Google Calendar synchronization is two-way on every plan, so a blocked calendar slot is intended not to be offered as an available appointment.

These are features listed by the publisher, not capabilities Roseram measured. A buyer should test the configuration with realistic business hours, service durations, staff assignments, breaks, holidays, buffers and rescheduling. Check whether the assistant confirms an appointment only after the calendar event is successfully written, and what the caller hears if calendar access is revoked or a write fails. Ask for a human takeover or message path for unusual requests. The page says the assistant takes a message when it does not know something, which is a sensible fallback to validate with actual test calls.


## WhatsApp, recordings and the caller experience

The offer emphasizes WhatsApp lead cards and, on Pro and Max, reminders that let clients confirm, cancel or move appointments by replying. This could make a booking easier to manage, but it means the business should verify the exact message templates, recipient identity, opt-in expectations and the role of the WhatsApp Cloud API. Ask what happens if the caller does not use WhatsApp or the number is not available for messaging. The service also says each call has audio, transcript and summary in the dashboard, subject to its settings and retention policy.

The privacy policy states that audio recording is enabled by the business and that callers are informed when recording is active. It lists a 30-day retention period for recordings and 90 days for transcripts in Spain. Buyers should confirm how recording is configured, whether the notice can be localized, who can access recordings, how deletion works and whether support staff can view them. The product’s AI disclosure and call-recording language are publisher statements; the business must still assess its own customer notices and applicable rules rather than treating a vendor feature as automatic compliance.


## 0miss pricing: €49, €99 and €199 monthly plans

The English pricing section lists Starter at €49 per month for 240 minutes, Pro at €99 per month for 540 minutes and Max at €199 per month for 1,200 minutes. It says VAT is included. Enterprise is custom-priced. Starter is positioned for one professional; Pro covers an unlimited number of professionals; and Max supports up to three locations. The public Capterra pricing page also shows the three entry prices, although the vendor’s own current checkout and terms should control the purchase decision.

The listed minutes are an important part of the cost, not a minor footnote. A business should estimate total call duration, busy periods, seasonal volume and the cost of missed enquiries before comparing plans. The site says that when the allowance is exhausted, the assistant changes to message-taking rather than adding surprise charges. The terms likewise describe a lower-cost message mode after included minutes. Confirm that this is the behavior on the plan you select, whether limits are measured per month or billing cycle, and if individual calls have duration caps.


## Seven-day trial wording conflicts with the service terms

A buyer should resolve a specific mismatch before entering payment details. The English product page labels Starter as seven days free and displays a “Try 7 days free” action. The service terms say there is no free plan or automatic hidden trial, while allowing the operator to grant trials at its discretion. These statements might describe an offered but conditional trial, yet the public wording does not explain eligibility, whether payment details are required or when a paid subscription begins.

Ask 0miss to confirm the exact trial for your account, the date and amount of the first charge, whether the trial converts automatically, and how to cancel before renewal. Keep a copy of the checkout summary and terms shown at sign-up. The same terms state that subscriptions renew monthly, can be cancelled for the end of the current period and are generally non-refundable except where law requires otherwise. The site says “no lock-in” and “cancel anytime”; read those phrases together with the actual cancellation and billing terms.


## What happens to calls, transcripts and calendar data

0miss’s privacy policy distinguishes business account information from data about the business’s callers. It says the business is controller for its customer data and 0miss acts as processor under a data-processing agreement accepted during registration. The policy lists caller names and phone numbers, appointments, transcripts and, when recording is enabled and disclosed, audio. It says Google Calendar data is used to read availability and create or update booked events, and that the service stores busy time blocks rather than event names, descriptions or attendees.

That policy gives more detail than a simple “data hosted in the EU” badge. It names EU infrastructure providers, call and messaging vendors, voice and AI services, post-call text processing, payment, email and error-monitoring tools. It also says some voice, AI and messaging providers may process data outside the EU, including in the United States, and describes standard contractual clauses and additional safeguards. A clinic or other business handling sensitive information should obtain the current DPA, subprocessors, data-flow map and deletion procedure and verify them for its sector. This review does not audit those arrangements.


## Privacy policy: useful details, still a vendor disclosure

The privacy policy sets stated retention limits of 30 days for audio recordings and 90 days for transcripts in Spain, followed by automatic deletion. It says credentials for connected calendars are encrypted and can be revoked, with synced data removed when the connection is disconnected. For account and business data, it says information is held during the subscription and removed after cancellation except for records retained under legal and tax rules. These details help a buyer formulate the right questions about sensitive caller records and access.

The presence of a policy, a DPA and a named provider list does not prove compliance or the behavior of a live system. Roseram did not inspect the database, verify tenant isolation, test deletion, check encryption settings, review contracts with subprocessors or measure actual retention. The policy is dated July 15, 2026 and can change. Before deployment, compare the DPA and the live account configuration with the current policy, including whether recording is enabled, which call data is sent to each provider, and how a data-subject request is handled.


## The call-loss calculator is an estimate, not a result guarantee

0miss’s product page includes an interactive calculator that asks for missed calls per week, the share of callers who wanted to book, and average ticket value. The example defaults shown in English produce an estimate of roughly €909 in monthly missed revenue and around 26 bookings. The page labels the calculation as an estimate and describes a simple multiplication of calls, booking share and ticket. It also says the calculation runs in the browser and is not stored.

That can be a useful prompt for a business owner to gather its own call logs, but it is not evidence that 0miss will recover the full amount. The result depends on the user-entered assumptions, average transaction value, repeat visits, cancellations, capacity, lead quality and how many unanswered callers would otherwise book. It also does not subtract plan cost, forwarding fees, messaging costs, refunds or operational overhead. A realistic pilot should compare baseline missed calls with completed appointments and collected revenue, using a consistent period and not counting hypothetical bookings as realized sales.


## Service reliability, fallback behavior and vendor dependencies

An AI receptionist sits in a chain that includes the caller’s carrier, conditional forwarding, telephony, voice processing, model inference, calendar access and messaging. Any one of those pieces may be unavailable or misconfigured. The service terms state that it depends on third-party telephony, WhatsApp and AI providers and that their outages or limits can affect availability. They also disclaim uninterrupted operation and say the service does not guarantee a particular call result. This is an important counterweight to “24/7” marketing language.

Before switching on a live number, ask for a short controlled pilot and record what happens when the calendar is disconnected, the minute limit is reached, WhatsApp fails, the caller asks for something outside the configured catalogue, or a person needs urgent help. Test how quickly forwarding can be disabled and whether ordinary voicemail remains available. The site says the assistant continues with message-taking at the limit and sends the business a notification for each call, but Roseram has not verified that failover. Businesses should keep a human route for sensitive, urgent or unusual conversations.

## Who should consider 0miss—and who should pause

0miss may be worth evaluating for a small Spanish salon, clinic or appointment business where missed calls are frequent, the catalogue and availability are predictable, and a caller can be served by an automated booking flow or WhatsApp follow-up. The existing-number pitch and fixed monthly minute bundles are easy to understand. A business with several professionals may need Pro; a multi-location operator should inspect the Max or Enterprise boundary and confirm what “up to three locations” means in the account.

Pause if your operating country, carrier, language, call volume or regulatory obligations are unclear; if callers commonly share medical or financial details; if you require guaranteed uptime, contractual response times or a formal security audit; or if an AI-only first contact is unacceptable to your customers. In those cases, compare a human answering service, carrier voicemail, booking software, or a hybrid reception model. A pilot with a dedicated test number is safer than pointing a high-stakes line at any unverified automation.


## Alternatives and a fair comparison method

The alternatives are not limited to another AI receptionist. A human receptionist or answering service can handle nuance and transfer callers, but may cost more or provide narrower hours. Carrier voicemail is inexpensive and already available, though callers must leave a message and staff must return it. A booking platform can capture web reservations without handling phone calls. A hybrid service can answer routine booking questions and forward exceptions to a person. Each choice has different staffing, privacy, language and caller-experience tradeoffs.

Compare tools using the same test calls and business schedule. Measure successful appointment creation, calendar conflicts, correct messages, time to receive a lead card, caller disclosure, handling of non-booking requests, failure recovery and total minutes billed. Check the vendor’s DPA and processor list against your data needs. For 0miss, verify the advertised monthly minutes, the trial/terms mismatch, Spanish carrier coverage and retention behavior; for another provider, ask the same questions. This avoids ranking products on slogans or the illustrative €909 calculation alone.


## Buyer checklist before starting the 0miss trial

Before checkout, confirm the trial eligibility and first charge date in writing; capture the price, VAT, monthly minutes, professional and location limits; and read the Spanish terms because those say the Spanish version prevails if language versions differ. Ask the carrier for forwarding and call-charge details. Obtain the DPA, current subprocessor list and any security documentation needed by your business. Confirm the exact retention period for audio, transcripts, messages and calendar metadata, and whether recording is enabled by default or requires a separate choice.

Before going live, configure a test calendar and business catalogue, then run normal calls, rescheduling, busy-line, no-answer and out-of-hours scenarios. Listen to the AI notice, inspect the transcript, verify the booked event and WhatsApp message, and test human escalation and disable-forwarding steps. Track actual calls and completed appointments against a preselected baseline. If the assistant misunderstands, double-books, drops a handoff or consumes more minutes than expected, pause the pilot and address the configuration before exposing more callers.
