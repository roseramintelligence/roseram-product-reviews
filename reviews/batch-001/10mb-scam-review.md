# 10MB review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-16  
**Category:** Free static website hosting  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

10MB is a free static-site hosting offer for HTML, CSS and JavaScript. Its site advertises a 10 MB file limit, automatic SSL, a getsite.co subdomain and the option to claim a published site in GetSite for editing, analytics and forms. The page capture available for this review says new uploads are temporarily disabled; it was crawled three weeks before the review date, so recheck live availability. The offer does not clarify whether 10 MB applies per file or to the whole site, nor publish a service-level agreement or a detailed 10MB-specific privacy policy. GetSite’s current pricing page separately lists monthly plans for sites published through its builder, while its linked Terms still carry a 2023 date and one-time-license language. Confirm which service, terms and bill apply before relying on the handoff.

## Editorial verdict

The official 10mb.site page identifies a real static-hosting offer and explains its intended workflow, headline file limit, free subdomain, SSL claim and GetSite handoff. The material reviewed does not substantiate calling 10MB a scam. The key buyer concern is availability and scope: the captured page says uploads are disabled, the 10 MB quota is not defined as per-file or aggregate, and the relationship between free 10MB hosting and GetSite’s separate account, paid publishing plans, terms and privacy policy is not fully explained. The advertised 99.9% uptime and “secure” SSL language are not backed on the 10MB page by an SLA or operational evidence. No upload, hosted site, DNS setup, certificate renewal, uptime, account claim, analytics, form or deletion workflow was tested.

## 10MB review: a small static-hosting promise with a narrow use case

10MB is presented as a simple place to upload a static website: HTML, CSS and JavaScript files, published online with a free getsite.co subdomain. The service page advertises automatic SSL, CDN delivery, a 10 MB limit and an option to connect a custom domain after claiming the site through GetSite. That pitch is attractive for a small portfolio, a course exercise, a project microsite or a demo that does not need a server. The important distinction is that the page describes static file hosting; it does not document a backend, database, server-side code, build pipeline or production application runtime.

There is one immediate availability caveat. The page snapshot accessible for this review says that new uploads are temporarily disabled while maintenance and improvements take place. That copy was crawled three weeks before the review date, so it is not evidence that uploads remain disabled today; it is a reason to check the live form before preparing a project around the service. No upload was attempted for this review. If the upload screen is still closed, the offer cannot currently serve as an onboarding path for a new site regardless of its advertised price.


## The 10 MB cap needs a precise definition

The site says the maximum file size is 10 MB, but its public FAQ does not define whether that means one file, the total upload, the published site’s aggregate storage, or another limit. A site with a few HTML and CSS files may fit; a portfolio with high-resolution photos, video, downloadable documents or generated assets may not. Before choosing 10MB, ask which files count, whether compressed archives are accepted, how large an individual upload may be, whether replacing a file creates a version, and what happens when the site exceeds the quota.

The same principle applies to the performance claims. The page advertises a global CDN and 99.9% uptime, but the material reviewed does not give a service-level agreement, uptime history, geographic coverage details, support response commitment or remedy for extended downtime. Automatic SSL is a useful baseline, not a complete security review: buyers still need to know how custom-domain validation works, how certificates are renewed, whether security headers can be configured and who controls the deployed files. Do not infer contractual guarantees from a marketing metric.


## Free hosting and the GetSite account handoff are separate decisions

The FAQ says an owner can claim an uploaded site through GetSite and then use an AI editor to change content, add images or GIFs, forms and analytics, and link a custom domain. This creates two connected but distinct parts of the offer: the free 10MB upload site, and an account in GetSite that can edit or extend it. The 10MB page does not fully explain the feature boundary, account transfer process, billing trigger, site ownership after claiming, or what the visitor sees if a paid GetSite plan is cancelled.

GetSite’s current pricing page lists Maker at $10 per month, Developer at $40 and Agency at $160 for its own website-building and publishing plans. Those are not presented as the price of 10MB’s basic free hosting, and a buyer should not assume that every 10MB site incurs them. The uncertainty is which parts of editing, forms, analytics, custom domains and hosting become paid after a claim. The linked GetSite Terms page is dated December 11, 2023 and contains a single-purchase/no-recurring-fee license description, while the current pricing page lists monthly subscriptions. Because the pages do not make the 10MB-to-GetSite legal and billing relationship clear, ask which contract and plan apply to the exact workflow you intend to use.


## Privacy and ownership need more detail before uploading sensitive material

The GetSite privacy page associated with the handoff is a short, general policy. It says personal information is collected when needed, may include a Google profile name and image, is retained as long as needed and is protected using commercially acceptable measures; it also describes GetSite as both controller and processor. It does not give a detailed 10MB-specific list of data fields, hosting subprocessors, retention periods for published assets, access logs, backups or a deletion/export process. The linked policy is dated 2023. A developer putting up a public demo may accept that uncertainty; a business site containing customer submissions, account credentials or regulated data should first obtain the current service-specific privacy and security terms.

Keep the first upload free of secrets and personal records. Static files become publicly accessible when published, so remove API keys, private configuration, unpublished customer data and documents that were not meant for public distribution. If using the GetSite editor or forms, check separately where submissions are stored, who receives notifications, how to delete a site, and whether a custom domain and its DNS stay under your own account. Ask for a written answer before treating an upload-only host as a managed data service.


## How 10MB compares with other static hosts

For a simple personal site, compare 10MB with a repository-based static host and a CDN-backed static deployment service. Cloudflare Pages currently documents a maximum individual asset of 25 MiB and a 20,000-file limit on its Free plan; paid Pages plans can raise the file count to 100,000 under stated configuration conditions. Those limits are a useful technical comparison, not proof that a static project will deploy successfully or that any particular support or uptime commitment applies. A builder interface and prebuilt subdomain may be easier for a beginner; a Git-based service may fit a developer who wants version history and repeatable deployment.

GitHub Pages is another familiar alternative for documentation and project pages, but GitHub explicitly says Pages is not intended or allowed as free hosting for an online business, e-commerce site or commercial SaaS. That makes it a poor fit when the site itself facilitates transactions or sells software. Select the host based on the use, file footprint, build process, custom-domain control, privacy expectations, portability and acceptable outage risk—not simply the word “free.” For any provider, retain a local copy of the site and test that it can be moved without proprietary editor state.


## What to check before using 10MB

A useful trial, once uploads are available, is a small, non-sensitive site with a handful of HTML, CSS and JavaScript files. Verify that the finished page resolves on the supplied subdomain, that links and relative asset paths work, that a changed file can be updated, and that the SSL certificate is active. Test from a phone as well as a desktop. Record the response headers and the deployed asset size if the project matters to a client. This review did not perform those checks, so they are suggested buyer steps rather than claims about the service’s performance.

Before adopting it for a business, ask support to confirm the 10 MB quota semantics, upload status, custom-domain requirements, retention and deletion, available backups, support channel, uptime remedy and terms applying after a site is claimed. If the website uses forms, analytics or an AI editor, ask what account, plan and data-processing terms enable those features. Keep the source code, domain registrar and any contact list in accounts you control. A host should make it easy to export and restore a site, not strand the only working copy in an account whose pricing or ownership rules you have not checked.


## 10MB scam review verdict: no scam evidence, but confirm current access and the handoff terms

The evidence reviewed describes a recognizable static-site hosting offer: free hosting, a getsite.co subdomain, an advertised 10 MB cap, automatic SSL, CDN delivery and an optional handoff into GetSite’s editor. It does not substantiate calling 10MB a scam. The practical risks are more ordinary and still important: upload availability may have changed since the page capture, the size limit is ambiguous, uptime is a marketing claim without a stated SLA, and the free-hosting-to-paid-editor relationship and applicable legal terms are not clearly mapped in the 10MB page.

This is a source-based desk review, not a live upload test, uptime study, security audit, privacy assessment or endorsement. The official 10MB page and associated GetSite pages were reviewed November 17, 2026, with the upload notice’s crawl date disclosed above. Check the current upload screen, ask which terms govern the service, confirm the total cost after claiming a site and keep a portable copy of every file before relying on it for a business or customer-facing project.

- Static website uploads using HTML, CSS and JavaScript, with no server-side runtime described on the 10MB page
- Free hosting and a getsite.co subdomain according to the provider
- A stated 10 MB maximum file-size limit, with the exact quota unit left unclear
- Automatic SSL certificate claim and provider-stated global CDN delivery and 99.9% uptime
- Option to claim an uploaded site in GetSite for AI editing, forms, analytics and custom-domain connection
- No code editor or build pipeline is specified for the standalone 10MB upload flow

## Pricing considerations

The 10MB landing page advertises hosting as free but the relationship to GetSite’s current paid builder plans is not fully specified. GetSite’s linked Terms page is dated December 11, 2023 and contains single-purchase/no-recurring-fee language that does not align cleanly with the current monthly pricing page. Confirm which current terms apply to a claimed 10MB site, including renewal, data export, domain ownership and cancellation.
