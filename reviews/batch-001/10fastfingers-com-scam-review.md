# 10FastFingers review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-17  
**Category:** Free typing tests, practice and multiplayer games  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

10FastFingers is a browser-based typing practice and competition site. Its current public pages describe a free one-minute typing test, advanced word lists, text practice, custom tests, competitions and multiplayer sessions. A free account saves results and progress; the public pages say no account is required to take a test and that the service is free. Its privacy notice describes both short-retention statistics for anonymous test results and longer retention for registered activity, as well as advertising and third-party cookies. The site’s legal pages identify Creationes Virtuales CV GmbH, while the footer also displays “10FastFingers, Inc.”; the entity wording should be clarified for a buyer or business relying on the service.

## Editorial verdict

The evidence reviewed supports a real, usable typing-test and practice service, not a finding of fraud. Its public pages offer the core tests without payment and provide an imprint, privacy notice, cookie information and account-deletion guidance. The meaningful diligence points are the ad-supported privacy model, a privacy document whose stated effective date is January 28, 2019 despite a newer detailed statistics section, and inconsistent company naming between the imprint/privacy text and the site footer. No account, data export, cookie opt-out, paid transaction or independent security assessment was performed.

## 10FastFingers review: quick typing practice with public competition

10FastFingers is a browser-based place to measure typing speed, practice text and compare results. The core test lasts one minute and offers a normal mode based on common words and an advanced mode with a larger word list where available. The site also lists text practice, custom tests, multiplayer and competitions. Because the test begins in a browser, it can be tried without installing a desktop program or creating an account.

This concise format is useful for a quick baseline or a short practice routine. It is not a complete typing curriculum by itself: the public pages focus on timed practice, user-created text and competition rather than a structured course that diagnoses technique and guides a beginner lesson by lesson. A learner can still use the repetition productively, but should pair speed scores with deliberate practice on accuracy, posture and the keys that cause errors.


## How the typing test scores speed and accuracy

The official test page says the result includes words per minute, accuracy, and correct and incorrect keystrokes. Its WPM convention counts five keystrokes as one word, which creates a consistent unit when evaluating a run. The site also notes that the test uses common words and omits capitals and punctuation in its standard baseline. Results therefore describe performance under that test’s particular conditions, not a universal measure of writing speed or workplace productivity.

Cross-language comparisons need care. Word length and character input differ across languages and keyboard layouts, and the site’s own guidance recommends comparing typists using the same language. A score can also move with the selected mode, text familiarity, keyboard, device, autocorrect behavior and whether a user corrects mistakes. For progress tracking, repeat the same language and mode, record accuracy alongside speed and avoid reading a single leaderboard position as a formal assessment.


## Practice modes, language choice and account features

The public product pages list a standard test, an advanced list, text practice, a custom mode, competitions and multiplayer races. Custom tests can use a chosen duration and the user’s own words; the FAQ describes a range from 10 seconds to 10 minutes. The current language selector lists 53 languages on the test page, while the homepage describes a separate multiplayer mode in 54 languages. Those counts refer to different parts of the product and should not be collapsed into a promise that every mode supports every language.

The site says an account is optional for testing. Its FAQ describes a free account as a way to save results, show progress, appear on high-score lists and access achievements and competitions. The public FAQ says everything on 10FastFingers is free. No paid consumer plan was identified in the homepage, test and FAQ pages checked on November 17, 2026. That is a time-bounded observation, not a guarantee about future offers or a claim that the site has no advertising revenue.


## Privacy: test records, retention and advertising cookies

The privacy page gives a fairly specific account of typing-test statistics. For visitors without an account, it says a completed test sends mode, language, speed, accuracy, keystrokes, words, duration, local hour and weekday, together with country derived from the IP address, device type and a pseudonymous daily identifier. The policy says the raw IP is not stored for that statistics purpose, the daily key is deleted after 48 hours, individual records are removed after three days, and daily aggregate totals remain. For registered users, the policy says game types per active day are kept for retention calculations for up to 400 days and are deleted when the account is deleted.

The same site policy describes essential, performance, functionality and targeting or advertising cookies, including Mediavine programmatic advertising and third-party tags. This is relevant even though the typing test itself is free. Users who prefer less cross-site advertising should read the cookie controls and available opt-outs, and understand that blocking cookies may limit some features without necessarily removing all ads. The document’s detailed statistics language appears alongside a stated effective date of January 28, 2019; that age makes it reasonable to ask the operator for the current notice and confirm whether the newer data-retention description is the operative policy.


## Company identity and account deletion

The imprint and privacy notice name Creationes Virtuales CV GmbH and give a Cologne address and commercial-register details. The website footer, however, displays “10FastFingers, Inc.” The materials reviewed do not explain the relationship between these names. This discrepancy is a verification question, not evidence by itself that the service is fraudulent. For a personal typing test the practical exposure may be limited; for a school, employer or procurement team, confirm which entity operates the service, controls user data and would answer a contractual or privacy request.

The FAQ says an account can be deleted from account settings and warns that deletion cannot be restored; it also says the same email cannot be used to create another account for 30 days. The privacy notice separately describes deletion of registered activity records when an account is deleted, though it may retain information for legal obligations and other listed purposes. Before deleting an account, export anything you need, review the current controls and consider whether public high scores or shared results have separate removal steps.


## What the free model means for users and organizations

The free access claim is clear in the product FAQ, but the policy’s advertising disclosures show how a free website can still be funded. An individual using an unregistered session avoids the saved-profile features, yet completed-test statistics and cookies remain relevant according to the privacy page. A school or employer should consider this distinction before directing many users to a public site, particularly if participants are minors or the activity is part of a tracked assessment. The product does not present itself in the reviewed pages as a certified testing or hiring-assessment service.

If a score will affect a decision, set the rules in advance: device and keyboard, language, test mode, duration, accommodation and whether the result needs to be supervised. A one-minute online result is a practice metric, not a validated employment credential. Avoid collecting usernames or screenshots unless they are needed, and explain the advertising and data practices to participants before use.


## Alternatives and a fair comparison

For a lightweight speed check, compare 10FastFingers with other browser-based typing tests and typing-practice sites. If the goal is learning touch typing, evaluate whether an alternative offers a progressive curriculum, lessons, feedback and accessibility support. If the goal is team competition, compare private-room controls, moderation, account requirements and data retention. No competitor was benchmarked in this review, so these are comparison criteria rather than a claim that one service is faster, safer or more accurate.

A fair pilot is simple: use the same device, language, mode and duration across several sessions, note speed and accuracy together, and see whether the result is saved only when signed in. Review cookie settings before the test and verify the account deletion path before adding a class or team. The most useful service is the one whose practice format motivates consistent use without collecting more information than your setting permits.


## Is 10FastFingers a scam? Evidence-based conclusion

The official site currently offers typing tests and practice modes without an account or payment, and publishes an FAQ, privacy and cookie information and an imprint identifying an operator. The evidence reviewed does not substantiate calling 10FastFingers a scam. The actual review findings are more specific: the service is free according to its public FAQ, it describes collection and retention of detailed test statistics, it uses advertising cookies including Mediavine disclosures, the privacy page carries an old effective date, and the site footer’s company name differs from the named entity in the legal pages.

This is a source-based desk review, not a hands-on score test, account deletion, cookie audit, privacy compliance assessment or verification of the company’s corporate relationship. Before using it for a school or business, read the current privacy notice, confirm the operating entity, test account and cookie controls, and decide whether a free ad-supported practice site fits the audience. Individual users can try the test without signup and avoid relying on a single WPM score as a full measure of typing ability.
