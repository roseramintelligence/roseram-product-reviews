# A2X review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-17  
**Category:** Ecommerce accounting reconciliation software  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

A2X converts marketplace settlement and payout activity into summarized accounting entries for systems such as QuickBooks, Xero and NetSuite. Its practical value depends on correct channel connections, careful mappings and a reconciliation process that catches duplicates and exceptions.

## Editorial verdict

The public evidence describes a genuine ecommerce accounting integration product, not a guarantee of error-free books or tax advice. A2X can reduce repetitive settlement entry, but the seller or accountant still needs to validate mappings, tax treatment, channel coverage, plan limits and every material reconciliation.

## A2X ecommerce accounting review: what the service actually does

A2X addresses a familiar bookkeeping problem: a marketplace payout is a net amount, while the underlying activity may include orders, refunds, fees, taxes, shipping, reserves and adjustments. Rather than treating the bank deposit as sales revenue, A2X imports settlement data and prepares summarized entries for an accounting system. Its support material describes Shopify payouts being posted in batches that can be matched to corresponding bank deposits. That is a sensible workflow for a seller whose current books contain one vague deposit where several financial events should be recorded separately.

The distinction matters for an **A2X accounting review**. The product is an integration and accounting workflow aid. It is not a substitute for an accountant's decisions about revenue recognition, tax liability, inventory policy, marketplace rules or the chart of accounts. A clean-looking entry is useful only if it faithfully represents the seller's real activity. The public documentation supports the narrower claim that A2X organizes settlement information for reconciliation; it does not prove that every configuration will produce correct financial statements for every business.


## A2X Shopify payout reconciliation and the trial workflow

A2X's Shopify setup guide says it imports payouts and sales and can represent sales, refunds, discounts, taxes and shipping for reconciliation. Shopify Payments payouts are described as batches corresponding to deposits, while other payment gateways may require their own clearing accounts and configuration. A seller should therefore begin with a small period containing a normal payout, a refund, a discount, multiple tax rates and any non-Shopify payment method actually used. Compare the A2X entry with the source payout and the accounting ledger line by line.

The trial should test the awkward settlement, not just the easiest one. A2X's support material says the trial fetches a limited set of settlements and provides a small number of entries to review, send or export; subscription plans determine additional history. That gives a practical proof-of-fit path, but the user should record what the trial has and has not imported before relying on it for a close. Ask whether the trial sample includes the relevant currencies, marketplaces and unusual transaction types in the intended account.


## Supported channels, accounting systems and connection scope

A2X lists integrations for Amazon, Shopify, eBay, Etsy, Walmart, TikTok Shop, PayPal and multi-channel workflows, with accounting destinations including QuickBooks, Xero and NetSuite on the published product pages. The support center also describes Sage and manual-export options in some channel setups. The exact combination matters: a seller should confirm the connector for the marketplace and accounting edition they use instead of inferring support from a logo elsewhere on the site. Regional availability, marketplace account type and accounting software permissions can affect the connection.

Connecting the service grants it a role in moving business financial data between sales channels and the books. A2X's public GDPR explainer describes the categories it imports and the accounting objects it can create, while current onboarding guidance notes that permissions may be required on both the ecommerce store and accounting system. Use a dedicated administrator-approved connection, limit access to the accounts in scope and document who can reconnect or change the integration. Before authorizing, review the permissions presented by the marketplace and accounting provider themselves.


## A2X mappings, tax categories and month-end controls

The central implementation task is mapping each transaction type to the right ledger account and tax rate. A seller should not accept a default mapping simply because the setup wizard completes. Compare sales, shipping, marketplace fees, refunds, promotional discounts, tax, gift cards, reserves and adjustments against the actual statement and the accountant's approved treatment. For cost of goods sold or inventory workflows, test a known SKU and purchase-cost example separately, because inventory accounting adds assumptions that do not follow automatically from payout reconciliation.

Treat the first close as a controlled parallel run. Keep the existing method in place, record the source settlement totals, inspect A2X's draft entry, send it only after review, then match the resulting transaction to the bank deposit. Investigate a one-cent difference rather than force a match; foreign exchange, timing, reserves or a configuration mismatch may explain it. Store the reconciliation notes alongside the period close so a second person can reproduce the conclusion. This makes the product's automation auditable instead of turning it into a black box.


## Avoiding duplicate revenue across Shopify and marketplace connectors

A particularly useful A2X support article addresses orders that pass through Shopify while also belonging to Amazon, Walmart or eBay. If the seller connects both the marketplace and Shopify without excluding the overlapping channel in one account, the same economic activity may be represented twice. A2X explains that direct connections to the individual marketplace capture settlement components that do not necessarily pass through the Shopify order integration, and recommends configuring exclusions where appropriate.

This is a setup risk rather than evidence of misconduct. It is also exactly the sort of detail that can quietly distort revenue if an implementation is rushed. Build a channel map before onboarding: every marketplace, store, payment gateway, settlement account and accounting connector should have an owner and a defined coverage boundary. Use a handful of known transactions to check that each sale is recorded once and that its related fees and payout are represented. Revisit the map when a new storefront, fulfillment integration or payment gateway is introduced.


## A2X pricing, order allowances and what to confirm before paying

A2X's pricing pages show different starting prices by channel. Several channel plans currently start at US$29 per month, Walmart begins higher, and the multi-channel calculator prices a combined order allowance and the number of connected channels. The public pages also show higher-volume tiers, and certain plans include more historical data, support or inventory-related functionality. A headline starting price is therefore not enough to estimate the actual cost for a multi-store business with a growing order count.

Before checkout, capture the exact plan name, billing interval, currency, monthly order threshold, number of connected accounts, historical import allowance, overage or upgrade behavior and the features required for the close. Ask how an unexpectedly busy month is handled and whether a threshold change triggers an automatic plan change. If the business is seasonal, price the peak period rather than only the annual average. These are routine subscription checks, but they matter because the workflow becomes operationally important once settlement posting depends on it.


## A2X data handling, permissions and privacy review

A2X's public privacy policy and support materials should be read alongside the connection scopes the user is asked to approve. The service necessarily processes settlement and accounting data; its documentation says certain marketplace imports avoid customer-identifying information, but the exact data depends on the channel and enabled workflow. A2X's GDPR explainer describes hosting and data-use details, though some of that page is several years old. For a present-day procurement decision, ask support for the current data-processing agreement, subprocessors, hosting region, retention schedule, incident process and the controls that apply to the chosen product.

A public privacy page is a starting point, not an independent security assessment. Finance records may reveal sales volume, supplier costs, refunds, tax treatment and seasonality even where buyer names are absent. Limit access to staff who need it, protect the connected accounting account with strong authentication and ensure that a former bookkeeper or agency loses access promptly. For a regulated or cross-border business, obtain written confirmation of data residency and transfer arrangements rather than relying on generalized marketing statements.


## A2X cancellation, export and operational continuity

A2X's current cancellation guide states that cancelling stops further billing but also ends the paid service immediately, returning the account to limited trial mode. The same guide advises users to export any information they need first and says cancelled paid accounts are automatically deleted after 90 days unless they resubscribe. That timing is consequential for a firm using the system as part of its monthly accounting routine. Cancellation is not simply a switch that leaves every imported record available indefinitely.

Before ending a subscription, export the settlement history, mapping configuration, reconciliation reports and invoices needed for tax records or an audit. Confirm with the accountant that the accounting ledger contains the finalized entries and that no future payouts remain to be processed. If switching providers, run a sample period through both workflows and reconcile the difference before removing the old connection. The cancellation guide is specific enough to support a checklist, but users should still verify their account's actual deletion date and any retention exceptions directly with A2X.


## A2X verdict: useful reconciliation layer with accounting-owner responsibility

For an ecommerce seller or bookkeeper who handles repeated marketplace settlements, A2X has a clearly defined job: turn payout activity into summarized entries that are easier to inspect and reconcile. The documented integrations, trial workflow, multi-channel plans, manual export paths and controls around posting make it reasonable to evaluate with a real sample month. The strongest fit is a business that already knows its accounting policy and wants help executing it consistently across several channels.

The main risks are implementation errors: wrong mappings, overlapping channel imports, unreviewed automated entries, insufficient plan history and a mistaken assumption that a net payout equals revenue. A2X's published evidence does not establish that the company is a scam. This is a source-based desk review, not a paid-account test, bookkeeping engagement or audit of the vendor. Before subscribing, verify the current plan and terms, reconcile a representative period in parallel and have a qualified finance owner approve the mappings and tax treatment.


## Features and decision criteria

- Marketplace settlement and payout imports
- Summarized accounting entries
- Sales, fees, refunds, discounts and tax mapping
- Multi-channel account linking
- Manual export and review workflows

## Pricing considerations

A2X publishes channel-specific plans starting at US$29 per month for several channels, with other channels and order volumes priced differently. Multi-channel pricing is based on combined monthly order volume and connected channels. Confirm current billing currency, order thresholds, history access and plan terms in the account before subscribing.
