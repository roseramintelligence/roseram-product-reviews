**Category:** Synthetic media detection and AI security platform  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Resemble AI's current homepage and pricing focus on detecting synthetic audio, images, and video, protecting meetings and identities, and verifying media provenance. The platform offers a free Flex account with usage-based credits, then Team and Business subscriptions with included seats and lower rates on selected services. ## Plans combine a subscription, seats and metered detection rates

Resemble AI's pricing page displays Flex at $0 per month, with one seat and pay-as-you-go credits. Team is listed at $350 per month or $280 per month when billed annually, with five seats. Business is $1,000 per month or $800 per month when billed annually, with 20 seats. Enterprise is custom priced. The plan comparison describes lower usage rates on selected services for Team and Business, with features such as batch uploads, larger files, meeting intelligence, SSO and enterprise service options varying by tier.

The subscription is only part of the cost. The same page lists audio detection by second, image detection by image, and video detection by second, with one rate for Flex and a lower rate for Team and Business. Intelligence, Identity search, watermarker encode and decode, and agent detection use still other units. A buyer should estimate workload from the real object and unit count: audio duration, videos processed, images, meetings, and calls. A low subscription cost does not mean unlimited operations, and an included seat count does not specify the full meter.


## Credits and annual terms deserve a line-by-line budget check

The official pricing FAQ says Flex users load credits and pay for services as used, without a subscription fee or minimum commitment, and that Flex credits do not expire. That can suit an evaluation or irregular workload, but it still requires estimating the cost of each operation. Team and Business make the recurring commitment visible and offer lower rates on selected services. Their annual prices are monthly equivalents that come with an upfront annual bill, not month-to-month rates.

Before selecting a paid plan, confirm how the service counts a multi-minute video, a long meeting, an image batch, and an identity search. Include seats, usage, taxes, and any annual minimum. The Terms page says a subscription renews for another term unless cancelled with 30 business days' notice, unless an order form says otherwise. Record the renewal date and cancellation method before checkout. If the seller provides an enterprise order form, compare its terms with the public pricing page rather than assuming the public plan table controls every detail.


## A detection score is evidence to inspect, not a verdict about a person

The Terms of Service state that detection results are probabilistic assessments and may include false positives and false negatives. They say the result depends on conditions beyond the provider's control, including network, device, codec, and media quality, and they prohibit relying on a result as the sole basis for adverse action such as denying access, discipline, or termination. This is an important product limit, not a minor disclaimer: the output is one signal for a review process, not proof that a person created or altered media.

A responsible workflow should preserve the original file, record how it was obtained, note transformations and compression, and send uncertain cases to a trained human reviewer. For account, employment, legal, or financial decisions, use corroborating evidence and a documented process with an appeal path. A detector may help prioritize inspection; it cannot infer intent, identity, permission, or truth from a score alone. Buyers should ask how confidence is represented, what context the tool returns, and how people can contest a mistaken result.


## Meeting detection shifts notice and consent duties to the customer

The Terms describe meeting capture through an automated participant that appears in the meeting roster or through a customer-enabled compliance-recording service. The customer selects which users, groups, and meetings are in scope. The customer is also responsible for required notices and consents, including requirements tied to recording, wiretap, all-party consent, and biometric privacy laws. The choice of integration does not remove those responsibilities. An invisible recording path therefore requires particularly clear governance and appropriate participant information.

Before enabling meeting detection, identify the meeting types, participant locations, legal basis, retention, and access roles. Make the integration visible to administrators and check that calendar permissions cover only what is needed. A pilot should use a meeting where participants have been informed and consented as required. Keep an audit record of what was captured and who reviewed the result. These are proposed compliance checks, not legal conclusions about any jurisdiction or assertions that Resemble AI handles a specific customer's meeting without authorization.


## Privacy terms distinguish customer voice data from training and detection telemetry

Resemble AI's Privacy Policy says it does not use customer voice data to train general-purpose models or for research or aggregated training beyond providing the contracted service. The Terms also say Detection Media is not used to train, fine-tune, or improve models unless the customer expressly opts in in writing. The Terms separately allow certain aggregated, derivative metadata that is not identifiable to a customer or content to be used for service improvement and related purposes. These statements concern different data categories and should not be collapsed into a blanket no-data-use claim.

The Privacy Policy describes voice and facial characteristics as sensitive biometric identifiers, used only for the service purpose and, where applicable, on customer instructions. It says such identifiers are destroyed once the initial purpose is fulfilled, and no later than three years after collection unless law requires retention. The Terms allow a customer to request deletion of specific content and say it will be deleted within 30 days, subject to legal retention. They also say Resemble does not maintain backups of customer content, so a buyer remains responsible for keeping its own authorized copies.


## Voice generation and cloning need evidence of permission

The company's terms define uploaded voice recordings as content used to create AI voice models and require the user to own the material or have necessary licenses, rights, and consent. They say Resemble may require consent from the person whose voice is cloned, in the form specified, and its voice-creation page describes consent verification as part of certain cloning workflows. A publicly available clip is not automatically permission to clone a voice. Teams should keep a recorded authorization that covers the actual generation and distribution plan.

The same account may encounter both generation and detection products, but permissions differ by purpose. Consent to create a voice model is not consent to capture a meeting, and permission to record a meeting is not permission to clone a speaker. Decide which service is being used and what content enters it before enabling integrations. If the workflow includes minors, public figures, biometric data, or meeting participants, obtain specialist review and confirm the current platform restrictions before proceeding.


## Evaluate detector performance on a balanced, authorized sample

A detector evaluation should include authentic and generated examples across the actual channels, compression levels, languages, and devices the organization expects to see. Keep labels and ground truth separate from the model output. Measure false positives and false negatives, and identify a threshold for human review rather than making a binary pass/fail rule. Ask the provider what its score means, what types of media it accepts, and how the result changes with a re-encoded or shortened clip.

This is a proposed buyer test; Roseram has not tested Resemble AI's detection performance or verified its advertised benchmarks. Do not collect private individuals' media without the appropriate legal basis and notice. For meeting use, document the enabled integration, participant communication, and access scope. Test deletion and confirm the output can be explained to a reviewer. A successful pilot should show the detector's role in a broader evidence process, not claim that software can determine intent or conclusively identify a speaker.


## Who may consider Resemble AI, and what should they compare?

Resemble AI may fit an organization that needs to inspect synthetic media, protect an enrolled identity, assess meeting content, or add an API-based verification step. The public pricing page offers a credit-based Flex path and recurring team plans, which can help buyers estimate operational fit. It may be unsuitable where a detector score would be treated as conclusive or where a team cannot meet participant notice, consent, retention, and escalation requirements. A review tool does not eliminate the human and governance work around a sensitive decision.

Compare other detection systems and forensic review procedures using the same authorized sample and cost model. Include false-positive handling, auditability, APIs, integration permissions, rate limits, retention, data residency, seats, recurring terms, and unit rates. If the need is voice generation rather than detection, evaluate that specific offering and license separately; the current homepage's emphasis does not prove all older voice products are self-serve. Ask for current product documentation and an order form when the purchase is material.
