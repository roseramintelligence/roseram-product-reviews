# RunwayML review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-18  
**Category:** AI video, image and audio production  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

Runway is a generative-media platform for video, image and audio workflows, with first-party tools and a changing set of partner models. Its web plans use credits for many generations; credit costs vary by model, duration, resolution and output format. Free access is a one-time exploration allowance, not a recurring monthly grant. Paid plans differ in monthly credits, model access, storage, team features and certain modes, while API and agent-driven use follow separate credit rules. Buyers should compare the cost of accepted footage, revision time and export needs rather than treating all generations as equivalent.

## Editorial verdict

This is a source-based buyer review, not a fraud finding, benchmark or claim of hands-on production. Runway publishes product, pricing and credit documentation that describes its tools and usage rules. Those sources do not establish that a particular generated clip will fit a campaign, that every model is available on every tier, or that a subscriber's intended use is legally cleared. No Runway project was generated for this review. Verify plan, mode, model cost, rights and checkout terms before purchase.

## Runway is a production workspace where model choice changes the cost

Runway combines a creative editor with generative tools for video, images and audio. Its model catalog includes Runway's own systems and third-party offerings, and the available options can change. That flexibility may help a producer compare different approaches without moving every project to a new service. It also makes plan entitlements and generation settings important: the same creative brief can have a different cost and result depending on model, duration, resolution, aspect ratio and whether audio or an edit is included.

The publisher describes Gen-4.5 text-to-video and image-to-video workflows with shot controls and durations from two to ten seconds. A short clip can be useful for an ad concept, storyboard or social cut, but it may take repeated generations to achieve a usable sequence. Treat a generation as an experiment, not a finished deliverable. Before paying, define how many accepted seconds you need, the format your editor requires and the number of revision rounds you expect.


## Free credits are a trial allowance, not an ongoing monthly plan

Runway's help center describes a one-time deposit of 125 credits for the Free plan, with 5 GB of asset storage and a limited selection of tools. The grant does not replenish each month. The same page says model availability can change and that videos generated on Free include a watermark. That makes the free plan useful for exploring the interface or testing whether a workflow is plausible; it is not a predictable recurring budget for regular publishing.

Check the current model list before building a project around an item you saw in a demo. If the model is unavailable on Free or the allowance is exhausted, the help page says you need a paid plan to continue generating or buying additional credits. Save your assets and notes outside the service if they are important. A free account can help you learn the workflow, but it should not be mistaken for commercial access or a guarantee that a specific generation mode remains available.


## Model-specific credit rates make a per-second estimate essential

The official Gen-4.5 guide lists a rate of 12 credits per second and output durations of two to ten seconds. At that published rate, a five-second generation uses 60 credits and a ten-second generation uses 120. The general credit guide also warns that listed rates are not always the full cost of a task. Output format, resolution, added audio, model choice and editing operations can affect the final amount. Check the current settings and cost display before each run.

For budgeting, estimate the number of attempts required to produce one accepted clip rather than counting only the final seconds. A 10-second result may consume credits each time you retry, and the first output can be discarded. Make a small pilot with the real brief, measure how many drafts pass review and include the cost of image references, edits and upscales. The resulting cost per approved asset is a more useful comparison than the lowest advertised rate.


## Monthly, annual, and team allowances need a like-for-like comparison

The pricing page showed Free, Standard, Pro, Max, Team and Enterprise options when checked on November 18, 2026. The displayed yearly rates reduce the monthly equivalent but commit the buyer to an annual bill. The credit guide lists 625 monthly credits for Standard, 2,250 for Pro and 9,500 for Max, with Team's pooled allowance described per seat. These numbers are useful only alongside the selected mode and eligible models. A monthly credit balance cannot be compared directly with one-time free credits or API credits.

The plan guide also describes changes to the Max offering and a transition from legacy Unlimited for existing subscribers. That is a reminder to check whether a name on an older page still refers to a current purchase option. Confirm the billing interval, total amount due, credit reset and rollover, storage and collaboration terms before checkout. For a team, test whether seats draw from a shared pool and who can buy top-ups. Budget for the plan as it exists today, not as a past promotional screenshot showed it.


## Web-app, MCP and API use are different billing paths

Runway says that credits purchased for the web product are separate from API credits. Its MCP documentation also says agent generations use the account's credit balance and do not inherit some modes that can be free or unlimited inside the web app. An action that costs no credits in a particular interface may therefore consume credits when called through an agent connection. A developer should treat every connected workflow as an independent cost center until the vendor's documentation confirms otherwise.

Before connecting an agent, write down which account is charged, which model is selected, whether a retry triggers another charge and where the usage can be reviewed. Use a small non-critical test and compare the balance before and after. For an application built on the API, maintain a separate budget from the subscription plan and keep a limit on retries. The billing distinction is easy to miss when products share a brand, but it can materially change the unit economics of an automated workflow.


## Creative quality must be tested against the brief and the final delivery format

Prepare a benchmark brief with a clear subject, action, camera movement, mood and ending. Include an image-to-video example if your real work depends on a brand image or product reference. Judge the output for motion consistency, subject identity, readable text, transitions, sound and suitability for the destination platform. Watch the export at its final display size. A visually striking preview can still have a distracting artifact, a wrong brand detail or an edit that does not fit the required frame.

Record which settings produced each accepted clip and how much time went into revisions. Compare Runway with another model using the same prompt and references, then evaluate the result with the person who approves the campaign. This process can reveal whether a particular model is reliable enough for your task. No benchmark, quality score or production experiment was performed for this review, and the publisher's description of a model's strengths should not be treated as independent proof.


## Check data, rights and team access before uploading client material

A creative workflow may involve customer images, unreleased product plans, faces, voices or licensed footage. Review Runway's current privacy, acceptable-use and content terms before submitting that material. Confirm who can access the workspace, which collaborators can export assets, how long source files remain available and what deletion controls are provided. A paid plan may add collaboration or administrative features, but the existence of a team tier alone does not establish that it satisfies an organization's security requirements.

Create a permission checklist for people and assets shown in generated media. Keep written consent and source licenses with the campaign. Determine whether the service's usage rights allow your intended distribution and whether third-party inputs carry separate restrictions. Where customer or employee data is involved, ask the vendor for the relevant contractual and retention information. This review does not offer legal clearance or a security audit; its recommendation is to resolve those questions before using real client or confidential information.


## Who should consider Runway, and what alternatives belong in the test?

Runway may fit creators who want image, video and audio tools in one web workspace and can budget for iterative, credit-metered production. It may be a poor fit for a team that needs a fixed predictable cost per finished minute, a model that is absent from its tier, strict offline operation or an integration that only exists through the API. Those are requirements to verify, not evidence against the product. A team with limited footage needs may also find that a conventional editor plus occasional generation is simpler.

Compare tools with the same source images, prompt, output duration, aspect ratio, quality rubric and review process. Include exports, watermark rules, asset storage, team access, API or agent costs and the work needed to finish the clip. If your pipeline depends on recurring volume, run several weeks of representative tasks before annual billing. Pick the tool that produces the most accepted, usable work within your real budget, not the one whose single showcase clip looks strongest.


## Features and decision criteria

- AI video, image and audio creation through a web-based creative platform.
- Gen-4.5 text-to-video and image-to-video generation with duration and aspect-ratio controls.
- A rotating catalog that includes Runway and third-party creative models.
- Credit-based billing with different monthly allowances and model-specific rates.
- Team workspace, storage and plan-dependent collaboration features.
- Separate web-app, agent/MCP and developer API billing pathways.

## Pricing considerations

The pricing page showed the monthly and discounted yearly USD rates above when checked; yearly plans are billed as an annual commitment. Plan names, prices and eligible features can change. The free 125-credit grant is one-time. Confirm the exact bill, renewal date, current model access, storage, credit rollover and terms on Runway's live pricing and checkout screens. API credit balances are separate from web-plan credits.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is RunwayML a scam?

This review found no source-based evidence for calling Runway a scam. Runway publishes a generative-media service, pricing and usage documentation. That does not independently certify model quality, uptime, output ownership or a user's particular purchase.

### Does the Runway free plan renew credits monthly?

The official help page describes a one-time 125-credit deposit that does not expire and does not renew after use. The available free models can change, and free-plan videos include a Runway watermark according to the current help page.

### How many credits does Runway Gen-4.5 use?

Runway's current Gen-4.5 documentation lists 12 credits per second, with durations from 2 to 10 seconds. Confirm the selected model and settings in the generation interface because rates and capabilities can change.

### Are Runway web credits the same as API credits?

No. Runway's official credit documentation says web-plan credits and API credits are separate balances. MCP and agent generations also consume credits and do not use some free or unlimited modes available in the web app.
