## 3veta review: one flow from discovery to a paid meeting

3veta is a scheduling and online-meeting platform for consultants, tutors, coaches, therapists and other professionals who sell time or appointments. It combines availability management, booking pages, calendar connections, meeting invitations, browser-based video and a payment option backed by Stripe. The site also describes a broader enterprise product for organizations that want a branded marketplace where clients can find and book providers.

The product’s appeal is consolidation. A solo professional may want one public link that explains a service, offers open times and leads into a video session; a small team may value shared calendars and repeatable booking steps. The tradeoff is that the business depends on several connected systems: calendar synchronization, email delivery, video calls, Stripe onboarding, payment reporting and customer records. Evaluate the entire appointment journey rather than judging only the booking-page editor.


## Plans, seat pricing and payment fees

The official pricing page currently lists Starter at US$3 per seat per month on both monthly and annual billing. Pro is US$15 per seat per month when billed monthly, or US$12 per seat per month when billed annually. The visible plan cards describe booking, scheduling, payments, browser video calls and integrations on Starter; Pro adds an extra free seat, 5,000-plus integrations and white-label options. The page also describes a Platform option for organizations interested in a marketplace, with pricing handled through sales.

The comparison table shows a platform payment-processing charge of 5% plus Stripe fees for Starter and 0% plus Stripe fees for Pro. These charges matter to a provider who collects payment through bookings: compare the combined platform and Stripe cost at realistic transaction sizes, including refunds and disputed payments. The table shows up to 50 participants in Starter meetings and up to 100 in Pro, while some recording, live-streaming and transcription capabilities are presented as contact-for-pricing add-ons. Confirm that the plan card and comparison table apply to the specific account and country before quoting a price to clients.

The trial information is inconsistent across 3veta’s own pages. The pricing page FAQ says the trial lasts 14 days, while the separate FAQ says 7 days. Both pages promote starting for free, but the exact checkout terms govern whether a payment method is requested and when billing begins. Save the plan selection and trial-conversion date, and confirm how to cancel or downgrade before starting a trial for a team.


## Scheduling, calendars and the customer booking journey

3veta’s scheduling materials describe two-way calendar synchronization, connections to Google, Outlook and iCloud calendars, availability controls, time-zone handling and automated invitations and reminders. Its booking pages can be shared or embedded, and the platform says professionals can offer public or private events. The FAQ says providers need a Stripe account to receive payments, so payment collection depends on separate onboarding and the payment provider’s country and eligibility requirements.

Test the booking flow with the calendars that the team actually uses. Create events on each connected calendar, change a time zone, block personal availability, reschedule an appointment and cancel it as both provider and client. Check for duplicate invitations, stale availability, reminder timing and who receives a cancellation notice. Where multiple staff share capacity, confirm how conflicts are prevented and how one member can take over a booking. Those behaviors affect client trust more than the number of advertised integrations.

The terms say a professional is responsible for their relationship and service with the end user, while 3veta provides the platform. The professional should therefore define session length, cancellation policy, refunds, no-show rules, service scope and support contact on the booking page. Before accepting payments, reconcile test bookings with Stripe and your accounting records. No live booking or transaction was run as part of this review.


## Video meetings, branding and useful operational tests

3veta describes browser-based audio and video calls, screen sharing, chat, waiting rooms, meeting moderation, polling and branded rooms. The feature page says users can add a logo, background and brand colors, and lists recordings, streaming and transcription as add-on capabilities. Its FAQ says the mobile app is available for iOS and Android. These are publisher descriptions; actual availability can vary by plan and should be checked in a trial account.

Before making it the default meeting room, test calls on the devices and networks your clients use. Check browser permissions, guest access, mobile joining, screen sharing, waiting-room admission, audio recovery after a dropped connection and how recording consent is presented. If a meeting may involve health, legal, education or financial information, document how recordings and transcripts are enabled, stored, downloaded and deleted. Do not assume that a feature being available means it is enabled on the plan or configured to meet a professional’s confidentiality obligations.


## Privacy, roles and older legal documents

The official Privacy Notice is dated January 5, 2022. It says the service processes account names and email addresses, optional photos, plan and payment information, calendar events and appointment details. It describes the professional as responsible for information relating to clients in the professional relationship and says the service does not access data exchanged during video calls or messages. The Provider Terms also state that the meeting connection is encrypted and that the provider processes end-user information as a processor for specified services, while the professional is the controller for their own client operations. These are published descriptions, not a separate technical audit.

The same notice says certain commercial records may be retained for up to 10 years, and the Provider Terms say some account and meeting records are retained for five years after registration ends. Providers can request retrieval of account data within one month after termination under those terms. Because the posted privacy and provider terms are several years old, teams should request the current data-processing agreement, subprocessors, hosting regions, retention schedule, recording policy and deletion process. For sensitive or regulated services, get the applicable terms and security details in writing before inviting clients.


## Payments, customer records and the business model

3veta uses Stripe for payment collection, and the FAQ says providers need their own Stripe account to receive payments. The Privacy Notice describes Stripe as a separate platform processing card information, while 3veta receives transaction details such as amount, currency, customer email and provider. A Stripe Partner Directory listing identifies 3veta in booking and scheduling and lists Stripe products in its partner profile; that establishes a partner-directory entry, not a guarantee about payout speed or a particular account’s eligibility.

A service business should reconcile the platform’s fee, Stripe charges, refunds, chargebacks, taxes and payout schedule before moving client revenue into a new workflow. Separate the subscription fee from transaction costs and determine which party handles customer questions about a service, a payment or a technical meeting issue. The Provider Terms say 3veta is not a party to the professional’s service contract with the end user and place operational responsibilities on the professional. Make the booking page clear about the business providing the service and the applicable cancellation and refund rules.


## Alternatives and a practical evaluation

Alternatives include scheduling products such as Calendly or Acuity, open-source scheduling tools such as Cal.com, appointment suites such as Square Appointments, and a separate video service such as Zoom or Google Meet. A separate stack may cost more to configure but can provide a familiar component for each task. 3veta’s advantage is its combined booking, video and payment workflow; the important question is whether that bundle handles the business’s actual calendars, branding, service catalog and payment policy with less effort.

Pilot with a real but low-risk service. Set up one provider, one calendar, a sample paid appointment and a client booking page. Measure the time to configure, check mobile usability, review the payment statement and export client/booking records. Try cancellation, refunds, staff handoff and a missed-call recovery. If the workflow passes, expand gradually and document who owns the Stripe connection, calendar integration, account recovery and data-export process. The review did not benchmark 3veta against alternatives or verify any customer testimonial.


## 3veta SCAM Review: conclusion and buyer checklist

3veta publishes product pages, plan prices, an FAQ, privacy documentation and terms for professionals. The sources reviewed support describing it as a commercial scheduling, booking and video-meeting platform; they do not substantiate a scam allegation. The strongest reasons to investigate further are concrete: annual versus monthly seat totals, the processing fee on Starter, the 7-day versus 14-day trial discrepancy, the age of the legal documents and the responsibility split for customer data and service delivery.

Before committing, confirm current trial and cancellation terms, transaction fees, seat and participant limits, recording and transcript availability, Stripe eligibility, and all plan add-ons. Request updated legal and data-processing documents, confirm export and deletion windows, and run a complete appointment from booking through payment and refund. ## Features and decision criteria

- Personal and team booking pages with appointment availability and calendar synchronization
- Browser-based audio and video meetings with screen sharing, chat and branded room options
- Stripe-connected payment collection for paid appointments and meetings
- Client and transaction records, email reminders, and integrations through connected services
- Enterprise marketplace and white-label options described separately from the self-serve Starter and Pro plans

## Pricing considerations

Prices were checked on 2026-11-17. The live pricing page says a 14-day free trial in its FAQ, while the separate official FAQ says 7 days. Confirm the trial duration, card requirement, plan after trial, taxes, user-seat counts and payment processing formula in checkout. The pricing comparison lists processing fees in addition to Stripe’s fees; confirm the exact calculation, payment types and any refunds or disputes charges before accepting customer payments.
