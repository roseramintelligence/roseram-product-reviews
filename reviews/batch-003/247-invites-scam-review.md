# 247 Invites review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-18  
**Category:** Digital invitation cards and video invitations  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

247 Invites sells customizable digital invitation cards and videos for weddings, birthdays and other events, with template editing, watermarked previews and paid downloads. Its site describes more than 1,000 designs and says user-uploaded images are deleted automatically 10 days after video creation; that is a publisher statement, not an independently audited retention guarantee. Individual listings show different prices—for example, a sampled engagement video page displayed a discounted price, a traditional wedding listing showed US$13.50, and a card listing showed US$2—so buyers should check the exact template and currency at checkout. A custom caricature job is quoted after the request. The privacy policy is dated May 23, 2018, while the general terms say they took effect in 2012 and may be changed without notice. The terms’ broad content license applies to material posted through listed open communication tools; buyers should confirm whether their uploaded invitation assets are treated differently. The evidence reviewed supports a functioning invitation business, not a substantiated scam allegation, but the dated legal documents and data-handling questions warrant care before uploading family photos.

## Editorial verdict

247 Invites offers a concrete product flow: choose a template, customize event details, preview, pay and download or share. The site lists templates and sample prices, but no single price covers every design or custom job. Its claims of instant previews, payment security and deletion of uploaded images are vendor statements. The privacy notice is dated 2018 and the terms 2012, so ask for current terms and a clear explanation of photo retention, account deletion, content licensing and support before uploading sensitive images. Nothing in the reviewed evidence substantiates calling the service a scam. A small test order, careful proofreading and a saved copy of checkout terms are sensible before relying on it for a time-sensitive event.

## What a customer can make with 247 Invites

The site presents editable video invitations and traditional card designs for several event categories. Its advertised editing options include event wording, visual style, envelope, background, photos, music and language selection, depending on the template. Some pages describe guest RSVP through a shareable invitation page. These features can be useful for a family or event organizer who wants a digital asset that can circulate through messaging apps without commissioning a fully custom design.

The exact options differ by design. A template that includes a photo or music option does not establish that every card supports those fields, and a shareable RSVP link is described for selected invitation formats. Before entering names, dates and venue details, open the specific template and confirm the fields, supported languages, output type, resolution and whether the final file can be edited after purchase. For a printed card, check dimensions, bleed and file format; for a video, check duration, aspect ratio and audio.


## Preview, payment and delivery flow

247 Invites says a buyer can personalize a design, see a watermarked preview and pay after reviewing it. Its FAQs describe redirecting the buyer to a download page after payment and keeping the invitation available in an account’s “My Videos” area. A preview-first workflow is valuable because event details are easy to mistype, but it should be tested rather than assumed to guarantee a final file with the same rendering across devices.

Before buying, proofread names, dates, venue, spelling and language in the preview at the size guests will see it. Check whether the watermark is removed from the paid file and download a local copy promptly. The site describes continued account access, but a buyer should not make the account the only archive for a one-time event asset. Save the final video and card file in at least two locations, and preserve the receipt and order number in case the download page later becomes unavailable.


## 247 Invites prices vary by format and template

There is no single public price that covers the entire catalog. At the time of review, the engagement invitation example displayed US$2.70 alongside a crossed-out US$4.50, while a traditional wedding invitation page displayed US$13.50 and the card directory showed examples at US$2. The homepage separately describes expert-created or caricature work with pricing shared after the request. These examples demonstrate variation; they should not be read as a complete or guaranteed rate card.

Check the currency, discount, included revisions, file resolution, taxes, payment method and custom-work scope at checkout. A low template price may not include a designer making bespoke changes. The site says changes beyond the preview can be treated as customization and charged based on effort. If the event requires a particular crop, additional language, more than one video revision or a printed file, get the total in writing before ordering. Keep a screenshot of the selected design and the price shown at the time of payment.


## Custom design and caricature work require a separate quote

The homepage offers custom invitations and caricature videos through a request process. It describes a two-to-four-day turnaround for a fully custom caricature project and says full pricing is shared after reviewing the request. A quote-based service can accommodate requirements that a template cannot, but the request itself is not a delivery guarantee. Clarify when the clock starts, how many previews are included, what happens if the customer requests revisions and what format is included in the final handoff.

For custom work, provide only the reference images needed to scope the concept. Ask who can view the images, whether subcontracted artists are involved, whether the deliverable is exclusive, and when working files are deleted. Confirm whether the quote covers commercial use if the invitation is for a business event. The public site’s general claim about deleting images after video creation does not answer every custom-work scenario, especially if the design needs multiple review rounds.


## Image uploads and deletion claim

The FAQ says uploaded images are stored on the service’s server and automatically deleted 10 days after the video is created. This is a useful retention statement, but it is a claim from the provider rather than independent evidence of deletion from primary storage, backups, logs or any production vendors. The public privacy policy explains general personal-data processing, but buyers should check whether it has been updated since its stated effective date and whether its terms describe the current custom-video workflow.

Avoid uploading images of children or private family members until you are comfortable with the retention and access rules. Ask whether the 10-day period also covers previews, failed jobs, support copies and backups, and whether a deletion request can be made sooner. Use the minimum-resolution image needed and remove location metadata before upload if it is not required. These precautions do not imply the service misuses images; they follow from the fact that a digital invitation may contain identifiable people and event details.


## The privacy policy is dated and merits an update check

247 Invites’ privacy policy identifies Inwiter Software Solutions Private Limited as the operator and shows an effective date of May 23, 2018. It describes personal data, account choices and ways to request changes or deletion. The site has since added or presents a broader set of invitation and custom-design workflows, so customers should ask whether this is still the operative notice and whether it covers current hosting, payment and design vendors.

The policy’s age alone does not prove poor data handling. It does mean the buyer should not assume the document reflects current processors, international transfers, retention periods, user rights or incident contacts. Request a current version before submitting sensitive photos or event lists. For corporate events, ask whether a data-processing agreement is available and whether guest RSVP data is collected, who can access it and how the event host exports or deletes it after the occasion.


## Terms of service: read the user-content clause in context

The general terms page says the agreement took effect on October 15, 2012 and may change without notice. It provides broad “as is” language and limits warranties and liability. It also grants a perpetual, irrevocable, worldwide, royalty-free, exclusive license for content posted through the open communication tools it lists, such as forums, comments or ratings. The clause expressly distinguishes that category from information supplied for registration, but the public text does not clearly explain every category of image upload used by the invitation editor.

Do not assume the open-communication license automatically governs a private design upload, and do not assume it cannot apply to material placed in a public gallery or review. Ask the company to identify the clause governing invitation photos, custom artwork, guest data and any public sharing. For original family images or client assets, get written confirmation of ownership, license scope, marketing use and deletion. A current, product-specific answer is more useful than interpreting a decade-old generic clause alone.


## Who operates the service and how to contact support

The privacy policy names Inwiter Software Solutions Private Limited as the operator, and the contact page publishes a support email, phone number and a United States mailing address. A contact page is a useful accountability signal, but it does not prove response quality or that a particular payment issue will be resolved. Save the order ID and use the official support route if a file is missing or details are wrong.

The site’s contact page lists a United States address and an Indian phone number, while the privacy notice names an Indian private limited company. Those details are not inherently inconsistent, but a buyer planning a large custom order should confirm which legal entity invoices, the payment beneficiary, the support time zone and the governing dispute venue. The terms refer to “our state” without making the specific state obvious in the text surfaced during review; ask for the applicable jurisdiction if it matters to your purchase.


## Testimonials and template counts are publisher-presented claims

The homepage advertises more than 1,000 designs and shows customer quotations about ease of use, delivery and price. These statements can help explain the intended value proposition, but they are selected by the vendor and do not provide a representative sample of all orders. A template count is also not the same as a count of distinct, currently available designs in the category a buyer needs.

Browse the actual category and inspect several templates before deciding that the catalog fits your event. For a high-stakes wedding, confirm the current preview generation and delivery time with a small test or a written support response. If reviews are central to the decision, look for independent evidence and pay attention to dates and whether the reviewer’s order can be verified. The materials reviewed do not establish an independent measure of average delivery time or customer satisfaction.


## Payment and refund questions to settle before checkout

The public material examined describes payment before download, but I did not verify a single general refund policy that clearly covers every template purchase, custom design, failed rendering and incorrect event detail. The FAQ says additional changes can be billed according to designer effort. That makes the preview and customization boundaries especially important. The checkout page and any order-specific terms should explain what can be corrected without a new fee.

Ask how the company handles a payment that succeeds while the file fails to render, a file that differs materially from its preview, a mistaken duplicate purchase or a requested cancellation before work begins. Confirm whether the payment provider is named at checkout and whether the refund goes to the original method. Do not rely on a general claim that payment processing is secure to answer what refund rights apply. Keep the order confirmation and correspondence.


## Alternatives to 247 Invites

Alternatives include a local print shop, a general graphic-design tool, a wedding planner’s invitation service, a freelance designer, or a simple event page built with an RSVP platform. A ready-made video template may be faster and less expensive than commissioning a custom animation; a print shop may be preferable when paper stock, proofing and delivery are central. A general design editor gives the organizer more control but requires more work and may not produce an animated invite.

Compare the complete workflow, not just the sticker price: preview quality, language support, guest RSVP, file ownership, revision cost, upload retention, print specifications and customer support. If the invitation includes a large guest list, check whether RSVP data can be exported and deleted. If the organizer needs a unique caricature, ask each provider about artist involvement, revision limits and rights to reuse the illustration. Choose the option that fits the event’s privacy and delivery needs.


## A sensible first-order checklist

Select the exact card or video, inspect its customization fields, confirm the output dimensions and language, review the final price and read the current privacy and terms documents. Proofread the preview with another person, especially dates and names. Ask for a written answer on photo deletion and revision charges if those details matter. Use a payment method that gives you a receipt and save a copy of the checkout terms.

After delivery, download the final asset, verify it on a phone and desktop, and store a backup. If you ordered custom work, confirm the file meets the agreed scope before approving the final handoff. Do not upload documents or sensitive personal data that the design does not require. This is a recommended buyer evaluation, not a claim that Roseram placed an order or tested the service’s image-deletion system.
