# 3-GIS Network Solutions review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-17  
**Category:** Telecom and utility network management software  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

3-GIS sells GIS-based software and services for telecom, electric and gas infrastructure. Its telecom products connect fiber and copper asset records, network design, field updates and operations; its utility portfolio covers GIS workflows, inspections, work visibility and joint-use management. The platform is aimed at organizations with complex network data, so implementation fit, integrations, data migration and commercial terms deserve more attention than a quick feature checklist.

## Editorial verdict

The public product pages describe a coherent infrastructure-management suite, named products, use cases, integrations and customer examples. No public evidence reviewed supports a scam allegation. The main diligence questions are enterprise-software ones: whether the system fits the operator’s data model, how much migration and services work is required, what deployment and support cost, and how records can be accessed if a customer later changes platforms. Pricing is handled through a sales process, so obtain a scoped written proposal and validate it against a representative network workflow.

## What 3-GIS does and who it is built for

3-GIS is enterprise software for organizations that plan, build and maintain telecommunications or utility infrastructure. In telecom, the central idea is a connected network record: locations and relationships for fiber, copper, ducts, equipment, splicing and other assets can be represented in a GIS environment and linked to design, construction and operations work. That makes the platform relevant to fiber operators, broadband providers, municipalities, engineering teams and organizations responsible for large, changing network inventories.

The company also markets products for electric and gas utilities. Its portfolio includes mobile inspection and field execution, GIS editing and validation, operational work management and joint-use workflows. These are related products, but they are not interchangeable modules with identical audiences. A prospective buyer should start from a specific operating problem—such as keeping as-built fiber records current or managing utility inspections—and map it to the exact product, edition and services proposed.


## Network records, design and operations

The published description of 3-GIS Web emphasizes a centralized representation of physical and logical network assets. Listed workflows include route planning, lateral design, work orders, project tracking, material placement, constructible work packets, connectivity tracing and network reports. In practice, the value of this approach depends on data quality: a map can look polished while still reflecting incomplete or stale asset relationships. The review question is therefore not simply “does it show the network?” but whether field changes, engineering decisions and operational records reliably converge on the organization’s authoritative data.

The company positions Mobile as a way to carry network context into the field, where teams can search records, document inspections, take photos, add redlines and report as-built changes. This can reduce the delay between construction and the office record, but only if offline behavior, synchronization conflicts, device support, permissions and quality checks fit the real field environment. Ask to see those workflows on representative data and the actual devices crews use. A short scripted demo should include a disconnected field session and a change that conflicts with an existing edit.


## Utility products and the importance of product boundaries

For utility customers, 3-GIS lists MIMS for mobile inspections and field execution, Productivity for Utility Network editing and automation, and Lifecycle for operational visibility and work management. The vendor’s FAQs describe Productivity workflows such as validation, tracing, versioning, map production and scheduled or event-driven processes. MIMS is described as a mobile application for iOS and Windows. These details help a buyer identify which product to assess, but marketing pages do not replace a technical architecture review.

Ask which components are included in the proposal, which require separate licenses, and how data moves between the products and the customer’s existing GIS, work management, asset, CRM or OSS/BSS systems. Confirm whether workflows use the customer’s current Esri Utility Network data model, what versions are supported, and whether implementation requires restructuring or cleansing records. The vendor FAQ says Productivity is data-model agnostic; validate what that means for the specific schema and custom rules already in production.


## Pricing, services and total cost of ownership

The public pages reviewed focus on product capabilities and invite buyers to contact the company; they do not provide a standard self-serve price list. For enterprise GIS, a quote may need to account for product scope, users, hosting or deployment, data conversion, integrations, training, support and professional services. A headline license number alone will not show the cost of moving an operational network record into the system or keeping it accurate over time.

Request a written proposal that separates recurring license fees from implementation, migration, integration, support and optional modules. Ask which costs renew, what constitutes a billable change request, whether test or training environments are included, and what price protections apply. Build a three-year estimate using the expected number of editors, field users, integrations and data volumes. The published case study can illustrate one deployment story, but its reported context should be treated as vendor-published evidence rather than an independently audited performance guarantee.


## Security, privacy and data governance questions

The 3-GIS privacy policy describes personal information collected through its website and communications. That policy is not a complete security assessment of a customer’s operational network deployment. Utility and telecom records can be sensitive, so buyers should ask for the service-specific security package: hosting locations, encryption, identity controls, audit logs, backup and recovery targets, incident notification terms, subprocessors, retention, export options and any independent assurance reports.

Also settle data ownership and exit mechanics before implementation. Identify which records are customer data, what export formats are available, how often a full extract can be made, whether metadata and attachments are included, and what assistance or charges apply when a contract ends. These are ordinary enterprise procurement checks, not evidence of misconduct. They reduce the risk that a technically successful deployment becomes difficult to operate or leave.


## Alternatives and a disciplined evaluation

Compare 3-GIS against the GIS and network-management tools already available to the organization, including Esri-centered workflows, specialist telecom inventory systems and internal applications. Keep the comparison grounded in the same test cases: design a route, trace a path, verify capacity, post a field redline, find an affected service area, run a data-quality check and export the resulting records. Score data accuracy, edit governance, field usability, integration effort, query performance, training and three-year cost.

Do not evaluate only through a scripted demonstration built on clean sample data. Use a small, representative dataset with known gaps and edge cases, then ask the vendor and customer team to show how errors are found, corrected and audited. Record what required customization. No 3-GIS implementation, field test, performance benchmark or customer interview was conducted for this review, so this is a source-led buying analysis rather than a hands-on endorsement.


## Is 3-GIS Network Solutions a scam? Review conclusion

The company publishes identifiable product pages, technical FAQs, privacy and website terms, partner information and customer case material. Its descriptions are consistent with a specialized GIS platform for infrastructure networks. Nothing in the sources reviewed substantiates describing 3-GIS as a scam.

The practical risk is buying a system before confirming the data model, deployment scope, integration responsibilities, implementation cost and exit rights. Ask for a scoped proof of concept, a detailed commercial schedule and written answers from the technical and security teams. This review did not independently verify customer references or inspect a live tenant; it evaluates the public information and identifies the questions a serious enterprise buyer should resolve.
