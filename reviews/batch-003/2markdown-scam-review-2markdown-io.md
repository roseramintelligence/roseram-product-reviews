# 2markdown review: features, pricing and buyer considerations

**Last editorial review:** 2026-11-18  
**Category:** Markdown conversion and document workflow  
**Method:** Source-led desk review; no hands-on testing is claimed.

2markdown.io is an independent Markdown converter and editor with browser-side tools, local extraction for native-text PDFs, and optional AI/OCR processing for scanned or complex documents. Its November 2026 plans list Basic free, Pro at US$9 monthly or US$60 annually, and Team at US$15 per seat monthly or US$120 annually, with capped AI/OCR credits and public-link conversions. The privacy policy distinguishes local conversion from consented remote OCR that can use temporary Cloudflare R2 storage and external AI providers. The pricing FAQ and Terms use different refund wording. The .com domain currently redirects to pdf.md, and the reviewed sources do not establish a connection between that domain and 2markdown.io. The public evidence reviewed does not substantiate calling 2markdown.io a scam.

## What 2markdown.io is

2markdown.io presents itself as a Markdown-focused collection of converters and editing tools. Its homepage groups tools into conversion to Markdown, conversion from Markdown, web-to-Markdown, tables and data, and Markdown utilities. The listed formats include PDF, HTML, DOCX, EPUB, LaTeX, images, CSV, JSON, Excel, RTF and Markdown itself; public-link workflows cover a selection of social and publishing platforms. The site advertises 37 tools, although a count of tools is not a measure of extraction accuracy or feature depth.

The intended job is practical: take a document or supported public page, make a Markdown representation, inspect it, then reuse it in notes, documentation, publishing or an AI workflow. This is a converter and viewer, not a document-management system, source-of-truth archive, general-purpose crawler or developer API. The pricing FAQ explicitly says API access is postponed and batch processing is not included. A buyer who needs automated ingestion should not infer those capabilities from the number of conversion pages or from the product’s AI-oriented positioning.


## The domain question: 2markdown.io versus 2markdown.com

The domain is a material identity check because the catalog contains similarly named products. At review time, opening 2markdown.com redirected to pdf.md, whose visible page said Coming soon. That is not the same destination as 2markdown.io. The 2markdown.io About page describes its own independent developer and links to a GitHub profile, but the evidence reviewed did not establish that the operator of 2markdown.io controls or is affiliated with the .com domain.

Treat the domains as separate unless the seller provides verifiable evidence that connects them. Follow the address shown in the browser before entering payment or document data; check the merchant name on hosted checkout and the support address in the terms. If a link, invoice or referral claims to be for 2markdown but lands on a different domain, pause and verify directly with contact@2markdown.io. A name resemblance is not proof of shared ownership, and this review does not transfer claims about one domain to the other.


## Browser-side PDF conversion and its limits

For a PDF with a selectable text layer, the product page says its standard path uses PDF.js in the browser to extract text and rebuild it as editable Markdown. The file should remain on the user’s device for that route, according to the privacy and product disclosures. The page describes an upload-and-edit workflow with preview, copy and Markdown download. This can suit ordinary reports, notes or specifications where readable text and basic structure matter more than visual fidelity.

The page is careful about what may not survive. Dense or merged tables, cross-page layouts, math, formulas, charts, images, handwriting and PDF hyperlinks require extra review or may not be rebuilt. A converted table should be checked cell-by-cell against the source when numbers matter. Markdown is a textual representation, not a faithful copy of page geometry. If the document has only scanned images, the normal text-layer route cannot read it; the site detects that case and directs users to a separate AI/OCR path. Do not treat the output as an audited transcription.


## AI/OCR is an opt-in processing path

Scanned or complex files have a different privacy path. The Privacy Policy says AI/OCR mode asks for confirmation before a file is sent. After consent, a PDF is uploaded to temporary Cloudflare R2 storage with a short-lived signed URL and forwarded through configured AI/OCR providers, which may include OpenRouter and selected model or parser services such as Google Gemini. The resulting Markdown returns to the browser; the policy says the file is deleted from R2 after conversion, with a one-hour fallback expiry.

That disclosure is more useful than a blanket private label because it distinguishes the default local route from remote processing. It also means the word local does not describe every conversion. Third-party providers apply their own terms to material they process. Before sending a confidential, regulated or client document, review the current provider and retention terms, remove information that is unnecessary, and confirm you are authorized to upload it. If remote processing is unacceptable, do not select AI/OCR mode.


## What the privacy policy does and does not promise

The privacy page says the service does not require an account, name or email for use, does not run advertising trackers or fingerprinting scripts, and does not retain native PDF contents or output. It separately describes Google Analytics for aggregate site measurement and Cloudflare analytics and operational logs, which can include request metadata such as IP addresses and timestamps. It says file contents, filenames and conversion results are not sent to Google Analytics.

These are published policy statements, not an independent audit. For AI/OCR, the same policy says a file or extracted text may pass through an external provider before returning to the browser, while the company’s own temporary R2 copy is deleted after completion or fallback expiry. Users should read the complete live policy rather than relying on a homepage tagline such as no data stored by default. That phrase needs the words by default: consented remote conversion involves temporary processing and infrastructure logs.


## Tools, supported formats and real workflow boundaries

The tool directory spans common document conversions, data tables, web links and editing. That breadth can make a single-purpose browser tool convenient when a user needs a one-off output without installing a desktop package. A viewer lets users inspect Markdown, and the product pages describe preview and export paths for several formats. However, availability of a tool page should not be read as proof that every format pair has equal fidelity, that a conversion preserves all metadata, or that the resulting file is ready for publication without review.

The site’s own PDF guidance names limitations for tables, image assets, hyperlinks, math and scanned pages. Each format has its own failure modes: HTML cleanup can remove scripts and styles by design; data-table conversions can lose typing or cell interpretation; and links to public platforms may expose only limited text. Start with a non-sensitive representative sample. Compare headings, lists, tables, links, special characters and numbers with the source. Keep the original file and treat the converted Markdown as a draft until someone checks it.


## Public-link conversions and platform variability

Paid plans list public-link conversions for sources such as YouTube, WeChat, Bilibili, Twitter/X, podcast pages or RSS, TikTok/Douyin, Xiaohongshu, Reddit and LinkedIn. The pricing FAQ warns that some pages do not expose transcripts, article text or comments reliably. In those cases the tool may return metadata and warnings instead of a complete article. That caveat matters: a link conversion is bounded by what the source makes accessible and what the product can retrieve at that moment.

The Terms prohibit using public-link tools to bypass paywalls, login walls, access controls, DRM, platform restrictions or third-party terms. That is a clear use boundary and should guide teams building research workflows. Do not use the converter to obtain material that is not legitimately accessible to you, or assume public visibility gives permission to republish. If output is metadata-only, that is an honest limitation rather than a successful full-text extraction. Confirm each platform’s current terms and permissions independently.


## Notion and Google Docs workflows: verify the current state

The Pro and Team plan descriptions include Notion and Google Docs Markdown workflows, while the Basic tier lists limited beta access. The pricing FAQ adds an important qualification: the current workflows are included, but OAuth and deeper account integrations are still treated as future product work. Buyers should therefore confirm the exact supported actions before assuming that a page can read or write a private account, sync continuously, or preserve all document metadata.

A workflow that handles a public or exported document differs from an authenticated integration that can access a private workspace. Ask what permissions are requested, whether the user authorizes each connection, what data is transferred and how to revoke access. If the task can be done by export and import, test that simpler path first. Avoid granting broad workspace access solely because a plan table names Notion or Google Docs; verify the actual in-product flow and scope at the time of purchase.


## 2markdown pricing: free, Pro and Team

The pricing page checked November 18, 2026 lists Basic at no charge, Pro at US$9 billed monthly or US$60 annually, and Team at US$15 per seat monthly or US$120 per seat annually. The annual equivalents are US$5 per month for Pro and US$10 per seat for Team when paid yearly. Team is presented as a sales-assisted plan. Prices, taxes, payment methods and availability can change; confirm the hosted checkout total before starting a subscription.

Basic includes browser-side Markdown tools with fair use, the viewer and editor, 25 AI/OCR credits per month, and limited beta access to public-link, Notion and Google Docs workflows. Pro lists 1,000 monthly AI/OCR credits, 500 content-link conversions, supported public platforms, and standard email support. Team lists 2,000 AI/OCR credits and 2,000 link conversions per seat per month, a shared usage pool, team seats and priority email support. Pro and Team also remove ads.


## Credits and conversion quotas are separate meters

AI/OCR credits and public-link conversions are distinct allowances. The pricing FAQ says credits cover higher-cost AI-assisted work such as OCR-heavy PDFs or complex extraction, while browser-side Markdown tools are separate from that pool. Pro and Team have monthly credit and link-conversion limits; Basic has 25 AI/OCR credits and only limited fair-use link access. A user who mostly converts native PDFs locally may value the free plan differently from someone processing scanned records or many public links.

The product says paid AI/OCR should stop at the monthly cap and reset in the next billing cycle; additional credit purchases are described as a future option, not a current entitlement. That makes a small pilot useful: estimate how often files trigger remote OCR, how many links return useful content, and which teammates consume the pool. Do not equate a conversion with a successful complete extraction, and do not budget on an assumption of unlimited use. Keep a contingency process for months when the cap is reached.


## Refund and cancellation language needs a pre-purchase check

There is a policy inconsistency buyers should resolve before paying. The pricing FAQ describes refunds for a new monthly subscription within seven days of the first payment and a new yearly subscription within fourteen days, subject to a condition that no more than ten percent of the included monthly AI/OCR credits or content-link conversions has been used. It also lists exclusions for renewals, unused credits, partial periods and abuse-related closures.

The Terms of Service instead say refund requests are reviewed case by case and may be declined for substantial credit use, policy violations or abuse; they do not repeat the precise seven-day and fourteen-day thresholds. The pages may be intended to work together, but the relationship is not explained clearly. Save the pricing and Terms pages at checkout and ask support to confirm which rule controls if a refund becomes relevant. The pricing FAQ says cancellation starts in Account > Billing and may open Creem; the Terms say to use the customer portal when available or contact support.


## Who operates it and how to contact support

The About page describes 2markdown.io as independently built and maintained by a developer based in Singapore, and points to a GitHub profile. It lists contact@2markdown.io for general, support, refund, cancellation and business questions. The Terms say the service is governed by Singapore law and aim for support responses within three business days. For a small utility, a direct operator and a published contact can be sufficient for individual use, but it is not a substitute for a vendor review. Organizations should establish who contracts with them, how invoices are issued, what happens to submitted content, where subprocessors act, and whether the service provides any required security or data-processing agreement. Do not infer enterprise assurances from the presence of a Team tier. The current plan page offers priority email support for Team but does not, by itself, specify a contractual service level.


## Security and responsible handling checklist

The browser-first approach can reduce unnecessary file transfers for supported native PDFs, but it does not remove ordinary browser, device or account-security risks. The service’s AI/OCR route introduces an external processing chain by design. The Terms also prohibit malicious files, abusive bulk conversions and attempts to circumvent limits. For business use, classify documents before upload and allow only staff who have the right to process them to use remote OCR.

A prudent evaluation should verify the selected mode before each sensitive conversion, test what happens when consent is declined, inspect the resulting output locally, and record which provider is named for AI/OCR. Use a scrubbed test document first. Confirm rate limits and caps, decide how employees should report a mistaken upload, and review current privacy, Terms and provider notices at procurement. These are recommended buyer checks, not security tests Roseram performed. We did not inspect the application’s code, infrastructure configuration, deletion logs or penetration-test reports.


## How it compares with Pandoc and other approaches

For a command-line document pipeline, Pandoc and other local conversion tools may be a better fit. They require installation and more technical setup, but can be scripted and integrated into developer workflows. 2markdown emphasizes an approachable browser interface, preview, common file conversions and a choice to use opt-in AI/OCR when ordinary text extraction is not enough. The right choice depends on repeatability, supported format pairs, privacy requirements and how much manual review the output needs.

A standalone PDF-to-Markdown converter may be cheaper when only one format is needed, while a note-taking or document platform’s built-in exporter can avoid a second processor. For repeatable large-scale ingestion, ask about API and batch support: the pricing page says API access is postponed and batch conversion is not currently implemented. Compare the cost per usable output, not only the monthly subscription. Include time spent correcting tables, formulas and metadata, plus the operational cost of running a local tool when assessing alternatives.


## Who may benefit from 2markdown.io

The product may suit an individual researcher, writer or developer who needs occasional Markdown conversion and values a browser-based workflow. The free tier can be enough for native text-layer PDFs and simple editing, while paid plans add defined allowances for OCR and public links. People preparing clean source material for notes or an AI workflow may appreciate being able to preview and revise the Markdown before reuse. The privacy disclosures make the local-versus-remote distinction explicit enough for a careful user to make a choice.

It may be a poor fit for an organization that requires a documented API, guaranteed batch processing, contractual uptime, a formal data-processing agreement or a thoroughly audited OCR chain. It is also risky for users who intend to upload regulated or confidential documents without reviewing provider terms. Someone converting complex scientific or financial PDFs should budget for manual comparison, because the product itself warns that formulas, complex tables, images and hyperlinks may not be preserved. A focused sample test will reveal more than a broad tool count.


## Questions to answer before subscribing

Before paying for Pro or Team, estimate your workload using actual examples: native PDFs, scanned PDFs, long documents, public links and team seats. Find out which jobs consume AI/OCR credits, what qualifies as a link conversion, how failed or metadata-only results count, whether plan limits reset monthly and what happens when a shared Team pool is depleted. Confirm the currencies, billing cadence, tax treatment and merchant name at hosted checkout.

Then ask support to resolve the refund language difference between the pricing FAQ and Terms, and to clarify what the current Notion and Google Docs workflows can access. For any private workspace connection, inspect the consent screen and requested permissions before granting access. For sensitive files, verify which provider handles AI/OCR and whether your organization permits that route. Keep a copy of the current policy pages and your checkout terms. None of these questions implies misconduct; they are ordinary checks for a service that processes documents and offers usage-capped subscriptions.
