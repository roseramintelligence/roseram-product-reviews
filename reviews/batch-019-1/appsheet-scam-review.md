## Spreadsheet familiarity does not remove data-design work

The homepage emphasizes connecting existing data and using spreadsheet-like expressions for advanced logic. That can make an operational prototype approachable to someone who already understands the underlying records. However, a spreadsheet's convenient layout may not be a reliable data model. An equipment register, for example, needs stable equipment identifiers, clear ownership and a way to distinguish a new inspection from a correction to an old one. Those decisions matter before the application starts generating attractive forms and dashboards.

Start with a small sample containing awkward but realistic cases: missing fields, repeated names and records that should no longer be edited. Decide which source is authoritative when two systems disagree. These are proposed preparation steps, not claims that AppSheet handles every example in a particular way. The product can reduce interface work while the business still needs to define what each record means. Without that definition, an easier form can simply make inconsistent data easier to enter at greater speed.


## AI-assisted creation needs requirement-based review

AppSheet's homepage describes using Gemini to help create and power applications. The pricing FAQ says AI-assisted app creation is available to users with paid Google Workspace licenses. That is a specific entitlement statement, not proof that every free prototype account or every plan includes the same AI capabilities. Confirm the organization's actual license and the particular AI function required. Do not infer availability from a promotional example without checking the account that will own the project.

Generated structure should be inspected against the business process. If a maintenance request needs supervisor approval before purchase, the application must preserve that distinction rather than merely display a status field. Write down permitted transitions and who can perform them. AI can help draft an implementation, but the saved pages do not establish the correctness of a generated application for this scenario. No AI request was made for this review, and no claim is made about generated accuracy or time savings for a new customer.


## Free prototyping is not unrestricted production deployment

The captured pricing page offers building and testing with up to ten users at no cost, followed by a paid plan when deploying more widely. Its FAQ encourages testing features during the prototype phase. That is useful for establishing whether the workflow makes sense, but a prototype allowance should not be treated as a permanent production entitlement for any size of team. Identify the intended launch population and distinguish test participants from employees, contractors and external users who will depend on the finished application.

Use the prototype to answer concrete questions rather than merely collect positive reactions. Can a field worker complete the form with the available connectivity? Can an approver distinguish a duplicate request? Can a former participant still open records? A ten-person evaluation can expose these issues without pretending to measure organization-wide performance. Record the unanswered questions before expanding. This review did not create such a prototype, so the proposed evaluation is not evidence of actual behavior in an AppSheet account.


## Per-user pricing requires a user-count model

The captured table lists Starter at $5 per user per month, Core at $10 and Enterprise Plus at $20. It also says Core is included in most paid Google Workspace plans, while related FAQ wording refers to some Workspace subscriptions. That combination is a reason to check the exact Workspace edition and purchase arrangement rather than assume inclusion from a general company account. The displayed figures are source observations, not a current quotation or a complete bill for every deployment.

The FAQ distinguishes signed-in people from guest or external use. It explains that an unsigned employee opening an application on two devices can count as two guest users, with device settings potentially generating another guest identity. That is commercially important for shared devices, contractors and public-facing workflows. Model the expected access pattern before estimating cost. A spreadsheet listing employees alone may not capture the licensed population if the application also permits unsigned or external access.


## Public apps trade away important access features

The saved pricing FAQ describes Publisher Pro for publicly accessible applications without sensitive data or user sign-in, at $50 per month per app. It says these applications include Core feature sets but exclude user sign-in options and security filters. That is a meaningful architectural boundary, not merely another price tier. A public directory and an internal personnel tool have different requirements. Unlimited public users do not make the public route appropriate for confidential records.

Decide whether information is truly intended for every visitor before selecting this approach. Hiding a screen or making a link difficult to find is not a substitute for an access boundary. The saved description itself limits the public use case, so buyers should not stretch it to avoid per-user licensing for sensitive work. This review has not tested authorization. The recommendation is to choose the deployment model from the data and audience requirements, then verify its documented controls with the responsible administrator.


## Connector availability must match the selected edition

The homepage lists Google Workspace, Office 365, Dropbox and Salesforce alongside SQL databases, APIs and other enterprise connections. The pricing descriptions distinguish basic spreadsheet and file-storage connections from Enterprise Plus's broader database, API, SaaS and enterprise-data scope. A homepage list therefore does not establish that the least expensive plan supports every source. Name the exact connector, authentication arrangement and direction of data movement in the requirements before asking for a quote.

Also clarify who owns the external connection and what happens when that person changes roles. A working prototype tied to a departing employee's account can become an operating risk even if the application interface remains intact. Check whether the planned process needs immediate updates, periodic synchronization or a manual refresh, and verify that expectation with the relevant documentation. No connector was exercised here, so compatibility with an individual database, spreadsheet or API remains unverified.


## Forms and field capture need real conditions

AppSheet describes forms, photographs, signatures, locations and barcode-related capture, with the pricing material also listing offline operation and background synchronization. These functions can suit field work, but the environment matters. A warehouse scan, a remote inspection and a customer signature have different requirements for device access, connectivity and record association. Define what must be saved locally, what the user sees before synchronization and how the office knows a record has actually arrived.

A useful evaluation would include interruption during entry and disagreement between a field device and a later office edit. These are proposed scenarios, not documented results from this review. Do not promise uninterrupted field operation merely because offline support is listed. The customer pages describe remote-use cases, which helps identify relevant questions, but does not verify behavior for a new device fleet, network pattern or data source. Operational reliability needs evidence from the intended workflow.


## Automation should distinguish requests from completed actions

The homepage describes triggers, conditions, branches, notifications and connected Apps Script functions. Those components can coordinate work, but they also create state transitions that need clear definitions. A request being entered, an automation starting, a message being sent and a purchase being approved are different events. Design the visible status so users can tell which event occurred. Otherwise a notification may be mistaken for authorization, or a scheduled process may be assumed complete before its downstream action succeeds.

Plan how the team recognizes an exception and who resolves it. A duplicate submission should not silently create duplicate business obligations. A missing response from a connected service should not become an unexplained success label. The saved marketing pages do not establish the exact retry and reconciliation behavior for a particular implementation. Consult the relevant technical documentation and test before depending on an automation for time-sensitive work. No message, webhook or connected script was run for this review.


## Security capabilities are not a completed security review

AppSheet presents encryption, verification, governance and a Zero Trust approach on its homepage. The pricing comparison lists role management, security filters, device-related controls, advanced authentication and organizational governance topics. These are useful areas to investigate, but their appearance in a feature list does not prove that an individual application is correctly configured. A business must still define who may read, create, change and export each category of record, then check the selected edition and implementation against those requirements.

For example, a manager seeing a department summary does not necessarily need access to every underlying personal record. Test denied access as deliberately as permitted access, using separate roles and relevant device conditions. Treat the outcome as specific to the tested application rather than a universal certification of the platform. This article has not inspected policies, identity-provider configuration or stored data. It therefore cannot certify compliance, confidentiality or suitability for a regulated workload.


## Database limits and governance affect the growth path

The captured comparison lists AppSheet database allowances of five, ten and two hundred databases, and row allowances per database of 2,500, 2,500 and 200,000 across the displayed tiers. Those are not interchangeable with every possible connected data source's limits. A buyer should confirm the intended storage architecture and applicable allowance rather than assume that a headline enterprise capacity applies to a basic built-in database. Include record growth and retention in the estimate, not just launch-day inventory.

The enterprise material also lists lifecycle management, activity tracking, audit-related exports, transfers and team governance. These capabilities become more relevant when multiple staff members build applications. Decide who can create or share an app, how ownership transfers and how an unsupported prototype is retired. The source does not establish every operational detail or retention period. Ask for those details where required, and distinguish the availability of a governance function from evidence that the organization has actually adopted it.


## Customer stories are useful prompts, not guaranteed outcomes

The saved customer page describes uses including transformer replacement tracking, race registration, remote pump installations and distributed organizational work. Those examples help explain why forms, mobile access and synchronization matter. They do not demonstrate that an unrelated company can reproduce the same implementation speed or business effect. The stories are selected and published by the vendor. This review has not interviewed those customers, inspected their applications or independently verified their reported results.

Use a relevant story to develop an evaluation question: what information was captured, who maintained the process and what happened when connectivity failed? Avoid turning a quoted success into a forecast for your own team. No new testimonial is supplied here. A useful buying decision should connect the platform's documented capabilities to a small, observable pilot and a clear ownership model. That provides stronger evidence than borrowing another organization's outcome without knowing its resources or constraints.
