## Core Transducer Catalog and Acoustic Frequency Range

The primary focus of the AdvantageTMS product lineup centers on airborne ultrasonic sensing devices. The documented portfolio encompasses 42 product families and OEM configurations, spanning an operating frequency envelope from 25 kHz up to 320 kHz for standard air-ultrasonic models, with specialty variants extending higher. Typical beam angles across these constructions range from narrow 7-degree directional patterns to wide 125-degree dispersion fields. These acoustic devices serve common industrial and commercial use cases, including non-contact tank level measurement, distance ranging, automated obstacle detection, guided robotics, and material analysis.

Acoustic components are split across distinct structural constructions to handle diverse target characteristics and ambient operating conditions. For clean, indoor, or temperature-stable operations, the catalog presents open-face transmitter and receiver pairs, such as the 250ST/R160 operating at 25 kHz and the 400ST/R160 at 40 kHz. These designs feature exposed resonant elements that provide high sound pressure levels and receiving sensitivity. Conversely, for demanding environments exposed to moisture, airborne dirt, or automotive washdown, the catalog lists closed-face pairs like the 400ET/R250 and stainless-steel variants such as the 400ET/R18S, which enclose the active element inside a sealed housing to defend against mechanical contamination.

In addition to separate dual-element pairs, the catalog covers single-element pulse transceivers that alternately ping and listen within a shared housing. Devices such as the 400PT160 and the wide-band 480EP900 reduce physical space requirements in compact obstacle-detection systems. The catalog also indexes matching-layer transceivers, including the 080SR365 and 320SR093, which use silicone-rubber or resin faces to manage acoustic impedance matching with air. These matching layers shorten transducer ring-down time, effectively shrinking the sensing blind spot or dead zone down to small distances for close-range measurement.

## Practical Hardware Workflow and Breadboard Testing

To understand how a design group would interact with components sourced from AdvantageTMS, consider a concrete engineering scenario: designing an outdoor liquid storage tank level monitor. An engineering team evaluating the catalog might select the closed-face 400ET/R250 pair or the 400PT160 sealed transceiver to protect internal circuitry against humidity and rain. To step up low battery voltages to adequate drive amplitudes, the design requires an impedance-matching transformer from the K4000001 family, alongside the PW0268 sonar ASIC to handle variable gain amplification as echoes attenuate across several meters.

A rigorous hardware evaluation workflow starts long before soldering parts to a custom printed circuit board. First, the buyer submits an RFQ requesting physical engineering samples, verified factory datasheets, and pin-out drawings. Upon receiving samples, the engineering group sets up bench testing using a calibrated acoustic test fixture. They drive the transmitter using burst pulses from a signal generator while capturing the receiver's millivolt output on an oscilloscope. The team measures sound pressure levels, verifies resonance frequency centering, and observes the acoustic ring-down duration to determine the minimum measurable target distance.

Next, the evaluation moves into an environmental stress chamber to observe thermal drift. Because piezoelectric ceramic capacitance and acoustic propagation speeds vary across temperature, the engineers document performance across extreme cold and high heat. If the transducer face exhibits excessive resonant frequency shifting, drive frequencies must be dynamically retuned or matching transformers re-tapped. Only after confirming that the physical samples consistently achieve the required detection range across environmental extremes would the engineering group consider finalizing schematic capture and signing volume purchase agreements.
